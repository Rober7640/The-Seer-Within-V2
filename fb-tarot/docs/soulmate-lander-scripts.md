# Soulmate lander scripts — tests A and B

Every bubble of the 19 soulmate landers written for `docs/fb-ad-test-queue.md` tests **A**
(soulmate × age) and **B** (soulmate × keyword), with the finding each read lands and the bans
it carries. Written to the Natural Tarot-Cut (`fb-tarot/docs/natural-tarot-cut.md`).

Seven cuts per card, three cards per lander, folded to four registry beats at wiring time —
beat 3 carries cuts 3–6, joined by newlines, and Version B serves them as separate chat
messages with a typing pause between each.

| Cut | Beat | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | **PICTURE** — one or two details literally on the art |
| 2 | 2 | **BRIDGE** — her question back, plus the card in plain English |
| 3 | 3 | **ANSWER** — answers her deepest fear, flat |
| 4 | 3 | **HIDDEN** — something she could not see |
| 5 | 3 | **TURN** — why the behaviour does not match the answer |
| 6 | 3 | **RECOGNITION** — the specific thing she has actually lived |
| 7 | 4 | **NEXT** — a narrow obstruction, aimed at the cause |

## Why these exist

Every destination in test A's table was an **existing** lander asking a different question —
the page prints `HEADLINES[hook]`, so pointing a new ad at an old hook puts one question in
the ad and another on the page. Test B's keyword arms had no landers at all. These 19 close
that gap.

## The two rules that shaped every read

**Her as the subject.** The `return-mhf` figures are all male, so leaning on one as the subject
of the read silently turns a soulmate lander into reunion copy — a reader hears a specific man
who is stalled or on his way back. The first drafts of test A did it in 36 of 144 middle
bubbles. Cut 1 is exempt: describing the art is its job.

**Directional bans.** The half she came for is allowed; the half with a victim is not. On these
families that means the hopeful answer may be given flat, but never attached to a date, a
duration, a place, a real person, or a claim about someone who has died.
