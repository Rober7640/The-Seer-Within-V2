# Bubble 1 — the CLAIM, card by card

Bubble 1 of the six-beat shape names the card and makes **one claim she cannot check yet**.
Bubble 2, the PROOF, then points at a real detail in the art and settles it.

**Claim, then proof.** She reads bubble 2 to find out what bubble 1 meant.

The operator chose this over a stock line (*"she's the warmest card in this deck"*) knowing the
cost: it needs a fresh claim per card, and per question. A stock line can be pasted anywhere,
and by the twentieth lander it sounds like it was.

---

## When a claim is working

> **Delete bubble 2. If the claim still makes complete sense on its own, it is too explicit.**

That is the whole test. A claim has to leave a gap.

| | |
|---|---|
| ✗ **too explicit** | *"your hand went to the card with a cup, a coin, a blade and a wand laid out on the table"* — that IS bubble 2. There is nothing left to prove, and no reason to read on |
| ✓ **working** | *"your hand went to the one who came ready"* — ready how? Bubble 2 answers it |

**The best claims mildly contradict what she is looking at**, so bubble 2 resolves the tension.
The Hanged Man is the clearest case: she is staring at a man strung up by one ankle, and the
claim calls him the calmest man in the deck. She has to read on to find out how that can be
true — and the answer is his face, which is exactly what the card is for.

Three rules that do not bend:

1. **The claim must be provable by a detail really on the card.** Never invent one. Every proof
   below is drawn from `client/src/content/tarotReads.ts`, where it is already in shipped copy.
2. **Bubble 1 is about the card and her choice. Nothing else.** No facts about her life — that
   is beat 3's job and beat 4's, under much tighter rules.
3. **Check `symbols.md` for the deck before picking a proof.** Some details are banned outright
   (the Fool's cliff, the Magician's snake belt) and some only work for certain questions.

## The two decks reach differently

| Deck | Facing | Verb | The framing |
|---|---|---|---|
| `return-mhf` | **down** | **You turned** | She reached for a **back**. She could not see it. The registry calls this *"the moment your hand reached for it"* |
| `arcana-eef` | up | You chose | She looked at three faces and picked one. *"the moment your eyes went to it"* |

🔴 **The face-down reach is the stronger claim, not the weaker one.** She could not have known
what she was reaching for, so "your hand found" carries real weight on `return-mhf` and is only
a figure of speech on `arcana-eef`. Use it.

🔴 **THE UNCANNY REACH IS THE DEFAULT — *"You couldn't see it, and your hand still went to…"***
Corrected 2026-08-24. This section used to list four openers as equal and say "vary the opener,
because one phrasing across a hundred landers is a tell." Written across 37 landers that
produced a **four-position rotation wheel**: *"Of the three backs"* ×53, *"Three backs, and"*
×37, *"Three the same"* ×9, *"Three backs, all the same"* ×6 — distributed evenly, with nothing
about the card, the question or the woman deciding which she got. It is the first sentence she
reads and it was the most obviously generated thing in the batch.

The four are not equal. Three describe **the deck**. One describes **her doing something she
cannot explain** — and that is the only one that is a hook. Beat 1's job is the uncanny reach:
she could not see the card, and her hand still went to the one that answers her question.

Vary the *wording* of that reach, never swap it for deck furniture.

# `return-mhf` — face DOWN — "You turned"

## I · The Magician — *will and intention*

⚠ Ration the four-objects proof. It is the card's strongest detail and it wants to open every
Magician lander; three in a row nearly did. Alternate with the others.

| | Claim | Proved by | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** ⭐ | You turned the Magician, dear. Of the three backs, your hand found the one who came ready. | the cup, coin, blade and wand already laid out on his table | anything asking whether she is enough / has done enough |
| **2** | You turned the Magician, dear. Three backs, all the same — and you reached for the one with nothing put away. | his table stands on the grass, out in the open, and the workbench is full | trust and honesty; "what am I not being told" |
| **3** | You turned the Magician, dear. You couldn't see it, and your hand still went to the worker of the three. | both his hands are busy and neither is resting | money; effort that hasn't paid |

**Recommended: 1.** *"Came ready"* is the claim that begs the question, and the table answers it
in one glance. Use 3 on money landers, where the proof needs to be about work rather than tools.

## XII · The Hanged Man — *the pause and a new angle*

⚠ The picture alarms people. Bubble 1 has a second job on this card: get ahead of it. All three
claims below do that by promising something the picture appears to contradict.

| | Claim | Proved by | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** ⭐ | You turned the Hanged Man, dear. Of the three backs, your hand found the calmest man in the deck. | his face — completely calm, and Waite put it there to say exactly this | anything where she fears something is wrong with her |
| **2** | You turned the Hanged Man, dear. Three backs, and you reached for the one held by a single thing. | one rope, at one ankle, and the rest of him hangs loose | block-native questions; "what is stopping this" |
| **3** | You turned the Hanged Man, dear. Your hand went to the only card here that's still growing. | the living wood he hangs from, with green leaves on it | waiting, delay, "how much longer" |

**Recommended: 1.** It is the strongest claim-and-proof pair on either deck, because the claim
openly disagrees with what she is looking at and bubble 2 wins the argument.

## 0 · The Fool — *new beginnings*

⛔ The cliff edge proves nothing good — see `symbols.md`. None of the proofs below touch it.
⚠ The little white dog is a **warning**, not a companion. Never claim it as a friend at his heel.

| | Claim | Proved by | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** ⭐ | You turned the Fool, dear. Three backs, and you reached for the one carrying almost nothing. | all he owns tied in one small bundle on a stick | "do I need to be more / fix myself / have it sorted first" |
| **2** | You turned the Fool, dear. Of the three backs, your hand found the one already moving. | his feet — one already moving, the other still on the ground | "when", timing, "has it started" |
| **3** | You turned the Fool, dear. You couldn't see it, and your hand still went to the only card here in full sun. | the sky is gold and there's not one cloud in it | "is it too late" — the picture answers before the words do |

**Recommended: 1**, and **3 whenever the ad hints at too late**. A woman who asks whether she has
run out of time is looking at broad daylight, and that lands before any sentence does.

# `arcana-eef` — face UP — "You chose"

## IV · The Emperor — *authority and structure*

⚠ Two details on this card are context-dependent and have already caused near-misses:
**the bare grey mountain** (nothing grows on it — it proves the opposite on any "will someone
come" question) and **his long white beard** (to a woman asking *when*, a very old man reads as
*you'll be old first*). Both are fine on trust and money landers.

| | Claim | Proved by | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** ⭐ | You chose the Emperor, dear. Of the three, your eyes went to the one that doesn't move. | his stone chair, and he doesn't leave it | "is this real / will it last / can I count on it" |
| **2** | You chose the Emperor, dear. Yours is the one of the three holding something solid. | the gold ball in his left hand | money; anything about what she is owed |
| **3** | You chose the Emperor, dear. You went to the only one of the three wearing armour. | armour on his legs, under the robe | trust, honesty, "what is he hiding" |

**Recommended: 1.**

## III · The Empress — *warmth and abundance*

⛔ **The red fruit on her gown is pomegranates — fertility.** Central to the card and unusable
here: this audience is 55+ and fertility reads as pregnancy. Never point at it.

| | Claim | Proved by | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** ⭐ | You chose the Empress, dear. Of the three, your hand went to the one that's already full. | the field of wheat at her feet, gold and grown | "when is it coming / why hasn't it come" |
| **2** | You chose the Empress, dear. Yours is the only card of the three with a heart drawn on it. | the shield by her feet carries a heart | love, feelings, "does he" |
| **3** | You chose the Empress, dear. Of the three, your eyes went to the only one sitting back. | she rests on soft cushions | "am I doing enough / should I try harder" |

**Recommended: 1**, and **2 on any love lander** — it is the most literally checkable claim on
either deck. She can count the hearts.

## 0 · The Fool — *new beginnings*

Same card as `return-mhf`, so the same proofs hold. Only the verb and the reach change.

| | Claim | Proved by |
|---|---|---|
| **1** ⭐ | You chose the Fool, dear. Of the three, your hand went to the one already on its way. | his feet, mid-step |
| **2** | You chose the Fool, dear. Yours is the one of the three carrying almost nothing. | the small bundle on a stick |
| **3** | You chose the Fool, dear. Of the three, your eyes went to the only card in full sun. | a gold sky with not one cloud in it |

## Where this was hard

**The Magician.** Its usable proofs nearly all say the same thing — *he has what he needs* — so
the three claims above are closer to each other than on any other card. The symbol table already
flags the collision risk; this is the same problem seen from the claim side. If the Magician is
carrying a lot of landers, this file will need more distinct proofs than the art comfortably
gives.

**The Empress, once fertility is off the table.** Her strongest symbols are the wheat and the
pomegranates, and one of those is banned outright for this audience. The heart shield is a good
third, but the card is thinner in practice than it looks.
