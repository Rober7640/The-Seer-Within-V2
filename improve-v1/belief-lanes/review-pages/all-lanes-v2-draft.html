Four decisions before anything is built

D1 · "By morning." The ad says "by morning there's a letter. Five pages." The chat promises "Within 24 hours, you'll receive a personalized 5-7 page reading via email." Five pages fits inside 5-7, so page count is fine. Timing is the real gap. Delivery runs on Mike's n8n flow, and I couldn't see how long it takes. Recommendation: ask Mike for the usual and the slowest time from purchase to email. If nearly every letter lands within about 12 hours, change the lane pitch to "By morning, my letter will be with you" and keep "by morning" on the pages. If not, the pages say "she writes at night" and promise no delivery time. Details

D2 · The ad implies the letter is free. "It's free to message Evelyn. She asks your first name, and by morning, there's a letter." Read together, that sounds like a free letter, and the $35 offer comes about 15 messages later. The pages fix this with one plain line: "Messaging Evelyn is free. If she offers to write your letter, she'll tell you plainly what it involves before you say yes." Recommendation: keep it. It's the only place the page can keep the "never imply the letter is free" rule without saying "price."

D3 · How Evelyn asks for the email. The brief says to frame it as where her letter will be sent. Said flatly, that promises a letter before she has paid. Recommendation: make it conditional: "If I sit down to write to you tonight, dear... where should the letter go?"

D4 · Lane identity (for the build). Recommendation: keep the lanes inside the existing /fb funnel (same pixel, same "- FB" Stripe products, same reports) and add one new lane tag. Registering three new funnels would change product names and split every report. Build notes

Every break between the ads and that experience

  1. Meta may be reading "psychic chat, reveal your destiny." If its crawler doesn't run JavaScript, that title and description are all it gets for every ad. They use a banned word, sell prediction, and say nothing about manifesting, the pattern or family. Even with JavaScript, the page talks about an "energy field."
  2. "A free reading." The page says the reading is free. The ad only says messaging is free. The chat later sells that reading for $35. This breaks the "never imply the letter is free" rule outright.
  3. Her name comes late. The ad said "Send your first name." Today's button says "Show Me What's Blocking My Path," and Evelyn asks for her name six bubbles in, after the city and "veil" lines. The lane pages keep the button and the flow. The button now says "Send your first name to Evelyn," and the name is Evelyn's second message.
  4. A different Evelyn. The ad's Evelyn writes letters by candlelight and finds where it started. The page's Evelyn "detected a disturbance in your energy field."
  5. "Dear friend." This is the first thing a woman hears straight after a story about being introduced as "my friend."
  6. The opener runs long and generic. City, "veil between worlds," "open the connection." The ad's Evelyn opens with one short line that lands.
  7. Topic buttons ask what the ad already told us: it's love.
  8. Email ask. "Where should I send them if more is revealed?" talks about visions rather than the letter, and comes right after an ad that said first name and nothing else.
  9. Scarcity and proof lines ("limited number of connections", "Trusted By 2,400+ Clients") aren't in the ad. Variant a says 1,111+ instead, so the number isn't something we can stand behind. The lane pages leave both out.
  10. Word and timing: "reading" vs "letter", "within 24 hours" vs "by morning", "5-7 page" vs "five pages". Only the timing is a real conflict (D1).
D1 in detail

"By morning, five pages" vs the pitch

  • The chat (both pitch arms): "Within 24 hours, you'll receive a personalized 5-7 page reading via email." The offer card: "Your personalized 5-7 page reading, within 24 hours."
  • The house rule elsewhere: the 04 tea-reading copy says outright: 'Never "by morning", "tomorrow" or "tonight" about the reading', because its delivery is "within 24 hours."
  • Who delivers: Mike's n8n flow, triggered by Stripe metadata product = energy_clearing_ritual. The repo doesn't hold its timing, and the n8n connector in this session lists no workflows, so I couldn't measure how fast the letter actually arrives.
  • Page count: "Five pages" sits inside "5-7." That's no conflict.

Recommendation. Get the real purchase-to-email time from Mike, both the usual and the slowest.
If almost all letters land within about 12 hours: change the lane pitch line to "By morning, my letter will be with you. Five pages, sometimes more." The pages keep "By morning, it's with you."
If not: the pages swap that line for "She writes at night, by candlelight," the chat keeps "within 24 hours," and nothing promises morning delivery.
