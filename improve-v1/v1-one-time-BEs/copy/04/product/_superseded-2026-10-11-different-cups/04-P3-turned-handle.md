### And this is yours to put on a door

The reading above is the thing to read. This is the thing to look at.

Print it, or copy it onto a card, and put it somewhere you'll see it without having to look for it. The
inside of a kitchen cupboard is a good place. Not the fridge, where other people read it.

### Your twenty-one days

*Day 1 is the first evening after you read this. Cross each day off before bed.*

○ **quiet**: you start nothing, you answer warm and brief, and you leave first · ● **you send one thing**

| Days | Leaf | | The word | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1–3** | the broken heart | ○ | **QUIET** | Start nothing. Answer warm and brief, and end it first |
| **4–6** | the bird | ○ | **QUIET** | Leave first, every time. No tests, no held-back replies |
| **7–9** | the boat | ● | **ASK** | One real question about him, day 7 or 8, as its own message |
| **10–12** | the lighthouse | ○ | **AWAY** | One real evening with your fixed point, done before day 13 |
| **13–15** | the bridge | ● | **SPEAK** | **Day 14, between six and eight in the evening.** One plain thing, sent on its own |
| **16–18** | the withering tree | ○ | **QUIET** | Nothing new about you two. Answer the ordinary. Don't refer back |
| **19–21** | the butterfly | ○ | **LOOK BACK** | What changed in you. On day 21, delete the long drafts |

### How to use it on a bad night

Your bad night won't come because he's silent. It'll come because he's written, flat again, and you've
had enough of warm and brief. It'll be around day five. Another one comes on day 15 or 16, when he
hasn't mentioned what you said on day 14.

On that evening, go and look at the door. Find the day. Read the word next to it, and do that and
nothing more.

If it says QUIET, you start nothing tonight. What you're feeling is the day it is, and that day feels
exactly like this for every woman who's done these days. It isn't news about him. It's an evening with a
number on it.

Answer him warmly if he's written, end it first, and go to bed. Cross it off, because you got through
it.

### One rule

**Don't read ahead to day 14.** You'll want to start drafting it in the first week, while every flat
message is fresh. That's how the thing you send on day 14 arrives carrying all of them. Cross the days
off. Don't work ahead.

### On the twenty-second day

Take it off the door. The days are finished, whatever's happened, and a calendar left up past its last
day stops being a calendar.
