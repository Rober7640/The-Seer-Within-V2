### And this is yours to put on a door

The reading above is the thing to read. This is the thing to look at, and in your cup you'll look at it
more than anyone.

Print it, or copy it onto a card, and put it where you'll see it without going to find it. The inside of
a kitchen cupboard is where most women end up putting theirs. Not the fridge, where other people read it.

### Your twenty-one days

*Day 1 is the first evening after you read this. Cross each day off before bed.*

○ **silent**: nothing from you · ● **you send one thing** · ◐ **his move**

| Days | Leaf | | The word | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1–3** | the broken heart | ○ | **SILENT** | Nothing sent. If he writes, answer once, warm and brief |
| **4–6** | the bird | ○ | **SILENT** | Nothing sent, and no looking at when he was last online |
| **7–9** | the butterfly | ○ | **SILENT** | The case stays shut. Not even on the evening of day 9 |
| **10–12** | the withering tree | ● | **SMALL** | One thing about nothing, day 10 or 11. No question |
| **13–15** | the lighthouse | ○ | **AWAY** | Two real evenings with your fixed point. Not about him |
| **16–18** | the boat | ◐ | **HIS MOVE** | Day 16: has he written since your small message? Yes: answer, ask one thing, let him row. No: nothing |
| **19–21** | the bridge | ● | **SPEAK** | **Day 20, between six and eight in the evening.** One sentence. No question |

### How to use it on a bad night

You'll have more than one. Day five is the first: you'll have decided that silence is the same as giving
up, and you'll be sure of it. Day nine is the second, when nine days feels like enough to have made the
point. Day twelve is the third, if day 10's message hasn't been answered yet.

On each of those evenings, go and look at the door. Find the day. Read the word next to it.

If it says SILENT, you send nothing tonight, and what you're feeling is day five or day nine. Those days
feel exactly like this for every woman who's done them. If it says SMALL and yours has gone, the answer
isn't due yet. Either way it isn't news about him. It's an evening with a number on it.

Then go to bed, and cross it off, because you got through it.

### One rule

**Don't read ahead to day 20.** One sentence written on day two will be a paragraph by day 20, and it'll
be carrying all nine silent days in it. Cross the days off. Don't work ahead.

### On the twenty-second day

Take it off the door. Whatever the boat did, the days are finished, and a calendar left up past its last
day turns into something you keep checking.
