### And this is yours to put on a door

The reading above is the thing to read. This is the thing to look at.

Print it, or copy it onto a card, and put it where you'll see it without going to find it. The inside
of a kitchen cupboard is where most women end up putting theirs. Not the fridge, where other people
read it.

### Your twenty-one days

*Day 1 is the first evening after you read this. Cross each day off before bed.*

○ **quiet**: you start nothing, and you answer warm and brief, hot day or cold · ● **you send one thing**

| Days | Leaf | | The word | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1–3** | the broken heart | ○ | **QUIET** | Send nothing. Answer him warm and short, whatever his mood |
| **4–6** | the bird | ○ | **QUIET** | Start nothing. If he does, you end it first, no reason given |
| **7–9** | the withering tree | ● | **SMALL** | One thing about nothing, day 8 or 9. No question. Once |
| **10–12** | the boat | ● | **ASK** | One question about his life, day 10 or 11. Not about you two |
| **13–15** | the lighthouse | ○ | **AWAY** | One real evening with your fixed point. Not about him |
| **16–18** | the bridge | ● | **SPEAK** | **Day 17, between six and eight in the evening.** One plain thing. No explaining paragraph |
| **19–21** | the butterfly | ○ | **LOOK BACK** | What changed in you. Nothing owed to him |

### How to use it on a bad night

There'll be an evening, around day five, when you've decided you're going to send something. Not on a
whim. You'll have thought it through and it'll feel reasoned. There'll be a second one, too: the evening
after one of his warm days, when warm feels like an invitation.

On that evening, go and look at the door. Find the day. Read the one word next to it, and do that and
nothing more.

If it says QUIET, you send nothing tonight. What you're feeling is the day it is, and that day feels
exactly like this for every woman who's done these days. It isn't news about him. It's an evening with
a number on it.

Then go to bed, and cross it off, because you got through it.

### One rule

**Don't read ahead to day 17.** Drafting on day four is how the thing you send on day 17 turns up
carrying everything you felt in the first week. Cross the days off. Don't work ahead.

### On the twenty-second day

Take it off the door. Whatever's happened by then, the days are finished, and a calendar left up past its
last day turns into a monument.
