## The governing rule of this product

**Every instruction is derived from a leaf's physical property. None is prescribed as advice.**
The bird has open wings, so nothing is sent while it is in flight. The bridge spans a gap and can be
walked from either end, so she builds a short one and stands on her side. That is what keeps this a
tea reading rather than dating advice — and dating advice is what the free letter's closing
paragraph was, which is why that paragraph is banned.

# %FIRSTNAME% — your cup is turned

%FIRSTNAME%,

It's turned, and it read clean. Your leaves were still soft, which means what follows has days on

I'm going to tell you what the positions say, and then I'm going to give you the twenty-one days.

## What the cup does that the leaves couldn't

In my letter I gave you seven symbols and told you they were the story. Here is the cup.

**The handle is you.** Everything sitting near it is close to you and it is soon. Everything
opposite is far from you and belongs to somebody you haven't met. **The rim is days** — the higher
up the wall, the sooner. **The base is what's buried**, and what's buried is old.

So: the same seven symbols, and now with a where.

<!-- §10 rule 3 · a difficulty overcome -->

One difficulty, and I'll be honest about it. Your bird sat awkwardly — half on the wall, half toward
the base, and I turned the cup three times before I'd commit to it. A bird high on the wall is a
fortnight old. A bird at the base has been in flight since long before you noticed.

Yours is neither, and the answer matters, so I worked it from the tail rather than the body: the
tail was still on the wall.

**Which means the going started roughly two months ago, and it started over something that has
nothing to do with the last three weeks.** The last three weeks are what you have been reading. They
are not what happened.

That is the most useful single thing in this reading, and I want it said before the sequence,
because it changes what the sequence is for. You have not been failing to fix something recent. You
have been trying to fix a symptom with the wrong tools and blaming your own handling.

## The twenty-one days

Counting from **the first evening after you read this.** Not from a date — from tonight.

Each stretch takes its instruction from the shape of the leaf that governs it. I'll tell you the
shape as well as the instruction, so you can see where it comes from and won't have to trust me
blindly.

### Days 1–3 — the broken heart. *Say nothing.*

The heart sits against the handle, so this is you, and it is now. It is in two pieces with a gap
between them.

**A broken thing is not mended by pressing the pieces together.** That is the property, and it is
the instruction: you do not close gaps this week. Not his, not yours, not the one in the
conversation.

Send nothing. Answer if he writes, warmly and briefly, and don't extend it.

These are the hardest three days of the twenty-one and they are also the ones that do the most work.
Everything after this depends on there being a stretch of quiet for it to happen against.

### Days 4–6 — the bird. *Let the distance be distance.*

Wings open, in flight, tail still on the wall.

**A bird in flight cannot be called back; it can only be waited out or chased further.** Anything
sent in this stretch is chasing, by definition — not because of what it says, but because of when it
arrives. A man mid-flight reads all contact as pull.

So: still nothing initiated. If he initiates, be entirely normal and be the one who ends the
conversation first, without ceremony and without a reason given.

That last part is the whole of days 4 to 6.

### Days 7–9 — the withering tree. *One small ordinary thing.*

Roots toward the base — old, deep, thinning at the top rather than dying at the bottom.

**A withering tree is watered at the root, never at the leaves.** The root is the ordinary
foundation the two of you had before any of this became a subject. The leaves are the subject.

So this is where you make contact, and it is about nothing. Something you'd have sent him two years
ago without thinking: a photograph of a thing he'd find funny, a line about something you saw. No
question in it, or a question so small it needs one word back.

**Nothing about the two of you.** Not a hint, not a "hope you're okay", not a softener at the end.
That is watering the leaves and it undoes the nine days you just spent.

Once, in this stretch. Not three times.

### Days 10–12 — the boat. *An exchange, not a statement.*

Turned toward the handle — which is the good news in this cup. The weather is coming toward you
rather than away, and a boat is the one symbol here that carries two people.

**A boat needs both oars or it turns in a circle.** So this stretch is where you ask rather than
tell. If days 7 to 9 got a response, this is where there is some actual back-and-forth in it.

You ask him something about himself — not about the relationship, about *him*, about whatever the
two months has actually been about — and then you let him answer at his own length without filling
the gaps.

This is the first stretch where you find out anything. Most women find out that it was never about
them.

### Days 13–15 — the lighthouse. *Go to the fixed point.*

Opposite the handle, high — far from you in the cup, and steady.

**A lighthouse does not go to ships.** That is the property and it is the instruction, and it is not
about him at all. This is the stretch where you go to your own fixed point: the friend, the sister,
the work, whatever it was that held you level before this started and has been getting the leftovers.

Do this properly, %FIRSTNAME%, and do it whether or not days 10–12 went well. It is in the cup at
this position for a reason.

Practically: one real evening with the person or thing this is. Not to discuss him. To be somebody
other than a woman managing a situation for one evening.

Everything in the next stretch works better from a woman who has done this one.

### Days 16–18 — the bridge. *This is the week.*

Between the handle and the lighthouse, mid-wall, both ends clear.

This is what you bought. **The bridge is the stretch where what you say lands as an invitation
rather than as pressure** — and it is the only one of the seven positions where that is true.

**A bridge spans a gap, and it is walked from both ends.** So you build a short one and you stand on
your side of it. Short is the property: a long bridge is a woman crossing the whole way herself.

In practical terms, one thing said plainly and briefly. Something true about what you want, with no
case attached to it, no history, and no request for a response.

Say it and then stop. Don't add the paragraph that explains it. **The explaining paragraph is the
bridge he walks away over** — it is the thing I warned you about in my letter, and it is the single
commonest way this stretch gets wasted.

If there is a message you have been drafting and rewriting for weeks, this is its week. Cut it to a
third of its length first.

### Days 19–21 — the butterfly. *Look at what changed in you.*

Rim, highest, nearest.

**A butterfly's change is finished before it is visible.** The work happened inside, out of sight,
and the emerging is just the last part of it.

This stretch is not about him and there is no instruction toward him in it. Look back at the
twenty-one days and notice what is different in how you did them — you will have gone about three
weeks without composing at two in the morning, and you will have found out whether you can.

Whatever he does or doesn't do, that part is yours and it doesn't reverse.

## Your cup: the Divided Rim

<!-- BRANCH — swap this block per her 04-C1 self-diagnosis. Default: Divided Rim -->

You told me it runs hot and cold, and that you have looked for the pattern and can't find it.

There isn't one. That is the finding, and it is worth having on its own.

A divided rim means the variation is in **him** and is being driven by something in his own week
that you are not being told about. You have been treating his temperature as a readout on you, and
running the last three weeks over and over looking for what you did differently on the cold days.

You did nothing differently. There is nothing to find.

This is why your quiet stretches are the long ones. A hot-and-cold pattern is fed by response — warm
days get rewarded with contact, cold days get met with reaching, and both teach him that his
temperature sets yours. Nine days of level, unbothered normality is what breaks that, and it is the
only thing that does.

## Your cup: the Turned Handle

<!-- BRANCH · she picked "he answers, but flat" -->

You told me he answers — promptly enough — and that there is nothing in it.

A turned handle means the contact is intact and the *weight* has gone out of it. That is a
different situation from the one most women in your position are in, and it is the better one to be
in, though it does not feel like it. Nothing is broken. Something has been switched off.

The finding is this: **you have been reading his tone for evidence, and tone is the one thing that
goes flat first and means least.** A man who is elsewhere in himself still answers messages. He
answers them exactly like that.

So your sequence changes shape. **Contact already exists, which means you do not have to build
it — you have to change what it carries.**

- **Days 1–6 stay quiet, and they are the hardest** for you specifically. Not answering is easy when
  he isn't writing; it is much harder when he is, and answering flatly back is what most women do
  here. Answer warmly and briefly and end it first. That is the whole of the first six days.
- **Days 7–9 collapse into the boat.** You don't need the small ordinary thing — you have small
  ordinary things every day. Go straight to asking him something about himself, at the front of
  the second week.
- **The bridge moves to days 13–15.** You reach it earlier than the standard cup because the line
  is already open. Say your one plain thing there, short, and then be quiet through 16–18 instead.
- **Days 19–21 are the butterfly, unchanged.**

⚠ One warning that belongs to this cup and no other: **do not test him.** A turned handle tempts a
woman into the small experiment — a deliberately delayed reply, a message left on read, something
to see whether he notices. He will notice, and it will read as the first interesting thing you have
done in a month, and it will work for about four days. Then you are in a dynamic instead of a
relationship. The bridge is a better instrument and it comes in a fortnight.

## Your cup: the Sunken Leaf

<!-- BRANCH · she picked "silence — I start everything or nothing happens" -->

You told me it has gone quiet and stayed quiet, and that you are now the one who begins everything
or nothing begins.

A sunken leaf is one that has left the wall and gone to the bottom of the cup. It has not left the
cup. That distinction is the whole of your reading, and I want it said before anything else:
**silence is not absence.** He is at the base — buried, and still in it.

The finding is harder than the other two, and you deserve it straight. **The initiating has become
the problem.** Not the amount of it — the fact that it is all yours. Every exchange you have had
recently began with you, which means neither of you has any information about whether he would
come to you unprompted, and you have been unable to stop generating contact for long enough to find
out.

That is what your sequence is for, and it is the slowest of the three.

- **Days 1–9 are silent, all nine.** No contact at all, not warm and brief, not the small ordinary
  thing. This is the longest quiet stretch of any cup and it is not a tactic — it is the only way to
  get a reading on a line where every previous data point was generated by you.
- **Days 10–12 are the first contact, and it is small.** One thing about nothing. No question.
  Nothing that requires a reply. You are opening a door, not knocking on one.
- **Days 13–15 are the lighthouse and they matter more here than anywhere.** Do this properly. Nine
  silent days followed by one unanswered message is the point at which a woman on her own decides
  the whole thing is finished. Be with somebody real that week.
- **The bridge moves to days 19–21**, at the very end. A silent cup cannot be hurried and the
  sequence must not pretend otherwise. What you say there is shorter than the standard bridge — one
  sentence, not a paragraph.

⚠ And the honest part, %FIRSTNAME%, because I would rather you had it from me. **Nine days of
silence may produce nothing.** If it does, that is information you have not had in months and could
not have got any other way — and it is worth more than another six weeks of you carrying the whole
thing alone and calling it a relationship. The leaf is still in the cup either way. What you find
out is whether it rises.

## Three things to expect

**One — the quiet stretch will feel like losing.** Days 1 to 9 will feel like giving up ground,
because doing nothing feels like doing nothing. It isn't; it is the only stretch in the twenty-one
where the situation stops being fed. Women who abandon the sequence abandon it here, on day
five.

**Two — he may go quieter before he goes warmer.** A man who has been getting steady contact and
suddenly isn't will often wait, sometimes for a week, to see whether it's a tactic. Days 4 to 6 are
frequently the coldest of the whole sequence. That is the sequence working, not failing.

**Three — one person is going to talk you out of this.** Somebody close to you — and I would guess
you already know who — is going to hear that a tea reader gave you a three-week plan and tell you to
just be honest with him and say how you feel. They will mean it kindly. They have watched you be
unhappy and they want it over.

Being honest with him is days 16 to 18. It is *in* the sequence. What they are actually suggesting
is doing it tonight, which is the one thing this reading exists to stop, and which is precisely what
the woman in my letter did.

Don't argue with them. Don't tell them what you're doing.

## What this reading cannot do

<!-- §10 rule 4 · name your own limit — this is the hinge into 05 -->

One honest limit, and then I'll let you go.

Everything above is about **what you say and when.** That is what leaves are good for and it is
genuinely most of what decides this.

What I cannot do with a cup of tea is anything about the third position — the one opposite the
handle, the one that belongs to somebody you haven't met. If there is a second presence in this, and
the bird's tail suggests something took his attention two months ago, then the sequence above works
*around* her. It does not touch her.

I can tell you the shape of it and what to say. I cannot make anything stop.

That is a different kind of work and it isn't this one. I'll write to you about it another time if
it turns out to matter.
