## The copy

### And this is yours to put on a door, %FIRSTNAME%

The reading above is the thing to read. This is the thing to *look at*.

Print it, or copy it onto a card, and put it somewhere you'll see it without going to find it — the
inside of a kitchen cupboard is where most women end up putting theirs. Not the fridge, where other
people read it.

---

### Your twenty-one days

*Day 1 is the first evening after you read this. Cross each one off before bed.*

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **Days 1–3** | the broken heart | **QUIET.** Don't close gaps. Answer warmly, briefly, don't extend |
| **Days 4–6** | the bird | **QUIET.** Don't initiate. If he does, end it first, no reason given |
| **Days 7–9** | the withering tree | **ONE SMALL THING.** About nothing. No question, or a one-word one. Once |
| **Days 10–12** | the boat | **ASK.** About him, not about you two. Let him answer at his own length |
| **Days 13–15** | the lighthouse | **GO TO YOUR FIXED POINT.** One real evening, not about him |
| **Days 16–18** | the bridge | **SPEAK.** One thing, plainly, short. No explaining paragraph |
| **Days 19–21** | the butterfly | **LOOK BACK.** What changed in you. Nothing owed to him |

### How to use it on a bad night

This is what the calendar is actually for, and it is worth two minutes of your attention.

There will be an evening — probably around day five, possibly day eleven — when you have decided
you are going to send something. Not idly. You will have thought it through and it will feel
reasoned.

**On that evening, go and look at the door.**

Find the day. Read the one word next to it.

If it says QUIET, then what you are feeling is day five, and day five feels exactly like this for
every woman who has ever done this sequence. It is not new information about your relationship. It
is a Tuesday with a number on it.

Then go to bed, and cross it off, because you got through it.

That's it. That's the whole use. A woman with a date on her waiting is doing something completely
different from a woman waiting.

---

### One rule

**Don't read ahead to day sixteen.**

You will want to. The bridge is the interesting one and it is the reason you bought the reading, and
there is a strong temptation to skip to the week where you get to speak and start drafting for it.

Drafting on day four is how the thing you send on day sixteen arrives three weeks stale, carrying
everything you were feeling in week one rather than what is true by then.

Cross the days off. Don't work ahead.

---

### On the twenty-second day

Take it off the door.

Whatever has happened by then, the sequence is finished and it should stop being a thing on your
cupboard. A calendar left up past its last day turns into a monument, and you are not building one
of those.

## Build notes

- **This is the offer's most-used asset and probably its most-loved.** The reading is read once;
  this is looked at twenty-one times. §6c's retention requirement is satisfied more literally here
  than by any other gift in the deck.
- **"How to use it on a bad night" is the copy that matters** — the table alone is a reference, and
  a reference doesn't stop anybody sending a message at nine o'clock. Naming day five specifically,
  before it happens, converts the crisis into a scheduled event. It is the same device as the
  product's *three things to expect*, made physical.
- **"It is a Tuesday with a number on it"** pays off the +24h nudge's *"waiting with a date on it is
  just a Tuesday"* — the buyer meets that line twice, once as a promise and once as the thing in
  her hand.
- ⚠ **"Don't read ahead to day sixteen" protects the sequence** in the way `04-C3` protects the
  evenings. Both exist because the offer's failure mode is acting early, and both are cheap.
- **"Take it off the door on the twenty-second day"** is the same refusal-to-extend as 03's
  *and then stop* and 02's *do it once*. ⚠ Consistent across the deck deliberately: none of Evelyn's
  free practices become open-ended disciplines, because an open-ended practice is a woman still
  managing a situation months later.
- **`%FIRSTNAME%` × 1.** It is a reference card; more would read as merge.
