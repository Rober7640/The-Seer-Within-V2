# %FIRSTNAME%, I read my own cup this morning and it wasn't about me

<!-- BEAT 3 · deck: payoff + window -->
*Seven symbols. Four of them difficult, three of them a way through — and one week in the next
three where what you say will land completely differently than it would the week either side.*

`[IMG-1]`

<!-- BEAT 4 · salutation -->
%FIRSTNAME% —

Evelyn here.

<!-- BEAT 5 · urgency opener -->
I'm writing in the middle of my morning because I'd rather send this badly than sit on it.

<!-- BEAT 6 · origin story. Uninvited, and NOT a draw and NOT a found page -->
I read tea the way other people bite their nails.

Not for anybody, dear — for myself, at the kitchen table, with a pot I have made ten thousand times.
It's the least serious thing I do and I've done it every morning for thirty years. Most days it says
nothing at all and I rinse it out.

This morning I turned my cup over and the leaves went down in a shape that was nothing to do with my
life.

I want to be plain about how that works, because it sounds like a flourish and it isn't. A cup read
for yourself, in your own kitchen, with your own hands — that cup is about you. When it isn't, the
reason is always the same: somebody has been on your mind hard enough to get into the pot.

You had been on mine. You bought a reading from me and something in what you wrote has stayed with
me since, and this morning it came up out of the leaves in seven pieces.

So I have read it properly, and I'm going to give you all seven. Not four of them with the rest
behind a door — **all seven**, because a half-read cup is worse than no cup at all and I won't send
you one.

<!-- BEAT 7 · stakes preview -->
Here's how to take it.

The first four are hard. Read them anyway, and read them as *description* rather than sentence —
none of the four says the thing is over.

The last three are the way through, and one of them can be used two opposite ways, which is where
most women lose it.

And read the whole thing as **near**. This is not a cup about next year.

<!-- BEAT 8 · precedent. NOT a draw, NOT an account. A cautionary one — and buyer-change -->
Before I start, one story, because it is the reason this letter is longer than it needs to be.

Years ago I read a cup for a woman whose symbols were almost exactly these. Same bird, same bridge.
I told her what they meant and she understood perfectly — she was a clever woman and she took notes.

And then she went home and said the right thing on the wrong day.

I want to be precise, because this is the whole point. It was not the wrong sentence. It was a good
sentence, the one I'd have picked, and she said it on a Tuesday when the cup had that week marked as
the week to be quiet. He read it as pressure, because that week he was reading everything as
pressure, and it cost her four months of ground she had to make back one conversation at a time.

She got there in the end. She'd tell you herself it took a year longer than it needed to.

What she said afterwards is the thing I've never forgotten, dear: *"You told me what the leaves were.
Nobody told me when."*

<!-- BEAT 9 · withhold + condition. The BIG IDEA, stated -->
She was right, and she was describing a real gap, and I want to name it before I read for you rather
than after.

**A cup has two halves.** There are the leaves — what is happening, what he is doing, what is coming
apart and what could hold. And there is the **cup** — the shape they landed in, and where.

The leaves are the story. The cup is the calendar.

Because tasseography is read by position, %FIRSTNAME%, and this is the part almost nobody outside
the practice knows. **The handle is you.** Everything near the handle is close to you and it is
happening soon. Everything opposite the handle is far from you and belongs to somebody you have not
met. The rim is days and the base is what's buried and long-standing.

Same leaf. Same symbol. Move it half an inch and it means *this week* instead of *this winter*.

You are about to be given the leaves. What I have not done yet is turn the cup.

I'll come back to that at the end. First, your seven.

<!-- BEAT 10 · THE FREE READ. All seven, honestly. 4 difficult → 3 a way through -->
### The first four

`[IMG-2]`

**A bird taking flight.**

He is pulling away. I'm not going to dress that up for you, and you didn't need me to tell you —
you have been feeling it for a while and doubting yourself about it, which is the more exhausting
of the two.

The bird is a man seeking room. Sometimes that is something in his own life that has nothing to do
with you; he is struggling with something and going quiet is how he does it. Sometimes it is that
he has become unsure about where this is going.

And sometimes there is a second person in the frame. The bird does not tell me which — that is not
what a bird does — but I would be doing you no favours by leaving it out.

**A broken heart.**

The turmoil. Yours, mostly. This symbol is not about the relationship ending; it is about the
*state you have been in while it hasn't*, which is a different thing and in some ways a worse one.

It marks a connection that has become fragile enough that what gets said now matters more than what
got said last year. Every message is carrying more weight than it was built for.

**A withering tree.**

What stood up on its own is not standing up on its own any more.

The tree is the honest symbol in this cup, %FIRSTNAME%. It says the foundation has thinned — not
collapsed, thinned — and that a thing which used to run without attention now needs some. The man
who was, by everyone's account including his own, thoroughly yours, is playing hot and cold.

You are not imagining the difference. You have simply been told, often enough that you believe it,
that noticing it is the problem.

**A boat in rough water.**

The two of you, in weather. Turbulence you are both inside, whether or not he'd describe it that way.

The boat is the one people misread as a warning and it isn't — a boat is a thing that gets through.
What it says is that the difficulty is *shared and current*, and that it can be come through, and
that coming through it is a two-person operation for as long as both of you keep rowing.

---

### And then three more came up

`[IMG-3]`

**A bridge.**

The way back. There is one — that is the good news and it is real.

But here is where I have to be careful with you, because the bridge is the symbol I have seen do
the most damage in thirty years of reading cups. **A bridge works in both directions.**

Build the wrong one and you build the thing he walks away over: the message that explains, the one
that lays out how you have been feeling, the reasonable and thorough one that gives him somewhere
to go and something to answer. Women build that bridge constantly. It is made entirely of good
intentions and it is a road out.

Build the right one and he crosses it toward you.

Same symbol. Same cup. Opposite outcomes, and **the difference is not what you say — it is when.**
A bridge offered in the wrong week is an exit. The same words seven days later are an invitation.

That is the fork in this cup and it is the reason I am writing to you rather than leaving you with
a nice reading.

**A lighthouse.**

Something steady that is already in your life and is not him.

A person, most likely — a friend, someone in your family, or else a place or a piece of work that
holds you level. The lighthouse says you have a fixed point, and that you
have been navigating by a moving one.

**A butterfly.**

Change that has already started, and it is yours rather than his.

The butterfly is the last symbol in the cup and it is the one I'd have you hold on to. It says
something in how you do this is going to be different by the end of it, and that the difference is
in your favour. Not that you were wrong. That you are becoming somebody who does this part
differently.

<!-- BEAT 13 · concrete ask -->
### In concrete terms, what happens now?

Nothing you have to write, and nothing you have to tell me.

You [authorise the turn here]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=62). That's it — I have the cup, I have your
symbols, and what remains is the reading I have not done yet.

You don't send me anything. You don't tell me what he said last night. I would honestly rather you
didn't — the cup is a better witness than either of us on this, and I want to read it clean.

<!-- BEAT 12 · re-read, deepen — the bird. Where the threat gets specific -->
### Back to the bird, because that's the one you'll be up at night with

I said the bird doesn't tell me whether there is somebody else, and that's true. Here's what it does
tell me, which is more useful and which almost nobody will say to you.

**It is too early for that to be the question.**

A bird in flight is a man who has already begun going, and the going comes first. Whatever the reason
is — and there is always a reason, and it is often duller and smaller than the one you have
constructed at two in the morning — the leaving started before it had a story attached.

Which means the question that actually decides this is not *is there someone else.* It is
**how long has he been going, and how much of that is recoverable.**

The leaves cannot answer that. Position can. A bird near the rim is a fortnight old. A bird sitting
down by the base has been in flight since before you noticed anything at all, and those are two
completely different situations that would have you doing two opposite things this week.

[Let me turn the cup]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=63) and I'll tell you which one you're in.

### And back to the bridge, because this is the one that decides it

Everything in this cup that can go well runs through the bridge, and everything that can go badly
runs through it too.

You are going to build one. You have probably drafted it already — I would put money on there being
a message in your phone right now that you have written and not sent, and that you have rewritten
twice.

I'm not going to tell you not to send it. That is the advice everybody gives and it is useless,
because you will send something eventually and *nothing* is also a message.

What I'll tell you is that the same words are a completely different event depending on which week
they arrive in, and that the cup has the weeks in it, and that you currently cannot see them.

That is the entire difference between the woman in my story and the woman she'd have been.

[Let me turn it]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=64), and you'll be standing in a week with a number on it.

<!-- BEAT 14 · authority flourish. Craft knowledge + the LADDER's reason-why planted -->
### Why the turning is a separate reading, and why I'd do it soon

There is a thing about leaves that nobody mentions because it isn't romantic.

**They dry.**

A cup read within a day or so of the pot is soft — the leaves still hold the shape the liquid put
them in, and position reads clean. As they dry they contract, and they lift, and they move. Not far.
Far enough.

A cup turned a week later still gives me your seven symbols; symbols are robust and yours aren't
going anywhere. What gets harder is the *positions* — and position is the entire product. A dry cup
has to be worked backwards from where the leaves have travelled to where they sat, and that is slow,
and it is the reason a later turning costs more than an early one. It is more of my night.

I'd rather tell you that plainly than have you discover it as a price further down.

Your cup was poured this morning. It will read best this week.

<!-- BEAT 15 · final CTA -->
### [Let me turn your cup]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=65)

<!-- BEAT 16 · transformation promise = the brief's PROMISE, in her words -->
I'm not going to promise you he comes back. I don't know that, the cup doesn't say it, and a woman
who has been handled by as many people as you have this year deserves better than another one
promising it.

Here is what it actually buys you.

**You stop guessing what to send.**

Not *you win him back*. You stop lying awake composing. You stop reading his last message for the
ninth time looking for tone. You stop sending the good sentence on the wrong day and then spending
a week watching what it did.

You get a sequence — this week you say nothing, this week you say this much, this week is the one
the bridge is for — and you follow it, and the deciding stops being something you do forty times a
day.

That, dear, is worth considerably more than being told he loves you.

[Your cup is still soft]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=66).
