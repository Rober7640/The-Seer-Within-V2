### The card

`[IMG-2 — the RWS scan: the Seven of Swords]`

A man walking away from a striped camp with five swords held against his body. He's looking back over his shoulder as he goes. Two more swords are still standing upright in the ground behind him. There are tents and small figures in the distance and not one of them has noticed.

Now look at the camp, not the man.

Nobody has looked up. Five things are going out of that camp in broad daylight and the people it belongs to are getting on with their morning.

That's what this card says when it comes up first. It isn't a card about theft, whatever you've been told — it's the card of a thing leaving quietly, without a row, without a decision, and without anybody in the place noticing it go. Including the person it's leaving.

So I'm not laying this as a past. Whatever's going out is going out now, this week, while you read this.

When the Seven of Swords opens a cut, I lay it as a problem.

There's better news in this card than you'd guess, and I'll show you where before the end.

### The problem

Here's what it says in that seat, and it isn't the same thing.

You'd tell me the problem is how much they ask of you. It isn't, and this is the hard part of the card. Look at his hands.

He's holding them by the blades. Not the handles — the blades, all five, against his chest. That's how you carry something nobody handed you. Getting a proper grip would mean standing still long enough to be seen doing it, and standing still is the one thing he won't do. So it cuts him the whole way out of the camp.

Nobody in that painting is being robbed. You haven't been taken advantage of by a camp full of people who don't care about you. You've been carrying things out before anybody got round to asking, and then waiting to be thanked for a favour nobody made.

I don't know what yours are. Some women tell me it's a grown son's washing. Some tell me it's the one at work whose job quietly became theirs and was never spoken about.

And I'll be plain, because this is where people flinch. Nobody made you do it and nobody is going to hand it back. That sounds worse than it is. A thing you started on your own is a thing you can stop on your own, and you don't need anybody in that camp to agree to it first.

Years ago I read for a woman called Thea. She could account for every hour of her week. Not one of those hours was hers.

She came to me about being taken advantage of, which is what it looks like from inside. It wasn't that. Nobody had asked her for most of it. She'd been picking things up before anybody got round to asking, then resenting them for a request they never made. What changed for Thea was that she started waiting to be asked.

Here's what the Seven of Swords keeps back from me. It shows me the picking up. It won't tell me the one thing to put down first, and it won't tell me what's different in your week once you have.

Two cards say. One can't.

### Back to the camp, and the better news

Go and look behind him. At the two he didn't take.

They're standing upright in the ground, in the open, in daylight. Nobody has hidden them and nobody has taken them away. They are exactly where he left them.

Two things that are actually yours are still standing where you put them down, {{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}. They didn't go anywhere while you were busy carrying everyone else's. You already know what both of them are — you thought of them somewhere in the last paragraph.

If you take one thing out of this letter, take this. Nobody has to ask you for a thing you've already picked up. Thea spent years being owed for favours nobody had requested.

### The question you came with

Most people write to me with some version of *why does nobody ever do anything for me*. Here's the straight answer rather than the kind one. I can't make anybody in your life offer, and a reader who says they can is selling you the one thing a deck can't do.

So don't ask me that. Ask me this, in your own words:

**What do I do about the thing I picked up that nobody asked me for?**

One thing, not the whole list. You thought of it two paragraphs ago. Put that in and the cards have something to sit on — *"why am I always the one"* isn't a question and I can't lay anything on it.

So [send me that one]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=2&s=problem-action-outcome). It doesn't have to be tidy and you don't have to come out of it well.

So, {{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}. What did you pick up this week that nobody asked you for?

[Put that in the box, and I'll turn the other two]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=3&s=problem-action-outcome).

I'm not going to tell you two cards teach a family to notice you. Nothing on this table has ever changed how anybody else behaves.

Here's what happens instead. One thing goes back down. You find out whether anybody actually comes to collect it, and that answer is worth more to you than another year of being owed.

— Marcus, at the table most mornings
