### The card

`[IMG-2 — the RWS scan: the Hermit]`

An old man in a grey robe on a snowy peak. He's holding a lantern up with a six-pointed star burning inside it, and a long staff in the other hand. His head is bowed, his eyes on the ground.

Now count the people in that picture.

One. Nobody climbing up behind him, nobody waiting at the bottom. Most cards in this deck have somebody else in them. This one has been emptied.

When the Hermit comes up first, that's what it tells me before it tells me anything else. It isn't a card about somebody else, because there's nobody in it to be about. It's the card of the thing you've been carrying on your own and haven't said out loud — not because it's a secret, and not because you're private. Because saying it makes it real enough that you'd have to do something.

So I'm not laying this as a past. There's no use telling a woman where a thing came from while she's still standing in it.

When the Hermit opens a cut, I lay it as a problem.

There's better news in this card than you'd guess, and I'll show you where before the end.

### The problem

Here's what he says in that seat, and it isn't the same thing.

The thing in your way isn't the thing. You could tell me what the thing is in one sentence — most women can, and have had it ready for months. What's in the way is that it has never once been out of your mouth.

It's been thought about, solved, re-solved and put away again, at your own table, at half past eleven at night. A problem you've only ever handled privately stops behaving like a problem after a while. It turns into a way of living, and nothing about it ever gets tested against another person.

I don't know what yours is. Some women tell me it's money nobody else in the house knows the shape of. Some tell me it's what they've already decided about their marriage and haven't told a soul.

And I'll say this plainly, because it's where people flinch. Nobody in that painting is hiding. He's up a hill, in the open, holding a lit lamp above his head. You didn't keep this to yourself because you're secretive. You kept it because the first time you nearly said it, you worked out how long that conversation was going to be, and you were tired. So you handled it, which you're good at, and being good at it is the trap.

Here's what the Hermit won't tell me. It shows me the problem is the handling, not the thing. It won't tell me the one move that's yours to make this week, and it won't tell me what your week looks like once you've made it.

Two cards say. One can't.

Years ago I read for a woman called Maud who'd been managing something on her own for a long time. She'd rehearsed the conversation so often she'd stopped being able to tell whether she'd had it.

What changed for Maud wasn't the right person or the right words. She said it once, badly, to somebody who wasn't the right person at all, and found out it took ninety seconds. She'd been carrying a thing she thought was an hour long.

### The spread this belongs to is called Problem, Action, Outcome

Three cards. One turned over. Two still face down.

I lay this one when something's live. Not the pattern of your whole life — the thing that's been sitting on you since Tuesday, and the move that's yours to make before Sunday. If you want to know why you're like this, that's a different morning and a different cut. This one's for what you do.

### Back to the lamp, and the better news

Go and look at what he's holding again.

It's lit, and he's got it up at head height, so the ground in front of him is lit for a good way. This is not a man in the dark. He can see exactly what he's standing on.

You have not been confused about this, {{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}. You've known what it is for months, in detail, probably better than anyone else could tell you. That's the better news and it's better than it sounds — because it means there's nothing left to work out. It only has to be said.

If you take one thing out of this letter, take this. A thing you've only ever said to yourself has never once been argued with. Maud carried an hour that turned out to be ninety seconds.

### The question you came with

Most people write to me with some version of *what should I do about this*. Here's the straight answer instead of the kind one. I'm not going to be the first person you tell. It shouldn't be a stranger with a deck, and if it is, that's the problem repeating itself in a new place.

So don't ask me what it is. Ask me this, and put it in your own words:

**What do I do about the person I haven't told?**

One person, not everybody. You already thought of them, and you thought of the reason as well. Put that in and the cards have something to sit on — *"what's going on with me"* isn't a question and I can't lay a thing on it.

So [send me that one]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=2&s=problem-action-outcome). It doesn't have to be tidy and you don't have to explain yourself in it.

So, {{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}. Who is it you haven't told?

[Put that in the box, and I'll turn the other two]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=3&s=problem-action-outcome).

I'm not going to tell you two cards make that conversation easy. Nothing on this table has ever opened anybody's mouth for them.

Here's what happens instead. It stops being a thing you run at half eleven at night and becomes a thing in a room, where somebody else can pick it up and hand it back with a bit missing. That's what you've been going without.

— Marcus, at the table most mornings
