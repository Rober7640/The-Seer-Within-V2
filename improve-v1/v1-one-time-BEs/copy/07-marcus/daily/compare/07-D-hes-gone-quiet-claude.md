# {{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}, the quiet isn't something that happened to him. It's something he's doing

{{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }} —

Marcus here.

I cut the deck before it gets light and read whatever is sitting on the cut. One cut, and nobody asked me for this one.

This morning nothing had moved.

I shuffle last thing every night. This card was sitting where I'd left it when I put the pack down, and it was still there when I cut. A whole night, and not one card had shifted.

Fourteen years. I know what a still deck is for, and it isn't a day that's about to start. It's about a thing that has stopped and hasn't been counted.

Have a proper look before you read what I make of it. It's all there already.

Years ago I read for a woman called Cecily, about a man who'd gone quiet on her. She could tell you what he'd said last and roughly when. She could not tell you how long it had been.

She worked it out at my table, out loud, with her fingers. It came to a lot more than she'd been telling herself. What changed was that she stopped calling it a bad patch, because a bad patch is a fortnight and this had a number on it.

Here's what one card gives me and what it won't. I can see the shape of the silence — what stopped and what carried on. What I can't see is what he's getting out of it, or the sentence he can't bring himself to start.

There's better news in this card than you'd think and I'll show you where before the end.

### How long it's really been: the Hermit

`[IMG-2 — the RWS scan: the Hermit]`

An old man in a grey robe, alone on a snowy peak. He's holding a lantern up in one hand with a six-pointed star burning inside it, and a long staff in the other. His head is bowed and his eyes are down.

Now count the people in that picture. One. There is nobody else in the frame at all — no one walking away, nobody at the bottom of the hill waving.

That's the first true thing about your quiet. There's no row in it. Nothing broke. It looks like nothing happened, and that's exactly why you can't get hold of it.

Look at what he's carrying. He climbed up there and brought his own light, so he can see perfectly well. This is not a man lost in the dark and not a man who forgot. He went up on purpose and he's standing there on purpose, every day, including today.

I don't know what shape yours takes. Some women tell me he still watches everything she puts up and says nothing. Some tell me he answers, and never once starts anything himself.

And I'll be plain, because this is where people flinch. You have not been dramatic about this. A thing with no row in it and no ending in it is genuinely hard to name, and you have spent months trying to name it fairly, which is more than he's spent.

Here's what the Hermit keeps back from me. It shows me the shape of it and roughly how long it's run. It won't tell me what the quiet keeps for him — and there's something, or it would have ended by itself long before now. It won't tell me the one sentence he'd have to send to finish it, or why that's the one he can't start.

Six cards say. One can't.

### The spread this belongs to is called He's Gone Quiet

Seven cards. One turned over. Six still face down.

I lay this one when you're waiting on an answer that hasn't come. Not only from a man — it reads the same on a sister who went quiet, or a job that never wrote back. If nothing is pending and nobody owes you a reply, this isn't your morning.

Then six, because a silence has parts and they're different questions. What landed on his side the week before he stopped. What the quiet keeps available to him. The sentence he can't begin. What the waiting has turned into for you. Your own end of it, and the terms that are yours. And what another stretch of this takes.

### Back to the staff, and the better news

Go and look at his other hand. At the staff.

It runs from his fist down to the ground, taking his weight. He's holding himself up. Nobody in that picture is holding anybody else up.

Which means your waiting isn't doing any work, {{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}. You've been treating it as a job — the thing that keeps this open, that stops it being over. It isn't. Nothing in that picture depends on you standing where you're standing. That's the better news. Stopping costs you nothing you're actually holding.

If you take one thing out of this letter, take this. A silence with nobody in it still has somebody keeping it. Cecily lost fourteen months to a thing she was calling a bad patch.

### The question you came with

Most people write to me with some version of *is he going to come back*. Here's the straight answer instead of the kind one. I won't put a month on another person's decision. There's a whole trade that will, and when the month passes they hand you a new one.

Here's the question I'd rather have off you.

**How long has it actually been?**

Work it out in weeks and send me the number with it. Most women find it's roughly double what they've been saying out loud, and the moment you have it, this stops being weather and starts being a fact.

So [send me that question]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=2&s=hes-gone-quiet), in your own words, with the number in it.

So, {{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}. How long has it really been?

[Tell me that, and I'll lay the other six]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=3&s=hes-gone-quiet).

I'm not going to tell you six cards make a man send a message. Nothing on this table has ever opened anybody's mouth for them.

Here's what happens instead. You stop checking a phone for a sentence that isn't in it, and you get your evenings back while this is still going on rather than after it ends.
