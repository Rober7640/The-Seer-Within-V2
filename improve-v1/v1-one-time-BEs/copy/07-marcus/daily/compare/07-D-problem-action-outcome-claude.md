# {{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}, the problem isn't how much people ask of you. It's that you pick it up before they've finished asking

*A man going away from a camp with five swords bundled against his chest, holding them by the blades. Two more still standing upright in the ground behind him.*

`[IMG-1 — the photograph: the cut, this morning. 1 turned, 2 face down]`

*The cut, this morning — one turned, two not*

{{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }} —

Marcus here.

I cut the deck before it gets light and read whatever is sitting on the cut. One cut, and nobody asked me for this one.

This morning it wouldn't stay cut.

I cut it and the pack slid back together. Cut it again, same thing. The third time it held, and it held on this.

Fourteen years. A deck that won't stay cut is a deck telling me the thing on top isn't finished.

Have a proper look at the picture before you read what I make of it. It's all lying there already.

Years ago I read for a woman called Thea. She could account for every hour of her week. Not one of those hours was hers.

She'd come to me about being taken advantage of, which is what it looks like from inside. It wasn't that. What her other two cards showed was that nobody had asked her for most of it. She'd been picking things up before anybody got round to asking, and then resenting them for a request they never made. What changed for Thea was that she started waiting to be asked.

Here's what one card gives me and what it doesn't. I can see what's actually in your way. What I can't see is the thing you could start this week, or what your week looks like once you have.

There's better news in this card than you'd guess and I'll show you where before the end.

### The problem: the Seven of Swords

`[IMG-2 — the RWS scan: the Seven of Swords]`

A man walking away from a striped camp with five swords held against his body. He's looking back over his shoulder as he goes. Two more swords are still standing upright in the ground behind him. There are tents and small figures in the distance and not one of them has noticed.

Now look at his hands.

He's holding them by the blades. Not the handles — the blades, all five, against his chest. That's how you carry something you never planned to carry. Getting a proper grip would mean standing still, and standing still is the one thing he won't do. So it cuts him the whole way out of the camp.

That's the problem, and it isn't the people in the tents. It's the picking up. Five things against your chest that were never handed to you, carried badly, at speed, so that it's done before anybody looks up.

I don't know what yours are. Some women tell me it's a grown son's washing. Some tell me it's the one at work whose job quietly became theirs and was never spoken about.

And I'll be plain here, because this is where people flinch. Nobody in that painting is being robbed. That's the hard part of this card. You have not been taken advantage of by a camp full of people who don't care — you've been carrying things out before anyone thought to ask, and then waiting to be thanked for a favour nobody requested. It's still costing you the same. It just doesn't have a villain in it.

Here's what the Seven of Swords keeps back from me. It shows me the picking up. It won't tell me the one thing to put down first, and it won't tell me what's different in your week once you have.

Two cards say. One can't.

### Back to the camp, and the better news

Go and look behind him. At the two he didn't take.

They're standing upright in the ground, in the open, in daylight. Nobody has hidden them and nobody has taken them away. They are exactly where he left them.

Two things that are actually yours are still standing where you put them down, {{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}. They didn't go anywhere while you were busy carrying everyone else's. You already know what both of them are — you thought of them somewhere in the last paragraph.

If you take one thing out of this letter, take this. Nobody has to ask you for a thing you've already picked up. Thea spent years being owed for favours nobody had requested.

### The question you came with

Most people write to me with some version of *why does nobody ever do anything for me*. I'll give you the straight answer rather than the kind one. I can't make anybody in your life offer, and a reader who says they can is selling you the one thing a deck can't do.

Here's the question I'd rather have.

**What did I pick up this week that nobody asked me for?**

That one has an answer, and it'll arrive faster than you want it to. It's sitting in the two I haven't turned over.

So [send me that question]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=2&s=problem-action-outcome), in your own words. It doesn't have to be tidy and you don't have to come out of it well.

So, {{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}. What did you pick up this week that nobody asked you for?

[Tell me that, and I'll turn the other two]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=3&s=problem-action-outcome).

I'm not going to tell you two cards teach a family to notice you. Nothing on this table has ever changed how anybody else behaves.

Here's what happens instead. One thing goes back down. You find out whether anybody actually comes to collect it, and that answer is worth more than a year of being owed.

— Marcus, at the table most mornings
