# {{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}, your higher purpose isn't waiting ahead of you. It's the thing you keep picking up and putting down

*A hand coming out of a cloud with a leafy branch in it. Beside it, a knight on a horse up on its back legs, holding the same branch.*

`[IMG-1 — the photograph: the cut, this morning. 2 turned, 4 face down]`

*The cut, this morning — two turned, four not*

{{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }} —

Marcus here.

I cut the deck before it gets light and read the card on the cut. One card. That's the whole of my method and nobody asked me for this one.

This morning I turned two.

The first was a hand out of a cloud holding a branch with green still on it. I wanted to know what was underneath, which I don't do, and I did it anyway. The card under it had the same branch. Same leaves, same shape, in a man's fist, on a horse.

Fourteen years, and the deck has never handed me the same object twice in one cut.

Have a proper look at both before you read what I make of them. It's all there already.

Years ago I read for a woman called Iris who asked me what she was for. She had a shortlist of grand answers and was waiting to be told which one was correct.

It wasn't on the list. It was a thing she'd been doing on and off since she was young, that she'd never once counted because nobody paid her for it. What changed for Iris was that she stopped auditioning for a purpose and started keeping the one she'd had the whole time.

Here's what two cards give me and what they don't. I can see what keeps coming back to you and what you keep leaving. What I can't see is what runs through both — and that line is the answer.

There's better news than that in the first card and I'll show you where before the end.

### What you keep coming back to: the Ace of Wands

`[IMG-2 — the RWS scan: the Ace of Wands]`

A hand comes out of a cloud holding a thick wooden staff sprouting green leaves. A few have already come loose and are falling.

Look at the leaves that are falling.

That branch has been cut. It isn't a plant in a pot — somebody cut it, and it's still putting out leaves, and losing some at the same time.

That's the thing you keep coming back to. You didn't choose it, {{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}, and that's why you've never given it much weight. It keeps getting handed to you.

I don't know what yours is. Some women tell me it's a notebook they've started and stopped more times than they'd admit to. Some tell me it's other people's children, and they say it apologetically, as if that's a small thing to be for.

And I'll be plain here, because this is where people flinch. This isn't a hobby that got out of hand. A hobby doesn't survive three different lives, and coming back that often isn't an accident of your free time.

Here's what the Ace keeps back from me. It shows me the thing keeps arriving. It won't tell me what's in it that brings you back — the one line running through it — and that's the card that names your answer.

### What you keep leaving: the Knight of Wands

`[IMG-3 — the RWS scan: the Knight of Wands]`

A knight in armour with a plumed helmet, on an orange horse reared up on its back legs. He's holding a sprouting staff. His tunic is covered in salamanders.

Now here's the part nobody notices. That horse is up on its back legs and hasn't gone anywhere.

Everything in that painting is about to happen. Nothing has. It's a leaving painted at the moment before the leaving.

That's your other half. You go, and you go fast, and you have a reason ready before anybody asks for one.

Here's the thing about the reason. It's the same one every time. Different year, different life, same sentence — and you've never put them side by side, because each time it looked like this particular thing not working out.

I'm not going to make you the villain in that. A reason that's worked that reliably has been protecting something. It has just never been examined.

What this one won't tell me is what the two of them have in common. That's not on either card I can see.

### The spread these belong to is called What Is My Higher Purpose In Life

Six cards. Two turned over. Four still face down.

I lay this one for the woman who's been asking what she's for, on and off, for years, and has never once counted the thing she keeps going back to. If you already know what you're for and you're getting on with it, this isn't your morning.

Two are free because the answer is a line through a pair, and you can't draw a line through one point.

### Back to the branch, and the better news

Go and look at the first card again. At the hand.

It's still holding it out. The branch isn't on the ground and nobody took it back. Every time you put this down, it got handed to you again.

That's the good news. You have not missed it. A thing that keeps being offered after that many refusals isn't waiting on you to deserve it.

If you take one thing out of this letter, take this. You can't lose a purpose by putting it down. You can only lose the years you spend deciding whether it counts. Iris gave that ten.

### The question you came with

Most people write to me with some version of *what am I meant to be doing with my life*. Here's the straight answer instead of the kind one. No deck hands out callings, and a reader who tells you yours is describing you, not finding you.

Here's the question I'd rather have off you.

**What have I picked back up more than twice?**

Not what you're good at. Not what you'd put on a form. The thing you keep returning to and apologising for.

So [send me that question]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=2&s=what-is-my-higher-purpose-in-life), in your own words.

So, {{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}. What have you picked back up more than twice?

[Tell me that, and I'll turn the other four]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=3&s=what-is-my-higher-purpose-in-life).

I'm not going to tell you four cards hand you a calling. Nothing on this table has ever told anybody who to be.

Here's what happens instead. You stop waiting for the grand version, and the thing you do on weekends stops being the thing you do instead of your life.

— Marcus, at the table most mornings
