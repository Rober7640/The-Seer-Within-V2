I cut the deck before it got light this morning. When I turned the second card, my sleeve caught the pencil beside it. It rolled across the table and stopped over the staff in the first picture.

I reached to move it — then saw the second staff. I’d been looking at the horse. That little interruption made me put the pencil down and leave the opening where it was.

I’m writing because these two positions read something you’ve already done more than once. You’ve returned to something across different years. You’ve also left something unfinished, with an explanation that sounds sensible each time.

Look at the picture properly before you read what I make of it — especially what each hand is holding. Give yourself a moment with the cards before you start trying to remember the right thing.

Years ago, a woman called Harriet told me she couldn’t answer a question about purpose. She’d crossed out everything she’d written because none of it sounded important enough to bring to a reading.

I asked her to leave the crossings-out where they were. She read one aloud, then told me about the other times she’d gone back to it — including one she’d forgotten. By the end, she’d stopped apologising for having written it down.

These opening cards show your returning and your leaving. They don’t answer the question those two actions raise together — that takes the four still face down.

There’s better news in the first picture than you’ll see at a glance. I’ll come back to it once you’ve looked at both.

### What you keep coming back to: the Ace of Wands

[IMG-2 — the RWS scan: the Ace of Wands]

A hand comes out of a cloud holding a thick wooden staff. Green leaves are sprouting from it. Below, a river runs through the landscape, and a castle stands some way off on a hill.

Look at the loose leaves — some have already come away. It’s easy to miss those when your eye goes straight to the hand.

Here, the staff reads the thing you’ve picked back up without deciding to make it your ambition. You were doing something else when you found yourself reaching for it again. Later, you realised how long it had been since the last time.

Some women tell me, “I opened the notebook to find something, and ended up writing.” Others say, “I was only showing someone how, and I wanted to carry on.”

The returning happened before you gave it a respectable explanation — that matters in this position. You didn’t need to put it on a list of goals to find yourself doing it again.

There’s nothing embarrassing about how ordinary it sounds. You can tell me what you actually picked up without making it sound worthy of the word “purpose.”

This card shows that repeated return. It can’t answer, “What does my returning have to do with my leaving?” The four remaining cards resolve that question.

### What you keep leaving: the Knight of Wands

[IMG-3 — the RWS scan: the Knight of Wands]

A knight in armour sits on an orange horse rearing onto its back legs. His helmet has a plume. He carries a sprouting staff, with pyramids behind him in the desert.

Notice the salamanders covering his yellow tunic — the horse takes so much of your attention that the pattern gets overlooked. There’s quite a lot to see once you stop following the movement.

In this position, he reads the thing you’ve started and put down repeatedly. Each time, you explain that you’ll return when the rest of your life has settled enough to give it proper attention.

Some women tell me, “I need to get this busy patch out of the way.” Others say, “I’ll come back when I can do it properly.”

You’ve changed the circumstances in that explanation across the years. You haven’t changed the requirement — everything else has to settle first. That repetition has escaped your notice because each interruption had a real name.

You weren’t foolish for responding to what needed you. There’s no shame in having put something down while you dealt with your life.

This card identifies the leaving and the reason you give yourself. It can’t answer, “How do I understand this alongside what I keep returning to?” That answer needs the four cards I haven’t turned.
