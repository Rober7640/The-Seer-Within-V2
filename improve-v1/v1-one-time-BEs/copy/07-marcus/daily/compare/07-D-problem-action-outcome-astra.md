### The problem: the Seven of Swords

[IMG-2 — the RWS scan: the Seven of Swords]

He’s walking away from a striped camp with five swords bundled against his body. His head is turned over his shoulder. Two swords remain upright in the ground behind him — and beyond those are the tents, with small figures in the distance.

Nobody has noticed him leave.

Now look at the detail that gets passed over. He’s holding the swords by their blades. The handles project beyond his grip — the parts made for holding are right there, but his hands aren’t on them.

Here, that awkward grip is the problem. You’re making an exchange easier for somebody else by removing the part that tells the truth about what it costs you.

Think of a message before you send it. There’s a sentence saying you can’t keep doing this. You read it again and hear how it could sound in their voice. Your thumb goes back over the words — by the time you press send, you’ve agreed.

Some women tell me they change “I can’t do that” to “I’ll work something out.” Others say they type a limit, stare at it, then replace the whole message with “No trouble.”

That’s the same deletion in different words. The person receiving it has no sentence to respond to except the one that says you can manage.

You learned to do this because a plainly stated need once made an ordinary conversation difficult. There’s no shame in having learned how to get through those conversations — and you don’t need to call yourself weak to recognise what your thumb is doing now.

But it has become automatic. You’re editing out the very information you wish somebody would understand.

This card names that act. It cannot answer, “What do I actually do about this?” The two cards still face down resolve that question — I haven’t turned them here.
