These two didn’t come up as a fork. They came up facing each other. One is what you have been shown. The other is what has been going on while you looked at it.

And before anything else. I read the cards, not the man. Nothing below is a claim about what any real person thinks or intends or is going to do — nobody’s deck can tell you that, and anyone who says otherwise is selling you something. What a spread reads is the shape of the thing between you, which is a different object and a more useful one.

Here’s the thing that decides this pair, {{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}.

An offer is not a decision. People confuse the two constantly, and the confusion is nearly always generous — she hears a warm thing said sincerely and files it as a commitment, because in her own mouth that is what it would have been.

What you see: the Knight of Cups

A rider in armour on a white horse, holding a single cup out in front of him. Wings on the helmet. A river running behind. And the horse is walking — not cantering, not charging. Walking.

This is the loveliest-looking card in the suit and I want to be precise about what it shows. He is holding the cup out. He is not handing it over. The arm is extended and it stays extended, and the picture never resolves.

Maybe you’d call it slow. Maybe you’d call it he’s been hurt before. Maybe you’d call it the nicest anyone has been to you in a long time, which is true and is also why it’s hard to look at squarely.

What this card won’t tell me is whether the arm ever comes down. A held-out cup and a given cup look identical in a photograph, and the difference is the whole of your next two years.

Two of the five behind this settle it. One card can’t.

What’s going on: the Seven of Cups

A figure in dark silhouette with his back to us, facing seven cups floating in a cloud. Each one has something different in it — a face, a wreath, a tower, jewels, a snake, a shrouded shape.

Every one of them is still in the cloud. Nothing has been taken down. This is the card for the state of standing in front of several futures and finding the standing itself comfortable — which it is, because in the cloud they all still work.

And this is not a card about a liar. That’s the read people jump to and it is usually wrong. Nobody in that picture is deceiving anybody; a man can mean every word of it while the arm stays out. You have not been stupid. You were shown a true thing that was never going to be a decision, and you responded to it the way a person with any warmth responds.

And what the Seven of Cups won’t tell me is which of the seven you are — the wreath, the tower, or the shrouded one. That is not cruelty, it’s just outside what two cards can reach.

Three of the five say which. Two can’t.

What you’re looking at is called the Other Chair

Seven cards. Two you can see. Five you can’t.

Seven isn’t a number I picked. It’s your side of the table, and then his laid out the way his would be — what he thinks he’s doing, what he’s protecting, what he’d have to admit, what he’s waiting on, and what happens if nothing changes. Five, plus the two you’re looking at.

You’ve seen two of the seven. That’s your side.

And the five aren’t more about how you feel. These two told you what you’ve been handed. The five read the same situation from the opposite chair — a different question, and your two cards cannot answer it from where they sit.

Here they are, still face down.

His side of the table

These 5 are still in my hand

So let me lay the five on his side, and I’ll tell you what the spread says the arm is doing.

Back to the horse, and the detail that gives it away

It’s walking.

Every other knight in the deck is in motion — wands charging, swords at a gallop, pentacles at a dead standstill in a ploughed field. This one is at a walk, on level ground, with a river he has not crossed behind him. The pace is the message, and the pace has not changed in the whole time you have been watching it.

So here’s the one to carry today. Don’t confuse being chosen with being kept in the cloud. One of those has a date attached and the other can go on for years without anybody doing a single thing wrong.

And the question you’re probably asking

Most people bring me does he love me. I’m not going to pretend a spread answers that. It doesn’t, nobody’s does, and the ones that claim to are guessing at a stranger.

Try this instead: what would have to be true for the arm to come down?

That one a spread can answer, because it’s a question about the shape of the situation rather than the contents of his head. It sits in the five I haven’t laid.

So put that in front of me, in your own words.

What’s on your side of the table?

Tell me, and I’ll lay the other five.

Do that and you stop reading a held-out cup as a given one, which is the single most expensive misreading in this whole deck.

— Marcus, at the table most mornings
