<!-- BEAT 10 · UNIT 1 — what you think it's about -->
### What you think it's about: the Five of Cups

`[IMG-2]`

A figure in a long black cloak, head down, looking at three cups tipped over on the ground with the wine run out of them.

Behind him, two cups are still standing. He hasn't turned round.

And past all of it there's a bridge, and a house on the far bank with lights in it.

That's the picture, and every part of it is doing work. **Three spilled, two standing, one bridge.** The card is not about loss. The card is about where a person is *standing* in relation to a loss — head down, back turned to what's left, with a way across already built and in view.

So this is the story you've been telling. That something went wrong, and it did, and the going-wrong is the thing you're dealing with. I'm not going to argue with you about the three cups. They're on the floor and the wine's gone into the grass.

What I'd have you notice is that this card has never once been about the spilling. It's about the *facing*. He's chosen a direction to look in, and he's held it long enough that the two upright cups have stopped counting as cups.

<!-- BEAT 10 · UNIT 2 — what's under it -->
### What's under it: the Moon

`[IMG-3]`

A path running from the bottom of the card up between two grey towers and off into hills. A dog and a wolf either side of it, both howling. And down in the pool at the front, a crayfish half out of the water.

The moon above has a face in it, and the face is not looking at you. It's looking down and to the side, the way somebody does when they're not going to say the thing.

This is the card for what you can't see straight, and stacked under the Five of Cups it says something quite specific. **The thing keeping you turned away from those two cups is not the loss.** It's that you can't currently tell which of your fears about it are real.

Look at the dog and the wolf. Same animal, roughly. One is safe and one isn't and in that light you cannot tell them apart — and that is the actual condition you're in. Not sad. *Unable to sort.* Every option looks like it might be the wolf, so you keep your head down and look at the wine, because at least the wine is a fact.

And the crayfish is the part people miss. It's coming up out of the water. Whatever this is, it's already surfacing on its own schedule. You're not deciding whether to look at it. You're deciding whether you look at it now or when it's fully out.

Now, what these two won't tell me.

They show me the current is there and roughly what it's made of. They don't show me **when it started** — and a thing like this always has a date. There's a first time. It sits years before whatever you'd point at, and it is not the event you'd name.

Five cards behind these give me the date, what's been feeding it since, and what actually cuts it. Two can't.

### Back to the Moon, and the part I want you to sit with

Nothing in that card is chasing anybody.

The dog isn't. The wolf isn't. The crayfish is minding its own business. The path is open, it's lit, and it goes somewhere.

The whole difficulty of the Moon is the light, not the danger. Everything in it is exactly as safe or unsafe as it would be at noon — you simply can't grade it right now, so you've stopped moving. And a person who stops moving in bad light decides the problem is the road.

That's how a thing gets called grief for years when it was something else. Not because anybody lied. Because in that light, the two look identical.

<!-- BEAT 13 · concrete ask -->
### In concrete terms, what do you do today?

Turn round, and count the two.

That's it. Not appreciate them, not be grateful — I'm not asking you to feel anything about them. Just name the two things that are still standing. Out loud, in the car, wherever. Most people find the naming harder than the grieving, and that difficulty is the reading.

<!-- BEAT 14 · authority flourish. ⛔ no price, no SLA -->
### Why an undertow gets seven

Because a current is not a thing, it's a *history*, and you cannot read a history off two cards.

So the five go: when it started, what has kept it alive since, who it still serves, how you'd recognise it the next time it surfaces, and what breaks it. The third of those is the one nobody expects. A current that has run for years is being fed by something, and often the person it protects is you — it keeps you from having to test whether the two cups are any good.

That's shadow work, and it's the reading I actually do. It's why I lay seven here and why I don't hand this one to anybody who hasn't told me what they're carrying.
