I don't choose the cards I lay in the morning. I want that clear before anything else, because it's the only reason this letter exists.

I cut the deck once, before it's light, and I read whatever is sitting on the cut. That's the whole method. No shuffling until something feels right, no second cut, no laying it again if I don't like what came up.

This morning the cut gave me two.

Not one card with another underneath it. Two, stuck face to face, come up off the cut together — which in fourteen years I have seen maybe a dozen times. Two cards off one cut is not a card about your day. It's a fork. It means you are standing at something, right now, and the deck knows it before you've said it out loud.

<!-- BEAT 7 · stakes preview -->
Read them in the order I've laid them. The first door is the one you're already standing in, so look at it plainly. The second is the one you keep deciding about and not deciding — take that one twice.

<!-- BEAT 8 · precedent. ONE name, distant, BUYER-change. ⛔ 07's own — not Ruth's story -->
The last time a cut handed me two like that, it was years ago, above a shop I used to read out of. A woman called Hester. Same shape — two doors, both of them open.

She'd been standing between hers for nine years. That was the thing. Not that she couldn't decide. That she'd been *deciding* for nine years and calling that a decision.

I laid her eight. And what the spread told her wasn't which door to walk through.

It was that she'd already walked through one, a long time back, and had been standing in the doorway ever since with one foot out, so she'd never have to admit it.

She stopped rehearsing it after that. That's all that changed, and it changed everything.

I'll show you both doors in a moment, %FIRSTNAME%. First, the thing that decides how you read them.

**You have already chosen.** That's the part people fight me on. A choice isn't a thing waiting in front of you — it's a thing you've been making, quietly, every morning for months. The spread doesn't decide it for you. The spread is how you find out what you already decided.

Right now you're being shown a quarter of that.

Because two cards is a quarter of an answer. The Two Doors is eight — the two you can see, and three behind each of them. Two cards tells you there's a fork. Eight tells you what each door costs, what it gives you, and who you turn into on the other side of it.

I'll explain how we lay the eight at the end. First, your two.

<!-- BEAT 10 · UNIT 1 -->
### Door one: the Devil

`[IMG-2]`

A horned figure squatting on a block. Two people chained at the neck below it. And the chains are loose — wide loops that would lift straight off over their heads if either of them stood up.

Nobody in that picture is trapped. They're staying.

That's what this card is for, and it's the door you're already standing in. **Nothing is holding you.** You're choosing it, every morning, and calling it something else — not the right time, not yet, not while things are like this.

Now look again, because there are *two* of them chained to that block. Not one. Somebody is down there beside you in this, and you knew their name before I said there was one.

Here's what the Devil won't tell me.

It shows me you're staying. It doesn't show me **which of the two of you stands up first** — and that decides the whole shape of it. If it's you, this ends one way. If it's them, it ends another, and you find out by being left.

The three cards behind this door tell me which. One card can't.

<!-- BEAT 10 · UNIT 2 -->
### Door two: the Eight of Cups

`[IMG-3]`

A figure with a stick, walking uphill into rocky ground, away from eight cups stacked neat behind them. Not looking back. The moon's out.

Eight cups is not nothing. That's the point of the picture — they're upright, they're arranged, they took years. **This is the door where you go, and it costs you every one of them.**

And it's the door you've been standing at long enough that you've stopped noticing you're at it.

What the Eight of Cups won't tell me is what's at the top of that hill.

The figure is walking into the dark and the card does not show you the other side. So I can't say from here whether this walk buys you a year or costs you one. Both look identical from the bottom of the hill, and women get that backwards constantly — they leave the thing that was working and stay in the thing that wasn't.

Three cards behind this door say which you're looking at. Two can't.

### Back to the Devil, and the part I want you to sit with

The loose chains are the whole card, %FIRSTNAME%.

This isn't a card about being held. Nothing in that picture is holding anybody. It's a card about *staying* — and staying is an act, done daily, by a person who could leave.

Which means the thing keeping you there was never the other person, and it was never the situation.

It's that walking out would mean admitting how long you stayed.

[Let me lay the eight]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=2&s=two-doors) and I'll tell you what that admission actually costs. It is not the thing people brace for.

### In concrete terms, what do you do now?

Two things, and the first one is free.

Today, catch yourself at the one moment you'd normally call it *not the right time*. Don't argue with it. Don't fix it. Just hear the sentence. That sentence is the chain, and hearing it once is more than most people manage in a year.

The second is that you [tell me what you're actually asking]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=3&s=two-doors). I don't lay somebody's eight off a guess — the six behind the doors are about *you*, and there's nothing for them to be about until you've said what you want to know.

<!-- BEAT 14 · authority flourish. Craft knowledge + scarcity of access -->
### Why yours is eight and not four

Most readers answer a fork with a two-card cross. One card for this, one for that, done in ten minutes.

I don't, and here's why. A door isn't one thing. It's what it takes from you, what it hands you, and the person you are at the end of it — and those three can point in completely different directions. Plenty of doors cost you almost nothing and leave you someone you don't want to be. Plenty cost you everything and are still the right door.

So it's three to a door. Six behind the two, plus the two you're looking at. Eight.

It's the longest of the seven I lay, and I only lay it on a morning the cut gives me two. That happened today.
