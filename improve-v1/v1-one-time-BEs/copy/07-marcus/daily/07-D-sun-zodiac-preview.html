Marcus.

Sunday, so you have the time for this one. It’s the longest thing I lay.

One cut before it’s light. Nobody asked.

Three came away, and they didn’t come as a fork or a ledger. They came up in houses, which needs a word of explanation before I turn them.

A zodiac has twelve houses and each one is a room of a life — what you hold, how you speak, where you came from, what you love, your days, who you’re tied to, what’s shared and what’s ending, what you believe, how you’re seen, who’s around you, and what’s hidden. Twelve rooms. This morning three of them lit up and the other nine stayed dark.

Which is the whole trouble, {{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}, and I’ll say it before I show you anything.

A house tells you where a thing is happening. That is what the zodiac layout is for and it is the only reading that does it. One card in the seventh house means something entirely different from the same card in the second, and three lit rooms cannot tell you what shape the house is.

The first house — you: the Queen of Swords

A crowned woman on a stone throne above the cloud line. An upright sword in her right hand. And her left hand raised, open, palm out — the oldest gesture there is for stop, and tell me the truth.

She is the most misread card in the deck. People call her cold. She is somebody who has already paid for what she knows, and the sword is upright because she is holding it, not swinging it.

Maybe you’d call it being realistic about money. Maybe you’d call it having learned the hard way. Maybe you’d call it the fact that you now read the whole contract, because there was a year when you didn’t.

The seventh house — the one you’re tied to: the King of Pentacles

A king on a throne carved with bulls’ heads, robe covered in grapevines, one hand resting on a coin and a castle behind him. Solid, settled, and entirely still.

The seventh house is partnership — a spouse, a business partner, whoever your money is actually entangled with. And what has landed in it is the card of the person who holds the position. Not a villain. A king in that house is somebody with the standing, the history and the last word on how things are done.

And I read the cards, not the man. That is what the spread says about the shape of the arrangement, not a claim about anybody’s intentions.

The twelfth house — what’s hidden: the High Priestess

A seated figure between one black pillar and one white one. A crescent moon at her feet. And a scroll in her lap, half covered by her robe, so you can read part of the word on it and not the rest.

The twelfth is the room of what has not been said out loud yet, and the card that landed in it is the card of information that exists and is not being shown. Note carefully: that is not the same as a lie. The scroll is right there in the open. It is simply not unrolled.

And plenty of what sits in a twelfth house was put there by the person it belongs to, for good reasons, years ago. There is often nothing to expose and nobody to catch.

Now, what these three won’t tell me. Individually they’re legible. What I cannot read is the traffic between them — whether what’s in the twelfth belongs to the seventh house or to the first, and which direction it’s travelling.

That’s not evasion. It is genuinely what the other nine houses are for: the second says what you actually hold, the eighth what is shared and what is ending, the tenth how it looks from outside. Nine rooms decide the meaning of these three.

What you’re looking at is called the Zodiac Spread

Twelve cards. Three you can see. Nine you can’t.

Twelve isn’t a number I picked. A zodiac has twelve houses, so a card goes in each — that is the whole design and it is a great deal older than I am. There is no version of it with nine.

You’ve seen three of the twelve. That’s a quarter of the house.

And the nine aren’t more detail on these three. These three told you which rooms are lit. The nine tell you what is in the rest of the house and which way it moves between them — a different question, and three lit rooms cannot answer it.

Here they are, still face down.

These 9 are still in my hand

So let me lay the nine that are still dark, and I’ll tell you which house that scroll actually belongs to.

Back to the twelfth house, and why it sits where it does

The twelfth is the last room, and in the old layouts it is the one directly behind the first.

That placement is the teaching. Whatever is in your twelfth house is standing at your own back — not across the table, not out in the world. It is the nearest thing to you that you cannot turn round fast enough to see, and a woman with a sword upright in the first house is exactly the person who would have stopped checking behind her.

So here’s the one to carry today. Don’t confuse a thing nobody has said with a thing nobody knows. Those are different rooms, and the second one is much rarer than people assume.

And the question you’re probably asking

Most people bring me some version of am I going to be all right. That has no answer in any spread, because it’s a whole life and the cards read a situation.

Try this one: which room is the money actually being decided in?

That one a twelve-house layout answers better than anything else I lay — because unlike every other spread, it is built to tell you where. It sits in the nine I haven’t turned.

So put that in front of me, in your own words.

Which of your rooms is it happening in?

Tell me, and I’ll lay the other nine.

Do that and you stop negotiating in the wrong room, which is how most people lose an argument they were right about.

— Marcus, at the table most mornings
