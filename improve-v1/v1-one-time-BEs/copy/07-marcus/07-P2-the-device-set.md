# 07-P2 — the device set

**Where the sentences come from.** 07's skeleton is [`02-E2`](../02/02-E2-esl-v1.md) — that decides
the *beats*. This file decides how the sentences inside them are built, and it comes from four
Brie 808 posts the operator supplied on 2026-09-02: the Lovers, the Ace of Pentacles, the Eight of
Cups, the Wheel of Fortune.

⚠ **They are social posts, not sales emails.** They carry no CTA, no withhold, no price, and run
250–320 words. Take the *sentence machinery*; never take the structure. The beats stay 02's.

---

## ⭐ The rule all four share: refuse the cliché first

Every one of the four opens by refusing what the card is conventionally reduced to.

> *"The Lovers is often reduced to romance, but…"* · *"It is not about wishing harder."* ·
> *"This is not impulsive walking away."* · *"The Wheel does not ask you to sit around waiting for luck."*

⛔ **Beat 3 refuses the cliché.** This is why they read as freshly *seen* rather than recited, and
it is the cheapest authority in the form — a reader who has met the card before learns something in
the first line. 07's Tuesday: *"The Devil gets read as addiction. It isn't. It's a card about
staying."*

**Sharpen by negation before you define.** Say what it is not, then what it is. Both halves earn.

## The devices

### 1 · The mirror stack — three parallel maybes
**Replaces guessing who she is.** This list cannot be segmented (§9 of the spec), so don't try —
offer three concrete mirrors and let her pick her own. It reads as recognition, not a filter.

> *"Maybe you'd call it patience. Maybe you'd call it not the right time, not while his mother's
> ill. Maybe you'd call it loving somebody through a rough patch that is now four years long."*

⛔ **It also fixes the register.** Marcus is direct, not accusing. The stack **hands her the words**
instead of correcting hers. A draft that says *"you're choosing it and calling it something else"*
is a lecture; the stack is a mirror.

### 2 · Shame removal — a conversion mechanism, not warmth
The offer depends on her **disclosing** something. If admitting it costs her shame, she never sends
the question, and the whole funnel stops at the email.

> *"None of it has to have been terrible… There can still be love in it. You don't have to hate a
> thing to be finished with it, and you don't need somebody to blame before you're allowed to go."*

Memory's `email-the-tell-winning-formula` — the single best-selling v2 email — lists *remove shame*
as an explicit step. This is that step.

### 3 · The question reframe — the bridge to the CTA
**The strongest device in the set**, because it *is* what 07 sells. Tell her the question she came
with has no answer, hand her the one that does, then ask for it.

> *"Most people bring me some version of will he change. That question has no answer in any spread
> I lay, because it isn't about you… Try this one instead: **what am I still getting from staying?**
> That one has an answer, and it sits in the six cards I haven't laid."*

She is taught her question was the wrong *shape* and then invited to send the better one. Beat 13.

### 4 · Anti-mysticism as authority
Refuse the thing the rest of the inbox is selling. Two flavours, both usable:

- against **magical thinking** — *"It is not about wishing harder."*
- against **fatalism** — *"The Wheel does not ask you to sit around waiting for luck."*

> 07: *"I'm not going to tell you to manifest a door open. Doors don't move because somebody wanted
> them to, and anyone selling you that is selling you the wishing, not the reading."*

Marcus's plain-spoken register owns this, and it differentiates him from every other tarot email
she gets.

### 5 · Concrete noun lists — how a topic gets an object
The drafts read abstract because they had no object. A short list of ordinary nouns fixes it without
knowing the reader.

> *"a modest job offer, a new client, a savings habit, a course, an introduction that does not seem
> important at first"* → 07: *"The friends who came with him. The Sunday routine. The version of the
> story you've told your mother for six years."*

### 6 · Name the cognitive error
One quotable diagnosis per email. It is the line she repeats to somebody else.

> *"do not confuse a slow season with a permanent one"* → 07: *"don't confuse a decision you keep
> making with a decision you haven't made."*

### 7 · Job assignment — what her job is not, and is
> *"Your job is not to control every movement. Your job is to stay grounded enough to move with
> what changes."*

### 8 · The one-line paragraph as a beat
All four use a bare sentence on its own line to land a turn. *"The value is often in what you build
from it."* · *"And yet, deep down, you know it is no longer enough."* Use sparingly — one or two
per email, never a run of them.

## What NOT to take

| | |
|---|---|
| **Their length** | 250–320 words. 07 runs 955–1,367 because it sells. Don't shrink to match |
| **Their open ending** | They close on a question with no link. 07 closes on three text CTAs |
| **Giving it all away** | They deliver the whole card. ⛔ 07's spread stays unfinished — that is the offer |
| **"We" and "us"** | Their register is inclusive. Marcus is first person singular and speaks to one person |

## The asset gap

All four photograph a **real card on velvet, held in a hand, with a candle and stones** — a table,
not a render. 07 currently uses a scanned card on white, and has **no card back at all** (104 files
in `assets/tarot-rws`, none of them a back).

⛔ **Worth a small shoot:** the backs first, because the face-down hero is mandatory on every send,
then the faces used most.
