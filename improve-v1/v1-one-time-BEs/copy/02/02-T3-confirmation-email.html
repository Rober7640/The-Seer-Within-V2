While you wait, let me tell you something

The man who taught me to read cards told me this one, a long time ago now, and I've never quite been able to put it down. It isn't about tarot. He didn't tell it to me as though it were.

A farmer kept a donkey. They'd worked the same fields together for years — not a pet, exactly, but the two of them understood each other the way you do after that long.

One hot afternoon the donkey went down a well.

The farmer heard him from the yard. He stood at the edge of it for most of an hour, listening to the animal bray, and worked out that he had no way of getting him up. The donkey was old. The well was dry and needed sealing anyway.

So he went for his neighbours, and they came with shovels, and they began to fill the well in.

The donkey understood immediately what was happening. He made a terrible noise.

And then he stopped — all at once — and the silence was so much worse than the braying that the farmer put down his shovel and went to look over the edge.

What he saw was this. Every shovelful of earth that landed on the donkey's back, the donkey shook off. And then he stepped up onto it.

Shake it off, step up. Shake it off, step up.

They kept shovelling because they didn't know what else to do, and the donkey kept rising, and when the well was full he walked out over the top of it and off across the field.

I've thought about that story on a great many bad afternoons, dear.

The thing that gets me isn't the cleverness of it. It's that from where the farmer was standing, he was burying a donkey. He'd have sworn to it. He was doing the kind thing and he was doing it carefully, and the whole time the animal was using every shovelful to climb.

What lands on you and what you stand on are not always two different things. Sometimes you only find out which it was afterwards.

If it's any use to you, send it on to someone. That's what a story like that is for, and it costs you nothing to be the one who passed it along.
