I drew three cards for you last night, in order — and nobody asked me to

One of them explains something that already happened and was never explained. One of them is a choice you’re making tonight without calling it one. The third is why I’m writing at an hour I don’t send — it starts turning inside five to seven days.

I’m writing tonight because I don’t think this will keep until morning.

I don’t draw for people one at a time, dear. I want to say that plainly before anything else, because it’s the whole reason this letter exists.

What I send you most days is written for everybody who reads me. Every word of it is true and I stand behind all of it — but it isn’t yours. It was never meant to be.

This is.

I’d finished for the night and I was closing the deck. Squaring it, wrapping it back into its cloth, the way I’ve done every night for longer than I care to count.

And a card came away in my hand.

Then, before I’d even set it down, a second.

Then a third.

Not fanned. Not spilled across the table. Three cards, out of a deck I was in the middle of putting away — each one on its own, a beat apart, not together the way it usually happens.

I’ve learned not to argue with that, dear. When cards come out of a deck on their own they’ve already decided who they belong to. These came away with your name on them, one at a time, in an order that meant something before I’d even turned the first one over.

So I turned them over in the order they’d come. The order told me almost as much as the cards did. The first was something already finished. The second was happening to you this week, unnamed. The third hadn’t happened at all. Not yet.

Here’s how I want you to read what’s below, dear. Take the first card and let yourself have it — it’s already yours. Sit with the second, because it’s asking something of you right now, whether you’ve noticed or not.

Then read the third one twice.

And read all three of them as near. This isn’t a draw about some distant stretch of your life — the first sign of the third card shows itself inside five to seven days, and that’s why I didn’t wait until morning.

The last time cards came out of a deck at me like that, it was years ago, in the back room I used to read out of before any of this. A woman named Corinne. Same shape of draw — something already paying off, a threshold she didn’t know she stood in, a wheel that hadn’t finished turning.

She let me lay the full spread.

And the wheel did turn, in the end, more or less when the cards said it would. I want to be honest with you about that. It turned anyway.

But she knew by then what her own luck was standing on. So when the turn came, she wasn’t spending down the very thing that was supposed to catch her. She still had it under her.

That’s the whole difference, dear. Not that nothing happens to you.

That you’re not the one holding nothing when it does.

I’ll show you all three in a moment.

But before I do, you should know what you’re holding.

Your future is already drawn, dear. That’s the part people struggle with. It isn’t waiting somewhere to be decided — it’s laid, it’s in the deck, and the only question left is how much of it you get to see before it reaches you.

Right now you’re being shown a quarter of it.

Because three cards is not the whole story. It’s the shape of one. A full spread is twelve — one for each house, one for each room of a life. Three tells you which room you stood in, which you’re standing in now, and which door is about to open. Twelve tells you what’s inside each room, and what to do about it.

I’ll explain how we do the twelve at the end. First, your three.

Your first card: the Empress

A woman crowned with twelve stars, seated in a field of wheat grown taller than it has any right to be, a stream moving behind her that never seems to run dry.

This is your past, dear — not because it’s old, but because it already happened, and it went right, and it never actually stopped happening.

It’s a trump card, and there’s no better one in the deck for a thing that’s already growing. When it comes up first in a draw it says one thing plainly. Something you did, or chose, or survived, already turned out well for you — and it hasn’t stopped.

This is the harvest of a thing you stopped crediting yourself for. The Empress throws real fertility around a woman — love first, and close behind it, money, both planted from the same root.

Because there’s a harvest sitting in this card, and the reading of it came back plain: something is already yours, and it’s larger than you’ve been letting yourself notice.

Now here’s what the Empress won’t tell me.

It shows me the harvest. It does not show me what set it growing.

And that matters more than it sounds, dear. A woman who doesn’t know what planted her own luck can’t tell good soil from a lucky year, and can make a bad decision standing right on top of what’s feeding her.

Twelve cards give me the root. Three can’t.

Your second card: the High Priestess

A woman seated between two pillars, one cut from black stone and one from white, the same height, the same width, holding between them nothing but a veil stitched with pomegranates. A crescent moon rests at her feet.

This is your present, dear. Coming second in your draw, it doesn’t just describe a threshold — it says the threshold is already under your feet.

You’re sitting between those two pillars right now. Not someday. Tonight.

The black pillar is the silence you already know how to keep — the sentence you keep almost saying, then folding back up. The white pillar is what happens if you say it instead.

From where you’re sitting they look exactly the same height. Nothing about this announces itself as a choice. It feels like another ordinary evening of not saying the thing.

And here’s what I feel sitting against the Empress, dear. There’s a hush in you at the moment — a sense that if you wait long enough, you’ll feel certain before you speak. You’ve been telling yourself that a while now.

The High Priestess says the certainty isn’t coming first. You say it before you’re sure, or you don’t say it at all.

Now the difficulty, and it’s the whole reason I’m writing tonight instead of tomorrow.

The High Priestess sits between the pillars. She doesn’t tell me which one you’re already leaning toward.

Because you are leaning, dear — nobody sits upright between two things for long. You’ve already tipped, and you know which way better than you’re letting on.

The twelve tell me which pillar. The house the High Priestess falls into is what separates a woman who’s about to speak from a woman who’s about to let the silence become permanent.

Your third card: the Wheel of Fortune

I don’t like this card and I’m not going to pretend otherwise.

A great wheel turning on its own axle. A sphinx seated motionless at the top of it, a sword across her knees. On one side, something is climbing. On the other, something is sliding down. Neither of them gets a say in which.

This is your future. Third in a draw, this card doesn’t warn about someday — it says the turn has already started.

Understand me: this isn’t a negative card. It’s a warning, and a warning is a kindness — a pressing one. Ignore it and it will undo the two good cards sitting in front of it.

And here is what most readers won’t tell you about the Wheel.

It does not arrive as news.

It arrives as a direction already underway in something you assumed was settled.

Either something that’s been quietly climbing for a while now — a job, a friendship, the very harvest your first card is showing you — is about to crest into full view.

Or something you built a good stretch of your life on top of, that’s been up there so long you stopped checking under it, is already coming down, and has been for longer than you know.

Which of the two is turning in your Wheel, I can’t tell you from a single card. There’s a real chance, dear, that they’re not even two separate things — opposite ends of the very same turn.

What I can tell you is that it is load-bearing. It’s the axle your other two cards are turning around — the harvest, the pillar you’re leaning toward. All of it sits on this.

Which end of the Wheel has your name on it, and how close it’s already turned: that is what the other nine cards are for.

In concrete terms, what do you do now?

Nothing complicated.

You authorise the full spread here. That’s the only thing between you and the rest of the answer. I don’t lay a woman’s twelve without her say-so — the cards won’t sit for it, and frankly neither will I.

Back to the High Priestess, and the choice that’s already open

The High Priestess points to something you’re going to have to say out loud inside the next few weeks, dear, and I’ll say plainly what it’s circling.

A twin flame. Not a pleasant thing to finally say out loud. The sentence that decides which pillar you end up standing beside.

That is what this deck of mine is for, and it’s why I read for the women I read for.

But a twin flame recognised and a twin flame missed are two different outcomes from the same evening, and women miss theirs constantly — because the sentence arrives on an ordinary Tuesday, in an ordinary kitchen, while they’re still waiting to feel sure.

The full spread gives me the room. That’s the thing three cards can’t do and twelve can — which house the pillar falls in, and which way you’re leaning inside it.

And look at where the veil sits in that card, because it decides everything. It hangs behind her, not in front. What’s hidden isn’t hidden from you. It’s hidden by you.

Leave the veil where it is and you’ll be sitting between the same two pillars a year from now.

Let me lay them and I’ll tell you which pillar you’re already closer to.

And back to the Wheel — this is the part I want you to sit with

Here’s what the Wheel is actually warning you about, dear. It isn’t the turn.

It’s finding out from underneath it.

The turning happens either way. Wheels turn; that’s what the card is. The only thing it’s offering you is the chance to already know which side you’re on when it does.

And that is not a small thing. A woman who’s seen which way her own Wheel is turning, weeks out, does not lose what a woman loses finding out on a Tuesday morning that the thing she stopped checking on is already gone. Two entirely different women, same turn.

One of them had time to decide what she wanted to be holding when it happened.

Draw the twelve with me, and you’ll know which side of the Wheel you’re already on.

Why I’m using the Major Arcana for yours

Every deck holds seventy-eight cards.

Twenty-two of them are the Major Arcana — the Empress, the High Priestess, the Wheel, the ones you already know by name. The other fifty-six are the minor: cups and swords and coins, the small daily weather of a life.

Your three came out of the twenty-two. All three of them.

That doesn’t happen by accident and it changes what kind of spread this has to be. So I’m going to pull your twelve from the Major Arcana only. No minors, no filler. Twelve trumps, laid one to a house.

It’s the heaviest reading I do. It takes the whole of a night and it takes something out of me, and I keep it for the women whose first draw asks for it.

Yours asked for it.

And they’ll be twelve new cards, dear. Not these three again. These three have already said what they came to say.

→ Follow me here, and let me lay them

I’m not going to promise you the twelve will make your life easy. That isn’t what a reading does, and you’d be right not to believe me if I said it.

Here’s what it does do.

You stop finding out from underneath things.

The next stretch of your life stops arriving as a run of turns you have to recover from, and starts arriving as a run of turns you were already standing ready for. The Wheel still turns. You’re just not the one caught under it.

That’s the whole of it. It’s worth more than it sounds.

Your twelve are waiting whenever you’re ready.

I’m on your side in this, dear.
