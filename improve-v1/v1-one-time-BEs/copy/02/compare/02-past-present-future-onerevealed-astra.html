Past, Present, Future. The Past is open below, dear. The other two stay face-down until you ask for the rest of the reading.

I’m writing tonight with two cards still face-down beside my hand.

I don’t draw for people one at a time, dear. What I send you most days is written for everybody who reads me. You know my letters. But knowing my letters and having a reading of your own are different things.

What I usually send isn’t yours.

This is.

I was squaring the deck to put it away when three cards came loose together, out of the middle. They landed on the cloth with their backs up. I had your name in mind before I reached to gather them.

So I left them there. Past on the left. Present in the middle. Future on the right.

I turned the Past. And I knew why I hadn’t put the deck away.

Years ago, a woman named Helena sat across from me with her handbag still on her lap. She had spent three years keeping Friday evenings free for a man who would only ever say, “I’ll let you know.” She called it being patient.

The full reading gave her something to watch for: whether he made room for her without being reminded. The next Friday, he cancelled again. The reading hadn’t stopped him. But Helena had made a plan of her own that evening, and she went.

She didn’t spend another night beside the phone, dear. That was the difference.

Tonight you get the first card, properly read. For the other two, I’ll give you only the question each position answers. Their faces stay against the cloth.

The rest belongs with the full reading: twelve new cards, laid in twelve houses. A house is simply a room of a life — home, partnership, the things you keep to yourself. Those rooms separate things that feel the same when you’re living through them.

Your Past: the Hanged Man

A man hangs upside down from a living tree, held by one foot. His other leg is bent. There is light around his head, and his face is calm. Look at that calm, dear. He has been still long enough for an uncomfortable position to look ordinary.

You learned to wait for love by putting your own life on hold. That is what this card says in the Past position.

You gave someone time. Then more time. You moved the conversation to a better evening, left a question out of a message, made yourself easier to fit around the life he already had. There was always a reason to wait a little longer, and you were good at understanding it.

That was a real gift you gave. Your patience was real. So was what it cost you.

After a while you stopped asking when your turn would come. You learned to take pride in how little you needed. The Hanged Man shows the sacrifice plainly: you kept the connection by being the one who could do without.

And the light around his head matters. You saw more than you said. You understood that the waiting had become the arrangement. You stayed anyway.

Here is the one piece this card does not settle: what held you there — the home you had built, or the relationship itself? Those are different reasons to keep a life suspended. The new cards in the houses of home and partnership are what separate them.

That is the unfinished part of your Past. I’ll leave it unfinished here.

Where is that old habit of waiting taking your attention in love now?

In concrete terms, what do you do now?

You authorise the full reading here. No letter explaining your history. No questions for you to answer first. Just your say-so to go beyond this first card.

Look once more at his face

He looks peaceful. That is why this card gets past people. They look for a woman in pieces and miss the woman who has become very good at waiting quietly.

But you didn’t come to me just to become better at waiting, dear. You wanted to recognise a love that meets you.

A twin flame. Someone you recognise without having to make yourself smaller to keep him.

That is what the full reading is for: recognition, the kind of connection to look for, and the window in which to pay attention. Let me take you through it.

Think of Helena with her handbag on her lap. She had been ready to leave the room the whole time she was telling me why she had to stay. What helped her was having something clear to notice the next time Friday came.

Read the full spread with me. Give yourself something clearer than another evening of going over the same conversation.

Why twelve cards?

The Hanged Man is one of the twenty-two Major Arcana, the cards that deal with the larger turns of a life. The full spread uses twelve new cards from those twenty-two, one for each house. The first three mark the questions. The twelve give us room to answer them.

Home and partnership are separate rooms, dear. What holds one together does not automatically make the other happy. That is why the distinction left open in your Past needs more than one card.
