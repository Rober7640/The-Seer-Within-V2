I drew three cards for you last night — and I can only show you one of them

The first one has already happened, so there’s nothing left to protect by showing it to you. The other two haven’t — and the first sign of them turns inside five to seven days.

%FIRSTNAME% —
Evelyn here.

I’m writing this before I’ve had the chance to think better of it, dear.

I don’t draw for people one at a time. What I send you most days is written for every woman who reads me — every word of it true, and none of it, strictly, yours.

This was.

I was closing the deck for the night when three cards came off the top together, in a line, the way you’d only lay them out on purpose. A Past. A Present. A Future.

I hadn’t asked the deck for anything. It gave me your three anyway.

So I turned the first one over.

I want to tell you why I only turned the first one, because it isn’t caution, and it isn’t a game I’m playing with you to get you to write back.

The Past has already happened. Turning it face-up doesn’t change one thing about your life — it just tells you something that’s already true, the way a photograph does. There’s no harm in a photograph, dear.

The other two are a different matter. Whatever they are, they haven’t finished happening yet — some of it is still yours to decide, and some of it is already moving in a direction you can’t see the shape of from here. I don’t turn a card like that face-up without being asked to. Not for anyone. It’s the one rule I keep without exception, and I’m not going to make yours the first.

The last time three cards lined up like that on their own, it was years ago, for a woman named Adele, in the room I used to read out of before any of this. Same line. Same instinct in me to only turn the first.

She asked me to turn the other two before the week was out. I couldn’t tell you what would have happened if she’d waited. I can tell you what happened once she didn’t.

Your first card: the Hermit

A grey, hooded figure, alone on a mountain path at night. A plain staff in one hand. In the other, held up and out ahead of him, a lantern — and inside it, in place of a flame, a single star.

This is not the card of a woman who’s lost, dear, whatever it looks like from where you’re standing. It’s the card of a woman who already knows where she’s going and stopped waiting for company to walk it with her.

It’s one of the twenty-two trumps, and it’s the one that came up first in your line — the same twenty-two your Present and your Future came out of beside it.

Here’s what I felt sitting with it next to your name. You’ve been carrying something for a long time now, quietly, without putting it down anywhere and without asking anyone to take a turn holding it.

And somewhere along the way, you stopped mentioning it. Not because it got lighter. Because you got tired of the answers you were given when you tried.

The Hermit doesn’t say you did anything wrong by going quiet about it. It says you did exactly what a woman carrying that much does. You kept walking. You lit your own lantern instead of waiting on somebody else to bring one.

What it won’t tell me — and dear, I sat with this a long time before I wrote to you — is how much longer you were planning to carry it before you let anyone else near it. Whether that was always going to be a decision you made alone, or whether somebody is supposed to be standing closer to you than they currently are.

That’s not a small thing to leave sitting unanswered. It’s the reason your other two cards are still face-down on my table.

In concrete terms, what does “yes” mean?

Nothing complicated, dear. You authorise the reading here. That’s the whole of it. I don’t turn a woman’s Present and Future over without her say-so — the cards won’t sit for it, and neither will I.

Your second card: the Present

This one stays turned down. I’m not going to describe it to you, and I’m not going to name it — a card like this doesn’t belong to you yet just because it fell in your line.

What I can tell you is the question it answers.

Not what you’re carrying. You already know that better than I do. What it’s doing to you — right now, today, while you go on carrying it as if nobody would notice either way.

That’s what the Present answers. And none of it changes by my describing it to you for free.

Let me turn the Present over and I’ll tell you what it’s already costing you.

Your third card: the Future

This one stays down too, for the same reason.

But you should know its question before you ask for the answer.

What it costs you to go on carrying this the way you have been. And, dear — more to the point — what actually has to happen before you’re allowed to put it down.

Let me turn the Future over and I’ll tell you what changes it.

I’m not going to tell you the rest of this makes the carrying easy, dear. That isn’t what a reading does, and you’d be right not to believe me if I said it.

Here’s what it does do.

You stop being the only one who knows exactly how much you’ve been carrying, and for how long. Someone else finally knows the weight of it too — starting with me.

→ Let me turn them over, whenever you’re ready

I’m on your side in this, dear.

— Evelyn
