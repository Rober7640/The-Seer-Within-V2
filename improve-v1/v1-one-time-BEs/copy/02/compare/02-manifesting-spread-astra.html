Alignment — the Four of Pentacles

Look at how much of the body is occupied. Both arms close around one coin. Both feet pin down two more. The fourth sits above the crown. There is a city behind the figure, but everything in the picture is being held in place.

Here, in Alignment, that grip is the sentence you add immediately after you admit what you want.

“But I’d settle for less than that.”

You do it even when nobody is listening, dear. You let yourself want the whole thing for a moment. Then you begin reducing it to an amount you could ask for without feeling foolish. By the time you imagine saying it aloud, you’ve already made the concession.

And there is relief in that. If you ask for very little, a refusal cannot expose how much you wanted.

You are protecting yourself from the embarrassment of wanting more than you receive. That is what this card is holding so tightly in your first position.

Getting into alignment means letting your own wish finish its sentence. When the little correction comes — I’d settle for — leave it off. Admit the full wish to yourself without attaching the smaller version you think you could bear to be refused.

You can be grateful for what you have and still say that it does not answer this particular longing. Gratitude does not require you to pretend you have stopped wanting.

There is one detail I cannot give you from this first card: which decision to handle differently first, so that your smaller request stops deciding for you. That belongs to the rest of this reading.

I remember a woman called Sylvia bringing a folded page to a reading long ago. She had written what she wanted, then crossed out the sentence. Underneath was a much more reasonable one.

When we finished, she drew a line through the replacement instead. She told me she had been rehearsing how little she could accept for so long that the first sentence had begun to feel rude.

She took the page home with her original words showing. That was the change in her, dear. She could look at what she wanted without immediately apologising to herself for it.

Why these four positions matter

A tarot card has a picture; a position gives that picture its question. I read the grip in your first card against Alignment — how you stand in relation to what you want. I cannot use that same grip to announce a date. Timing has its own place in this spread.

And once the remaining cards are turned, I read them together. The first stays on the cloth. Its meaning is part of the answer, not something discarded when you ask me to continue.

The rest of your Manifesting spread answers these three questions:

Action — face-down

What concrete action can I take to bring my desire into my life?

Mastery — face-down

What do I have to get better at, or stop doing, to bring my desire into my life?

Timing — face-down

When will my desire manifest?
