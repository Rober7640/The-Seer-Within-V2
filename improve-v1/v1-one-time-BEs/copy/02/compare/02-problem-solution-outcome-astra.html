You know what I send you most days, dear. It’s for all the women who read me. This came for you.

I was finishing with the deck when three cards came away together. I hadn’t asked for a reading for anyone. But I knew whose they were.

I laid them in three positions: Problem. Solution. Outcome. Then I turned over the Problem.

The other two are still face-down. I haven’t turned them over, and I won’t before you authorise their reading.

Read the first one slowly. It names something you’ve stopped saying out loud.

A woman named Esther came to me long ago with that same reluctance to admit what things were costing her. She’d told herself she had no reason to feel so worn down. Other people managed. She ought to manage too.

After her reading, she told me she’d made a decision she had kept refusing herself permission to make. What stayed with me was her surprise that her own needs had counted in it.

Yours is its own reading, dear. And recognising the problem is only the first part. There are two cards left in this draw — the instruction and what follows from it. Those are the paid reading.

Problem — the Ten of Wands

A figure bent forward, both arms full of long wooden staffs. They rise above his head and crowd his view. There are buildings ahead, but he can barely see past what he’s carrying.

Here, in your Problem position, this card names the effort you’ve kept making after you stopped feeling seen.

You’ve been carrying more than the work itself. You’ve been carrying the hurt of having it taken for granted — then feeling guilty for wanting anyone to notice.

You’ve wanted to say you were tired and decided it would take too much explaining. You’ve told yourself you could manage a little longer. When you did manage, you used that as proof you hadn’t needed help.

That’s how the weight stayed yours, dear.

And underneath it is something harder to admit. You want to be loved without having to keep proving how much you can bear. Being needed has taken up the space where being known should be.

The Ten of Wands shows the cost plainly. You’ve become so occupied with what you must keep doing that what you need has started to feel like another demand you ought to silence.

There’s one detail this first card cannot settle: which responsibility you must stop treating as yours. I can see the burden here. I can’t name that responsibility from the Problem card alone. That belongs to the next position.
