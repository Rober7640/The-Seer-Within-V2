The first of your three cards shows why. The other two are still face-down.

Evelyn here. I know this is an hour you don’t expect a letter from me.

I was putting the deck away. As I folded the cloth over it, three cards slipped free together, their backs toward me. I hadn’t asked a question. I hadn’t begun a reading.

I don’t draw for people one at a time, dear. But I knew these were yours, and I couldn’t put them back.

I laid them in three positions: Problem, Solution, Outcome. Then I turned the first.

That is the card I’m showing you tonight. I’ve left the other two exactly where they are.

Problem — the Eight of Swords

A woman stands blindfolded, her arms bound against her body. Eight swords stand in the ground around her. There is water at her feet and a castle behind her, far off.

Look carefully at the swords. They do not make a closed ring. There is ground she could step onto, but she cannot see it from inside the blindfold.

Here, in the Problem position, this is the choice you keep taking back from yourself.

You let yourself want something. For a moment you knew you wanted it, without having to explain why.

Then you began asking whether wanting it made you selfish. You went over what it would cost, what you might regret, whether you had any right to disturb what you already had.

And when you told yourself, I don’t have to decide yet, you felt your whole body ease.

You took that easing as proof you had made the right decision, dear.

It was relief at no longer having to choose.

That is the bind this card names. Every time you approach the choice, you require yourself to feel no guilt before you make it. The guilt returns. You withdraw. The relief comes back and persuades you to leave things as they are.

Nothing has been settled. Your own wish is still there, waiting for you to finish judging yourself for having it.

The blindfold matters because you have begun calling this uncertainty. You know what you want. What you don’t know is how to act while part of you still objects.

There is one detail I am leaving unanswered: the first action to take when that guilt starts speaking again. The Problem card names the bind. That instruction belongs to the next position.

I remember a woman named Beatrice who would explain a wish, then spend longer explaining why she shouldn’t have it. When she returned after her reading, she caught herself doing it. She stopped halfway through an apology and let the wish stand.

She was still uncomfortable. She simply finished saying what she wanted. I remember how surprised she sounded to hear the whole sentence.

Each position gives its card a particular question, dear. A picture read as the problem is doing a different job from a picture read as the action. That is why I laid these three in order, and why I won’t turn a description of your bind into an instruction it hasn’t given.

These are the questions waiting under the two cards still face-down.

Solution — face-down

What is the first action I need to take when guilt makes me withdraw my choice?

Outcome — face-down

What changes in my life if I take that action and follow it through?

→ Give me permission to turn your other two cards

Your Problem / Solution / Outcome reading is this same draw completed. I turn the two remaining cards and read their answers alongside the Eight of Swords you have seen here.

Authorise the rest of your reading here, dear. Those two questions are what you are asking me to answer.

I won’t promise that acting on an answer feels comfortable. Beatrice still felt the discomfort. But she could hear her own wish all the way to the end.

That is what I want for you: room for your own wishes in the decisions you live with.

Let me complete your three-card reading.

I’m on your side in this, dear.

— Evelyn
