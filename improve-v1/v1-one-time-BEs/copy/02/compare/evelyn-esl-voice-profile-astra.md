## 3 · HOW SHE SOUNDS

**Intimate authority, with limits on specificity rather than uncertainty about the prediction.** “Luck is going to favour you” is flat. “Which door” is withheld. “It arrives as a person” is flat. Which person remains unresolved. README decision 3 explicitly records removal of “usually / tends to / almost never”; E2 calls that softening the first draft's largest problem. Do not turn every assertion into “might,” and do not remove the withhold to make her sound more certain.

### Address, rhythm, and the admission

- **“Dear” belongs here.** E4 uses it in the practice disclaimer, reassurance, interpretation, warning, and final allegiance. B1/B2 combine it with the reader's name; README explicitly restored body personalization. “No dear” would reverse the source, not improve it.
- **Let short lines terminate a longer explanation.** “This is.” “Then read the third one twice.” “It's the surprise.” “Yours asked for it.” The card units also contain substantial multi-sentence paragraphs; E4 includes roughly 60-word paragraphs, so a rigid tiny-paragraph maximum would misdescribe it.
- **Use the table-side vocabulary already present:** square a deck, wrap a cloth, a door, a room, ordinary clothes, a phone, something to show a friend. Turn those objects into distinctions she can use: door = where; room = which domain; facing = preparedness. [E4]
- **Em-dashes and reversals are native.** E2/README explicitly permit em-dashes and flag paragraphs with two or more. E4 uses “It isn't the fall. It's the surprise,” paired contrasts, and “Not … Not …” fragments. There is no source basis for importing a blanket ban on either reversal or aphoristic cadence.
- **Admit the normal broadcast.** “Written for everybody … but it isn't yours” is the deliberate setup for the claimed exception. Do not replace it with a generic warm greeting. [E4; README decision 5]
