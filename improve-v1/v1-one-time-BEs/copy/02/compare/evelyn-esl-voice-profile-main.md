# EVELYN CROSS — TWIN FLAME TAROT VOICE PROFILE

Source: `02-E2-esl-v1.md`, the first letter of this offer, plus its own build notes. Three things:
who this letter is for, what beats it's built from, and how she actually sounds. Every rule below
is sourced to a real line in that file — nothing here is imported from a voice guide built for a
different offer or a different persona.

> A woman who already trusts Evelyn interrupts her own routine because three cards came away
> uninvited. She hands over two blessings and a warning, states all three flat, and sells
> permission to see what a quarter of a spread can't finish saying.

---

## 1 · WHO

**Not a stranger.** She's a prior buyer on a women-only gated list, and she already gets Evelyn's
daily mail. The letter's whole premise — "what I send you most days ... isn't yours. This is." —
only works because she has a baseline of ordinary Evelyn correspondence to contrast against. Don't
write this as a cold intro to who Evelyn is.

**Already a believer.** She's bought a reading before. The job here isn't convincing her tarot is
real — it's convincing her *this specific draw* is hers. That's why the letter can state
predictions flat ("luck is going to favour you," "it arrives as a person") with no persuasion-of-
the-premise underneath them. A reader who needed convincing that psychics are real wouldn't have
opened mail from one in the first place.

**The actual ache, in the letter's own words:** *"There's a tension in you at the moment. A sense
that not enough attention is paid to what you do — and, far worse, none at all to who you actually
are underneath it. You've been carrying that a while and you've stopped mentioning it to anyone."*
That's the real target. Not loneliness, not romantic impatience — an unseen, uncredited effort she's
stopped voicing because voicing it stopped working. Whatever card or frame a new letter uses, that
specific ache is what it should be locating.

**The actual fear:** finding out last. The Tower unit isn't "something bad will happen" — it's
*"it does not arrive as an event. It arrives as a person ... someone still very much here, who has
already begun leaving and hasn't said so."* The fear isn't the ending. It's being the only one in
the room who doesn't already know.

**Love is the stated domain, not necessarily the root feeling.** The World unit says the good
period runs "in love first, and close behind love, money" — so love is where the *offer* points.
But the ache underneath (carrying an uncredited weight) and the fear underneath (finding out last)
are not intrinsically romantic. A widow, a woman deep in a marriage, a woman carrying a family
alone — all of them can recognize the ache and the fear before the letter ever specifies who or
what she's carrying it for. Keep the feeling load-bearing and let "love" arrive as the answer, not
the premise.

## 2 · THE BEATS

The letter is one continuous read, not a template with slots — but it has a fixed order and each
beat is doing one specific job. Skipping a job, not moving its position, is what breaks it.

| Beat | Job | What it actually says |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | State the whole claim in one breath, before any explanation | "I drew three cards for you last night — and nobody asked me to" |
| Deck line (italic) | The payoff and the window, in one sentence | Two good, one troubling, first sign inside 5–7 days |
| Hero image | Face-down, always | Never reveal a card here — the backs are the question, the faces are the payoffs, and each face appears only at its own unit |
| Salutation | Plain, no ceremony | "%FIRSTNAME% — / Evelyn here." |
| Urgency opener | Why tonight, specifically | "I don't think this will keep until morning" |
| Origin story | The act must be uninvited | She doesn't draw for individuals — daily mail is for everyone, this isn't. The deck did this, not her choice |
| Stakes preview | Tell her how to read what's coming, before she reads it | Keep the first two, sit with the third, read all three as *near* |
| Precedent | One name, distant, undated | Ruth — a buyer whose life changed, not a third party's outcome being promised |
| The big idea, withheld | Name the structural gap before showing anything | Three cards is a quarter of an answer; twelve is the whole one |
| Unit 1 — blessing | Picture, meaning stated flat, one withheld detail | The World: luck confirmed; withholds *which door it comes through* |
| Unit 2 — blessing + fork | Same shape, now with two indistinguishable readings | The Lovers: renewal or new encounter; withholds *which one she's been handed* |
| Unit 3 — threat | Named as a shape, never a locked identity | The Tower: a person, one of two possible shapes; withholds *which one, and whose face* |
| First CTA | Only after all three units are complete — roughly 60% into the letter | Permission to draw the twelve. Nothing is asked before this point |
| Concrete ask | Say literally what "yes" requires | Authorize the spread. That's the entire action |
| Re-read — the Lovers | Deepen the fork into a named outcome | "A twin flame" — names the offer's own product inside the letter that sells it |
| Re-read — the Tower | Reframe the threat | Not the fall — the surprise. The event happens either way; the offer sells facing the right direction |
| Authority flourish | Justify the price and the novelty claim | 22 Major Arcana, all three cards came from it, twelve new trumps, one per house |
| Final CTA + promise | State what it does and doesn't do | Doesn't promise an easy life. Promises she stops finding out last |
| Sign-off | Relationship, not role | "I'm on your side in this, dear. — Evelyn" |
| P.S. (optional, not in this specimen) | A/B challenger only | Ship without it first; test it as the one variable |

**Non-negotiable from the build notes, not inferred:**
- Six links, one destination, verbs only from *permission · authorise · let me · draw · follow*.
  Never "buy."
- No price or SLA anywhere in the letter — first mention lives on the booking page.
- The withhold is the engine of the sale, not a hedge, and must stay exact: *"I can't tell you
  which of the two from a single card"* is doing real work and should never be softened away.
- Read the spread, not the card: what the World means here comes from what it's sitting next to in
  *this* draw, not from a tarot dictionary or an earlier email's take on the same card.

**Identify the spread before deciding what's withheld — "the twelve" is not a universal law.**
This letter's paid product is a stand-alone twelve-card reading, one card per *house* — a real,
separate tarot structure where each position is a life domain (home, partnership, career, money,
and so on), not a time period. None of the free three cards belong to it; that is a deliberate
feature of *this specific offer*, not a fact about how Evelyn's readings work in general. A
different letter built on a different, self-contained spread — a three-card Past/Present/Future
draw, for instance — has its own withhold: the remaining cards of *that same draw*, nothing more.
Never import the twelve-house upsell into a spread it wasn't built for. Houses and time periods are
incompatible position systems, and a letter that mixes them can no longer say what she's actually
paying for — she can't be buying both "the rest of tonight's three cards" and "an unrelated
twelve-card reading of your life's houses" in the same sentence.

**How much of a self-contained spread to reveal is a judgment call, not a formula.** Enough to feel
substantive against the size of the whole spread; never a majority. A three-card spread revealing
one card, and a twelve-card spread revealing three, both satisfy that test — pick whatever count
reads as generous-but-incomplete for the specific spread in front of you, rather than applying a
fixed ratio across every spread size.

## 3 · WRITING STYLE

**Claims are stated flat.** *"Luck is going to favour you." "It arrives as a person."* The build
notes call out hedging ("usually / tends to / almost never") as the single biggest thing wrong with
an earlier draft — a reader who wanted maybes wouldn't have opened mail from a psychic. Only the
structural withhold gets to sound uncertain; the claim itself never does.

**"Dear" is load-bearing.** It shows up at the emotional turns, not randomly — right after a hard
truth, right before an ask. It's not a tic to trim.

**Short declaratives, one longer sentence to carry the explanation, contractions throughout.**
*"This is." "That's the whole difference, dear."* Single-line paragraphs are common and are doing
real work, not filler.

**Em-dashes are her published cadence, not an AI tell.** Roughly one per paragraph, matching her
live tarot sends. Flag a paragraph only if it's carrying two or more.

**The generalization rule this letter needs, sourced from its own two best and worst lines:**

The Lovers unit says: *"You've been carrying that a while and you've stopped mentioning it to
anyone."* That's specific — it names an internal act (carrying, going quiet about it) — and it
reaches anyone who's ever done that, regardless of who or what she's carrying it for.

The Tower re-read says: *"A woman who's seen the shape of it coming weeks out does not lose what a
woman loses finding out on a Tuesday night with her phone in her hand."* Also specific — but "a
Tuesday night," "her phone in her hand" are external props of one particular way bad news arrives
now. A woman who got her news by letter, by silence, by a phone call, by simply noticing — none of
them are in that sentence.

Same letter, same writer, both lines equally vivid. The difference is where the specificity is
aimed. **A detail that describes what she did, felt, or told herself travels with her no matter what
the actual circumstance was. A detail that names the medium, the day, or the other person's exact
role only reaches the reader whose situation happened to look exactly like that one.** Before
shipping a scene, ask which kind of specific it is — not whether it's specific enough.

**Carrying is this offer's actual anchor feeling, not "waiting."** It's already in the source
letter, attached to the Lovers unit, and it's an internal-specific in exactly the sense above. Any
new letter or spread built on this offer should locate its ache in what she's carrying, not invent
a new frame (waiting on someone, checking her phone) that happens to fit whatever new card gets
drawn but drifts away from the vocabulary this offer already proved works.
