I already turned one of your three cards over — the other two, I’m leaving exactly as they fell

The first card told me where this actually started, and it’s further back than you think. The other two are still face-down on my table. Whatever they’re holding is already acting on you — the first sign of it shows up inside five to seven days, whether you read them or not.

I’m writing tonight because I don’t think this will keep until morning.

I don’t draw for people one at a time, dear. I want to say that plainly before anything else, because it’s the whole reason this letter exists.

What I send you most days is written for everybody who reads me. Every word of it is true and I stand behind all of it — but it isn’t yours. It was never meant to be.

This is.

I’d finished for the night and was closing the deck — squaring it, wrapping it back into its cloth, the way I’ve done every night for longer than I care to count.

And three cards came away in my hand. Not fanned. Not spread across the table. Three of them, together, face-down, out of the middle of a deck I was in the middle of putting away.

I’ve learned not to argue with that, dear. These came away with your name on them.

Here’s what I’m going to do, and I want you to understand why before you read any further.

Three cards laid like this only ever fall one way. One for what already happened. One for what it’s doing to you right now. One for where it’s taking you if nothing changes. Past. Present. Future.

I’m turning the past one over for you, right now, for free. I can do that honestly, because it’s already happened. It isn’t deciding itself anymore. There’s nothing left in it to protect by keeping it face-down.

The other two are different. They’re still moving — still becoming whatever they’re going to become — and I don’t lift a card like that for a woman who hasn’t said yes. Not because I’m holding it over you. Because it wouldn’t be a fair reading. A present or a future card, read without your say-so, is just me guessing out loud and charging you nothing for the privilege.

So they stay exactly as they fell. I’ll tell you the question each one is sitting on. I won’t tell you the answer. Not yet.

The last time a card came away on its own like that, it was years ago, in the back room I used to read out of before any of this. A woman named Della. Same shape of draw — one card that wanted turning over immediately, two that didn’t.

I turned hers over the same way I’m turning yours. The past card, free, first. And she went home satisfied with it for exactly seven days.

Then it stopped feeling like an answer.

Because the past card only ever tells you where you’re standing, dear. It doesn’t tell you why you’re still standing there, and it doesn’t tell you what happens if you don’t move. Della came back for the other two. It took her a week to find out her first card was the smallest part of the draw, not the whole of it.

Your first card: the Hanged Man

A man hanging by one foot from a wooden beam, arms folded loosely behind his back, perfectly still. Look at his face before you look at anything else. There’s a calm there that doesn’t belong on someone upside-down against their will. A faint light sits around his head, like this was the exact position he needed to be in to see something the right way up.

This isn’t a card about being trapped, dear, whatever it looks like at a glance. It’s a card about a woman who put part of her life on hold. On purpose. Even if it didn’t feel like a decision at the time.

You stopped moving on something on purpose. Not because you couldn’t go on. Because some part of you decided that staying exactly still, for a while, was safer than whatever came next.

That’s not weakness. The Hanged Man is one of the few cards in the whole deck that turns waiting into a kind of strength — provided the woman doing it actually knows what she’s waiting for.

And here’s what the card won’t tell me. How far back this actually goes.

You’ve probably already got a date in your head — the week it started, the conversation, the thing that ended and left you hanging here. I’d put money on the date being wrong. Not the event. The timing. This kind of stillness doesn’t start with the thing you think it starts with. It starts earlier, with a smaller, quieter version of the same decision, one you made so long ago you stopped counting it as a decision at all.

This one card can tell you that you’re hanging, and that you chose to be. It can’t tell you when the choosing actually started.

Your second card: still face-down

still face-down

I’m not turning this one over, dear. Not yet.

What I will tell you is the question it’s sitting on, because you should at least know what you’re not looking at.

Whether the stillness that started back then is still doing what it was for — or whether somewhere in the last few months it quietly turned into something that’s just costing you, and you haven’t noticed the switch yet.

That’s not a small question to leave face-down. It’s the one this card is holding. I’m not going to answer it for you with a guess dressed up as a reading.

Whatever the true answer is, it’s already acting on you. The first sign of it you’d recognise yourself, if you knew to look, shows up inside five to seven days.

Your third card: also face-down

still face-down

This one stays down too. Same reason. It’s still deciding itself, and I won’t read a card like that without you asking me to.

What it costs you, going forward, if you stay exactly where the first card found you — and the one specific thing that’s already positioned to cut you down from it, whether you use it or it happens to you instead.

That last part matters more than the warning does, dear. There’s already something moving toward the end of this. The only open question is who’s holding it when it arrives.

In concrete terms, what do you do now?

Nothing complicated.

You say yes to the other two. That’s the only thing standing between you and either answer. I don’t turn a woman’s present and future over without her say-so — the cards won’t sit for it, and neither will I.

What happens when you say yes

I turn the other two over, in front of you, the way I turned the first. Full picture, full reading, nothing held back this time.

And I read all three together, because a past, a present, and a future card were never meant to be read one at a time. The meaning sits in how they lean against each other. Right now you’re only looking at a third of it.

Let me finish the draw.

I’m not going to promise you the other two make this easy. That isn’t what a reading does, and you’d be right not to believe me if I said it.

Here’s what it does do.

You stop guessing at your own life from the outside.

Right now you know a third of your own story, and you’re filling in the other two-thirds with worry — because that’s what an unfinished draw does to a woman. It hands her imagination the two hardest jobs and none of the facts.

The present and the future don’t get easier once you’ve seen them. They just stop being blank.

→ The other two are waiting whenever you’re ready.

I’m on your side in this, dear.
