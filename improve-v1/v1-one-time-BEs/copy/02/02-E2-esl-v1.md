**Images** — briefs in `02-E5`.

`IMG-1` hero: **three cards lying FACE-DOWN**, side by side, on dark cloth. Shot from above, a
real table and not a set. ⚠ **Never face-up.** Two reasons, and both are load-bearing:

1. **It would spoil the reveal.** The letter's engine is three units delivered one at a time —
   blessing, fork, threat. A hero showing all three faces hands her the whole draw before the
   first sentence and there is nothing left to turn over.
2. **It would contradict the copy.** The origin story says they came away *"together, face-down,
   out of the middle of a deck"*. Face-down IS the moment being described — the cards as they
   left her hand, before she turned them.

The backs also do the harder job: an unturned card is a question. `IMG-2/3/4` (the World, the
Lovers, the Tower) are the payoffs, and each one appears only at its own unit.

<!-- BEAT 6 · origin story — the act must be uninvited -->
I don't draw for people one at a time. I want to say that plainly before anything else, because
it's the whole reason this letter exists.

What I send you most days is written for everybody who reads me. Every word of it is true and I
stand behind all of it — but it isn't *yours*. It was never meant to be.

This is.

I'd finished for the night and I was closing the deck. Squaring it, wrapping it back into its
cloth, the way I've done every night for longer than I care to count.

And three cards came away in my hand.

Not fanned. Not spilled across the table. Three of them, together, face-down, out of the middle
of a deck I was in the middle of putting away.

I've learned not to argue with that, dear. When cards come out of a deck on their own they have
already decided who they belong to. These came away with your name on them.

So I turned them over. And I sat with them a while, because two of them I was glad to see and
the third one I wasn't.

<!-- BEAT 7 · stakes preview — two goods, one threat, and the window -->
Here's how I want you to read what's below. Take the first two and let yourself have them —
there's real luck in there and you're allowed to keep it.

Then read the third one twice.

And read all three of them as *near*. This is not a draw about some distant year of your life.
The first of it shows itself inside five to seven days. That's what the arrangement of them tells
me, and it's why I didn't wait until morning to write.

<!-- BEAT 8 · precedent — ONE name, distant, buyer-change not third-party outcome -->
The last time cards came out of the deck at me like that, it was years ago, in the back room I
used to read out of before any of this. A woman named Ruth. Same shape of draw — two open doors
and one closed one.

She let me lay the full spread.

And the thing the third card had been pointing at did arrive, more or less when the cards said

But it didn't knock her over. She'd seen the shape of it weeks before it showed up, so when it
came she met it standing.

That's the whole difference, dear. Not that nothing happens to you.

That nothing happens to you *from behind*.

<!-- BEAT 9 · withhold + condition — the BIG IDEA, stated -->

Your future is already drawn, dear. That's the part people struggle with. It isn't waiting

Right now you're being shown a quarter of it.

Because three cards is a quarter of an answer. A full spread is twelve — one for each house, one
for each room of a life. Three tells you the weather. Twelve tells you the day, the door, and
who's standing at it.

I'll explain how we do the twelve at the end. First, your three.

<!-- BEAT 10 · UNIT 1 — BLESSING -->
### Your first card: the World

`[IMG-2]`

A woman dancing at the centre of a great green wreath, a wand held easy in each hand, four winged
watchers looking on from the corners.

It's a trump card, dear, and there is no better one in the deck. Its power for happy change is
greater than anything else in the twenty-two.

When it comes up first in a draw it says one thing plainly. **Luck is going to favour you.**

This is the end of a run of misfortune and the start of a cycle of luck. I don't say that lightly
and I don't say it often. The World throws real protection around a woman, and it opens a good
period — in love first, and close behind love, money.

Because there is a windfall sitting in this card. The reading of your near future came back the
same way: something substantial coming toward you. And this is the part that made me sit up, dear
— it isn't the only one. There's a second behind it, and the second is the one that changes your
love life.

Now here's what the World won't tell me.

It shows me the windfalls arrive. It does not show me **which door they come through**.

And that matters more than it sounds. A windfall you aren't standing in front of, you don't
catch. It goes past you to somebody who was.

Twelve cards give me the door. Three can't.

<!-- BEAT 10 · UNIT 2 — BLESSING + THE FORK -->
### Your second card: the Lovers

`[IMG-3]`

A man and a woman in a green garden, a great winged angel spread above them in blessing, and
behind each of them a tree — hers with a serpent coiled in the branches, his alight with small
flames.

This card is love itself, and coming second in your draw it carries a double meaning. You must
not take it lightly.

The first meaning is renewal. Something you already have is about to come back to life.

The second is an encounter — a new one, close, and strong enough to change the whole shape of
your love life.

And here's what I feel from the way it sits against the World, %FIRSTNAME%. There's a tension in
you at the moment. A sense that not enough attention is paid to what you do — and, far worse,
none at all to who you actually are underneath it. You've been carrying that a while and you've
stopped mentioning it to anyone.

The Lovers says that ends. **The person on your mind is going to become more attentive toward you
than they have ever been.**

And the encounter the second reading points to is not a stranger. It's someone you already know.

Now the difficulty, and it's the whole reason I'm writing.

The Lovers gives you two. It does not tell me which of the two you're being handed.

Because from the inside they look identical, dear. The serpent tree is appetite — *reach out and
take it, never mind the cost*. The tree of flames is the thing that's lastingly alive for you.
Both of them feel exactly like love in the first weeks. One of them costs you a year.

The twelve tell me which. The *house* the Lovers falls into is what separates them — whether this
is the renewal of something already in your hands, or the arrival of something that isn't yet.

Same card. Opposite instruction. And a quarter of a spread cannot tell you which one you've been
given.

<!-- BEAT 10 · UNIT 3 — THREAT. Names a person as a SHAPE, never an outcome about a real one -->
### Your third card: the Tower

`[IMG-4]`

I don't like this card and I'm not going to pretend otherwise.

Lightning striking a tower. Two figures falling out of a window. It is the card for the year that
takes the roof clean off — the thing you did not see coming, arriving anyway.

Understand me: this is not a negative card. It is a warning, and a warning is a kindness. But it
is a pressing one, and it wants something done now, whether or not you feel ready and whether or
not you feel brave enough. Ignore it and it will undo the two good cards sitting in front of it.

And here is what most readers won't tell you about the Tower.

It does not arrive as an event.

It arrives as a person. And not a stranger — someone already in the frame.

Either it's somebody from before, coming back into view at exactly the wrong moment to ruin what
you were finally starting to build.

Or it's the reverse: someone still very much here, who has already begun leaving and hasn't said
so. That one is worse, dear, because there is nothing to point at. Nothing you could show a
friend. The card sees it before the person does.

Which of the two is standing in your Tower, I can't tell you from a single card. There are too
many things here that are specific to you, and they can arrive abruptly.

What I can tell you is that it is load-bearing. It's the thing your other two cards are arranged
around — the luck, the windfalls, the choice between the trees. All of it is sitting on top of
this.

Its name, its face, and how close it is: that is what the other nine cards are for.

<!-- BEAT 12 · re-read, deepen — the Lovers -->
### Back to the Lovers, and the encounter that's coming

The Lovers points to a meeting inside the next few weeks, %FIRSTNAME%, and I'll say plainly what
it looks like from here.

A twin flame. Not a pleasant man. Not a nice few months. The other half of your own current.

That is what this deck of mine is for, and it's why I call it what I call it.

But a twin flame *arriving* and a twin flame *recognised* are two different events, and women
miss theirs constantly. They miss it because it turns up on an ordinary day in ordinary clothes,
while they were braced for lightning.

The full spread gives me the day. That's the thing I cannot do with three cards and can do with
twelve — which door he comes through, which week, and what he looks like when he uses it.

And look at where their eyes go in that card, because it decides everything. The woman doesn't
gaze at the man. She looks up, past him. And *he* looks at her. That's the order, and the order is
the teaching: she tends to her own knowing first, and he meets her there.

Get it backwards and you'll hand your twin flame to the serpent tree and call it love.

[Let me lay them]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=3) and I'll tell you which day to be looking.

<!-- BEAT 12 · re-read, deepen — the Tower. This is where the threat gets specific -->
### And back to the Tower — this is the part I want you to sit with

Here's what the Tower is actually warning you about, dear. It isn't the fall.

It's the surprise.

The falling happens either way. Towers fall; that's what the card is. The only thing it's
offering you is the chance to be facing the right direction when it does.

And that is not a small thing. A woman who's seen the shape of it coming weeks out does not lose
what a woman loses finding out on a Tuesday night with her phone in her hand. Same event. Two
entirely different women at the end of it.

One of them had time to decide who she wanted to be about it.

[Draw the twelve with me]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=4), and you'll know which direction to be facing.

<!-- BEAT 14 · authority flourish — justifies price, the count, and "12 NEW cards" -->
### Why I'm using the Major Arcana for yours

Every deck holds seventy-eight cards.

Twenty-two of them are the Major Arcana — the World, the Lovers, the Tower, the ones you already
know by name. The other fifty-six are the minor: cups and swords and coins, the small daily
weather of a life.

Your three came out of the twenty-two. All three of them.

That doesn't happen by accident and it changes what kind of spread this has to be. So I'm going
to pull your twelve from the Major Arcana only. No minors, no filler. Twelve trumps, laid one to
a house.

It's the heaviest reading I do. It takes the whole of a night and it takes something out of me,
and I keep it for the women whose first draw asks for it.

Yours asked for it.

And they'll be twelve **new** cards, dear. Not these three again. These three have already said
what they came to say.

<!-- BEAT 15/16 · final CTA + transformation promise = the brief's PROMISE, in her words -->
### [Follow me here]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=5) and let me lay them

I'm not going to promise you the twelve will make your life easy. That isn't what a reading does,
and you'd be right not to believe me if I said it.

Here's what it does do.

You stop finding out last.

The next stretch of your life stops arriving as a run of things you have to recover from, and
starts arriving as a run of things you were already standing ready for. The Tower still falls.
You're just not underneath it.

That's the whole of it. It's worth more than it sounds.

[Your twelve are waiting whenever you're ready]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=6).

## Build notes

- **Six links, one destination.** Every CTA is a sentence carrying an instruction, never a
  button, and the verb is always *permission · authorise · let me · draw · follow*. Never "buy".
- **No price, no SLA in this letter.** Both live on the booking page. Statement 6 is where money
  is first mentioned, after five yeses.
- **Read the spread, not the card.** Every meaning here comes from *this* draw — the World sitting
  in front of the Lovers and the Tower — not from what any earlier email said about the same card.
  Same card, different company, different reading. The World is luck and a windfall in this letter
  because of what it's standing next to.
- ⚠ **The origin story is load-bearing and it depends on a fact about the list.** Evelyn writes
  daily (the reframe deck, 6:30pm SGT) but she does **not** draw for individuals — that's what
  makes an unbidden three-card draw an event rather than a Tuesday. The one-card daily tarot
  series was retired 2026-07-26; do not write copy that assumes it, and re-check this beat if the
  sending programme changes again.
- **The window is concrete**: five to seven days, in the deck line and again in the stakes
  preview, as the source ran it.
- **The claims are stated, not hedged.** *Luck is going to favour you* · *the person on your mind
  is going to become more attentive than they have ever been* · *it arrives as a person*. Softening
  these was the single biggest thing wrong with the first draft — a reader who wanted maybes
  wouldn't have opened a letter from a psychic.
- ⚠ **Don't reach for a decay device** here ("the draw goes stale"). Nothing to do with
  compliance — it's 04's ladder engine (the cup dries), and the deck sells the same woman both.
- **The withhold is not a hedge.** *"I can't tell you which of the two from a single card"* is the
  engine of the offer and must stay. What's gone is the epistemic softening *around* the claims.
- **Em-dashes are deliberate.** Evelyn's live tarot emails average roughly one per paragraph;
  that's her published cadence, not an AI tell. Flag only if a paragraph carries two or more.
