I drew three cards for you last night — and nobody asked me to

Two of them signal a large change in your love life — and the first sign of it lands inside five to seven days. The third card is the reason this is in your inbox at an hour I don’t send.

I’m writing tonight because I don’t think this will keep until morning.

I don’t draw for people one at a time, dear. I want to say that plainly before anything else, because it’s the whole reason this letter exists.

What I send you most days is written for everybody who reads me. Every word of it is true and I stand behind all of it — but it isn’t yours. It was never meant to be.

This is.

I’d finished for the night and I was closing the deck. Squaring it, wrapping it back into its cloth, the way I’ve done every night for longer than I care to count.

And three cards came away in my hand.

Not fanned. Not spilled across the table. Three of them, together, face-down, out of the middle of a deck I was in the middle of putting away.

I’ve learned not to argue with that, dear. When cards come out of a deck on their own they have already decided who they belong to. These came away with your name on them.

So I turned them over. And I sat with them a while, because two of them I was glad to see and the third one I wasn’t.

Here’s how I want you to read what’s below. Take the first two and let yourself have them — there’s real luck in there and you’re allowed to keep it.

Then read the third one twice.

And read all three of them as near. This is not a draw about some distant year of your life. The first of it shows itself inside five to seven days. That’s what the arrangement of them tells me, and it’s why I didn’t wait until morning to write.

The last time cards came out of the deck at me like that, it was years ago, in the back room I used to read out of before any of this. A woman named Ruth. Same shape of draw — two open doors and one closed one.

She let me lay the full spread.

And the thing the third card had been pointing at did arrive, more or less when the cards said it would. I want to be honest with you about that. It came anyway.

But it didn’t knock her over. She’d seen the shape of it weeks before it showed up, so when it came she met it standing.

That’s the whole difference, dear. Not that nothing happens to you.

That nothing happens to you from behind.

I’ll show you all three in a moment.

But before I do, you should know what you’re holding.

Your future is already drawn, dear. That’s the part people struggle with. It isn’t waiting somewhere to be decided — it’s laid, it’s in the deck, and the only question left is how much of it you get to see before it reaches you.

Right now you’re being shown a quarter of it.

Because three cards is a quarter of an answer. A full spread is twelve — one for each house, one for each room of a life. Three tells you the weather. Twelve tells you the day, the door, and who’s standing at it.

I’ll explain how we do the twelve at the end. First, your three.

Your first card: the World

A woman dancing at the centre of a great green wreath, a wand held easy in each hand, four winged watchers looking on from the corners.

It’s a trump card, dear, and there is no better one in the deck. Its power for happy change is greater than anything else in the twenty-two.

When it comes up first in a draw it says one thing plainly. Luck is going to favour you.

This is the end of a run of misfortune and the start of a cycle of luck. I don’t say that lightly and I don’t say it often. The World throws real protection around a woman, and it opens a good period — in love first, and close behind love, money.

Because there is a windfall sitting in this card. The reading of your near future came back the same way: something substantial coming toward you. And this is the part that made me sit up — it isn’t the only one. There’s a second behind it, and the second is the one that changes your love life.

Now here’s what the World won’t tell me.

It shows me the windfalls arrive. It does not show me which door they come through.

And that matters more than it sounds. A windfall you aren’t standing in front of, you don’t catch. It goes past you to somebody who was.

Twelve cards give me the door. Three can’t.

Your second card: the Lovers

A man and a woman in a green garden, a great winged angel spread above them in blessing, and behind each of them a tree — hers with a serpent coiled in the branches, his alight with small flames.

This card is love itself, and coming second in your draw it carries a double meaning. You must not take it lightly.

The first meaning is renewal. Something you already have is about to come back to life.

The second is an encounter — a new one, close, and strong enough to change the whole shape of your love life.

And here’s what I feel from the way it sits against the World, dear. There’s a tension in you at the moment. A sense that not enough attention is paid to what you do — and, far worse, none at all to who you actually are underneath it. You’ve been carrying that a while and you’ve stopped mentioning it to anyone.

The Lovers says that ends. The person on your mind is going to become more attentive toward you than they have ever been.

And the encounter the second reading points to is not a stranger. It’s someone you already know.

Now the difficulty, and it’s the whole reason I’m writing.

The Lovers gives you two. It does not tell me which of the two you’re being handed.

Because from the inside they look identical, dear. The serpent tree is appetite — reach out and take it, never mind the cost. The tree of flames is the thing that’s lastingly alive for you. Both of them feel exactly like love in the first weeks. One of them costs you a year.

The twelve tell me which. The house the Lovers falls into is what separates them — whether this is the renewal of something already in your hands, or the arrival of something that isn’t yet.

Same card. Opposite instruction. And a quarter of a spread cannot tell you which one you’ve been given.

Your third card: the Tower

I don’t like this card and I’m not going to pretend otherwise.

Lightning striking a tower. Two figures falling out of a window. It is the card for the year that takes the roof clean off — the thing you did not see coming, arriving anyway.

Understand me: this is not a negative card. It is a warning, and a warning is a kindness. But it is a pressing one, and it wants something done now, whether or not you feel ready and whether or not you feel brave enough. Ignore it and it will undo the two good cards sitting in front of it.

And here is what most readers won’t tell you about the Tower.

It does not arrive as an event.

It arrives as a person. And not a stranger — someone already in the frame.

Either it’s somebody from before, coming back into view at exactly the wrong moment to ruin what you were finally starting to build.

Or it’s the reverse: someone still very much here, who has already begun leaving and hasn’t said so. That one is worse, dear, because there is nothing to point at. Nothing you could show a friend. The card sees it before the person does.

Which of the two is standing in your Tower, I can’t tell you from a single card. There are too many things here that are specific to you, and they can arrive abruptly.

What I can tell you is that it is load-bearing. It’s the thing your other two cards are arranged around — the luck, the windfalls, the choice between the trees. All of it is sitting on top of this.

Its name, its face, and how close it is: that is what the other nine cards are for.

In concrete terms, what do you do now?

Nothing complicated.

You authorise the full spread here. That’s the only thing between you and the rest of the answer. I don’t lay a woman’s twelve without her say-so — the cards won’t sit for it, and frankly neither will I.

Back to the Lovers, and the encounter that’s coming

The Lovers points to a meeting inside the next few weeks, dear, and I’ll say plainly what it looks like from here.

A twin flame. Not a pleasant man. Not a nice few months. The other half of your own current.

That is what this deck of mine is for, and it’s why I call it what I call it.

But a twin flame arriving and a twin flame recognised are two different events, and women miss theirs constantly. They miss it because it turns up on an ordinary day in ordinary clothes, while they were braced for lightning.

The full spread gives me the day. That’s the thing I cannot do with three cards and can do with twelve — which door he comes through, which week, and what he looks like when he uses it.

And look at where their eyes go in that card, because it decides everything. The woman doesn’t gaze at the man. She looks up, past him. And he looks at her. That’s the order, and the order is the teaching: she tends to her own knowing first, and he meets her there.

Get it backwards and you’ll hand your twin flame to the serpent tree and call it love.

Let me lay them and I’ll tell you which day to be looking.

And back to the Tower — this is the part I want you to sit with

Here’s what the Tower is actually warning you about, dear. It isn’t the fall.

It’s the surprise.

The falling happens either way. Towers fall; that’s what the card is. The only thing it’s offering you is the chance to be facing the right direction when it does.

And that is not a small thing. A woman who’s seen the shape of it coming weeks out does not lose what a woman loses finding out on a Tuesday night with her phone in her hand. Same event. Two entirely different women at the end of it.

One of them had time to decide who she wanted to be about it.

Draw the twelve with me, and you’ll know which direction to be facing.

Why I’m using the Major Arcana for yours

Every deck holds seventy-eight cards.

Twenty-two of them are the Major Arcana — the World, the Lovers, the Tower, the ones you already know by name. The other fifty-six are the minor: cups and swords and coins, the small daily weather of a life.

Your three came out of the twenty-two. All three of them.

That doesn’t happen by accident and it changes what kind of spread this has to be. So I’m going to pull your twelve from the Major Arcana only. No minors, no filler. Twelve trumps, laid one to a house.

It’s the heaviest reading I do. It takes the whole of a night and it takes something out of me, and I keep it for the women whose first draw asks for it.

Yours asked for it.

And they’ll be twelve new cards, dear. Not these three again. These three have already said what they came to say.

I’m not going to promise you the twelve will make your life easy. That isn’t what a reading does, and you’d be right not to believe me if I said it.

Here’s what it does do.

You stop finding out last.

The next stretch of your life stops arriving as a run of things you have to recover from, and starts arriving as a run of things you were already standing ready for. The Tower still falls. You’re just not underneath it.

That’s the whole of it. It’s worth more than it sounds.

Your twelve are waiting whenever you’re ready.

I’m on your side in this, dear.
