I have more for you from the deck.

I told you last time that I don’t draw for people one at a time.

It has now happened twice. With you. Inside the same week — three new cards, and they carry the thing the first three only gestured at.

Take the first two and keep them; there’s real luck in both. Then read the third one twice.

And read them as near, dear. This is a draw about the weeks directly in front of you, not some distant year.

I’ll show you all three in a moment. But you should know what you’re holding first.

Your future is already drawn. It isn’t waiting somewhere to be decided — it’s laid, it’s in the deck, and the only question left is how much of it you get to see before it reaches you.

Right now you’re being shown a quarter of it.

Because three cards is a quarter of an answer. A full spread is twelve — one for each house, one for each room of a life. Three tells you the weather. Twelve tells you the day, the door, and who’s standing at it.

First, your three.

THE FIRST CARD

The Star

A woman kneels at the edge of a still pool under an open night sky. She’s bare — nothing hidden, nothing held up to defend herself with. In each hand a jug, and she’s pouring: one stream back into the pool, one onto the dry land, where it runs off in five small rivers through the grass.

Above her the sky has opened. One great eight-pointed star, seven smaller ones set around it.

This is the Star, dear, and in the deck it comes directly after the Tower. That’s not decoration. It is painted to arrive after the disaster, not instead of it — after the lightning, the falling, the year that took the roof off. The deck’s quietest promise: you lift your eyes and the sky is still there. Still full. Still yours.

I know which women this card comes for. It comes for the one who stopped expecting good news. Who learned somewhere along the line that hoping is just arranging to be hurt again, and decided it was safer to keep her eyes on the ground.

The Star says the ground was never the last word.

And it says more than that. This card is renewal and clarity and a run of genuine luck moving into place around you — the whole weather of your life turning in your favour. Your dreams are inside arm’s reach again, dear, which they have not been for some time. It has already started.

Now hear what it doesn’t say.

Look at her hands. She’s pouring into two places at once — half back into the water, half onto the dry land. The Star tells me you’re being replenished. It does not tell me which of your two pourings is the one being filled back up.

And you’re doing two, {{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}. I can see that much from the way the other cards sit.

THE SECOND CARD

The Emperor

Stone throne, ram’s heads at the corners, a man sitting perfectly still with the whole weight of a kingdom arranged underneath him.

The Emperor is the card of structure. Order, authority, the shape a life is held in. In love he shows up as the one who provides the frame — steady, reliable, the man who decides where things go.

And here is the question this card is actually asking you, dear, which almost nobody gets to on the first pass.

Is the structure you’re standing inside one you built — or one you’re being kept in?

Because they feel identical from within. That’s the trap in this card. Being held and being contained produce exactly the same quiet in a woman. The same sense that things are settled. The same reluctance to test it.

One of them is a floor under you. The other is a room with the door shut, and you’ve stopped trying the handle because for a long time now there’s been no reason to.

The Emperor tells me you’re inside one of them and that a decision about it is coming toward you.

It doesn’t tell me which one you’re in.

The twelve would. The house the Emperor falls into is precisely what separates the two — whether this structure sits in the house of your home, or the house of what owns you. Same card. Opposite instruction.

THE THIRD CARD

The Moon

A full moon low in a night sky, its face half-turned, shedding a pale light down like falling dew. Below it a path winds up from a still pool, past two dark towers, and off into hills you can’t quite see. A dog and a wolf lift their heads and howl at the light. Out of the pool, awkward and half-submerged, a crayfish is crawling onto the shore.

Everything is silver and uncertain. Nothing in that whole picture is the colour it would be by day.

This is the Moon, dear. The most beautiful and most deceiving card in the deck. And I turned it up for you because I think you’ve been lying awake reading a man by moonlight.

Moonlight is the one light in the world you must never trust.

Here’s why, and it’s the whole card. The moon has no light of its own — it only reflects. In moonlight everything familiar goes strange and silver. A bush becomes a figure. A coiled rope becomes a snake. A short reply becomes a verdict.

Because when your eyes can’t see clearly, your mind quietly supplies the rest.

So when he goes quiet, you don’t actually experience his silence. You experience the story your fear paints onto the silence.

And then — this is the cruel part, and it’s why this card is your warning and not just your weather — you react to the paint.

You answer a thing he didn’t say. He receives an answer to a thing he didn’t say. And something that was nothing at nine o’clock is something real by morning, and you built it, in the dark, out of good intentions and no information.

That is the Moon’s warning, {{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}, and it’s already running. Not coming. Running.

What I can’t tell you from one card is which silence is the real one.

Because there is one. The Moon doesn’t turn up for a woman imagining things; it turns up for a woman with too little light and too much at stake. Somewhere in what you’ve been decoding, one piece is genuinely what you think it is — and the rest is paint.

Sorting the one from the rest is what the other nine cards do.

In concrete terms, what do you do now?

Nothing complicated.

You authorise the full spread here. That’s the only thing between you and the rest of the answer. I don’t lay a woman’s twelve without her say-so — the cards won’t sit for it, and frankly neither will I.

Back to the Emperor, and the decision in front of you

The Emperor doesn’t ask you to be certain. He asks you to be the one deciding.

That’s the difference the card is drawing, and it’s sharper than it looks. Most of the women who get this card aren’t trapped. They’ve simply stopped being the person who chooses — they’re waiting to see what he does, and calling the waiting a decision.

It isn’t one. It’s a seat you’ve given up.

Twelve houses would tell you where you gave it up, dear, and it won’t be where you think. It isn’t the relationship. It’s the house of money, or the house of work, or the house of health — one room where you handed over the deciding, and the rest followed quietly after.

Let me lay them and I’ll show you which room it started in.

And back to the Moon — this is the part I want you to sit with

Everything you currently believe about him, you assembled at night.

Not all of it is wrong. That’s not what I’m telling you. Some of what you’ve worked out in the dark is exactly right, and you should trust it, and it’s probably the piece you keep talking yourself out of.

The trouble is you can’t tell which piece that is. Not from inside the same dark you built it in.

That’s the entire service, dear. Not comfort. Light.

Draw the twelve with me and stop deciding at two in the morning with a phone in your hand.

Why I’m using the Major Arcana for yours

Every deck holds seventy-eight cards.

Twenty-two are the Major Arcana — the Star, the Emperor, the Moon, the ones you already know by name. The other fifty-six are the minor: cups and swords and coins, the small daily weather of a life.

Your three came out of the twenty-two. All three. Twice now.

So I’ll pull your twelve from the Major Arcana only. No minors, no filler. Twelve trumps, laid one to a house.

It’s the heaviest reading I do, it takes the whole of a night, and I keep it for the women whose draw asks for it.

Twelve new cards. Not these three again, and not the three before them. They’ve said what they came to say.

Three more cards, dear, and again each one stopped short of the answer.

THE STAR You’re being replenished — but not which of your two pourings.
THE EMPEROR A decision is coming — but not whether you built the room or were put in it.
THE MOON One piece of it is genuinely real — but not which piece.

The other nine cards are where those three answers live.

I’m not going to promise you the twelve will make your life easy. That isn’t what a reading does.

Here’s what it does do.

You stop finding out last. You stop assembling a man out of moonlight and then living with what you built. The next stretch of your life starts arriving as things you were standing ready for, instead of things you have to recover from.

Your twelve are waiting whenever you’re ready.

I’m on your side in this, dear.
