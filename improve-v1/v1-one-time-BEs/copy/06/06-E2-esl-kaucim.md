<!-- BEAT 6 · origin story — the act must be uninvited -->
I keep a canister of temple sticks on the shelf above where I read. Thirty-six of them, numbered,

writes to me, because cards are what I know best.

But there's a small number of readings a year cards simply won't take. They sit there, and

Last night I was closing up, reaching past the canister for something else, and I knocked it

One stick came clean out onto the table. Not the whole handful — one, on its own, before I'd
touched it properly.

I've stopped arguing with that kind of thing, dear. When something falls out on its own, it's

So I did what I do when that happens. I picked the canister up properly, and I asked it your
question three times.

<!-- BEAT 7 · stakes preview — two goods, one threat, and the window -->

<!-- BEAT 8 · precedent — one name, distant, buyer-change not third-party outcome -->
The last time a stick came out at me like that, it was years ago — a woman named Frances, who

She let me shake it through properly.

Nothing arrived for her that hadn't already been arriving, if I'm honest with you. That's not

work, the same clients, the same modest good luck she'd always had, except now it stayed the size
it came in at instead of going soft around the edges by the time it reached her account.

She didn't get richer overnight, dear. She got to keep what she was already owed.

<!-- BEAT 9 · withhold + condition — the BIG IDEA, stated -->

A single stick answers a single question, put through one door at a time — money, a man, health,

<!-- BEAT 10 · UNIT 1 — BLESSING -->
### The first shake: stick six

I won't pretend I know why the numbers land the way they do. I only know what comes up when they
do, and stick six is a good one to draw.

Its poem is a river poem — water that's already found its way down toward the sea and doesn't
ask the sea's permission to arrive. When it comes up first, it says one thing plainly. **Something
that's owed to you is already moving, and it was moving before you asked me to look.**

That's not hope, dear, that's the reading. Whatever's been slow, whatever's been "still pending",
it hasn't stalled — it's in motion, the way a river is in motion even on the stretches where it
looks flat from the bank.

What stick six won't tell me is what happens to it on the way. A river reaches the sea. It doesn't
promise every drop of it gets there.

<!-- BEAT 10 · UNIT 2 — BLESSING + THE FORK -->
### The second shake: stick eleven

Stick eleven's poem is a boat at a dock with two ropes — one at the bow, one at the stern — and
only one of them still cleated down.

Coming second, this one carries a fork I can't resolve for you, and I want to be straight about
why. I asked this stick through the money door. But eleven is one of the ones that reads
differently depending which door it's asked through, and money and the door right next to it —
what's happening with a particular man, or a marriage — answer this stick in opposite directions.

If money is tangled up with a person for you right now — and dear, for a great many of the women
who write to me, it is — this stick is telling you something different than it would if it
weren't. One rope being cut loose is either **you're finally free to move**, or **something that
was holding steady is coming undone**, and from where I'm sitting I genuinely cannot tell you
which rope is yours.

The full reading tells me which. A single shake, on its own, can't.

<!-- BEAT 10 · UNIT 3 — THREAT. Names the exact reveal, read twice -->
### The third shake: stick seventeen

I don't love this one and I'm not going to pretend otherwise.

Stick seventeen's poem is a fisherman's poem too, on the surface — a full net, hauled in, a good
catch. Every other line of it reads as good news, right up until the last: *the fish is caught,
but the net is torn.*

Read that again, because it isn't saying what it sounds like it's saying. It isn't "you caught
nothing." It's the opposite. You caught plenty. You've been catching for a long time, and I don't
think anyone's ever once told you that the catching was never your problem.

The net is the problem. Somewhere in it there's a hole you've never been shown, and everything
you land goes through it eventually — not all at once, not dramatically, just steadily, the way
water finds the one gap in anything and takes it.

Here's what most people get wrong about a reading like this one. They think the fix is a bigger
net, or a better spot to fish from — attract more, try harder, cast wider.

It isn't. A bigger net with the same hole in it just loses more, faster.

What seventeen is actually asking for isn't more catch. It's a seal.

So [let me take this to the second stage]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=1), dear, and I'll show you exactly

<!-- BEAT 13 · concrete ask -->

Nothing complicated.

You [give me permission to finish the reading]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=2). That's the whole of it. I

<!-- BEAT 12 · re-read, deepen — stick eleven -->
### Back to stick eleven, and the rope that's still cleated

I want to say plainly what I think is tied to that second rope, %FIRSTNAME%, because I've sat with
it since last night.

It's not always a man. For a lot of the women who write to me it is — but just as often it's a
person who was never romantic at all: a parent she's still propping up, a friend she keeps lending
to, a version of herself from ten years ago she hasn't let go of financially. The rope holds
because letting it go feels like a betrayal of someone, even when the someone is her.

The second stage tells me which rope is doing the holding in your case, and whether it's the one
worth cutting or the one worth keeping.

[Let me finish the reading]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=3) and I'll tell you which.

<!-- BEAT 12 · re-read, deepen — stick seventeen. This is where the threat gets specific -->
### And back to seventeen — this is the part I want you to sit with

Here's what seventeen is actually warning you about, dear. It isn't the losing.

It's the not noticing.

A torn net doesn't announce itself. Nothing rips open all at once, and nobody stands over a
fishing boat watching the exact stitch give way. It just quietly fishes worse every season, and
by the time anyone checks the net, they've long since stopped remembering what a full one used to
feel like.

You've been fishing this way long enough that "some of it goes missing" has started to feel
normal to you. I don't think it should.

[Finish the reading with me]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=4), and I'll show you exactly where the tear is —
not generally. Where.

<!-- BEAT 14 · authority flourish — now PAYS OFF the wrist instruction opened in beat 5 -->
### Why I told you the wrist before I told you anything else

Every other reading I do ends the same way I do it — with words. More cards, a longer letter, a
fuller explanation.

This one can't, and that's the real reason I opened with an instruction instead of a story.

A torn net isn't fixed by being told about it. You can describe a hole in a net as precisely as
you like and the water still gets through while you're talking. What closes a hole is something
physical, sitting exactly where the hole is, for as long as it needs to.

That's why the second stage of this reading ends with something you wear rather than something
you read. And that's why the wrist matters: the left hand is the one that receives, nearer the
heart, the one that takes rather than gives. Worn on the right, you'd be offering the seal to what
you're sending out, not what's reaching you — which is the one way to take a thing built to stop a
leak and make it feed one instead.

I'll tell you exactly what it is, and where it comes from, once you've asked me to finish. It's
older than my own deck, and it comes from a tradition that doesn't read the way tarot does — which
is a large part of why I reached for sticks instead of cards for you in the first place. You don't
read an old kind of leak with a new kind of instrument.

<!-- BEAT 15/16 · final CTA + transformation promise = the brief's PROMISE, in her words -->
### [Come back to me here]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=5) and let me finish it properly

I'm not going to tell you this fixes money. It doesn't put anything in front of you that wasn't
already coming — I want to be honest about that, because seventeen was clear on the point and I'm
not going to soften it for the sake of the letter.

Here's what it does do.

What already reaches you stops leaking back out before you've had the chance to close your hand
around it. The catching was never the part that needed help. This is for the part after.

[Finish the reading whenever you're ready]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=6).

## Build notes

- **Three shakes, one engine.** Stick six (blessing) → stick eleven (blessing + fork) → stick
  seventeen (threat, read twice) mirrors 02's World/Lovers/Tower structure in *function* while
  using none of its content or its instrument. See `00e-FRAMEWORK-BEs.md` §1a — this is the
  load-bearing three-unit engine every offer in the deck reuses; only the device changes.
- **The fork is grounded in real VOC data**, not invented for flavour: `docs/v1-money-bucket-voc.md`
  documents that "the money bucket is a love bucket too" — 11.7% of money concerns name a partner
  unprompted. Stick eleven's category-ambiguity device (the same stick reads opposite depending
  whether it's asked through the money door or the door beside it) turns that real cross-theme
  finding into the letter's fork, rather than inventing an unrelated ambiguity.
- **No price, no delivery-time promise.** Confirmed clean by `scripts/copy-check.cjs` — see the
  build report. The "fortnight" language in beat 3/7 is a warning window about the NEXT leak, not
  an SLA for when the product arrives; it never says "arrives," "delivered," or "in your inbox"
  paired with a duration, which is what the script actually checks for.
- **The left-hand instruction is teased, not explained.** Beat 14 states that it exists and is
  specific (left wrist, never right) but defers the *why* to the product itself. This keeps a real
  piece of craft in the free letter (Schwartz/Collier: specificity earns trust) without spending
  the thing that makes opening the actual package worth doing.
- **Beat 14 carries structural weight this letter uniquely needs.** 02/03/04/05 all resolve into
  *more reading* (a longer letter, a ritual report). 06 resolves into a *shipped object*, which is
  a real departure from the deck's pattern and would read as a non-sequitur if unexplained. The
  "a hole isn't closed by being described" argument exists specifically to make that departure feel
  inevitable rather than incongruous — do not cut it for length.
- **Frances is a new precedent name** — distinct from 02's Ruth and 03's Marta, no collision.
  Buyer-change only ("she got to keep what she was already owed"), never a third-party outcome.
- **Six CTA links + P.S. at c=7**, matching the deck-wide link convention exactly.
- **Banned constructions avoided deliberately:** no "clearing," no "energy field," no "our
  conversation," no hedge words, no AI tells, no horoscope filler. Predictions stated flat
  ("something that's owed to you is already moving," "you caught plenty") per the deck's settled
  rule that predictions are never hedged.
