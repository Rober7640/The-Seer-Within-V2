**What do you wish would happen after hello?**

### Give the wish another sentence

Stay with that question for a moment.

Perhaps you want him to say he's missed you. To ask when he can see you. To make a plan and follow through with it.

Perhaps you want to speak without measuring every word, wondering whether asking for a little affection will make him disappear again.

Perhaps the thing you want most is the relief of knowing you matter to him.

That's the part I want you to write underneath his name.

**“I wish to feel…”**

Finish it in your own words.

Chosen.

Cherished.

At ease with someone who wants to be there.

You can spend so long wishing for a person that you forget to describe the love you want to share with them.

Give that love a sentence of its own, dear.

### Imagine the ordinary evening after he comes back

The first message is easy to picture.

You've probably pictured it already. What it might say. How quickly you would answer. Whether you would tell anyone he'd been in touch.

Go a little further this time, %FIRSTNAME%.

Imagine an ordinary evening together, after the excitement of hearing from him has settled.

Can you tell him what's on your mind?

Does he ask about your day and listen to the answer?

When you reach for his hand, do you feel welcome?

These small things can hold so much of what you've been missing.

And they deserve a place in your wish just as much as his name does.

You might find yourself writing:

*“I wish for another chance with him, with affection we both show.”*

Or:

*“I wish to feel loved without having to wonder where I stand.”*

Read the sentence back to yourself.

Does it include you?

You may still be deciding whether you want another chance with him.
