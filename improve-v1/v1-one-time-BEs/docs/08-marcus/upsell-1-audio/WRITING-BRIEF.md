## Joel’s direction

Lead with a clear warning about skipping straight to the bottom of the written reading to get the answers. Explain why that is a bad way to use the reading, then establish why an audio version could help.

The previous drafts failed because they offered a pleasant listening option without making the problem or consequence clear. They began with emotional reflection and never gave the buyer a strong reason to change how she approaches the report.

## Accepted argument

1. **The impulse:** she has a question she wants answered, so she may scroll straight to the conclusion.
2. **The mistake:** treating the final advice as if it stands alone, without reading how the cards and her personal card inform it.
3. **The consequence:** she may miss what the advice refers to, why it applies within this interpretation, and how to use it. The warning must name that consequence; “you’ll miss the deeper meaning” is too vague.
4. **The example for healing:** a suggestion to accept support is more useful when she has considered what she has been withholding, what she blames herself for, and what kind of help she could accept. Going straight to “accept support” can leave those practical questions unanswered for her, even though the report addresses them.
5. **The role of audio:** offer a guided way to follow the report in sequence, hearing the explanation before its conclusion. Pause and replay remain available. Audio does not force attention or guarantee understanding.
6. **The integrity of the written product:** the whole explanation is already in the written report. Reading it carefully is a valid way to get the full value. Audio sells an easier-to-follow listening experience for someone who prefers that format; it does not repair an incomplete purchase.

## Tone

Firm, plain and specific. Warn against the reading habit, not against the customer’s intelligence. Lead with the mistake and why it matters; the empathetic explanation follows. Do not soften the whole offer into “another way to spend time with your reading.” Do not invent harm, missed destiny, urgency or a requirement to buy audio.

“Everybody who purchases scrolls to the bottom” is Joel’s audience premise, not verified customer data in this project. We can warn directly about skipping to the end without stating an unsupported universal claim or fabricating Marcus’s past customer experience. If observed customer evidence is supplied, reflect its actual scope.
