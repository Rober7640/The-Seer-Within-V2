## What changed

- Made the cost of skipping tangible: agreeing with advice while still not knowing what to do differently.
- Connected the audio format directly to that problem: choose to set the page aside and hear the explanation in order, then pause or replay.
- Removed editorial asides such as “this is an illustration, not a preview.” Hypothetical examples remain clear through “suppose,” “imagine” and “consider.”
- Replaced long process descriptions and repeated position inventories with ordinary situations and clearer connections.
- Gave the closing audio use a specific setting: returning to the passage about support while considering what to say to someone.
- Preserved the three styles: A uses direct instructions and short questions; B uses a personal request and connected letter prose; C develops a hypothetical phone/text scene.
- Kept the main written purchase complete, prices unresolved, and accept/decline accurate. No invented statistics, customer stories, urgency, narration identity or promised healing results.
