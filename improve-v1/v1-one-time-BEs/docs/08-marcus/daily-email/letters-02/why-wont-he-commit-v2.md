Subject: He can care—and still leave you waiting

%FIRSTNAME%,

If affection were enough to settle the question of commitment, you might not still be wondering where you stand.

This morning's six Rider–Waite cards begin with that difference. I'm using “he” here; read it as the person whose commitment matters to you.

**One — what affection can offer. The Knight of Cups.**

A knight rides a white horse, holding a cup out before him. The horse moves quietly. There is no charge, no weapon raised. The knight approaches with an offering.

Look at the cup he extends. He isn't hiding it against his chest. The gesture is open, and I read it as an invitation to come closer.

Affection can offer real reassurance. Being wanted, listened to, or welcomed can help you feel that a relationship is worth pursuing. You don't have to decide those moments meant nothing because commitment remains uncertain.

Yet the knight is showing us an approach. The picture doesn't show an agreement about what happens next.

That distinction matters if you feel close to someone but still cannot say what you have agreed to build together. Warmth can tell you that you enjoy being together. Commitment also asks what each of you is prepared to do to sustain the relationship.

**Two — what remains undecided. The Two of Wands.**

A man stands on a battlement above the land and sea. In one hand he holds a staff; in the other, a small globe.

The globe puts a whole world before him. But his feet remain on the battlement. Considering where to go has not yet become a departure.

Here, that image helps separate a future you can imagine from a direction you have agreed to take. A possibility can feel very convincing before either person has made a decision.

Beside the knight's outstretched cup, the man with the globe asks you to look beyond the welcome. What follows it? Can you name an agreement you have made together? Is there follow-through you can rely on?

These cards cannot tell me his private reasons for hesitating. They can give you a more useful question than whether the affection was real: what does the relationship offer you beyond affection?

Those are the two face-up cards in this morning’s reading.

You may be patient because there is something here you value. You can acknowledge that without treating every uncertainty as yours to endure.

Perhaps you need a clearer agreement. Perhaps you already asked, and now need to consider his response. The next step depends on what you need from this relationship, as well as what he is willing to offer.
