**One — what it does to you. The Five of Pentacles.**

Falling snow, at night. Two people out in it. A man on wooden crutches, a bell at his neck. Beside him a barefoot woman in a ragged shawl, head down. Behind them a church wall. A tall window with five gold pentacles set in the glass.

The window is lit. It sits directly above them. Neither of them looks up at it.

That's the cold. Not the weather — the walk. Head down, same route, past the same lit glass. You've decided a warm room with people already in it is something other people get handed. You'd rather stay out here than look up and be wrong about that.

You did that walk this week.

**Two — what you already know. The Seven of Swords.**

A man in a red cap creeping away from a camp on tiptoe. Five swords bundled up in his arms. Tents behind him. Two more swords left standing upright in the ground. He's looking back over his shoulder.

Look at his hands. He has them round the blades. Five swords, and he's carrying every one of them by the sharp end.

That's the grip. You took something out of a place you used to live in and you've carried it every day since. You could name it in one sentence. And you hold it in the way that keeps opening your hands.

You picked it up like that yourself.

**Three — what you've worked out. The Eight of Pentacles.**

A young man on a wooden bench with a hammer and a graver, cutting a pentacle. Five finished ones mounted up a post beside him, one above another. One more on the bench in front of him. A small town in the distance.

Look at the ground by his foot. There's one lying there. He hasn't picked it up.

That's the post. You've done the work on this. Five things about yourself. Worked out, fixed, mounted where you can see them. And there's one on the ground by your foot that you step over on your way to the bench.

You stepped over it this morning.

Look at the numbers. Five at the top of the tree. Seven and eight side by side underneath it. Everything you've added has gone in below the five. The five hasn't moved.

That's the three I can turn.

The other seven are the places it shows up.

**The fifth is what you want owed back.** You've been keeping that count a long time — without ever calling it a count.

**The sixth is the love** — what's left of it.

**The seventh is what you want to win.** You'd know it if you got it.

**The eighth is what you show people.** You're good at that one.

**The ninth is home** — what it feels like.

**The tenth is what it costs in money.** Not whether. What it has already cost.

And you've been honest about it, %FIRSTNAME%. You haven't made anyone answer for what somebody else did. You've turned up as yourself. You've kept your side of it with people who didn't keep theirs. That's a straight way to treat people and it's the harder one.
