Subject: 🕯️ %FIRSTNAME%, the light you keep waiting for only shows the ground

%FIRSTNAME%,

Marcus. The Cross this morning, on one question: the risk I won't regret. Two up, three face down. Rider–Waite.

**One — what's on its side. The Fool.**

A young man mid-stride in a bright flowered tunic. A white rose in one hand. A small bundle tied to a stick over his shoulder. A little white dog leaping at his heels. Snowy peaks below him, a white sun behind. Numbered 0.

Look at his head. It's tilted up and back, away from the drop. His front foot is going over the edge and he is not looking at the edge.

That's the case for it. Not that it's safe — the drop is drawn under his boot. It's that he goes with his head turned away, carrying a bundle the size of a fist. The part of you that keeps looking down at the drop was never the part that moves.

He's taking almost nothing with him.

**Two — what's set against it. The Hermit.**

An old man alone on snow. A long grey hooded cloak down to his boots. Grey beard, head bowed, eyes down. A lantern held up high in one hand. A long staff in the other. Numbered IX.

Look inside the lantern. There's no flame in it. Not a flame — a six-pointed star, sat behind the glass, and he's holding it up over his head and looking at the ground anyway.

That's the case against it. One more thing to know first — the year right, the money right, the last question answered — and then you'd go. That's the lantern. It's been up a long while and it lights about a foot of ground.

Look at the numbers on them. Nought on the side that's for it. Nine on the side that's against. Not two arguments the same size — everything with any weight in it sits on the against side.

That's the two I can turn. The other three are the ones with your name on them — what you're really weighing, what settles it, and what you're left holding.

That last one I couldn't turn if you asked me to. In this spread the middle card is never drawn. It comes out of the other four. Until they're face up there is nothing in the middle to turn.

And you've been careful, %FIRSTNAME%. You've kept working. You haven't dropped this on anyone. You've waited for the year that would make it easy on everybody. That's a careful way to handle a life other people are standing in.

Your foot hasn't moved since you started being careful.

Give me the go-ahead and I'll turn the other three. Here's how it goes.

1. You click below.

2. I ask you for your first and last name. That's the only thing I need.

3. Your name gives me your lens — one card in this deck that's yours, and stays yours.

4. The three still face down get read through it. Without your lens, what you're really weighing is somebody else's decision.
