Subject: Grateful—and still missing what you lost?

%FIRSTNAME%,

If you've ever followed an honest "I'm still upset" with "but I have so much to be grateful for," the first card in this morning's reading deserves a closer look. I've laid six Rider–Waite cards on what needs your care now, with two face up.

**One — what still hurts. The Five of Cups.**

A cloaked figure stands over three fallen cups. Their contents have spilled onto the ground. Two more cups stand upright behind him.

The picture holds both things at once: something has been lost, and something remains.

Those standing cups matter. But their presence doesn't make the other three full again.

I read this as permission to acknowledge a disappointment without having to defend your right to feel it. Something can still hurt even when other parts of your life are good.

Perhaps you hesitate to say what's wrong because you can immediately think of something that's going well. You begin explaining why you shouldn't be upset before you've finished saying why you are.

The Five of Cups asks you to acknowledge what's upset you before explaining why you shouldn't feel upset. What, exactly, are you sad about? What did you hope would happen that didn't?

An honest answer doesn't make you ungrateful. It gives the hurt a name instead of turning it into a fault in your character.

**Two — what gentleness can change. Strength.**

A woman bends towards a lion, her hands gently at its jaws. Flowers circle her waist. The animal is powerful, yet her hands are careful.

That care is part of the strength in this picture.

Alongside the Five of Cups, I read it as a different way to meet a difficult feeling. You can acknowledge the feeling without immediately ordering yourself to do better.

If your first response to sadness is "I should have moved on," try noticing what that demand adds. The original disappointment is still there. Now you also have a standard you feel you've failed to meet.

Strength invites a gentler response: "This still matters to me. What would help me through today?"

That question leaves room for an answer. Perhaps you need to talk without apologising for bringing the subject up. Perhaps you need to accept company without pretending you're cheerful.

Those are the two face-up cards in this morning’s reading.

Together, they separate feeling hurt from judging yourself for it. Gratitude can stay. So can the admission that something has been difficult. You don't have to stop feeling sad before accepting a little care.

The next question is what deserves that care now—and what makes support hard to accept.
