You can be grateful for what you have and still hurt over what you lost. This morning's reading begins there—with feelings you keep telling yourself you should be past by now.

I've laid six Rider–Waite cards on "what part of myself needs the most healing right now?" Two are face up. Let's begin with the Five of Cups.

**One — what still hurts. The Five of Cups.**

A figure in a dark cloak looks down at three fallen cups. Behind him, two cups are still standing.

It's tempting to point straight at those two. Look at what you still have. Look at what hasn't gone wrong.

But the fallen cups are there too. Turning around won't put them upright.

I read this card as the hurt you keep trying to talk yourself out of. Perhaps a relationship ended, a friendship changed, or something you worked toward didn't happen. You can name the good things in your life and still miss what you wanted to keep.

When a memory catches you, do you let yourself admit that it hurts? Or does the next thought sound like, "I should be over this"?

That second thought gives you another thing to feel bad about. Now you're hurting, and judging yourself for hurting.

**Two — what that hurt needs. Strength.**

A woman in a white dress stands beside a lion. Her hands rest at its jaws. She isn't holding a weapon. She leans toward the animal, handling its mouth gently.

Notice how different that is from trying to frighten the lion into obedience.

Beside the Five of Cups, Strength asks you to reconsider what being strong requires. You may have become very good at getting through the day without letting anyone see how much something still matters.

But there's another kind of strength in saying, "I'm having a difficult day," and letting someone stay with you in that conversation.

The Five of Cups gives the loss its place. Strength changes how you respond when you feel it. Together, I read them as an invitation to stop making recovery another test you can fail.

The question now is what you've found hardest to admit you need.

Maybe you don't want to bring the subject up again. Maybe you're quick to reassure someone who asks how you are. You can protect the person listening so carefully that they never get a chance to care for you.
