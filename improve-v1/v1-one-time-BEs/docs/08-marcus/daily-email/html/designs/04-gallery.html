Marcus. Six cards on one question this morning: why he goes quiet. Three up, three face down. Rider–Waite.

I’ll say him. If yours is a her, swap it as you read — the cards don’t care and neither do I.

 
 
WHY THEY GO QUIET
THIS MORNING · SIX CARDS
RIDER–WAITE ON PRINTED CLOTH
 
PLATE I · TURNED
One — what the quiet actually is. The Four of Cups.
 
 
FOUR OF CUPS
PAMELA COLMAN SMITH · 1909
CHROMOLITHOGRAPH · 70 × 120 MM
 

A young man sitting on the ground under a tree, knees up, arms folded across his chest. Three gold cups on the grass in front of him. And out of a cloud beside him, a hand, holding out a fourth.

He isn’t looking at it. He’s looking at the three he’s already got.

That’s the quiet. He hasn’t left and he hasn’t stopped caring. He’s sat down inside himself and he’s staring at what’s already in front of him. The hand is out. He isn’t seeing it.

 
PLATE II · TURNED
Two — what it is not. The Three of Swords.
 
 
THREE OF SWORDS
PAMELA COLMAN SMITH · 1909
CHROMOLITHOGRAPH · 70 × 120 MM
 

A red heart with three swords straight through it. Grey cloud, rain coming down.

Here’s the thing about that card, and it’s why it’s in position two. Nobody is holding those swords. They’re just in it.

The quiet is not a wound. It isn’t something he did to you. It feels exactly like this card and it is not this card — and if you’ve been reading his silence as an injury with your name on it, that’s the first thing to put down this morning.

 
PLATE III · TURNED
Three — what it costs you while it lasts. The Eight of Swords.
 
 
EIGHT OF SWORDS
PAMELA COLMAN SMITH · 1909
CHROMOLITHOGRAPH · 70 × 120 MM
 

A woman standing in wet ground, wrapped and bound, a cloth over her eyes. Eight swords stuck upright in the earth around her. A castle up on the rock behind.

Look at the swords. They don’t join up. There’s a clear gap in front of her, and her feet aren’t tied.

That’s the cost. Not heartbreak — stillness. While he’s sitting under his tree, you’ve stopped moving, and nothing in the picture is actually holding you.

 

That’s the three I can turn.

The other three are the ones you want.

 
PLATE IV · FACE DOWN
The fourth is what he’s protecting.

A man who sits down inside himself is always guarding something, and it’s almost never the thing she’d guess.

 
PLATE V · FACE DOWN
The fifth is you —

what you’ve been doing inside his quiet. That’s a different question from what you’ve been feeling about it.

 
PLATE VI · FACE DOWN
The sixth is what ends it.

Not whether. What.

 

And you’ve been decent about it. You’ve given him room. You haven’t chased him, you haven’t made him explain himself, you’ve let him have whatever this is. That’s a generous way to treat someone and it’s the right instinct.

 

You’ve just been standing still to do it.

 
PLATES IV–VI · ON REQUEST

I can’t turn those three for a list. What a man protects depends on the man, and I don’t know yours.

So here’s this morning’s job, and it’s one line. Write down what he did on the day it changed. Not how you felt about it. What he did. One sentence.

That sentence is all I need. Open the other three here — there’s a box on that page, and that’s what goes in it.

 

Then, today: name one thing you’ve been holding until he comes back, and do it anyway.

 
Marcus
 
 

P.S. Her feet aren’t tied. That’s the whole card.
