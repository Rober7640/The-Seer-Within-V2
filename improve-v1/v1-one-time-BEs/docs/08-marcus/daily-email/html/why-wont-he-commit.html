MORNING EDITION SIX CARDS · ONE QUESTION
 

%FIRSTNAME%,

He talks about the things you’ll do together. But when you ask where the relationship is going, the answer gets less definite. If that sounds familiar, you know how hard it is to make sense of the difference.

This morning’s cards look at that gap. I’ve laid six Rider–Waite cards on "why won’t he commit?" Two are face up. The first is the Knight of Cups.

 
THE TABLE THIS MORNING — six cards on one question. Two of them are looking back at you. Four of them are not. Rider–Waite–Smith deck, 1909.
 
 
 
One — what he’s offering. The Knight of Cups.
 
FIG. I · POSITION ONE
Turned. The Knight of Cups.

A knight sits on a white horse, holding a cup out in front of him. His horse moves quietly. There’s no charge, no raised weapon. He approaches with something to offer.

The cup is the detail I want you to notice. He’s presenting it openly. This is a card of romantic attention: the invitation, the affectionate message, the evening when you feel close again.

Those moments can matter. You don’t have to dismiss them as an act to admit that you’re still unsure where you stand.

But the knight’s gesture tells us about his approach. It doesn’t tell us what he will sustain. An affectionate evening can answer "does he enjoy being with me?" while leaving "are we building a relationship?" unresolved.

 
 
 
Two — what remains undecided. The Two of Wands.
 
FIG. II · POSITION TWO
Turned. The Two of Wands.

A man stands high on a battlement, looking out over the land and water. He holds a small globe in one hand and a staff in the other.

He can see a wide world from where he’s standing. He can consider where he might go. But he’s still on the battlement.

That is the difference this card brings to the reading: imagining a future and deciding to pursue it are separate things. He might talk warmly about a trip together, yet leave the dates unsettled. He might picture you in his life without agreeing on what being together means now.

Beside the Knight of Cups, this card points to a relationship that can keep feeling promising while its direction stays undecided. It doesn’t settle his private reasons. It does give you something clearer to examine: what happens after the affectionate words?

Does he help make a plan? Does he answer the question you actually asked? Does an agreement become something you can rely on?

 

The question now is what you need to do with that uncertainty.

 
 

You may have been patient because the closeness feels worth giving a chance. That’s understandable. But you also get to ask whether the relationship, as it stands today, gives you enough.
