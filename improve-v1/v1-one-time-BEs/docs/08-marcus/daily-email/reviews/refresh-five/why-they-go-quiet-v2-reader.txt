Subject: What his silence still hasn't answered

Margaret,

When someone you care about goes quiet, an unanswered question can become the thing you think about most.

This morning's six Rider–Waite cards begin with that silence. What can you understand while you are still waiting to hear from him?

One — what silence leaves unanswered. The Four of Cups.

A man sits beneath a tree with his arms folded. Three cups stand on the grass in front of him. Beside him, a hand emerges from a cloud and offers a fourth cup.

He hasn't reached for it. The offered cup is there, but no answering movement meets it.

You can see that much in the picture. His reasons are another matter.

That's a useful distinction when you haven't heard from someone. You know a response is missing. You may not know why. Silence can leave room for a hopeful explanation, then a painful one, without giving you evidence for either.

If you have been turning those explanations over, you may be trying to get an answer from the only thing available: the absence of one.

The first card lets you stop at what you know. You haven't received the response you wanted. You don't have to supply his reason before acknowledging how that affects you.

Two — what waiting can restrict. The Eight of Swords.

A woman stands blindfolded, with cloth binding her body. Eight swords rise from the ground around her. She cannot see where she could move.

The bindings are real. But her feet aren't tied, and the swords have gaps between them.

I read both parts of that picture together. She is restricted, yet the picture leaves possibilities she cannot see from where she stands.

Waiting for a reply can make your own choices feel just as difficult to see. You may feel you cannot decide anything until he explains himself. Even a choice about what you need can seem to depend on what he says next.

His answer could matter a great deal. Still, there are questions you can consider before it comes. What communication do you need? What would you want to say if the silence continues? What part of your own life needs your attention now?

Those are the two face-up cards in this morning’s reading.

The Four of Cups shows an offer without a response. The Eight of Swords brings the reading to the person left waiting. Together, they shift the question from guessing his reason to considering your needs while the reason is unknown.

You can miss him and want an explanation. You can also decide that your own needs deserve attention before he supplies one. Caring about the connection doesn't require you to put every other part of your life on hold.

I’d like to continue with what that means for you, Margaret: what you need to know, what waiting is costing you, what you can ask for, and where you can begin. Those are the questions for the four cards still face down.

Explore your next step while he's quiet.
