Subject: Does love always have to feel this hard?

Margaret,

When romantic love feels difficult, trying harder can seem like the decent thing to do. Wait a little longer. Try to understand the other person’s feelings. Give the relationship another chance. But how do you know when the effort itself has become too much?

I've turned two of six cards on that question this morning. The first shows a man carrying more than he can comfortably hold.

One — what effort has become. The Ten of Wands.

Ten long wooden staves fill his arms. He bends forward under their weight, gathering the whole bundle against his chest as he walks towards a town.

It's the whole bundle I want you to notice. Each staff might be manageable on its own. Here, one man is carrying all ten together.

In love, a little more patience can seem reasonable. So can starting a conversation, adjusting your plans, or being the one who brings a difficult conversation back to kindness. Each can be a loving thing to do.

The weight changes when you become responsible for doing all of them.

If that feels familiar, look at the work as a whole. You can understand why you took on each part and still find that the total asks too much of you. Being able to keep carrying it doesn't tell you whether it should all be yours.

Two — what you can afford to give. The Six of Pentacles.

A standing man gives coins to a kneeling figure. A second figure kneels nearby, waiting. In his other hand, the man holds a pair of scales.

He gives coins with one hand and holds scales with the other. The scales remain part of the picture while he is being generous.

That detail matters beside the Ten of Wands. You can care about what you offer and also consider how much time and energy you can offer. You can be generous while considering your own limits.

In love, that means being willing to ask what comes back to you. Does care reach you when you're tired? Is there room for your needs as well as the sympathy you offer?

You don't need to keep a tally of every kindness. There will be times when you give more, and times when you need more. But if you respond to receiving too little care by offering still more of your own, your effort can grow while the closeness you want stays out of reach.

Those are the two face-up cards in this morning’s reading.

Together, these cards separate being devoted from taking responsibility for both sides of love. More effort from you cannot, by itself, make the giving mutual.

That leaves a more useful question than “How can I try harder?” What would sharing the effort actually look like for you?

You may be clear that you're tired without being clear about what would help. And asking for something specific can feel harder than quietly doing without it.

I'd like to continue there, Margaret. The remaining four cards explore what you need back, what you keep taking on, what you can ask for, and where you can begin.

I'll need your first and last name and your date of birth. Your name identifies a separate personal tarot card. I use the strengths and habits represented by that personal card to interpret the four remaining cards—especially what they suggest about asking for care in love. We'll look at how you can express a need instead of immediately making room for somebody else's.

Let's look at what you need from love.

Marcus

P.S. The man with the coins hasn't put down his scales. You can be generous and still consider what you're giving.
