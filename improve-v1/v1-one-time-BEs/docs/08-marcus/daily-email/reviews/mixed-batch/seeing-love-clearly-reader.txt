Subject: What are you not seeing in love?

Margaret,

When you think about love, how much of what you see is happening now—and how much is what you hope will happen?

The first of this morning's six Rider–Waite tarot cards gives that question a picture.

One — what hope can obscure. The Seven of Cups.

A figure faces seven cups suspended in a cloud. Each holds something different: jewels, a castle, a wreath, a creature. There is plenty to catch the eye.

But these are visions. The castle looks inviting; it isn't somewhere the figure has arrived. The jewels appear within reach, but nothing in the picture shows them being held.

That's the distinction I want to bring into your love reading. The thought of a relationship can move you before you have much experience of being close to that person.

You might see a future in someone's kindness. Or picture the relationship you want so vividly that any real beginning seems disappointing beside it. Whether there is someone on your mind or you are hoping to meet someone, imagination can get ahead of experience.

There is nothing foolish about wanting love. The question is what happens when your imagined relationship becomes your measure of the connection you actually have.

You can give a promising connection credit for a closeness it hasn't developed yet. You can also overlook a small, genuine kindness because it doesn't resemble the grand beginning you imagined.

Notice whether you are crediting a connection with closeness it has yet to develop, or overlooking a kindness you have actually received.

Two — what asking can clarify. The Queen of Swords.

The queen sits with her sword upright in her right hand. Her expression is serious. Yet her left hand reaches outward, open.

That open hand matters. With the sword alone, the picture could seem like a warning to keep your distance. Her open hand gives me a different way to read her: she can welcome a conversation while keeping her sword upright.

You don't have to become suspicious to see love clearly. You can stay interested while finding out what is actually being offered.

That might mean asking about something you have been assuming. It might mean giving a new connection time to reveal itself before deciding what it must become. You can keep hoping while allowing the other person to tell you what they want.

The Seven of Cups shows how much you can imagine. The queen brings you back to what you can learn. Together, they ask you to leave a little space between a possibility and a conclusion.

Those are the two face-up cards in this morning’s reading.

There is something useful you can notice today: when you describe your love life to yourself, where does "this is happening" become "this could happen"?

You don't need to cross out the second part. Just know where it begins. That distinction can help you appreciate what is real without asking hope to fill every gap.

The next question is which hope deserves more time, and which uncertainty needs an answer.

I'd like to explore that with you, Margaret: what you keep hoping for, what needs closer attention, what you need to ask, and what your next step is. Those questions belong to the four cards still face down.

I'll need your first and last name and your date of birth to continue. Your name identifies a separate personal tarot card. I’ll use the strengths and habits represented by that personal card when interpreting the remaining four cards. Here, that means exploring how you approach possibility in love and how you could ask for clarity while staying open to closeness.

Explore what needs your attention in love.

Marcus

P.S. The queen's hand is still open. Asking a clear question doesn't have to close the door.
