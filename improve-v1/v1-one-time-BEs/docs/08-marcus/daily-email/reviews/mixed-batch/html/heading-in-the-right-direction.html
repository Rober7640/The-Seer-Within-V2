MORNING EDITION SIX CARDS · ONE QUESTION
 

Margaret,

You can know how to do the work and still wonder whether it’s the work you want to keep doing.

 

That’s the question behind the first two cards I’ve turned this morning. Six Rider–Waite cards on your working direction. Let’s look at what the first one gives you credit for.

 
 
THE TABLE THIS MORNING — six cards on one question. Two of them are looking back at you. Four of them are not. Rider–Waite–Smith deck, 1909.
 
 
 
One — what you have learned. The Eight of Pentacles.
 
FIG. I · POSITION ONE
Turned. The Eight of Pentacles.

A man sits on a wooden bench, hammer raised, working a star into a gold disc. Beside him, finished discs hang down a wooden post. Each carries the same five-pointed star.

He hasn’t made one good piece and stopped. The repeated stars show the practice behind the work in his hands.

This is the card of learning how to do something properly. Knowing which detail needs checking. Finding a way through a problem you once needed help with. Becoming someone who can be relied upon.

That experience counts, whether you’re working now, considering a return, or deciding what comes next. Even work you wouldn’t choose again can have taught you something you want to keep.

The question is where you want to use it.

 
 
 
Two — what work leaves out. The Eight of Cups.
 
FIG. II · POSITION TWO
Turned. The Eight of Cups.

A figure in a red cloak walks towards rocky ground, leaning on a staff. Behind him, eight cups stand in two rows beneath the moon.

Look at the cups. They’re still upright. He isn’t walking away from a heap of broken pieces.

I read that detail as a reason to take dissatisfaction seriously before everything goes wrong. Work can be going well by the usual measures and still leave something important out of your day.

Perhaps you want more say in how you work. Perhaps you miss learning, or seeing whom your effort helps. Those are different needs, and each could point towards a different change.

The departing figure doesn’t tell you to leave a job. He raises a question: what do you need that doing the work well hasn’t given you?

Put these cards together. The first shows ability built through repetition. The second shows that something can remain intact and still fail to satisfy.

Being good at work tells you what you’re capable of. Feeling fulfilled tells you something about whether you want to keep giving your days to it. When those answers differ, another achievement may not resolve the question.

You don’t have to dismiss what you’ve built to examine where it’s taking you, Margaret. Your experience can be useful even if your next direction changes.

 

Those are the two face-up cards in this morning’s reading.

 
 

The next question is what would make your working life feel more worth the effort—and where a change could begin.

I’d continue with the four remaining cards: what matters in your work, a strength you can use, what deserves reconsideration, and a place to begin. That gives us a way to explore direction without assuming the answer is to start over.

I’ll need your first and last name and your date of birth. Your name identifies your personal tarot card. Its meaning helps me interpret the remaining cards in relation to how you use your abilities and approach responsibility, so we can explore what you want your work to give back to you.

Explore your working direction with me.
