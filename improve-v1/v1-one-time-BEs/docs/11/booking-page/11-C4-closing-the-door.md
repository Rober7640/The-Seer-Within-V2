## The page

### Closing the Door

Nobody's going to shut this door for you. Not me, not the letter. The letter gave back what was taken. The door's your job.

What they took went out that door with them. However it was left, it's been open on your side ever since. Every time you go back for one more look, you're standing in it. What was taken is back now. Shut the door behind it.

Do this once, the morning after you've read the letter, not the same night. It takes five minutes and nothing you don't already have.

1. Go to the door you come home through. It doesn't matter whether they ever walked through it.
2. Open it and, from inside, look at what's out there: the step, the street, the weather. That's all it opens onto.
3. Put your hand flat on the door and say it out loud, once: "It's back, and it's staying." Quietly is fine.
4. Shut it with that hand, slowly, till you hear the latch catch. Then go make yourself something hot. Don't go back to check it.

Anybody who sees will think you're letting some air in. Nobody hears the words but you, and it isn't an answer to anything in the letter. It shuts the way they went, and nothing else. It doesn't shut out anybody new.

Don't do it again, even on a bad day. Doing it twice says it came open. It didn't. When the pull comes, that's habit, and your reading told you what to do with it.

You'll know it's shut on an ordinary afternoon. You'll come in with your arms full, bump the door shut with your hip, and be putting the milk away before you notice you never looked back over your shoulder.

## Notes

- **The promises in 11-C3 and 11-T1.** It's one page, 290 words. "Do this once, the morning after you've read the letter" and "Don't do it again, even on a bad day" cover "done once, after I've read the letter" and T1's "Don't do it until you've read the letter." "Nobody hears the words but you" and "Anybody who sees will think you're letting some air in" cover "Nobody sees it." Nothing is written, sent, looked up or posted, and nothing needs them, so "I don't write to anyone" holds.
- **The get_back fork.** In all 12 reports that fork ends the letter with an ask ("Come back. Or…write to me"), and Evelyn says to do nothing with it. Shutting a door right after that could read as answering "no", so the page says "it isn't an answer to anything in the letter."
- **How it fits the reports.** What was taken went out that door with them. The letter brings it back, and she shuts the door behind it. That fits the reports' empty-room picture ("it's back in its room"). Her one line, "It's back, and it's staying," picks up "It's back" from both endings ("Go and use it" in SU 1–7, "What you do with it is on you" in SU 8+), and it doesn't fight "use it": she still comes and goes by that door. The pull goes back to the reading's own "Stop going back" fix. The page doesn't offer a new one.
- **Traps in the reports, handled.** SU 1's ex "pulled the door shut behind me" and SU 2's "walked out", so the page never says they left it open ("However it was left"). SU 9 and SU 22 treat a door shut on new people as her problem, so: "It shuts the way they went, and nothing else. It doesn't shut out anybody new." SU 4's habit is trying the front door at night, so the page is set in the morning, never mentions a lock, and says "Don't go back to check it". That also sits well with SU 4's letter line "Lock the door once."
- **Different from the 12 closes and the other bumps.** It has no key, hook, porch light, bulb, radio, stepladder, honey, smoke alarm, chair, dents, oven timer or phone in another room. It's also not "the one thing to do", which in every report is repeated daily or weekly. Against the other bumps: no salt, nothing laid across the doorway and no stepping over it (she stays inside), nothing burned, nothing nightly or repeated, and nothing written.
- **Which door.** "The door you come home through", not "the front door". Many US homes use a side or garage door every day, and the sign at the end (arms full, bumping it shut with her hip) needs the door she actually uses. "It doesn't matter whether they ever walked through it" covers the friend or family member who never lived there.
- ⚠ **Risks.** (1) A buyer who left someone abusive and fears they're nearby had to open her door wide and look out. On review (2026-11-06) step 2 became "Open it", still from inside and in daylight; the look out stays. An open door can also let a cat or dog out. (2) At 293 words it's close to the cap. (3) Contractions run higher than ~40%: about half the sentences have one, and there are no formal expansions.
