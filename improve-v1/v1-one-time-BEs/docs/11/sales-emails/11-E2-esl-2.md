<!-- salutation, as ESL 1 -->
%FIRSTNAME% —

Evelyn here.

<!-- BEAT 2 · BOLD CLAIM, line 1 after the greeting. Stated as certain -->
**Something has been standing in for the part of you they took.**

<!-- BEAT 3 · the idea: the space didn't stay empty. Stock line from the reports, word for word. Widows line (11-A), fresh words -->
When they took it, the space didn't stay empty. Something moved in to keep the place, and I call it the stand-in.

The stand-in is what turns decent people away. Somebody kind comes knocking for you, the stand-in opens the door, and the poor soul goes home thinking that was you.

Its whole job is to stop you getting hurt the same way twice, and it's good at the job.

The stand-in isn't a second thing to get rid of. It's only what grows in a room that's been left empty, and it goes quiet once what belongs there comes back.

The one who took it from you is alive, dear. If someone you loved has died, this letter isn't about them.

<!-- BEAT 4 · the line-up (self-check). Names as the reports name them. Habits from outside, or what somebody said -->
### Which stand-in answers your door

Here are four I see a lot. The people around you spot yours long before you do.

**The Inspector.** Goes over everything you do before anybody else can. Whoever says dinner was lovely gets told what went wrong with the gravy.

**The Lookout.** Watches for the let-down before it comes. You text the night before to check a plan's still on, every time. A friend said it to your face: "You've gotten so careful."

**The Chaperone.** Makes sure nobody gets carried away, you least of all. At every birthday you're the one taking the pictures, so you're in hardly any.

**The Watchman.** Never goes off duty. Anybody who's stayed the night has heard you get up to try a lock you'd already locked.

If one of those sounds like you, it is. If none does, yours goes by another name. Your twelve cards name yours.

<!-- BEAT 5 · why the usual fixes feed it -->
### Why getting back out there keeps the stand-in busy

People have told you what to do. Keep busy. Get back out there. Let a friend set you up.

Keeping busy just gives the stand-in extra places to stand guard.

Get back out there, and the stand-in goes with you and does the talking.

A friend sets you up with someone lovely, and the stand-in gets to the table first and takes your seat.

All three send you out the door. None of them brings back what was taken, so the stand-in stays on duty.

<!-- BEAT 6 · the free card. PICTURE FIRST, then meaning. Leaves open: what the three cups are -->
### The card that shows your stand-in

I drew one card for you, %FIRSTNAME%: the Four of Cups, at the top of this letter.

A young man sits under a tree with his arms folded. Three cups stand upright on the grass in front of him. Out of a little cloud beside him comes a hand, holding out a fourth cup. He doesn't look at it.

Here's what it means.

The folded arms are the stand-in, folded before the cup ever came out of the cloud.

The cup held out is the decent person. Somebody kind, offering something good, and getting no look at all.

His eyes stay on the three cups on the grass. Those three are what was taken from you, and he looks at them instead of up at the hand.

What's in them, the card won't say. I won't guess for you.

<!-- BEAT 7 · mechanism + withhold. Framed as what the cards show (11-C) -->
### What gets the stand-in to stand down

The cards are plain on this. The stand-in stands down when what was taken comes back, and not before. You can't argue it out of the room. Nobody new can either, however kind.

What was taken comes back through the letter they never sent: the words you were owed and never got. Once you've read them, what belongs in that room is back in it, and the stand-in has nothing left to keep.

One card shows me your stand-in, but not what was taken, and not the letter. Your twelve show all of it.

### What happens when you say yes

You [put two things in on this page]({{BOOKING_URL}}?c=22): your birth name, as it's written on your birth certificate, and your date of birth. Your birth name and date set how I lay your twelve cards.

Card 1 names what they took, and the stand-in that moved in when it went. Every card after that answers one question you've been carrying, with one flat answer.

When the twelfth card's down, I write out the letter they never sent, start to finish, in their voice.

Your reading comes by email as a PDF within 24 hours. I write it out myself, and that takes a day.

There's nothing to send back and nobody to contact. You read it, and you're done.
