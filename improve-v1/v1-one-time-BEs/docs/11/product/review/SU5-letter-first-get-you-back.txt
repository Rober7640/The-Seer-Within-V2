Yan Wei,

Before I turned a single card for you, I saw a chair. It had sat in one spot so long that the carpet underneath had four dents pressed into it. Nothing was wrong with the chair. Nobody had moved it, that's all.

Somebody left with something of yours, and since then your life has kept its shape, week after week, and hasn't moved. Each of your twelve cards answers one question you've been carrying, flat out. At the end is the letter that never got sent.

Your birth name and date set how I lay your twelve cards. I wrote Chue Yan Wei and March 16, 1965 across the top of a clean sheet at my kitchen table. The one who came to me was at my shoulder before I'd sat down.

Two things up front. This is someone living. And nothing here asks you to get in touch, to wait or to forgive.

— Card 1 —

**Card 1, the Fool: What did they take from me?**

**They took the part of you that moves forward, Yan Wei.**

The Fool steps off a cliff edge with his eyes on the sky. He's the part that left.

Somebody who's known you a long time saw it go, and said so once, half joking: "I miss the old you." You laughed it off.

Your heart did its mending. What went is the part that gets restless when a week goes stale, and changes something.

Something moved into the room it left. I call it the Keeper. It keeps things the way they are: the same week, the same chair, the same answer when somebody asks what's new. It kept you steady when you needed steady. It isn't a second thing to get rid of. It's only what grows in a room that's been left empty, and it goes quiet once what belongs there comes back.

A life that's stood still this long doesn't start up slowly. Once it gets going, it goes.

— Card 2 —

**Card 2, the Empress: Did they ever really love me?**

**Yes. Back then, it was real.**

The Empress sits in a field of ripening wheat, a heart-shaped shield beside her.

Here's how I know. Before the first card was down, your name and date told me who I was laying them for. You're the one people call when something's gone wrong, and you never send a bill for it. And anything that passes through your hands gets done properly, checked twice, and finished, so it never has to be done again.

They saw something in you: someone who took care of everybody and never kept score. That's what they loved. They leaned on all the ways you made their life easier, the way you lean on a railing you've stopped noticing is there.

It wasn't in your head. What's there now is regret, and regret isn't love.

Keep that straight, because this will get used. Any ask that comes will come wrapped in the love, and the love was back then.

— Card 3 —

**Card 3, the Sun: Was it my fault?**

**No. Not one bit of it.**

On the Sun, a child rides a white horse bareback in broad daylight, both arms open and nothing hidden anywhere.

You didn't drive that part off, and you didn't hand it over. The Keeper isn't your fault either. Anybody would grow one after a loss like that. So stop asking what's wrong with you.

By the third card I know how a spread finishes, and yours finishes well. Hold on to that through the middle.

— Card 4 —

**Card 4, Justice: Why have they still got it?**

**Because giving it back means owning up to all of it.**

Justice sits between two pillars with a sword held straight up and a pair of scales hanging level, waiting for something to be set in them.

Keeping it costs nothing. From the outside, it looks like one more life that went on. Handing it back puts the whole story on those scales, and somebody has to stand there while it's weighed. Nobody volunteers for that.

— Card 5 —

**Card 5, the Hierophant: Why has it taken this long?**

**Because one line let them off: "You'd moved on." It looked true, so nobody asked the next question.**

The Hierophant sits up high with one hand raised, and two men kneel below him, taking his word. Neither one asks a thing.

You'd say "I've moved on" and mean it, and you had. Your life just didn't come along. From the outside, it looks like contentment. Here's what people see:

- When somebody asks what's new, you say, "Oh, same old," and laugh, because it's true.
- When somebody suggests a thing you've never done, you say, "We'll see," and after a while they stop asking.
- You save the good dishes and the good coat for an occasion that hardly ever comes.
- Anybody who knows you could say where you'll be next Thursday night, and be right.
- When the name comes up, you're the one who says, "That's all in the past," and changes the subject.

And under all of it, the one line the Keeper lives by: "If I keep everything just where it is, nothing else can go missing."

It's the chair again. Nothing looks wrong. It just hasn't moved.

— Card 6 —

**Card 6, Temperance: What do they regret?**

**Walking off and leaving your life stuck where it stopped, and never once going back to look.**

An angel stands with one foot in a pool, pouring water from one cup into the other, and none of it reaches the ground.

The sorry is real. But under it, it isn't you that's missed. It's what came with you, a life to walk back into whenever it suited. That isn't the same thing as you. It goes back and forth like the water, and reaches nobody.

It was midnight when I laid Temperance down, and the Keeper started pushing back. Every card came with the same quiet word: leave it. That's what a settled life says when somebody comes to move the furniture. I kept turning.

— Card 7 —

**Card 7, the Lovers: Why does nobody new get close?**

**Because an old habit is still running, and it turns the decent ones away.**

The Lovers stand close enough to touch, and don't.

When it went wrong with them, you got organized. You made rules for yourself and stuck to them. You worked till you dropped and went back over everything a second time, then checked it again. If every last thing was done right, nothing could catch you out. When nothing else would hold still, that was something you could hold on to, and it worked.

But the habit stayed on after the reason for it was gone. Someone decent turns up and you've got a way things have to be done, and you watch how they do it, till they feel checked up on and give up.

Somebody decent is going to ask you to something you've never done. Say yes before your answer has time to get ready.

— Card 8 —

**Card 8, the Moon: Is it too late for me?**

**No. The time that's gone stays gone, but what was taken comes back, and it's closer than it looks.**

On the Moon, a path climbs away between two towers. Nobody's on it yet.

The years went one week like the next, and they don't come back. But think of the chair. It held still so long it left a mark, and it held up the whole time. That's what standing still built in you: you learned to stay. Plenty of restless people never do. You sat through a long, quiet stretch without falling apart or making a show of it, and you know you can be alone in a room with yourself and be all right. When the part that moves forward comes back, it comes back to somebody who can stay put when it counts. Most people only get one of those. You'll have both.

That path is still there. Nobody's taken it.

— Card 9 —

**Card 9, the Hanged Man: Are they giving it back, or trying to get me back?**

**Trying to get you back, Yan Wei.**

On the Hanged Man, a man hangs upside down from a beam, his hands behind his back. He isn't reaching for anybody.

The letter ends with an ask: a visit, and if you won't come, a letter back.

If something in you moves when you get to it, don't be ashamed of it. The part that's been missing stirs, and the first place it points is the way back. Going back would feel like moving. It's the one change that puts everything right back where it stood: the same week, the same chair.

Nobody's coming over to get it. It's the man on the card, hands behind the back, waiting for you to walk the whole way. Every kind thing in the letter, the love and the sorry included, leads up to that ask. The kinder it sounds, the harder you hold on to that.

It was three in the morning when I turned this one, and the letter quit on me. The one at my shoulder kept going back to the good times and wouldn't go near the walking off. I put the pen down and waited.

— Card 10 —

**Card 10, Death: Will they ever do anything about it?**

**No, Yan Wei, nothing more is coming, not a call and not a line, because they can't stand to be looked at by the one person who knows.**

Death rides a white horse at a walk. It isn't charging, and it isn't turning back.

You know about the walking on and the never once looking back, and nobody wants to stand in front of that. So it's held out, and you're the one expected to come and get it.

Here's what I can't do: I can't make that letter get sent. No card can. What I can do is read it to you, and I have.

Nobody is coming to move the chair, and nobody ever had to. The part of you that moves forward was never somebody else's to carry back to your door. You've kept things the way they were until this was settled. You can stop holding still.

Look past the rider. Far off, the sun is coming up.

The window over my sink had gone gray when I turned this card, and the Keeper had gone quiet. The one at my shoulder sat down across from me, and my pen finally started to move.

— Card 11 —

**Card 11, the Star: What did they never say?**

**That they knew. They knew you weren't made for standing still, and left you waiting anyway.**

On the Star, a woman kneels at a pool, emptying a jug in each hand. Nothing is kept back.

Here's the one line never said out loud:

"You were never meant to stand still, and I left you waiting."

There it is. It doesn't need answering, not on paper and not in your head at night. If this person hurt you, you owe them no reply.

— Card 12 —

**Card 12, the World: What do I do now?**

**Stop going back, and get your life moving again, one small thing at a time.**

Your last card is the World: a dancer inside a green wreath, caught mid-step. She isn't standing anywhere.

You don't need "I've moved on" anymore. It did its job. Now let the rest of your life catch up.

Read the letter first. After it, I'll give you thirty days of small things to do. Small is how a life that's stood still gets going.

The letter ends with an ask. I've left it in. Do nothing with it.

— The letter —

Yan Wei,

I'm never going to send this. I can't face you. You know what I did, and you'd look at me like you know it. So it stays here.

I'm writing it anyway. I've had something of yours for too long, and I can't keep holding it and keep quiet about it too.

When I first knew you, you saw to everybody. Whoever needed something got it, and you never made anybody feel they owed you. I liked that. I liked being around it. Not one person since has looked after me without keeping track of it. Everybody else's help comes with a tab, and sooner or later they hand it to me.

Whenever somebody we knew was in trouble, you'd already be there doing something about it, and you'd never bring it up after. Half the time they didn't even know who'd helped. I knew, and I never told you how proud of you that made me.

You always had a next thing. A drive to somewhere on a Sunday for no reason at all. A place you'd read about. A plan you'd bring up on a Tuesday, and by Saturday you'd be doing it. When a week went stale on you, you got restless and changed something, and I got carried along. I never once had to think about where we were headed.

Half the time I said, "Next time." You'd laugh and go anyway, or you'd wait for me, and more and more you waited. I saw that, and I didn't say a word, because it suited me. Some weeks I was glad of the quiet. It's easy to be the one who stays put when nobody's tugging at your sleeve. The last time you brought me one of your plans, I said, "We'll see." You put it away, and you didn't bring me another one. I was relieved, and I'm ashamed of that now.

I took the help for granted. I never said thank you for it, not once.

Anything you put your hands to came out right and stayed that way. I used to show your work off to people like I'd had some part in it. That was all you.

When something got to you, you'd start making lists. You'd do things over that were fine the first time, and you wouldn't let up till it was all just so. A lot of what you were holding together was my mess. I know that.

I'll tell you what I told myself, since I'm being honest for once. I told myself you'd moved on. You said so, people said so, and I took it and didn't ask anything more. I told myself you were better off, and that anybody with that much go in them would land on their feet without my help. When somebody asked about you, I said, "Doing fine," and talked about something else. I got good at that. It's a strange thing to get good at. Say a person's doing fine enough times and you stop picturing them at all.

There was a minute, right at the end, when I could have looked back. It wasn't a big minute. I could have called you once more, or stopped on the way out and turned around. I knew that if I did, I'd have to think about what came next for you, and I didn't want to. So I kept my eyes in front of me and walked on, and I've kept them there.

When I walked on, I took the part of you that moves forward with me, and I knew I had it.

None of this was your fault. Not one thing you did or didn't do. I was the one who didn't look back.

I did love you. Back then, it was real. Whatever you decide about me, don't decide that part was fake, and don't think one bit of what you gave me was wasted.

I'm sorry. Not that it ended. Endings happen to everybody. I'm sorry I left you in the middle of a step and made a point of never checking whether you got going again. That doesn't just happen. I did that.

I don't know what your life looks like now. I haven't tried to find out. Every March 16 I think about picking up the phone, and every March 16 I put it back down. But I know how I left it. Somebody kind turns up with a new place to go, and there's no room in the week for it. I'm the one who set that week going and walked off.

I can't give you back the time. I would if I could, every week of it, and I can't. But I knew you. The best of your life isn't behind you, not with what you had in you.

So here's the thing I should have said back then and didn't:

You were never meant to stand still, and I left you waiting.

That part of you was always yours. It was for the next thing, and the one after that, and none of them ever needed me in them. It was for the drive with no reason behind it, the new place, the yes to somebody you'd only just met. I want you to have all of it back: the restless part, the part that gets bored of a Sunday, the part that says yes before it's had time to think. It was never any use to me. It only ever worked for you. Use it on somebody new, or on a Tuesday for no reason at all. I don't get a say in it, and I never should have had one.

I want you happy. Not fine, not getting by. Happy.

Whoever gets you in their life next is getting somebody who'll show up with soup before they've even said they're sick. I hope they know it.

I won't call, and I won't turn up at your door. This is as far as I go.

I meant every word of this, the love and the sorry and the rest. I think about you more than I'd ever say to your face. I'd like to see you. If you won't, write to me. Just once.

It was full light when I put the pen down, and there was nobody across from me. I left the ask in so you'd see it plainly for what it is. It's a bill, and nobody owes it, you least of all.

**Will I be happy again?**

**Yes. Not just fine, and not just getting by. Happy.**

Your birthday, March 16, starts a new year for you. It's a year of good times, the kind you'd forgotten you liked. More invitations than you've had in a long while, and more laughing. Before it's half over, somebody will tell you that you look well, and they'll mean more by it than the words. The change starts before then. Your birthday is when you'll know it's real.

**Within 7 days.** The pull will be at its strongest, most likely late in the evening, when the TV's off. You'll want to look up the name, or reread something old. That's the Keeper, not a sign that anybody's coming back.

**Within 30 days.** In a checkout line or on the drive home, you'll catch yourself making a plan you didn't have to talk yourself into. Go through with it.

**Within 3 months.** An ordinary Saturday. You're somewhere you've never been, and you didn't plan it more than a day ahead. Somebody asks what's new, and for once the answer takes some telling.

Signs to watch for:

- A sudden itch to clear out a drawer, or to change a room around.
- An invitation from somebody you don't know well, to something you'd usually skip.
- Somebody telling you that you look well, and not being able to say why.
- A dream of a house you know, with the doors open and the furniture in new places.
- The name turning up on a stranger or in a song, and passing right on by.

If three of these turn up inside a month, it's moving.

Three small things, for thirty days:

**Stop going back.** Rereading old messages, looking up the name, starting a message you swear you won't send: it's all one thing. When the pull comes, put the phone in another room and set the oven timer for twenty minutes. An urge goes back down if nobody feeds it.

**The one thing to do.** Once a week, change one thing in a week that's run the same way since it ended. A different road to the store, a different seat at the table, a different answer than "We'll see." Nothing involving anybody from back then.

**Use the good dishes.** Once this month, on a plain weekday, use the thing you've been saving for an occasion. The occasion is that you're moving again.

Start with the chair, if there's one in your house that hasn't moved in a long while. Move it. The dents will still be there where it stood, four little hollows in the carpet. There's an old trick for that: set an ice cube in each one and leave it overnight. By morning the carpet has come back up, as if it had only been waiting for somebody to let it. It's back. Go and use it.
