Yan Wei,

You let me lay your twelve cards because somebody left with something of yours, and some things between you were never settled. This reading settles them. It tells you what was taken, whether the love was ever real, whether it was your fault, why it's taken this long, whether it's too late, and what to do now, one flat answer each and no "only you can know." Then it gives you the letter that never got sent, written out start to finish.

Your birth name and date set how I lay your twelve cards. I wrote Chue Yan Wei and March 16, 1965 across the top of a clean sheet at my kitchen table and made a pot of ginger tea. The one who came to me was at my shoulder before I'd sat down.

Two things up front. This is someone living. And nothing here asks you to get in touch, to wait or to forgive. If this person hurt you, you owe them no reply.

Each card answers one question, and each one brought me a piece of the letter. I've kept the pieces back so you get the letter whole, at the end. Read the cards in order.

— Card 1 —

**Card 1, the Fool: What did they take from me?**

**They took the part of you that moves forward, Yan Wei.**

The Fool is a young man at the edge of a cliff with one foot already out over the drop. His eyes are on the sky, and a small white dog jumps at his heels, trying to keep up. He's the part that left.

Somebody close to you saw it go before you did. A sister, a friend who's known you forever, or one of your kids said it once, half joking: "I miss the old you." Or "You've changed." You laughed and said you were just getting settled, and nobody pushed it.

Nobody meant your heart. Your heart did its mending. What went is the part that gets restless when a week has gone stale, and changes something.

That part of you was something special. It's what said yes first, got everybody out the door on a Sunday for no reason, and made a plain week worth telling about. Not everybody has it. You had it, and everyone around you lived better for it.

Nothing broke when it went. You did the week, and did it well, and then you did the same week again. When somebody asks what's new, you say, "Oh, same old," and it's true.

The Fool brought me the part of the letter that owns up to taking it, and to knowing it all along.

Here's the good news. A life that's stood still this long doesn't start up slowly. Once it gets going, it goes.

— Card 2 —

**Card 2, the Empress: Did they ever really love me?**

**Yes. Back then, it was real.**

The Empress sits back on cushions in an open field, with the wheat ripening in front of her. Beside her rests a shield shaped like a heart, with the sign of Venus cut into it.

Here's how I know. Before I turned a single card, your name and date told me who I was laying them for. You're the one people call when something's gone wrong, and you never send a bill for it. And anything that passes through your hands gets done properly, checked twice, and finished, so it never has to be done again.

They saw something in you: someone who took care of everybody and never kept score. And they were right. People like you hold everybody else together, and most of you go without a word of thanks for it. That's what they loved. They leaned on all the ways you made their life easier, and you never once came up short.

So don't let anybody tell you it was all in your head. What's there now is regret, and regret isn't love. You don't miss the person. You miss what went with them.

Keep that straight, because this will get used. Any ask that comes will come wrapped in the love, and the love was back then.

— Card 3 —

**Card 3, the Sun: Was it my fault?**

**No. Not one bit of it.**

On the Sun, a child rides a white horse bareback in broad daylight, both arms open and nothing hidden anywhere.

I told you before that I'm not blaming you for any of it. Neither does the letter. The Sun carried the part of it that says so, flat out, with nothing tucked behind it. So stop asking what's wrong with you. A life out in the open is how a life is supposed to be.

By the third card I know how a spread finishes, and yours finishes well. Hold on to that through the middle.

— Card 4 —

**Card 4, Justice: Why have they still got it?**

**Because giving it back means owning up to all of it.**

Justice sits between two pillars, a sword held straight up in one hand and a pair of scales in the other. The pans hang level, waiting for something to be set in them.

Keeping it costs nothing. From the outside, it looks like one more life that went on, and nobody thinks twice about that. Handing it back puts the whole story on those scales, and somebody has to stand there while it's weighed. Nobody volunteers for that, so it's stayed put.

This is the part of the letter that asks why, and answers it straight.

— Card 5 —

**Card 5, the Hierophant: Why has it taken this long?**

**Because one line let them off: "You'd moved on." It looked true, so the questions stopped there.**

The Hierophant sits up high with one hand raised, and two men kneel below him. Both take his word as he gives it. Neither one asks a question.

It held because you didn't call, and you didn't cry where anybody could see. You'd say "I've moved on" and mean it, and you had. Your life just didn't move with you. Your Sundays go the way they've gone for a long time: the same chores, the same phone call, the same show at night. When the name comes up, you're the one who says, "Oh, that's all in the past," and changes the subject, so nobody gets to the next question: where has your life gone since?

— Card 6 —

**Card 6, Temperance: What do they regret?**

**Walking off and leaving your life stuck where it stopped, and never once going back to look.**

An angel stands with one foot on the bank and one in a pool, pouring water from one cup into the other. The water goes back and forth, and none of it reaches the ground.

The sorry is real. But under it, it isn't you that's missed. It's what came with you, a life to walk back into whenever it suited. That isn't the same thing as you. That kind of regret goes back and forth like the water, and reaches nobody. It won't set a thing moving for you.

It was midnight when I laid Temperance down. The tea had gone cold in the pot, and I hadn't written a word of the letter yet.

— Card 7 —

**Card 7, the Lovers: Why does nobody new get close?**

**Because an old habit is still running, and it turns the decent ones away.**

The Lovers stand close enough to touch, and don't. This card brought me the part of the letter about the people who'd come along after.

When it went wrong with them, you got organized. You made rules for yourself and stuck to them. You worked till you dropped and went back over everything a second time, then checked it again. If every last thing was done right, nothing could catch you out. When nothing else would hold still, that was something you could hold on to, and it worked.

That kind of care is what people trust with the important stuff, the things that can't be allowed to go wrong. Nobody hands those to someone sloppy. Back then it worked. But the habit stayed on after it ended. Someone decent turns up and you've got a way things have to be done, and you watch how they do it, till they feel checked up on and give up.

Somebody decent is going to ask you to something you've never done. Say yes before your answer has time to get ready.

— Card 8 —

**Card 8, the Moon: Is it too late for me?**

**No. The time that's gone stays gone, but what was taken comes back, and it's closer than it looks.**

On the Moon, a dog and a wolf howl up at a big moon in the dark, and a crayfish crawls out of a pool. A path climbs up from the water and away between two towers, and nobody's on it yet.

The letter says the best of your life isn't behind you. I'll say it plainly myself: it's true.

"I've moved on" did its job. You can say the name without your voice changing, and you were right to be a little proud of that. But the years went one week like the next while your life stood where it was left. I'd bet there's a chair in your house that hasn't moved in so long it's left dents in the carpet.

That path is still there. Nobody's taken it.

— Card 9 —

**Card 9, the Hanged Man: Are they giving it back, or trying to get me back?**

**Trying to get you back, Yan Wei.**

On the Hanged Man, a man hangs upside down from a beam of living wood, his hands out of sight behind his back. He isn't reaching for anybody, and he isn't going to.

The letter ends with an ask: a visit, and if you won't come, a letter back.

If something in you moved when you read that, don't be ashamed of it. Nothing in your life has moved without you pushing it for a long while. Then this comes, the part that's been missing stirs, and the first place it points is the way back. Going back would feel like moving. It's the one change that puts everything right back where it stood.

The ask is to find you where you were left: the same week, the same Sundays, the life that waited. And nobody's coming over to get it. It's the man on the card all over again, hands behind the back, waiting for you to walk the whole way over.

I told you that if this turned out to be the one that wants you back, you'd need to treat it as a trap. This is that one. Every kind thing in the letter, the love and the sorry included, leads up to that ask. The kinder it sounds, the harder you hold on to that.

It was three in the morning when I turned this one. I had pieces of a letter and no letter, and I didn't know yet if it would come at all.

— Card 10 —

**Card 10, Death: Will they ever do anything about it?**

**No, Yan Wei, nothing more is coming, not a call and not a line, because they can't stand to be looked at by the one person who knows.**

Death rides a white horse at a walk, holding a black flag with one white rose on it. It isn't charging, and it isn't turning back.

You'd be looking straight at them, and you know about the walking on and the never once looking back. They can't stand in front of that. So it's held out, and you're the one expected to come and get it. Not a single step comes your way.

Now the turn. They were never the ones who had to move. The part of you that moves forward was never somebody else's to carry back to your door. You've kept things the way they were until this was settled, and nobody ever called it waiting. You can stop holding still.

Look past the rider. Far off, between two towers, the sun is coming up. Once that part is yours again, your life goes on its own.

The window over my sink had gone gray by the time I turned this card. The one at my shoulder came around the table and sat down across from me, and my pen finally started to move.

— Card 11 —

**Card 11, the Star: What did they never say?**

**That they knew. They knew you weren't made for standing still, and left you waiting anyway.**

On the Star, a woman kneels at a pool under a big yellow star, emptying a jug in each hand. Nothing is kept back in either one.

This is the one line I'll give you before the letter, because it's the one never said out loud:

"You were never meant to stand still, and I left you waiting."

There it is, and you can stop looking for it. That sentence doesn't need answering, not on paper and not in your head at night. If this person hurt you, you owe them no reply.

— Card 12 —

**Card 12, the World: What do I do now?**

**Stop going back, and get your life moving again, one small change a day.**

Your last card is the World. A dancer moves inside a big green wreath with a wand in each hand, caught mid-step. She isn't standing anywhere. She carried the part of the letter that hands it back to you, every bit of it.

You don't need "I've moved on" anymore. Now let the rest of your life catch up.

The pull will be at its strongest inside the next five to seven days. That's about you, and it says nothing about anybody turning up.

**Stop going back.** Rereading the old messages, looking up the name, starting a message you swear you won't send: it's all one thing. When the pull comes, put the phone in another room, set the oven timer for twenty minutes and give your hands something to do. An urge goes back down on its own if nobody feeds it.

**The one thing to do.** For seven days, once a day, change one thing in a week that's run the same way since it ended. Take a different road to the store. Sit somewhere else at the table. Move the chair nobody's moved in ages. Nothing that involves anybody from back then.

You'll know it's working by what stops. Your hand stops going to the phone late at night, and "same old" stops being the true answer.

The letter ends with an ask. I've left it in. Do nothing with it.

— The letter —

Yan Wei,

I'm never going to send this. I can't face you. You know what I did, and you'd look at me like you know it. So it stays here.

I'm writing it anyway. I've had something of yours for too long, and I can't keep holding on to it and keep quiet about it too. So I'm going to say what I did, all of it, and then I'm going to give it back.

When I first knew you, you saw to everybody. Whoever needed something got it, and you never made anybody feel they owed you. I liked that. I liked being around it. Not one person since has looked after me without keeping track of it. Everybody else's help comes with a tab, and sooner or later they hand it to me.

Whenever somebody we knew was in trouble, you'd already be there doing something about it, and you'd never bring it up after. Half the time they didn't even know who'd helped. I knew, and I never told you how proud of you that made me.

You always had a next thing, and I got to come along for it. A place you wanted to drive out to on a Sunday. Something you'd read about and wanted to try. Somebody you wanted me to meet. You'd bring up some plan on a Tuesday, and by Saturday you'd be doing it. When a week went stale on you, you'd get restless and change something, and the next week came out a different shape from the one before. I never had to think about where we were headed. You did that for both of us.

Half the time I said, "Next time." You'd laugh and go anyway, or you'd wait for me. More and more, you waited for me. I saw that, and I didn't say a word, because it suited me. The last time you brought me one of your plans, I said, "We'll see." You put it away, and you didn't bring me another one. I was relieved, and I'm ashamed of that now.

I did love you. Back then, it was real. Whatever you decide about me, don't decide that part was fake, and don't think one bit of what you gave me was wasted.

I took the help for granted. I never said thank you for it, not once.

Anything you put your hands to came out right and stayed that way. I used to show your work off to people like I'd had some part in it. That was all you.

When something got to you, you'd start making lists. You'd do things over that were fine the first time, and you wouldn't let up till it was all just so. A lot of what you were holding together was my mess. I know that.

I saw all of that too, and I let it go on.

There was a minute, right at the end, when I could have turned around. It wasn't a big minute. I could have called you once more, or stopped and looked back, and I knew that if I did, I'd have to think about what came next for you. I didn't want to think about it. So I didn't look. I kept my eyes in front of me and I walked on, and I've kept them there ever since.

None of this was your fault. Not one thing you did or didn't do. I was the one who didn't look back.

When I walked on, I took the part of you that moves forward with me, and I knew I had it.

Not every day, and not in so many words. But I knew. I knew there were plans still waiting with my name in them, and I left them sitting there for you to sort out. You don't carry something like that around without feeling the weight of it now and then. When I felt it, I put it out of my mind and got on with my week.

You got over me, and I never doubted you would. Getting over me and getting going again were never the same thing, and I knew it.

I told myself you'd moved on. People said so, and you said so yourself, so I took it and didn't ask a single thing more. I told myself other things too. That you were better off. That anybody with that much go in them would land on their feet without any help from me. That none of it was my business anymore. If anybody asked about you, I said the same thing every time: doing fine, moved on. Nobody asked me the next question, and I made sure I never asked it of myself.

Why did I hang on to it? Because giving it back means saying what I did. As long as I had it, I was just somebody whose life went on, the same as anybody's, and nobody thinks twice about that. Giving it back means writing down that I walked off with the one part of you that kept your life going, and that I knew. So I held on to it, and I kept my eyes front.

I don't know what your life looks like now. I haven't seen it, and I haven't tried to. Every March 16 I think about picking up the phone, and every March 16 I put it back down. But I know how I left it, and I know what leaving a person like that does. The door shuts, and the good people who come along later get turned away for something I did. Somebody kind turns up with a new place to go or a new thing to try, and there's no room in the week for it. I'm the one who set that week going and walked off.

What I regret isn't that it ended. I regret leaving your life standing right where I left it, and never once going back to see if it got moving again. I'm sorry. Endings happen to everybody. Leaving somebody in the middle of a step, and then making a point of not checking, doesn't just happen. I did that.

I can't give you back the time. I would if I could, every day of it, and I can't. Nobody can. I won't tell you it went by fast, or that it doesn't matter. It mattered. Every week of it was yours, and all that time I was holding something you needed for every one of them.

But I knew you. The best of your life isn't behind you, not with what you had in you.

So here it is, the thing I should have said back then and didn't. I'm saying it now because I can't put this down any other way.

You were never meant to stand still, and I left you waiting.

That part was always yours. It was for going on to the next thing, and the one after that, and none of them ever needed me in them. It was for the drive with no reason behind it, the new place, the yes to somebody you'd only just met. It was never meant to sit in my hands. I want you to have all of it back. All of it: the restless part, the part that gets bored of a Sunday, the part that says yes before it's had time to think. I've carried it long enough to know it was never any use to me. It only ever worked for you. Use it on somebody new, or on a Tuesday for no reason at all. I don't get a say in it, and I never should have had one.

I want you happy. Not fine, not getting by. Happy.

Whoever gets you in their life next is getting somebody who'll show up with soup before they've even said they're sick. I hope they know it.

I won't call, and I won't turn up at your door. This is as far as I go.

I meant every word of this, the love and the sorry and the rest. I've thought about you more than I'd ever say to your face. I'd like to see you. If you won't, write to me. Just once.

It was full light when I put the pen down. I'd written every word of it out by hand, and when I looked up, the chair across from me was only furniture again, with nobody in it. I left the ask in so you'd see it plainly for what it is. It's a bill, and nobody owes it, you least of all.

**Will I be happy again?**

**Yes. Not just fine, and not just getting by. Happy.**

Your birthday, March 16, starts a new year for you, and I can see what kind of year it is. It's a year of good times, the kind you'd forgotten you liked. More invitations than you've had in a long while, and more laughing. Before it's half over, somebody will tell you that you look well, and they'll mean more by it than the words. The change starts before then. Your birthday is when you'll know it's real.

Here's what I see six months or so out. An ordinary Saturday. You're somewhere you've never been, and you didn't plan it more than a day ahead. Somebody asks what's new with you, and for the first time in a long while the answer takes some telling.

And the next time someone reaches for you, there's someone there.

It's back. Go and use it.
