Wanda,

Before I turned a single card for you, I saw a porch light on at four in the morning, the only one lit on the street, with a few moths still at it. Nobody was coming up the walk. It had been left on every night for a long time, that's all, just in case.

Somebody walked off with something of yours. Since then you've kept your guard up, and you'll say so. It's kept a lot out, rest included. Your twelve cards each answer one question you've been carrying, and the letter that never got sent comes last.

Your birth name and date set how I lay your twelve cards. I wrote Wanda Ann Testerly and April 12, 1961 on a clean sheet at my kitchen table. The one who came to me was at my shoulder before I'd switched on the lamp.

Two things before we start. This is someone living. And nothing here asks you to get in touch, to wait or to forgive.

— Card 1 —

**Card 1, the Emperor: What did they take from me?**

**They took the part of you that could rest easy, Wanda.**

Your birth name carries a 4 at its heart. The card numbered 4 is the Emperor: a bearded old man seated square on a stone throne, with a suit of armor under the red robe. He's sitting down and still dressed for a fight.

A friend from way back said it once: "You used to be able to sleep anywhere." You said you'd always been a light sleeper. You hadn't.

What went missing is the part that could lock the door once, lie down, and let the night look after itself.

Something else moved into the room it left, and I call it the Watchman. The Emperor is its face. It listens for cars slowing out front, gets you up to try a lock you already locked, and keeps the porch light on till morning. It isn't a second thing to get rid of. It's only what grows in a room that's been left empty, and it goes quiet once what belongs there comes back.

— Card 2 —

**Card 2, the Tower: Did they ever really love me?**

**Yes. Back then, it was real.**

On the Tower, lightning comes out of dark cloud and knocks a gold crown clear off the top. If none of it had been real, there'd have been nothing up there to knock off.

Here's how I know: your name and date told me who I was laying them for. Not much gets past you: a new haircut, a word left out of a story, a price that crept up a dollar since last week. And whatever's missing, you've already handled it. The extra chair's out, the coffee's on, and whoever needed a hand has had one before anybody thought to ask.

They saw something in you: someone who noticed things other people walked right past. That was what they fell for. They leaned on the part of you that noticed what they missed, and back then you leaned right back without checking first.

— Card 3 —

**Card 3, the Fool: Was it my fault?**

**No. Trusting the ground under you was never the mistake.**

The Fool stands at the edge of a cliff mid-step, his eyes on the sky in front of him, not on the drop below.

That's how you walked back then, taking the ground on trust, the way anybody does who's been told it'll hold. The Watchman isn't your fault either. Anybody would grow one after a fall like that. Nothing was wrong with how you walked.

By the third card I could see your spread ends well. Hang on to that through the harder cards.

— Card 4 —

**Card 4, the Hierophant: Why have they still got it?**

**Because handing it back means admitting why you sleep so light.**

The Hierophant sits high between two stone pillars, one hand on a staff and the other raised. Two keys lie crossed on the floor at his feet, one gold and one silver, and neither hand goes near them.

Hanging on to it is free. From up on that seat, it looks like an ordinary ending. Handing it back would mean getting down, bending for those keys in front of you, and owning what's kept your door double-locked since.

— Card 5 —

**Card 5, Justice: Why has it taken this long?**

**Because one line let them off: "You can take care of yourself." Anybody could see it was true, so nobody asked how you were.**

Justice sits between two pillars, a sword held straight up. Only one foot shows under the hem of the red robe. The rest is out of sight.

Everybody saw the sword, and nobody looked under the hem. Here's what anybody can see:

- At a restaurant you take the seat facing the door, and your friends have quit arguing.
- When the phone rings after nine, you answer "What's wrong?" instead of hello.
- Anybody who's stayed over has heard you get up to try the front door a second time.
- When somebody offers a ride, you say, "I'll meet you there," so you've got your own way home.
- When somebody says you seem wound up, you shrug and say, "I keep my guard up."

Under all five sits the one line the Watchman lives by: "If I never stop watching, nothing can come at me out of nowhere again."

That's the porch light, burning at four in the morning for an empty street.

— Card 6 —

**Card 6, Temperance: What do they regret?**

**Ending it with no warning, and leaving you on watch ever since.**

On Temperance, an angel pours water from one cup to another, one bare foot in the pool and the other flat on the bank.

The sorry's real enough. Nobody over there wants you back. You do come to mind, more often than you'd think, and it goes no further. It stays one foot in and one foot out, like that angel, and never wades across to you.

It was midnight when Temperance went down, and every card I turned after that asked the same small question your Watchman asks: what was that? I turned the next one anyway.

— Card 7 —

**Card 7, the Lovers: Why does nobody new get close?**

**Because something in you sits in on every first evening, and it's busy watching the door.**

On the Lovers, two people stand close enough to touch and don't. He's looking at her. She's looking up past him at the winged figure, the way you'd check the sky for weather.

When it went wrong with them, you took care of everybody in sight. If people were busy being helped by you, nobody was close enough to see how you were doing. You ran yourself ragged, and some nights you were fed up with all of them and never said so. Being the one everybody needed gave you somewhere to stand, and it kept you going.

That was your guard, and it kept its post after the reason was gone. Someone decent turns up and you jump in and start taking care of them, and there's never an opening for them to do a thing for you, so they go find somebody who'll let them.

— Card 8 —

**Card 8, the Moon: Is it too late for me?**

**No. The nights spent listening don't come back, but the part of you that could rest does, and it's nearer than you'd think.**

On the Moon, a dog and a wolf howl with heads raised, while the moon above them looks down with its eyes closed.

The years went by like that, head up, listening, and they don't come back. But think of the porch light. It did its job every night. It was just never told it could stop.

Here's what those nights built in you. You know what a quiet house is worth, because you went without one, and you got yourself through every night of it with nobody watching your back but you. When rest comes back, it comes to somebody who'll know it for exactly what it is.

Up top, the moon's eyes are shut, and nothing below has come to harm for it.

— Card 9 —

**Card 9, Wheel of Fortune: Are they giving it back, or trying to get me back?**

**Giving it back, Wanda. Not trying to get you back.**

On the Wheel of Fortune, a snake goes down the left side of the wheel, head first. It's on its way down, and it isn't reaching for anything.

The letter asks you for nothing, not even a reply, and it means it.

Whoever wrote it just wants it off their hands. That's a gift, though not a warm one. Nothing's hoped for in return.

Nobody's coming to sit across from you and explain. The letter is all there'll ever be. If you've been waiting up for that talk, you can go to bed.

By three in the morning the letter had stopped coming. The one at my shoulder kept starting a line and going still, like somebody listening for a car door. I waited it out.

— Card 10 —

**Card 10, Death: Will they ever do anything about it?**

**No, Wanda, nothing more is coming, not a call and not a knock at the door, because they're too proud to be the one in the wrong.**

Nobody's died. Here Death means something's finished. A skeleton in black armor rides a white horse at a walk, holding nothing but a black flag with one white rose on it. No weapon at all.

Anything more would mean facing you and owning what the leaving did, and pride doesn't bend that low. So it's being put down where it stands. Nobody's bringing it over.

There's one thing I can't do: promise you nothing will ever go wrong again. No card can. What I can tell you is that this one's over. What you've been sitting up for already happened. You can call off the watch yourself.

By the time I turned Death, the sky outside had gone gray and the cards had stopped asking what was that. The one at my shoulder finally sat, and the letter came.

Far off on the card, between two towers, the sun is coming up without anybody keeping watch for it.

— Card 11 —

**Card 11, the Sun: What did they never say?**

**That your guard went up for a good reason, and the reason was them.**

On the Sun, a child sits bareback on a white horse under a big, bright sun, arms spread wide, out in front of a low gray wall.

Here's the line that never got said:

"You were right to keep watch. I'm the reason you had to."

There it is, in daylight. You weren't jumpy. You were right. You don't have to answer it, on paper or in your head. If this person hurt you, you owe them no reply.

— Card 12 —

**Card 12, Strength: What do I do now?**

**Stop going back, and let the Watchman stand down, one night at a time.**

Last comes Strength: a woman in white, closing a lion's jaws with both hands. The lion's head is bowed, and all that holds it is a chain of flowers.

You don't need "I keep my guard up" anymore. Let your guard rest, like that lion.

If you aren't safe where you are, that comes first. A guard that's up for something happening now isn't the Watchman, and I'd never ask you to drop it. Tell somebody who can help.

The letter comes next. After it, thirty days of small things to do.

— The letter —

Wanda,

I'm never going to send this. I'm too proud. I've never once gone to anybody hat in hand, and I'm not going to start with you. So it stays here.

I'm putting it on paper all the same. Something of yours has been with me too long, and carrying it around without a word has gotten heavy.

When I first knew you, you caught the little things nobody else did. Not much got by you. I liked that. I liked being around it. Nobody since notices a thing about me unless I point it out. You'd have caught it before I opened my mouth.

Every time we were out with people, you'd pick up on what was going on underneath: what had changed, who was saying one thing and meaning another. Later you'd tell me, and you'd be right. I never said so, but it scared me a little, how much you could see.

And you could rest. I don't think you knew that was anything special. You'd fall asleep in the car with your head against the window before we were out of town. You slept through thunderstorms. If somebody said six o'clock, you took it as six o'clock and got on with your afternoon. When the phone rang late, you picked it up and said hello like it could be anybody. On a Saturday you'd let the morning go wherever it wanted. You'd leave the back door open on a summer afternoon and forget about it. You'd sit through a whole movie with your back to the door and never once turn around. I've never known anybody who could put a day down the way you could.

I let you think the ground under us was solid. Whenever you asked, I said everything was fine, and you took my word for it. That suited me. It let me off saying where things really stood.

Near the very end there was one minute when I could have told you straight. One plain sentence, and you'd have seen it coming. I didn't say it. I let everything look the same as ever right up until it wasn't, because that way I never had to watch your face. So when it came, you had no warning at all, and I'd made sure of that.

I took the sharp eyes for granted. Not one thank-you, in all that time.

You never missed a thing anybody was short of. I used to tell people you'd have what somebody needed before they'd noticed they needed it, and you never once wanted thanks.

When something upset you, you'd go make yourself useful to somebody. Everybody got your time but you, and you'd end up worn out and mad about it. I let you carry me right along with the rest of them. That's on me.

I'll be straight about what I told myself. I told myself you could take care of yourself. It showed. You always knew where the flashlight was when the power went out. I told myself somebody that careful would never get caught twice, so there was nothing left for me to worry about. When anybody asked about you, I said, "Oh, don't worry about that one." I said it so often that I quit asking whether it was true.

Why keep it this long? Giving it back means admitting what I did to you, and what it's done to you since. While it stayed with me, I was only somebody who left, and people leave every day. Nobody thinks twice about that.

I knew you'd sleep light for a long while after I went. You can't leave the way I left and not know it. Whenever that came into my head, I put it out again and went to bed. I always slept fine. Of everything in here, that's the part I'm least proud of.

I don't know how you live now. I haven't asked anybody, and I haven't looked. But I can guess what I left behind. Somebody kind turns up and means every word, and they still get watched from across the room while you wait for the catch. They're paying for what I did.

None of this was your fault. You weren't careless, and trusting me wasn't stupid. The ground was supposed to hold. I knew it wouldn't, and I let you stand on it anyway.

I did love you. Back then, it was real, every bit of it. If you throw out everything else in this letter, keep that.

I'm sorry. Not for ending it. I'm sorry for the way I did it, all at once, with nothing said first, so that you've been watching for the next one ever since. That was no accident. That was me.

I can't hand back the nights. If I could, I'd hand back every one. I know who you were, though. The best of your life isn't behind you. Not for somebody who used to sleep through thunderstorms.

So here's the thing I never said, and should have:

You were right to keep watch. I'm the reason you had to.

The part of you that could rest easy was always yours. It was for Sunday afternoons with nothing on them, for naps on other people's couches, for picking up the phone at night and hearing a friend. I couldn't use it. Nobody could but you. Take all of it back: the deep sleep, the head against the car window, the evening with nobody to listen for. What you do with it is none of my business anymore, and it never should have been.

I want you happy. Not on guard, not holding it together. Happy.

Somebody's going to come home in a new sweater, or with a rotten afternoon behind them, and never have to point at either. You'll have seen both from the door.

I won't call, and I won't show up on your step. This is the last of it.

I don't want anything from you. No answer, no visit, no forgiveness. Don't come looking for me. You can put me down now. Lock the door once, and go to sleep.

The sun was well up by the time I'd finished writing, and nobody was at my shoulder.

**Will I be happy again?**

**Yes. Happy, and sleeping through the night.**

Your birthday, April 12, starts a new year for you. It's a year for clearing out. Old papers, old habits, old hurts: they go. It feels like an ending at first. Then you notice how much room it's left, and what starts moving into it. The change doesn't wait for the date.

**Within 7 days.** The pull hits hardest late, after you've tried the front door and gone to bed. You'll be tempted to search the name, or dig out something old to read. That's the old watch keeping you up, and it doesn't mean anybody's coming back.

**Within 30 days.** The phone will ring after nine, and "Hello" will come out before "What's wrong?" does.

**Within 3 months.** An ordinary evening out. You'll sit with your back to the door and not think about it until the check comes.

Signs to watch for:

- Waking up and not remembering whether you got up to try the lock.
- A friend staying over and never once hearing you up in the night.
- The one who said "You used to be able to sleep anywhere" finding you asleep on the couch.
- A noise in the night, and you staying in bed.

Three of those inside a month means it's settling.

For the next thirty days, three small things:

**Stop going back.** Looking up the name, rereading old messages, typing one you swear you won't send: it's all one habit. When the pull comes, leave the phone on the counter and go to another room for twenty minutes. By then it will have gone back down.

**The one thing to do.** Once a day, take ten minutes off watch. Sit in a chair at home with the phone out of reach and nothing to listen for, and let your eyes close if they want to. Leave anybody from back then out of it.

**Let somebody else drive.** Once this month, when a ride's on offer, say yes and leave your keys on the hook. Don't work out how you'd get home.

Start with the porch light, if there's one by your door that's burned every night since. Get a bulb with a motion sensor in it. They fit the same socket. Screw it in, leave the switch on, and go to bed. The step goes dark like the rest of the street. If anybody ever does come up the walk, the light's on before they reach the door, and you didn't have to sit up for it. The night is yours again. It's back. Go and use it.
