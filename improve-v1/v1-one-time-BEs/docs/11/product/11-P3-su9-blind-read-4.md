# Testina's verdict, blind read 4

I read the free letter first, then A, then B, on my phone in bed. They're the same reading for most of the way. The differences are in the middle (cards 7 and 8) and in one line of the letter, but those are the parts that decided how I felt about the woman writing to me.

---

## Reading A

**1. Where my attention dropped**
"I got up from the table at that point, which I don't do..." (Card 8). I'd just been hit with the faucet-running line and wanted to sit with it. Then I got a paragraph about Evelyn going upstairs, the lamp smoking, and the ghost standing by the door. Honey, this is my reading, not yours. I skimmed down to "Here's the deal." I did the same at Card 9 ("swore at my own hands", "Three in the morning"). One of those night-vigil bits is atmosphere. By the third or fourth it starts to feel like she's padding it out.

**2. Where I felt seen**
- "you were a little proud of yourself for it." (I am. That's the truth.)
- "patting her back twice and moving off." (I do exactly that.)
- "then did with the faucet running so even the walls wouldn't hear."

Close behind: "You say sorry before you ask for anything," and "told it later like a funny story."

**3. Where it lost me**
- "a woman who could take charge and get things done." That's a guess, and I could feel it being a guess. It happens to fit, but it's the sort of thing every woman my age would say yes to.
- "Card 4" and "Card 6" are thin. Two or three lines each, and they mostly say the same thing again ("they kept it because..." and "their regret is..."). They felt like filler between the good cards.
- The ghost theatrics: the chair across the table, "the weight gone out of them," "only furniture again." I've had readings before and I know a stage set when I see one. Once is fine, but it keeps coming back.
- "Look at the card again. Nothing in it is waiting for anybody." It's a bit of a sermon, but it's gentle, so I let it go.
- Card 7 does tell me I've been shutting out the decent ones. But it says "You had good reason to" and "Nobody switches a thing like that off," and it puts the price on the person who set it. I can take that. It's honest without making me the bad guy.

**4. The cards**
They helped, more than I expected. I don't know tarot, but "he isn't gripping it or hiding it, anybody could lift it out of his fingers" made me see myself. So did the tower with no door, and the woman with her hands on the lion. The Sun one (the horse already past the wall) was the one I'll remember. A couple of the pictures I skimmed (the Hierophant, the High Priestess) because they didn't do much.

**5. The letter**
Mostly yes. "I kept the handy part. The rest I made you pay for" sounds like him, the way he'd talk if he were ever honest, which he never was. "You weren't weak. I made it expensive" got me. I had to put the phone down. "I'm not stupid" is a very him thing to say. The ending, "Come back. Or if you won't, at least write me back," gave me a jolt. Evelyn warned me it was coming and called it a bill, and that worked. It made the whole letter feel more real, because he would absolutely end on asking for something. "When something gets to you, you go real quiet. You turn it over in your head for days" is right, and it's kind.

**6. Scores**
- Kept me reading: 8
- Felt written for me: 7
- Worth the $35: 7
- I trust Evelyn: 7
- I'd tell a friend about her: 5

## Reading B

**1. Where my attention dropped**
"Some of what's happened since is yours. I'm telling you because..." (Card 7). My back went up straight away. I paid to find out what HE took, and halfway through it turns into a list of what I've done to everybody else. I read the rest of that card fast and with my arms crossed, so to speak. The same Evelyn-goes-upstairs bit in Card 8 lost me too.

**2. Where I felt seen**
The same lines as A, because most of it is word for word:
- "you were a little proud of yourself for it."
- "patting her back twice and moving off."
- "then did with the faucet running so even the walls wouldn't hear."

**3. Where it lost me**
- "If somebody's beside you now, they're living on the bits of you that slip through." Ouch. I didn't ask for that, and I don't need a stranger telling me how my people are getting short-changed.
- "They're paying a debt they never ran up." Now I'm the one making people pay? That's what HE did. This makes me sound like him.
- "I can count what it's cost you, and it's more than they ever took." No. You don't get to tell me that what I did to protect myself was worse than what he did. That's the line that would make me close the email.
- "You taught them to stop asking." That's blaming, said flat. In A the people just "stopped asking." Here it's my fault they left.
- "I'm on your side, so here's the rest, straight." When someone says "I'm on your side" right before the knife, they aren't. My sister does that.
- The letter line "You clam up, you chew on it, and you won't move till you're sure" is the same phrase Evelyn used about me in Card 7. Hearing him say her exact words made the letter feel written by Evelyn, not by him.
- Same theatrics and same thin cards (4 and 6) as A.

**4. The cards**
The same pictures as A, and they helped the same way. The Tower is missing the lightning in this one, which is a shame because that was a strong image. Without it, the tower is just a building.

**5. The letter**
Nearly the same as A, and it still landed. "You weren't weak. I made it expensive" is still the line. But because it repeats Evelyn's "clam up, chew on it" wording, I noticed the seam. For a second it read like a form letter with my name in it. That's the one thing I've been burned by before.

**6. Scores**
- Kept me reading: 7
- Felt written for me: 6
- Worth the $35: 6
- I trust Evelyn: 5
- I'd tell a friend about her: 4

## Which I'd rather have received

Reading A, without a doubt. They're nearly the same reading, so it came down to how Evelyn handled the part where I'm not perfect. The free letter already told me "that part is yours, and it's costing you," and I accepted that because she'd also said what they did was on them. A keeps that balance. It tells me I've been shutting out decent people, but it says I came by it honestly and that the price was set by somebody else. B takes the same moment and leans on me: it's "more than they ever took," I "taught them to stop asking," I'm making people pay "a debt they never ran up." I'm 61. I've had enough people tell me what's wrong with me, and I paid $35 to hear for once that it wasn't all me. A gave me that and still told me the truth. B made me feel lectured right when I'd let my guard down. I'd go back to the A Evelyn. I'm not sure I'd go back to the B one.

## Gut: what felt off or not quite human, in both

- The all-night ghost story. Evelyn tells it five or six times (the tea, the pen put down, the upstairs window, swearing at her hands, three in the morning, the window going grey, the empty chair). It's too much stagecraft, and it makes her feel like she's performing for me rather than reading for me.
- "Testina Rose Testerly ... November 5, 1962" at the top made me feel like an order number for a second. Seeing my full birth name written out by a stranger is a bit strange.
- The repeating little "notice what your face does" and "notice where your eyes go" homework, plus "the twenty minutes and the seven days." It's useful, but it starts to sound like a pamphlet from a therapist's waiting room.
- She keeps calling back to the free letter ("I told you...", "back in the first letter"). That's fine a couple of times, but there are at least six, and it makes it feel built from parts.
- Neither one ever says what HE was actually like: no habit, no moment, nothing I could picture. It's all about me and "softness." That's why it's a 7 for me and not a 9.
