## Soul Urge 11 — the voice

**The one idea:** her voice is the part of her that says what she thinks *out loud* and lets it *stand*.
It runs both ways: the thought goes out before she's checked it, and once it's out she leaves it there
instead of taking it back. They talked over her until she stopped. So now every opinion gets tidied first
(softened, shortened, a "but what do I know" on the end) or kept in, and she calls that "easy-going". She's a
little proud of it: nobody finds her difficult. It comes back when she reads the admission that she was
right to say it. The seven days let one untidied opinion out a day.

⚠ **Keep what they did general.** They talked over her, it suited them, they let it stay that way. No scenes,
no kind of relationship, nothing that says they lived together or that they stayed.
⚠ **No money words** (price, bill, cost, pay…) except the close's required "bill nobody owes" on the get-back path.
Card 8's job is "the bill": say it as what the years carried off.

**The row (11-acts.json → soul_urge → "11", as written):** took = your voice · cover word = easy-going ·
regret = talking over her until she stopped · unsaid = "You were right to say it, and I stopped listening on
purpose." · do = Once a day, say what she thinks before she tidies it: an opinion, a preference.

**Card 5's one excuse:** "She'd have said if it bothered her." It held because she looked easy-going: no
complaint on record, so it sounded fair. She backed it up by smiling and nodding along; never her fault,
because saying it got talked over. They knew enough to let it go on. She still does their work: "but what do
I know" on the end of an opinion, "does that make sense?" after she's made perfect sense.
Letter: "I told myself you'd have said if it bothered you." (and: you did say. I talked over it.)

## Her everyday details (where her life is shown)

Card 2: "you pick" when asked where to eat; a thumbs-up in the group text instead of what she thinks.
Card 5: "but what do I know"; "does that make sense?". Card 8: eating the wrong order rather than send it
back; the right thing to say arriving in the car on the way home. Card 1 (now, a little proud): she can't
remember the last time she argued with anybody.
