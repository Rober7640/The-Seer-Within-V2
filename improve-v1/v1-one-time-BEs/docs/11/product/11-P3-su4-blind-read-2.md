# Sandra's verdict, SU4 round 2 (blind read, A vs B)

I read the free letter first, then A, then B, all the way through, on the couch the way I'd read it on my phone at night.

---

## Reading A: "They took your sense of being safe"

**1. Where my attention dropped**
- "Being safe isn't just nothing bad happening..." (Card 1). It opens with a definition: "It runs two ways. One is... The other is..." That's a lecture, and I'd only just found out what they took. I wanted the picture of me, and I got a dictionary entry first. The Fool paragraph pulled me back in.
- "Now the part everything else hangs on..." (Card 10). By then it's watch, duty, shift, on the clock, off the clock, over and over. My eyes started sliding. It's the same point said a sixth time.

**2. Where I felt seen**
- "When the phone rang, you picked it up to find out who it was, not what had gone wrong."
- "You check with a friend before you'll trust your own read."
- "Relief isn't the same thing as rest." That one stopped me. I put the phone down for a second.

**3. Where it lost me**
- **Repetitive.** "Coat off" shows up again and again (Card 1 twice, Card 2, the letter). The first time it was me. By the fourth time it's a writer's trick.
- **About some other woman, maybe.** The whole reading assumes a moody, controlling partner who told me I was imagining things. Plenty of women had that. If mine just walked out, half of this isn't me. It's a big guess, and it's built on top of everything else.
- **Preachy, and it nearly blamed me.** "Part of you would sooner go back to trouble you know than learn to rest around people you don't." Don't tell me what part of me would sooner do anything. "Some part of you is relieved to read that." Is it? You don't know that.
- **Contradiction.** Card 10 says "They don't know they're doing any of this." Then in the letter they write "I knew that the whole time. It was never hard to see." Which is it? I noticed, and it made me trust it less.
- **The theater.** Evelyn swearing at the deck, standing at the back door, the clock ticking too loud, three in the morning, the ghost moving to the hall door. Once is atmosphere. Here it runs through every card, and it started to feel staged for me to watch.
- **"If you're not safe where you are right now, put these pages down and tell someone who can help."** It's kindly meant, but it reads like a pamphlet at the doctor's office. It took me right out of it.

**4. The cards**
I read them. Justice looking straight out at you instead of at her scales was good, and so was the sleeping Moon with the dog and wolf howling all night. The Fool not looking down was lovely. But a few felt bolted on. The Chariot and the Wheel of Fortune didn't add much, and Death made me flinch before it explained itself. I'm 61, so don't put Death in front of me without warning.

**5. The letter**
"You were right to feel what you felt. It was never in your head." That one did land, I'll admit it. It's the sentence you wait years for. The rest of the letter sounds partly like him and partly like a counselor wrote it for him ("I don't think I set out to do it. That doesn't change it."). Men I've known don't write like that. And the ending, "Come back to me. Or if you won't, at least answer this," in a letter he says he'll never send, is strange. Evelyn explains it, but it still felt planted.

## Reading B: "They took your softness"

**1. Where my attention dropped**
- "Your softness is the part of you that lets things reach you..." (Card 1). It's the same kind of definition paragraph, though shorter, so I got past it faster.
- "I got up from the table at that point..." (Card 8). This is the long bit where Evelyn goes to the upstairs window, the street's dark, the lamp needs trimming. It came right after the best paragraph in the whole reading, and I didn't want to hear about her lamp. Let me sit with what you just said about me.

**2. Where I felt seen**
- "You just noticed one day that you hadn't cried in front of anybody in a very long time, and you were a little proud of yourself for it." Yes. I was.
- "Every cry you saved until the house was empty, then did with the faucet running."
- "Years of birthdays where you smiled for the photo and went home and sat in the car."

Also "patting her back twice and moving off," and "You say sorry before you ask for anything." Both are me.

**3. Where it lost me**
- **Card 7 doesn't belong.** It's all of a sudden about "reading into everything" and watching for trouble, but the rest of this reading is about shutting down and going quiet. It felt pasted in from somewhere else. Then the letter says the same thing again ("Anything bothered you and you'd go hunting for what it meant").
- **"They're admiring a door you shut."** That stung, and not in a good way at first. I'm proud of being strong, and being told it's a shut door is a lecture. The Strength card at the end earned it back ("That's what strong was supposed to mean"), but for a few pages I was bristling.
- **"Some part of you still wants to be soft toward the one person who made it cost."** It's the same as A: it tells me what I secretly want. It's a little blaming, even if she says don't be ashamed.
- **The same contradiction.** "They're doing all of this without knowing it," and then the letter says "I knew that the whole time. I'm not stupid." The "I'm not stupid" bit sounded more like him, at least.
- **The theater, again.** Mint tea, the upstairs window, the lamp, swearing at her own hands, three in the morning. It's even longer than A. I don't need that much of Evelyn's night.

**4. The cards**
These helped more. The Emperor with armor under his robe is me in a picture. The Tower with no door drawn on it ("didn't think about how anyone inside was ever going to get out") gave me goosebumps. The Sun with the horse already past the wall was hopeful without being sappy. Strength with soft hands on the lion is a good ending. The Hierophant and Temperance felt like filler.

**5. The letter**
"You weren't weak. I made it expensive." It's a strong line, but it sounds like a greeting card or a therapist, not a man. "I made you sorry for it. I didn't always say anything. I didn't have to." That part sounded real. So did "I said it to other people too." That's the bit that hurts, that he told people I was too much. "Too much" is a thing a lot of us got called, and it hit harder than "imagining things." The ask at the end is the same odd move as A.

## Which I'd rather have received

B. It's about a thing I actually do: going home and crying in the car, saying "fine" with that flat face, waving off help, calling it being strong. I didn't have to have been with a particular kind of man for it to be true. A only works if he was the moody, gaslighting type, and it leans hard on making me the woman who was told she was crazy. That's close to the victim story I don't want handed to me. B's "too much" is broader and closer to home, its cards actually look like what it's saying (the Emperor's armor, the doorless Tower), and its homework, "tell a friend you missed her, in those words," is something I could do tomorrow. A's "ten minutes off duty on a bench" is fine, but it's forgettable. Both made me bristle at least once by telling me what "part of me" secretly wants, and both are too in love with Evelyn's long night at the kitchen table.

**What felt off or not quite human (gut only):**
- Both: the ghost and the clock (midnight, three a.m., grey window, full light) march along card by card like a stage play, and after a while I felt managed rather than read.
- Both: Evelyn says they don't know they're doing it, then the letter says "I knew the whole time." A real reader would catch that.
- Both: the ex writes in tidy, wise, rounded sentences. Real men who did this don't write "That doesn't change it" or "I made it expensive." It's too finished.
- Both: the free letter's lines come back word for word ("most women guess wrong," "fault by Thursday," "someone reaches for you, there's someone there"). A little of that feels like follow-through. This much starts to feel like a template.
- A: repeating "coat off" and "on watch" until they stop meaning anything.
- B: Card 7 reads like it came from a different reading.
