# Patty's verdict: Reading A vs Reading B

I read the free letter first, then A, then B, on my phone in bed. Here's how it went.

---

## Reading A ("They took your laugh")

**1. Where my attention dropped**

- **"When it went wrong with them, you started reading into everything..."** (Card 7). Up to there it had been all about my laugh, and I was right there with it. Then this part could have been about anybody who ever went through a bad breakup. It only comes back to the laugh at the very end. I skimmed it.
- **"Here's where it all turns around. They were never the ones who had to move."** (Card 10). The sunflowers and the wall went on too long. I understood "stop waiting for them" the first time. She said it three or four more ways. I scrolled.
- The bits about her kitchen through the night (the clock ticking, swearing at the deck, three in the morning) were nice the first time. By the fourth I was thinking, all right Evelyn, I get it, you were up all night.

**2. Where I felt seen**

- "you laughed at your own stories, sometimes before you got to the end." I put the phone down for a minute after that one.
- "watching some other woman laugh like that across a restaurant and thinking, good for her." I've done exactly that, and I've never told anybody.
- "You were never too much. I liked you smaller." That's the sentence. If he'd ever said that, I think I'd have been done with him years ago.

**3. Where it lost me**

- **The homework.** "say it to the cashier", "Call a friend... voices and all." I'm 61. Telling me to crack jokes at the checkout girl for a week felt a bit like a magazine quiz. It made something big feel small.
- **"I was only teasing."** It fit me well enough, but it's a big guess about how he hurt me. If he'd been the cold, silent type and not the teasing type, the whole middle of the reading would be about some other woman.
- **Contradiction.** She says twice that they're doing this "without knowing it". Then the letter says "I knew better the whole time" and "I noticed. I didn't stop." So which is it? That bothered me.
- **"You still do their work, in small ways."** It's a little close to blaming me, even with the "never your fault" right after it. The "you've been the one paying it" part in Card 8 too. I know she's being careful. I could still feel the finger pointing.
- "you owe them no reply" comes up three or four times. Once is caring. Four times sounds like a lawyer wrote it.

**4. The cards**

They helped more than I expected, mostly because they're short and she tells me what's on the card before what it means. The Hierophant with "two fingers raised and two folded down" as half a laugh was clever, and I looked twice. The Fool's little dog with its mouth open made me smile. The Moon with its eyes shut ("It isn't looking down at you, and neither are they") stung in a good way. Justice's one foot showing felt like a stretch. I skimmed the Emperor.

**5. The letter**

Mostly yes. "I'm no good at writing things like this, so I'll just say it" sounds like a man. "Sometimes I did it in front of people, because I knew you wouldn't make a fuss there" is a real detail, and that's the line that made me believe it. "People turned around to see who it was, and I was glad it was me you were with" hurt, because that's how it was at the start. "I'd like to hear it again. Come back" is exactly what he'd do: say something decent and then ask for something. It landed. The only wrong note was that he's a bit too clear-eyed about himself. Real men don't usually see themselves that well, even in a letter they never send.

## Reading B ("They took your softness")

**1. Where my attention dropped**

- **"Two ministers in white kneel on the Hierophant card..."** (Card 6). The card felt forced and the section was thin. It was the first place I thought she was filling space.
- **Card 10, the wall again.** Same as A: the point was made and then she kept making it.
- **Card 8, the paragraph after she goes upstairs** ("The lamp needed trimming and I let it smoke"). I didn't need the lamp.

**2. Where I felt seen**

- "you leaned into it instead of patting her back twice and moving off." That pat on the back is me, every time.
- "Every cry you saved until the house was empty, then did with the faucet running." That one hurt to read.
- "Years of being the one other people lean on, and leaning on nobody." True, living alone like I do.

**3. Where it lost me**

- **Generic.** "You don't call it a gap. You call it being strong." I've heard that from every reader, every daytime show and every self-help book. Every woman my age who's been hurt gets told her strength is a wall. The free letter said "most women guess wrong", and softness is exactly what most women would guess. So it didn't surprise me the way it should have.
- **Preachy about my strength.** "They're admiring a door you shut." I'm proud of getting through what I got through. Being told my strength is really a wound felt like a small lecture. I bristled.
- **All the money talk.** Bill, price, expensive, charged, "haven't paid a cent", "ran the bill up". After a while it felt like someone had picked a theme and stuck to it.
- **"She was too much."** That's the line everybody's ex says. It's not wrong, but it doesn't feel like it came from mine.
- Same "they don't know they're doing it" vs. "I knew that the whole time" contradiction as A. Same "you owe them no reply" over and over.

**4. The cards**

They were mixed. The Emperor with armor under his robe was good, and I could see it. The Tower with "no door drawn anywhere on it" was the best card in either reading. The Moon with the empty path and the dog and wolf howling with "nothing answers them" was lonely in the right way. The Fool holding a rose, the kneeling ministers and the High Priestess's scroll felt like she was reaching to make the card fit what she'd already decided to say. I skipped a couple.

**5. The letter**

Less convincing than A's. "I made you sorry for it. I didn't always say anything. I didn't have to" is fine, but it's vague. There's no single real moment in it the way A's had the in-front-of-people line. "You weren't weak. I made it expensive" doesn't sound like a man talking. It sounds like a line off a greeting card, or a therapist's. "I'm not stupid" did sound like him. "Come back. Or if you won't, at least write me back" sounded real. It got to me less than A's did.

## Which one I'd rather have received

A, though not by much. B's Card 8 (the faucet running, the birthdays, being the one everyone leans on) is the truest thing in either reading about the life I actually have now. If I could have that page stuck into A, I would. But the free letter promised me I'd guess wrong about what they took, and with B I'd guessed it before I started. Softness, strong, shut door: I've paid for that reading before. A told me something I hadn't thought of. Then it made me remember laughing at my own stories, and I sat there trying to remember the last time I did. That's what a reading is supposed to do. A's letter was also the one I believed: "I liked you smaller" is something I'll still be thinking about next week. Where A loses me is the homework. Telling jokes to a cashier is too small a fix for something that size. B's "tell a friend you missed her, in those words" is better advice for a woman my age.

**Gut on what felt off or not quite human, in both:** They're built the same way, so if I ever got both I'd know they came off a template. Every card ends with "next time, notice..." Evelyn is up all night in both, swears at the deck at three in the morning in both, and stands at a door or window after Card 8 in both. "You owe them no reply" comes up again and again. And she says "they don't know they're doing it" and then hands me a letter where he knows exactly what he did. Both are a bit too tidy. A real reader rambles somewhere and gets something a little wrong. These never do, and the polish is what made me trust Evelyn a notch less, not more.
