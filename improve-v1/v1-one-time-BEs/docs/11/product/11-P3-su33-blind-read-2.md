# Diane's verdict: Reading A vs Reading B

I read the free letter first, then A, then B, on my phone in the evening like I would have anyway. Here's what I thought, plain.

---

## Reading A ("They took your softness")

### 1. Where my attention dropped
- **"I told you it makes no difference to whether they've still got it, ten years or thirty..."** (Card 8). By then I'd just read a whole paragraph of "Every hand someone put on your arm... Every time you said you were fine... Every cry you saved...", and then she kept going about "the bill." I got it the first time. Three goes at "the bill" felt like being sat down and told how much I've wasted. That's when I started skimming.
- It sagged a bit earlier too, in Card 1, at the long "You used to be like her with people..." list. Some of it was lovely, but it went on.

### 2. Where I felt seen
- "you hadn't cried in front of anybody in a very long time, and you were a little proud of yourself for it." That one got me. That's me.
- "You cut a story short at dinner in case it runs on. You say sorry before you ask for anything."
- "Some of the time I was glad of it, because you were easier to be around when you weren't asking me for anything." (from the letter. It hurt, and it sounded true.)

### 3. Where it lost me
- **Piling on.** Card 8 and its "bill" read like a list of everything I've lost. "Years of birthdays where you smiled for the photo and went home and sat in the car." That's a good line, but by then I felt like I was being told my life was a waste. It says it's their fault, but it still feels like a lecture.
- **Evelyn's drama.** Going upstairs to the window, "The lamp needed trimming and I let it smoke," "I cursed my own hands out loud." It started to feel like a performance about her night, not my reading. And who has a lamp that needs trimming?
- **"Softness" over and over.** The word gets used so much it stops meaning anything. And "when you went soft on me" in the letter sounds like an insult, not a kindness.
- **"They went for it on purpose"** (Card 3). That's close to making me into a victim, which I don't care for.
- **"If this person hurt you, you owe them no reply"** shows up three times. It reads like fine print.
- **The last line, "What you do with it is on you."** A little cold for a goodbye. It made me feel like it's my fault now if nothing changes.

### 4. The cards
I read them because they were short. Some helped a lot: the Fool holding the rose loose so anybody could take it, the horse already past the wall on the Sun, the woman's soft hands on the lion. Some felt bent to fit, like the Lovers ("your eyes go somewhere else") and the two ministers with their backs to us. The Emperor with armor under his robe was good.

### 5. The letter
Mostly yes. "I'm not stupid," "I didn't have to," "I saw that. I didn't do anything about it" all sound like a real man who'd never say it out loud. But the big line, **"You weren't weak. I made it expensive,"** doesn't sound like anybody I've ever known. Nobody talks about "making it expensive." It sounds like a writer. It landed in my chest at "you were easier to be around," not at the big line.

## Reading B ("They took your wanting")

### 1. Where my attention dropped
- **"The Empress sits back in an open field, and in front of her a whole field of wheat..."** (Card 2). I didn't follow what uncut wheat had to do with me. Then Justice's scales in Card 4 felt like a school lesson. Both were short, so I didn't lose much.
- The Magician bit in Card 1 ("His arm goes up the way a hand goes up...") was a stretch, too.

### 2. Where I felt seen
- "you say, 'Oh, nothing. I've got everything I need.'" I've said those exact words this year.
- "you're the one who says, 'I'll let you go, I know you're busy'... 'Just my health,' and laugh." Every phone call with my kids, word for word.
- "said 'good for you,' and meant it, and then sat a long while after she'd gone." That one made me put the phone down.

### 3. Where it lost me
- **"They took your wanting."** It's a strange way to say it, and at first I thought, wanting what? It took me a card or two to warm to it. "Softness" hits harder at first.
- **"Each day, before anybody else speaks, say what you want."** I live alone. Most days nobody else speaks. I knew what she meant from the friend example, but it was clumsy.
- **Card 10, "They knew enough, back then, to let it go on, but this reaching for you now happens without them meaning it."** That confused me. Did they know or didn't they?
- **Same fine-print line** ("you owe them no reply"), and the same cold last line, "What you do with it is on you."
- It's less preachy than A. The "what it's cost you" part is one picture (the friend's trip, the "someday" thing) rather than a list, so I didn't feel scolded.

### 4. The cards
These mostly helped. The Fool's bag hanging behind him where anybody could reach in, the Hanged Man with his hands behind his back so he can't take what's offered (I recognized myself), and the sunflowers growing over the wall on their own. That one I'll remember. The Empress wheat and the Magician felt forced. The World at the end was nice and short.

### 5. The letter
Yes. This one could be him. **"I'd make a face, and you'd let it drop. You'd start to say what you wanted and stop halfway, and look at me first."** That's exactly how it went, and that's where I cried. "Most of it was small. I made it big" sounds like a real person finally admitting something. The big line, "You were allowed to want things, and I made that too much to ask," is a bit polished, but it's closer to how people talk than "I made it expensive," and the word "allowed" did something to me.

## Which one I'd rather have received

B, though not by a mile. A has the single sharpest moment, the bit about not crying in front of anybody and being a little proud of it, and I'd probably remember that line longest. But A wore me out. The Tower section kept handing me the bill for my own life, Evelyn's midnight theatrics got bigger than my reading, and the letter's big sentence sounded written, not said. B felt like it knew what a 61-year-old woman living alone actually says out loud: "oh, nothing," "just my health," "I'll let you go, I know you're busy." It didn't scold me. The cost was one quiet picture of a friend's trip, not a list. The letter in B sounded like the man, with the face he'd make and me stopping halfway through a sentence. I'd pay $35 for B and not feel had. With A I'd feel moved, and also a little lectured.

## What felt off or not quite human (gut only)
- Both repeat "you owe them no reply" like a disclaimer. It's kind once, and lawyerly by the third time.
- Both end on "What you do with it is on you." It's cold, and it feels like it hands the blame back to me.
- A's Evelyn is too theatrical (lamp smoking, cursing her hands, going upstairs to the window). It felt like a story about her.
- A's "I made it expensive" doesn't sound like a person talking.
- B's "before anybody else speaks" doesn't fit a woman who lives alone.
- Reading both back to back, whole paragraphs are word for word the same (the opening, the Card 7 start, the oven timer, the ending). On its own I'd never notice, but if a friend showed me hers and it matched, I'd feel like I bought a form letter.
