# Carol's verdict: SU5 round 3

I read the free letter first, then A, then B, on my phone in the evening. I read every word of both.

---

## Reading A: "They took your freedom"

**1. Where my attention dropped**
Card 1: "Here's what used to be in it. Your freedom is the part of you that goes where you like..." She stops reading me and starts defining a word for me. "It has two halves." I don't need a dictionary entry, I need to know if she's right. Then "That's what it costs you, though: the Fool's step." I read it twice and still couldn't tell you what it means. I skimmed until the Emperor.

**2. Where I felt seen**
- "There's a trip you've been meaning to take, and every spring you say next year." (Yes. Damn it.)
- "You turn down an invitation before your friend has finished asking"
- "The page you tore out of a magazine and put in a drawer, and the drawer you haven't opened since."

**3. Where it lost me**
- **About some other woman.** I live alone. I don't ask anybody's permission to go anywhere, because there's nobody to ask. So "goes where you like without asking anybody first" and "do one thing you don't clear with anybody" made me think: clear it with who? My cat? Then I thought, maybe she means something deeper. But I had to do that work for her.
- **Repetitive.** "Your world got small around them" comes up over and over. By card 8 it felt like a chorus.
- **The charity bit (card 7).** "You gave to good causes and to people you'd never meet." Where did that come from? It doesn't connect to freedom at all. It feels pasted in from somebody else's reading. And "the people nearby always seemed to come up short" stings, and not in a good way. I don't remember asking her to grade my friends.
- **A little preachy.** "I'm saying it next to you, not at you." When someone tells you they're not lecturing you, they usually are. Card 10 goes on about permission and waiting on their say-so, and by then I'd got it.
- **"A woman who lit up a room and made them feel like somebody."** That's what they say to everyone.

**4. The cards**
Some of them helped, and those were my favorite parts. The keys lying on the floor with nobody reaching for them, "Nothing was ever locked." The Hanged Man, look at his face, then look at his ankle. Death riding with no scythe, at a walk. The Wheel and the World felt stretched. "No ground drawn under her feet, and she's doing fine" is reaching.

**5. The letter**
Parts of it sounded like a real man. "I liked your life small, with me in the middle of it. It was easier for me." That one hurt, because it could be true. But "I kept the sparkle. The going I let you lose." No man I've known talks like that. That's a writer. "You were always allowed to go, and I made you think you weren't" is a good line, but it's a little long for something someone never said. It landed halfway. I believed some of it, not all.

## Reading B: "They took your softness"

**1. Where my attention dropped**
Card 10: "Now the turn, and the whole reading hangs on it. They were never the ones who had to move..." I'd been with her the whole way, and here she started explaining her own point back to me: signature, permission, the wall. The Sun picture saved it, but that stretch was the first time I wanted to scroll. Card 4 (the High Priestess scroll) was also thin, but it was short so I didn't mind.

**2. Where I felt seen**
- "you leaned into it instead of patting her back twice and moving off" (I do exactly that. Exactly.)
- "You say sorry before you ask for anything. You keep a bad week to one line in a text."
- "Years of birthdays where you smiled for the photo and went home and sat in the car."

And "She was too much." I've been told that. I put the phone down for a minute after that one.

**3. Where it lost me**
- **The same charity paragraph (card 7).** Same problem as A. I'm not sure I'm a woman who gives to strangers, and "the people nearby always seemed to come up short" makes it sound like I'm hard on my friends. The Lovers picture that comes after it was good, though, and it won me back.
- **"Every cry you saved until the house was empty."** My house is always empty. Small thing, but it reminded me she doesn't know my life.
- **Harsh in places.** "On their side there's no place kept for you at all." That's hard to read at 9pm on your own. I think it's honest, and I'd rather have honest. But I felt it in my chest, and some women would close the phone right there.
- **"The lamp needed trimming and I let it smoke."** Who has an oil lamp? That's set dressing. It's the one moment her kitchen felt like a stage.
- **"A woman who lit up a room."** Same flattering line as A.

**4. The cards**
These helped a lot. The Fool holding the rose loose, "Anybody who walked up could lift it out of his fingers." That's how it happened to me. The Emperor with armor under his robe. The Lovers standing close but her eyes going past him. The Tower with no door, "didn't think about how anyone inside was ever going to get out." Strength with soft hands on the lion, "that's what strong was supposed to mean." Every card pulled its weight. I looked at the pictures because she told me what to look for.

**5. The letter**
Yes, mostly. "Some of the time I was glad of it, because you were easier to be around when you weren't asking me for anything." That is a real person being ugly and honest, and it's the line that made me believe the rest. "I'm not stupid." Yes, that's how he'd say it. "You weren't weak. I made it expensive." Short, plain, the thing I never heard. It landed. "It was the best thing about you, and I acted like it was costing me." That one made me cry a little. I wouldn't admit it.

## Which I'd rather have received

B, and it isn't close. A is about a woman whose husband kept her home. I live alone and nobody's stopping me going anywhere, so half of it I had to translate into my own life. B is about what I actually did after it ended: shut the door, called it being strong, and got proud of not crying in front of anybody. B never calls me a victim. It says the strong thing worked and I came by it honestly, and then it shows me the bill. That's the only kind of hard truth I'll take. B's letter also sounds like a man, and A's sounds like a writer doing a man. Neither tried to sell me anything else, which I noticed and appreciated.

## Gut: what felt off or not quite human

- Both readings have the same paragraph about giving to strangers, word for word, and the same "lit up a room" line. If I ever saw both, I'd know it was a template. Even reading just one, the charity part felt like it belonged to someone else.
- Evelyn's long night (pen won't move, they leave at 3am, come back at dawn) is a nice story, but it's the same story in both, beat for beat, down to the time on the clock. Once is atmosphere. If my friend got one too and we compared, I'd feel like a fool.
- "You'd believe it while you said it" shows up in both, twice in A. It's a phrase she leans on.
- A small one: she wrote my birth date as October 30, 1958. I'm 61, so that isn't my birthday. If that's what got typed in, fine. But if a reader gets your birthday wrong, you stop trusting the rest.
