# Sharon's verdict: Reading A vs Reading B

I read the free letter first. It got me. "You know the last message word for word" hit, and so did "by Thursday you've found the fault". That's why I paid. Then I read A on my phone one evening and B the next. Here's how they went.

---

## Reading A ("They took your softness")

**1. Where my attention dropped**
- The opening few paragraphs, "Read the cards in the order I turned them…". I've read this already in the free letter. Just get on with it.
- Card 8, "Every hand someone put on your arm that slid off…". That list goes on and on. Each line is true enough, but it piles up until it feels like being hit with every sad thing at once. Then straight after, Evelyn goes upstairs to the window, lets the lamp smoke, and the presence goes and stands by the door. I skimmed that. It's her story now, not mine.
- Card 9, "I cursed my own hands out loud". It's a strange thing to say, and it took me right out.

**2. Where I felt seen**
- "you hadn't cried in front of anybody in a very long time, and you were a little proud of yourself for it."
- "the line was 'She was too much.'" That one stopped me. I've been called that.
- "You cut a story short at dinner in case it runs on. You say sorry before you ask for anything."

**3. Where it lost me**
- **The letter makes him cruel.** "Some of the time I was glad of it." "I made you sorry for it." Maybe that's somebody's ex, but it isn't quite mine. He wasn't a monster. He just took and didn't give. Reading him as someone who punished me on purpose feels like a different woman's story. It also made me feel a bit worse about myself, as if I'd stayed with a man like that.
- **The "bill" part in Card 8.** "Some of them would have stayed for the real one." "every one of those years went on your bill." She says it's on them, then hands me the invoice anyway. At 61 I don't need it counted up for me. That felt like blame dressed up nicely.
- **Too much "softness."** It's said so many times that after a while the word stopped meaning anything.
- "You weren't weak. I made it expensive." It's a clever line. No man I've known talks like that. It sounds like a writer.
- The last line, "What you do with it is on you", is a bit cold for a goodbye.

**4. The cards**
Mostly they helped. The Fool with the rose held loose ("Anybody who walked up could lift it out of his fingers") was lovely, and that one I understood. The Sun with the horse already past the wall was good too. The Hierophant and the Emperor I half-skipped. They felt stuck on to a point she'd already made.

**5. The letter**
Parts of it, yes. "I kept the sparkle. The rest I made you pay for" made me catch my breath. But overall it reads like the man I'd imagine on a bad night, the monster version, and not the man I actually knew. It landed hard, though not in a way I felt I could keep.

## Reading B ("They took your sense of your own worth")

**1. Where my attention dropped**
- Same opening as A. Same complaint.
- Card 4 (Justice), "Nothing's on them yet, so nothing tips." I had to read the scales part twice and I'm still not sure I followed it. It's too clever by half.
- Card 5 opens with the presence moving to the door before she tells me anything. I wanted the answer, not the stage directions.

**2. Where I felt seen**
- "You drink out of the chipped mug and put the good ones out for company." That's me. That's actually me.
- "told everybody not to make a fuss over your birthday, and then been a little hurt when nobody did."
- "When somebody does something thoughtful for you out of the blue, it throws you, and you thank them twice."

**3. Where it lost me**
- **"Your sense of your own worth."** My first reaction was ugh. That's the most worn-out phrase on the self-help shelf, and every reader says it. The examples saved it, but the headline nearly lost me before I got to them.
- "I knew what I was paying." I don't know what that means. Paying what? He didn't pay anything, which was the whole point. It's supposed to be the big sentence and it confused me.
- The Wheel of Fortune bit ("going around on its own") was vague, though the paragraph after it was good.
- A few lines, like "a woman who lit up a room", are the same as A, so I suspect it's a bit of a template.

**4. The cards**
Better than A on the whole. The Magician with everything laid out on the table ("Anybody who walked up could help themselves"), the crown knocked off the top of the Tower and never put back, the sunflowers growing over the wall without asking, and the whole wreath on Strength, "Not one flower tucked behind her ear." I read every card description in B. The Justice one I skipped.

**5. The letter**
This one I could believe. "I'm not much good at writing things like this." "It suited me, so I let it stay that way." "I just never said why you'd quit asking." That's how a man like him would talk if he ever owned up: plain, a bit lazy, a bit ashamed. He isn't a villain, just selfish, and that's closer to the truth for me. It stung in a way I could live with, except for the "knew what I was paying" line in the middle.

## Which I'd rather have received

B, though not by a mile. A hits harder in places. "She was too much" got me more than anything in B. But A makes the man cruel and then tallies up everything my life cost me, and by the end I felt worse about myself, not lighter. B is about smaller, everyday things, like the chipped mug, the tags left on, and thanking people twice, and those are the parts of my life I'd never have said out loud but recognise straight away. Its letter sounds like a real, ordinary, selfish man, not a monster from a film. B also gave me something to do that I could actually try ("leave off the 'only if it's no trouble'"). I'd feel a bit braver after B. After A I'd feel a bit sadder.

## What felt off (gut only)
- In both, Evelyn's night in the kitchen is choreographed too neatly. The presence moves to the shoulder, then the door, then leaves at exactly 3am, then sits in the chair at dawn, card after card. One or two of those moments would be eerie. A dozen feels staged.
- Both letters talk like Evelyn. They use the same rhythm and the same short sentences, and both say "I kept the sparkle." A real man wouldn't say "sparkle."
- So many lines are identical in the two readings that I'd wonder, if I ever saw a friend's, whether hers says the same thing. That's my old fear about psychics, and it'd come right back.
- "It only comes back whole" and "the next time someone reaches for you, there's someone there" are lifted straight from the free letter. I noticed, and I didn't like noticing.
