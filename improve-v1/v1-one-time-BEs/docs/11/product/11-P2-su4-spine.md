## Soul Urge 4 — a sense of being safe

**The one idea:** being safe is being able to go *off duty*: stop watching the door, the mood, the phone,
because she trusts that if something were wrong she'd know, and her knowing would count. It runs two
ways: trusting the place she's in, and trusting her own read of it. They took both. They made every day
a watch, and when she felt something was wrong they told her it wasn't real. With the second gone she
can't trust the alarm, so she can never switch it off. She calls that "alert". It comes back when she
reads that she was right all along; an alarm she can trust is one she can let rest. The seven days give
it ten minutes of rest at a time.

**The row (11-acts.json → soul_urge → "4", used as written):** took = your sense of being safe · cover
word = alert · regret = making her days something to get through · unsaid = "You were right to feel
what you felt. It was never in your head." · do = ten minutes a day off duty, somewhere she's safe,
phone away · ⚠ safety line once, plain voice: if she isn't safe where she is, that comes first; tell
someone who can help (put in card 12, beside the practice that already says "somewhere she's safe").

**Card 5's one excuse (written into the report):** the line was "She's imagining things." It fits this
row exactly: it is the lie the unsaid sentence takes back. The letter echoes it: "I told myself you
were imagining things."
