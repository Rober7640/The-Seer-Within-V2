# Marsha's verdict: Reading A vs Reading B

I read the free letter first, on my phone, after supper. It got me. "You say you're over it. Then how do you still know the last message word for word?" That one sat with me, so I paid. Then I read both of these all the way through.

---

## Reading A ("They took your trust")

**1. Where my attention dropped**

- *"You let other people drive, book the table, read the map and settle the check..."* (Card 1). Up to there it was fine. Then the list just kept going: six o'clock, the secret, the money, the sorry, the friend who's fine, the present, the map, the kind message. I got the point three items in. After that it felt like she was trying everything to see what stuck.
- *"On Justice, a figure sits between two pillars in a long red robe. One foot shows under the hem."* I skimmed here. Justice is the one with the sword and the scales, and she's telling me about a foot. It felt like she was stretching the card to fit what she wanted to say.

**2. Where I felt seen**

- "Every promise after that came with a question mark on it."
- "Nobody gets anything past you. Nobody gets the chance to come through for you either." That one stung, in a good way.
- "She believed what she wanted to believe." I have heard that exact line said about me. Not those words, but that line.

**3. Where it lost me**

- **Repetitive.** "Careful" over and over. Careful, careful, careful. By Card 8 I was tired of the word.
- **Confusing.** "They kept the use of you, the part that showed up every single time." What does that mean? And on the Hanged Man, "That line is where they've kept their hands all this time." I read it twice and still didn't follow.
- **Assumes a lot.** The whole thing rests on him lying to me and letting me go on believing it. Some of that fits me. But if he hadn't lied, the whole reading would fall apart, and it never hedges on that.
- **Too much Evelyn.** The black tea, the shifted chair, swearing at the deck, three in the morning. A little of that is nice. It got to be a lot.
- **A little preachy** in Card 8: "Years of sitting where you could see the door." I know, dear. I know what it cost me. I didn't need it itemized like a bill.

**4. The cards**

They mostly helped. The Moon was the best one: the tame dog and the wild wolf making the same noise, so careful treats them both as the wolf. That's exactly it. The Tower, "you'd grab the first hand you saw," was good too. Strength with the chain of flowers, after the Devil's chains in the first letter, was clever, and I liked it. Justice and the Hanged Man felt forced, and I skimmed them.

**5. The letter**

Partly. "You believed me, and I let you" is something he could say if he were ever honest, which he wasn't. "I could have set it straight any time. I didn't." Yes. But "Your believing me, I just used up" doesn't sound like a person, and "Something upsets you and you go make yourself useful to somebody" sounds like Evelyn, not him. He never noticed a thing like that about me. "I was the one who wasn't true" is a bit old-fashioned and stiff. It landed about halfway. The ending, "Come back to me," and then Evelyn saying "It's a bill, and nobody owes it," was the best part. That I'll keep.

## Reading B ("They took your softness")

**1. Where my attention dropped**

- *"Two ministers in white kneel on the Hierophant card..."* (Card 6). It was short and thin, and I didn't really get what the ministers had to do with me. I sped up.
- *"I went up to the upstairs window, because I could see what this card was doing..."* (Card 8). Here's Evelyn pacing her house and trimming a lamp at midnight. I wanted to get back to me. The lamp that "needed trimming" made me wonder what century her kitchen is in.

**2. Where I felt seen**

- "You just noticed one day that you hadn't cried in front of anybody... and you were a little proud of yourself for it." I put the phone down after that one.
- "You cut a story short at dinner in case it runs on. You say sorry before you ask for anything."
- "Every cry you saved until the house was empty, then did with the faucet running."

**3. Where it lost me**

- **A bit heavy.** "Years of birthdays where you smiled for the photo and went home and sat in the car." It's true, but there was a lot of it in a row in Card 8. For a minute it felt like being told how sad my life is.
- **Close to the line.** "She was too much," "needy," crying being "a scene." That's what was said to me. But the reading also tells me I shut the door on everybody. For a second I bristled: so now I'm closed off too? It pulled back in time ("That part is theirs, every bit of it"), so it stayed on the right side. Just.
- **Evelyn's theater.** Mint tea, the upstairs window, the smoking lamp, "swore at my own hands." Same as A, and a touch more of it.
- "Your softness" is a soft word. When I first read the name I thought, oh, that's a bit greeting-card. By Card 3 I'd stopped minding.

**4. The cards**

These helped more than in A. The Emperor with armor under his robe ("He's dressed for a court and he's armed for a fight"). The Tower with no door ("didn't think about how anyone inside was ever going to get out"). The Lovers with her eyes going past him. The Sun with the horse already past the wall, and nobody opening a gate. Each picture said the thing before she explained it. I only skipped the Hierophant.

**5. The letter**

This one could be him. "Some of the time I was glad of it, because you were easier to be around when you weren't asking me for anything." That's cruel and it's honest, and it's exactly the kind of thing he'd never say out loud and absolutely thought. "I'm not stupid" at the end of a paragraph sounds like a real man being defensive. "If you needed me, you said so, right out. I kept the steady part. The rest I made you pay for." Yes. Two lines didn't sound like him. "I made it expensive" is a little too neat, more a writer's line than his. And the same "make yourself useful to somebody" line as in A sounds like the reader talking. But it landed. I'd read it again.

## Which one I'd rather have received

B. A was clear and sensible, and the advice was good. Letting someone carry the groceries in without checking is the kind of thing I'd actually try. But A kept telling me the same thing, that I'm careful, and some of the cards felt bent to fit. B got under my skin in a way A didn't. The crying in the car, the faucet running, saying sorry before I ask for anything. Those weren't flattering, and they weren't vague. They were small things only somebody watching would know, and that's what I paid for. B's letter also sounded more like a real, proud, slightly mean man than A's, which made it hit harder when he said I wasn't weak. If B had cut half of Evelyn's midnight walking around and trimmed the long "bill" list in Card 8, I'd give it a 9.

**Gut check, what felt off or not quite human:** In both, the late-night Evelyn story is a little too staged. The man at her shoulder moves around the kitchen card by card, she swears at the deck at three a.m., and the window goes grey right as the letter starts. It's nice once. Across twelve cards it starts to feel like a show put on for me. Both letters also have one paragraph where the ex suddenly talks like a therapist ("Something upsets you and you go make yourself useful to somebody"). He never once noticed that about me, and a reader would. And honestly, both readings felt like they had the same bones: same openings, same "here's the deal," same "the next time someone...", same ending. If my friend bought one too and we compared, I'd worry we'd find the same sentences.
