# Testina's verdict: Reading A vs Reading B

I read the free letter first, then A in one go on the couch, then B the next evening. First thing I'll say: these two are nearly the same reading. Same opening, same softness, same twelve cards in the same order, same letter at the end. I had to read B slowly to find what was different. The differences are small, but they're in the places that matter to me.

---

## Reading A

### 1. Where my attention dropped
**"Getting on for midnight. When I turned this card, they were standing over by the door..."** (end of Card 5).
By then I'd had Evelyn's kitchen four or five times: the shoulder, the pen, the blank sheet, the lamp. I don't need a weather report on the ghost after every card. I started skimming her bits and reading only the parts about me. I came back properly at Card 8 ("Strong worked, Testina").

Second dip: **"Left alone, an urge climbs, peaks and dies down."** Card 12 turns into a pamphlet from a counselor's office. It's fine advice. It's just not a reading anymore.

### 2. Where I felt seen
- "you hadn't cried in front of anybody in a very long time, and you were a little proud of yourself for it."
- "Years of birthdays where you smiled for the photo and went home and sat in the car."
- "They didn't stop caring. You taught them to stop asking."

That last one I read three times. My daughter stopped asking a while back. I told myself she got busy.

### 3. Where it lost me
- **"a woman who could take charge and get things done."** That's not me, not really. I work part-time. I was the one who kept the peace. This felt like it was about some other woman, a manager type.
- **"One word doesn't make a letter."** What one word? My name? I read it twice and still don't know what she meant.
- **The softness list in Card 1** (crying at movies, leaning into a hug, taking compliments). True enough, but it's true of most women. It's a little like a horoscope there.
- **Card 7, "Some of what's happened since is yours."** I braced at this. But she said "that was fair, you'd been hurt" and "that's the part you can change," so it didn't feel like a slap. I could take it.
- **"a room made of paper"** (Card 10). Lost me. Too clever.
- **Card 8, "Leaving the door off the tower made sense with them."** I liked this. She's saying I wasn't stupid to do it, only that I kept it up too long. That I can hear.
- **Evelyn's all-night drama** (going upstairs to the window, swearing at her hands, three in the morning). Once is moving. Five times and it starts to feel like she wants credit for how hard my $35 was on her.

### 4. The cards
There were no pictures, only her describing them. But she named them: the Empress, the Emperor, the Fool, the Tower, the Moon. I've had readings before, so I know those names, and it made it feel like a real spread was on a real table. The descriptions were good. The Lovers one (her looking past him) and the Tower with no door stuck with me. The Sun with the horse already past the wall was the best one. I didn't skip any of them.

### 5. The letter at the end
Some of it landed hard. "Some of the time I was glad of it, because you were easier to be around when you weren't asking me for anything." That one hurt in the right way. That is a thing that person could have thought.

But mostly it sounds like Evelyn wrote it, not them. It uses her exact words: "take charge and get things done," "going soft costs you," "made it expensive." The person I'm thinking of never talked like that. They'd never say "I'm not good at this and I'm not going to get better at it." It's too tidy and too self-aware. And it never says a single thing only the two of us would know. No place, no habit, no nickname. So it moved me, but I didn't believe it was them.

"You clam up, you chew on it, and you won't move till you're sure." That part did sound like somebody who knew me. That's how a person talks.

## Reading B

### 1. Where my attention dropped
Same spot, the Card 5 ending: **"When I'd asked my question out loud, the fourth card answered it, and they didn't like the answer."** This was a bit longer than A's and it was all about Evelyn and the ghost walking to the door. I skimmed.

And again at Card 12: **"Here's why the timer works."** Longer than A, more explaining. I felt like I was getting a handout.

### 2. Where I felt seen
Same three as A, same words:
- "you hadn't cried in front of anybody in a very long time, and you were a little proud of yourself for it."
- "Years of birthdays where you smiled for the photo and went home and sat in the car."
- "They didn't stop caring. You taught them to stop asking."

### 3. Where it lost me
- **"Now your half, and you won't like it."** (Card 7.) My back went up right there. Don't tell me what I will and won't like. I paid for this. In A she said it more gently and I listened. Here I was already arguing with her before I got to the next line.
- **"But the door you left off the tower, that was you."** (Card 8.) Flat blame. In A she said it made sense back then. Here it's just "that was you." I know it was me. I don't need it pointed at me like a finger.
- **"Go back up and read that line again, and catch the small lift it gave you, the warm drop low in your stomach."** (Card 9.) This one was the worst. It's telling me what my own stomach did. It didn't do that. I felt a little sick, if anything. When somebody tells me what I felt and gets it wrong, the whole thing turns fake for me. It felt pushy, and a bit creepy.
- **"it's the lowest point in this whole reading"** and **"That's how low this goes."** Calling me low for still having a soft spot for someone. That's shaming. A said "don't be ashamed of it." B does the opposite.
- **"you went quiet, went into it, and wanted proof before you'd take a step."** "Went into it" doesn't mean anything to me. It sounds like a personality test. A's "clammed up, chewed on it" sounded like a person.
- **"someone who could run things and make them happen."** Same problem as A, that's not really me. Somehow even vaguer here.
- **"The woman on this card... The man on this card... The young man on this card..."** Every card is "the woman on this card." It got repetitive, and it made me wonder why she wouldn't name them. Like she was hiding something.
- **Same "One word doesn't make a letter"** confusion, and same "room made of paper."

What B did better: **"Fold laundry. Wash a pan."** That's real. I'd actually do that. And the Temperance card made more sense in B; she explained the water.

### 4. The cards
Without the names, they felt more like pictures in a magazine than a tarot spread. The descriptions are the same good ones as A, and I still liked the Sun and the Tower. But I missed knowing which card it was. When you've had readings before, the name is half of it.

### 5. The letter at the end
Same letter as A, almost word for word. Same strong line about being easier to be around. Same problem: it sounds like Evelyn, not them, and it has nothing in it only they'd know. The one change is "You go quiet, you go into it, and you want proof before you'll take a step," and that line sounds less like a real person than A's "clam up, chew on it."

## Which one would I rather have received?

A. They're the same reading wearing two different coats, and what tips it is how she talks to me when she gets to my part of the blame. In A she tells me the hard thing and still stands next to me: it "made sense with them," "don't be ashamed of it," "that's the part you can change." I could take that, and I took it. In B the same moments come out as "you won't like it," "that was you," and "the lowest point," and then she tells me my stomach gave a warm little drop when I read that they want me back. It didn't, and the moment she told me what I felt, I stopped trusting her. I bristle at being lectured, and B lectures. A also names the cards, which made it feel like a real spread on a real table. The only thing I'd steal from B is the laundry-and-pans bit in the last card, and the clearer Temperance explanation.

## What made either one feel off or not quite human

- **The letter doesn't sound like them.** It sounds like Evelyn wearing their coat. It repeats her phrases word for word, it's far too self-aware for the kind of person who did this, and there isn't one private detail in it. A real letter would have something only the two of us knew. Without it, I believed the feeling but not the writer.
- **It never says "he" or "she."** Not once in the letter or the reading. I noticed. It felt careful in a way a real person isn't.
- **The overnight theater.** The ghost moves around the kitchen after nearly every card, the sheet stays blank until dawn, she goes up to the window, she swears at her hands. It's built like a story with a clock on it. Once or twice, lovely. Every card, and it feels staged.
- **"take charge and get things done."** It decided what kind of woman I am and got it wrong, then put the same line in the ex's letter, like the ex was reading off the same notes.
- **The last card turns into a self-help sheet.** Timers, seven days, urges peaking. Good advice, but it reads like a different writer took over.
- **Both are nearly identical**, which I wouldn't know as a customer, but reading both, the sameness made me notice how much of it is a pattern that would fit lots of women: the softness list, the crying in the car, finding the fault by Thursday.
