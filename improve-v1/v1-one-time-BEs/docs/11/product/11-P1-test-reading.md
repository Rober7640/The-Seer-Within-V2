# The Unsent Letter

*Written out for Diane Ruth Marshall*

Diane,

Sit down for this, somewhere nobody's going to walk in on you.

I'm going to tell you what happened at my table, in the order it happened, because the order is the whole thing. Twelve cards went down one after another and each one changed what came next. A few of them will look as if they're arguing with each other. Keep going. Read every card in the order I turned it, and don't skip ahead to the letter. It only comes back whole, and if you jump to the end you'll get some words on a page and nothing else.

Two things first, so they're said. This is someone living. And nothing in these pages asks you to contact them, wait for them or forgive them. If this person hurt you, you owe them no reply.

The night your order came in, I cleared the table and took three cards out of the deck: the Hermit, Judgement and the Devil. They've said what they came to say, so they went on the windowsill. That left nineteen. I took a clean sheet and wrote your full name at the top, Diane Ruth Marshall, and the second of September 1967 under it. Your birth name and date set how I lay your twelve cards, so they go down before anything else does.

Then I made a pot of tea and sat.

The one who came to me the first time was at my shoulder before I'd finished pouring. No face and no voice, same as before. I didn't say hello. I turned the first card.

---

## What they took

### The first card: the Fool

**They took your trust, Diane.**

You've felt the gap for a long time and called it other things. You'll have guessed your confidence, or your youth, or your best years, and I told you most women guess wrong. It's your trust.

A young man's at the very edge of a cliff with one foot about to go over. He's got a bag on a stick across his shoulder and a white flower in his other hand, and his chin's tipped up at the sky. He isn't looking at the drop. A little white dog's jumping at his heels and he isn't looking at that either. He's got the easiest face in the whole deck.

People see that card and think it means stupid. It shows someone putting a foot down without checking what's under it, because it hasn't crossed his mind the ground won't be there.

You used to walk like that. I don't mean you were young, or silly. You took people at their word. Someone said they'd be there and you stopped thinking about it. You told them things, then you went to sleep. I said you had this before them and you haven't had it since, and this is what I meant. A ring wouldn't still be hurting.

When the Fool went down, the one at my shoulder came in closer. I felt it on the back of my neck. They know what they're holding.

I picked up my pen, looked at your name, and put the pen down again. I had one word. A letter takes more than that.

Tonight, when you're on your own, say it out loud once: they took my trust. You've only ever used the polite versions. I want you to hear the real one in your own voice.

### The second card: the Chariot

You've gone without it for so long, Diane, that you've given the gap a nicer name. You call it being careful.

A man in armour stands up in a square stone cart with a roof of stars over him and a walled town at his back. Two sphinxes lie in front of him, a black one and a white one. He looks like a winner coming home. Give it a minute. He's got no reins in his hands. The sphinxes aren't pulling anything, they're lying down. What you're looking at is a man stood in a stone box with his armour on, and everybody else is back in the town.

Here's the proof of how you've lived without it.

You read a text three times for the tone before you'll answer it.

Someone says "I'll call you" and you decide on the spot that they won't, so it can't catch you out when they don't.

You do the job yourself rather than hand it over, and you tell people it's quicker.

You keep one thing back from everybody. Even the ones who love you have only ever had most of you.

When something's going well, you're already looking past it for the catch.

And when anyone asks how you are, you say you're fine.

People tell you how capable you are. You've had a lot of that, and it's true, and it's no use to you. Look at those sphinxes again. Nobody in that box is going anywhere.

This week, stop saying "fine". When someone asks, say anything else that's true. "Not bad" will do. "Not really" is better. You don't have to tell them your life. I just want you to say one true thing out loud to another person and see that nothing happens.

### The third card: the Empress

You've spent a long time asking how you could've been so stupid, and you've got it backwards, Diane. They went for your trust because of what they saw in you, and they knew exactly what they were looking at.

A woman sits on a heap of cushions in a long dress printed with fruit, twelve stars round her head. Wheat's growing right up to her feet, ripe and ready to cut. Behind her there's a stand of tall trees and a stream coming down through them. Everything on this card has been grown and kept going. Nobody gets a field like that in a week.

They saw someone who stays. You do what you say you'll do. You turn up on the bad days as well as the good ones, you keep things running, and you've kept your word when it cost you. You took it for granted that other people did the same, because people who keep their word believe other people's. So when you gave your trust you gave it the way you do everything. Properly, and all of it.

They'd never been handed anything like that, and they knew what it was worth. They leaned on it and it held, so they leaned harder.

You believed someone. Everything after that is on them, every bit.

So stop asking what was wrong with you. Nothing was, dear. Every time you ask, you go looking for a fault that was never yours, and you've done enough looking.

Three cards down. The one at my shoulder had gone very still. I had what they took, how you live without it, and what they saw. But nobody hangs on to something all this time for no reason. So I did something I don't normally do. I said it out loud to the empty kitchen: *why have you still got it?*

And I laid the fourth.

---

## Why they kept it

### The fourth card: Justice

They've kept it because giving it back means saying what they did.

A figure in a red robe sits between two stone pillars with a crown on, looking straight at you. The right hand holds a sword dead upright. The left holds a pair of scales, and the scales are level. Nothing in the picture is moving. It's the stillest card I own and it doesn't look away.

While they've got your trust, Diane, they're still someone who was believed. Think what that's worth to them. There's one person on earth who took them at their word, all the way down, and as long as nobody says otherwise, that still stands. Give it back and the scales come level. They'd be the person who did that to you, with nothing left to stand behind. So they've held on.

I told you they hadn't lost it or thrown it away. Now you know why. They can't afford to.

I'd asked and I'd been answered, and they didn't like it. The weight went off my shoulder. For the first time that night they moved, back across the kitchen as far as the door, and they stopped there. They didn't leave. They just didn't want to be anywhere near that card.

You've got a conversation you go over, the one where they finally say it. You've had it in the car and lying awake and you've got your side word perfect. Stop rehearsing it. You keep going over a scene that needs them to do the very thing they've held on to your trust to avoid.

### The fifth card: the Hanged Man

It's taken this long because they had a sentence that let them off. *She'll be fine.*

A man hangs upside down from a living tree, tied by one ankle. His other leg's crossed behind his knee and his hands are behind his back. What gets people about this card is his face. He's calm. There's a ring of light round his head, he isn't struggling, and there's nobody standing by who put him up there. He's been there so long he's got used to it.

That's them. They've been stuck with this since it ended, and they got used to it, and one line made that bearable. She's strong. She's the sort who's fine. She'll get over it. They needed that to be true, Diane, because if you were fine then nothing much had happened.

You've backed that story up. You didn't mean to, and it's got nothing to do with why they did what they did. But every time you told someone you were over it, every time you looked well across a room, you were saying the same line they were.

Now the months I told you about. A sentence like that wears out. It stopped holding, and they started going over it, and that's when this began reaching for you, without them knowing a thing about it. The months are what it looks like when an excuse runs out.

It was getting on for midnight and they were still at the door. I made a fresh pot, mostly to have something hot in my hands.

You don't owe anybody a good impression of how well you've done. Next time this comes up, don't tell anyone you're long past all that. Say nothing. Let it sit there.

### The sixth card: Temperance

What they regret is letting you go on believing them. They don't regret losing you. You've been writing them a different regret.

An angel with red wings stands with one foot on the grass and one foot in a pool, pouring water from one cup into another. Not a drop's going on the ground. Behind the angel a long path goes up to a light in the hills, and the angel isn't looking at it. All that's happening on this card is water going from this cup to that cup and back again.

That's regret when it's left alone. It goes round and it never arrives. There was a stretch, before it ended, when what they were telling you had stopped being true. They knew and you didn't. They could see you believing them and they let it run, because that was easier than watching your face change. They go back to that again and again. They can't put down the knowing, and doing nothing about it.

It was heavy. I told you it was heavy, and I felt it from across the kitchen, come to rest on the table like a wet coat. It pays for nothing, Diane. They've had months of feeling bad and you haven't had one thing out of it.

Here's what you'll do with this, so catch yourself doing it. You'll read "they regret it" and something in you will sit up. You'll take it to bed with you. Put it down before you get that far. It's their water, going round in their cups, and not one drop has reached you. Don't read it as a kindness.

I finished the pot. Six cards down, gone midnight, and still nothing on your sheet but your name. That was all the half about them. I looked at the deck for a while. Then I went on.

---

## What it has cost you

### The seventh card: the Moon

Now your half, Diane. They took it once. Since then you've been going through every decent person who came near you, looking for proof you shouldn't let them.

A big pale moon with a face in it looks down. Under it a dog and a wolf stand with their heads back, howling. A crayfish is crawling up out of a pool in front of them. A path runs from the water up between two grey towers and away, and there's nobody on it. Look at that dog. I'd swear it's the little white dog off the first card, the one jumping at the Fool's heels. So that's where he ended up. Awake all night, howling at something that doesn't answer.

When you're hurt, you go quiet and you go into it. You've done it with them. Late, with the house still, back through the old messages, looking for the exact place it turned, the one that'd explain it. You want to know for certain. You've never found it, because if you had, you'd have stopped.

Then someone decent turns up and you do the same thing to them. You go quiet. You say you're tired, you say you're busy. You watch. You want proof before you'll take one step, and nobody can give you that. There's no proof of a person in advance. So you find a fault instead. Too keen, or the wrong shoes, and by Thursday it's over, and you call that standards.

If someone's beside you now, this is about them as well. They've been stood outside that stone box all this time, and they get what's left.

None of those people did anything to you. They're the ones who've been paying for it.

Get a bit of paper. Write down the last decent person you found a fault with, and write the fault next to their name. You're not calling anybody. You're just going to look at the size of it. Keep the paper.

### The eighth card: the Tower

The careful worked, Diane. Nobody's done that to you again. Nobody's done anything to you at all.

A tall grey tower stands on a rock under a black sky. Lightning has hit the top and knocked the crown off it. There's fire in the three windows, and two people are falling head first with their arms out, flames dropping round them.

I got up from the table. I don't do that. I went to the back door, opened it and stood in the cold in my slippers, because I could see what this card was doing here and I needed a minute before I looked at it again.

You'd take it for the day it ended, and it is. You've been through that and you came out the other side. That's not why I got up. I got up because of the tower. Look at it. Three windows and no door. There's no way in anywhere on it. That's what you did afterwards. You took every stone of what came down and you put it back up with no door, so nobody could do that to you twice, and then you went and lived at the top. That's careful, seen from outside.

It's cost you more than they did. They took one thing, once, and that's on them, and I'd say so to their face. But the bill's been running every day since and you're the one who's been paying it. However long it's been, that's how long you've taken bad news with nobody let all the way in. Add every person who'd have been across the table from you if they'd got past Thursday.

Here's what I went to the door with. I can get you your trust back. I can't get you those years. Nobody can, not them and not me, and there's no card in any deck that does it. They're gone, dear, and I won't dress that up.

I stood out there till I couldn't feel my feet. Then I came in and shut the door.

Take that bit of paper. Under the fault, write how long it's been. Do that sum once, on paper, in daylight, and look at it. Then leave it. You don't get to do it at night any more.

### The ninth card: the Lovers

**They're giving it back, Diane. They aren't trying to get you back.**

There's your flat answer to the fork. It isn't the one you wanted.

I sat down, turned the ninth, and when I saw what it was I said a word I won't write here.

A man and a woman with nothing on stand in a garden. Over them a great angel comes out of a cloud with its arms spread. There's a tree behind each of them, one with fruit and a snake round the trunk, one with flames for leaves. For about a second I thought this was the card you've been waiting for. Then I looked at them properly. They aren't touching. There's a good stretch of bare ground between them. He's looking at her. She's looking up past him at the angel. Neither of them is taking a step.

If they wanted you back, they'd be holding your trust out and waiting for you to come and get it, and I'd have seen the reach on this card. It isn't there. They want to put it down. That's all the message is. They're tired of carrying it and they want it out of their hands, and that's for their own sake. They aren't doing it out of love, and they haven't been waiting for you. They aren't coming back, and there's no day ahead when they turn up and it all gets explained and they stay.

You've told people you gave up on that day long ago. It's still there, right at the back, under the anger. A small place you kept, just in case. Some of the rereading was for that. I told you they were calling you, and they are. They're calling you to take this off them. If I'd drawn the other answer you could at least have told yourself you were wanted, and I didn't draw it.

When I looked up, the door was empty.

You know how you can tell someone's left a room. All night they'd been at my shoulder or across the kitchen, and now there was nobody. Nine cards down, getting on for three in the morning. Your name at the top of a sheet of paper and nothing under it. Not one word of your letter. I've told you it only comes back whole, and there I was with three cards to go, and whoever was carrying it had walked out.

I didn't know if I'd get it. That's the truth. I sat there and I had nothing for you, Diane.

Next time you hear yourself think "you never know", stop. You do know. I've just told you.

## How it comes back

### The tenth card: Death

**They won't do it, Diane. No call, no message, not one line. Awake, with their own name on it, they're never going to send this.**

Before you look at this card: nobody's died. This is someone living, same as I said at the start. It's got the worst name in the deck, so let me show you what's actually on it.

A skeleton in black armour rides a white horse, carrying a black flag with a white rose on it. A king's flat on his back under the hooves with his crown off. A bishop in gold stands in the road with his hands together, begging. A young woman has turned her face away, and a child's kneeling, looking up. The horse isn't stopping for any of them. And right at the back of the card, small, the sun's coming up between two towers.

I knew those towers. They're the two grey towers from the Moon. That path with nobody on it comes out here.

What's over is the waiting. That's what's riding that horse. The king can't stop it, and neither can the bishop, however nicely he asks. They won't act because they can't stand to be looked at by the one person who believed them. And they don't even know they've been doing this. What reached me came the way you talk in your sleep. Wake them and ask, and they'd look you in the eye and tell you they never think of you. So don't go asking.

I looked up from that card and the window had gone from black to grey. Then I looked at what I'd been doing. I'd spent the whole night pulling at them. I'd demanded to know why they kept it. I'd watched them to the door. And for the last hour I'd sat staring at a blank sheet, waiting for them to come back and give me the first line. You've been doing that, Diane, for a lot longer than one night, and I'd got exactly what you've got for it.

So I stopped. I said to the empty kitchen, "Right. They're not coming. It's hers whether they come or not." I picked up the pen to write that down for you, because it was all I had.

The chair across from me wasn't empty.

Not at my shoulder this time, and not at the door. Across the table, where you'd sit. Still no face. But the weight had gone out of it, the way it goes out of someone who's finally let their breath go, and my pen was moving. I looked down and the first line of your letter was on the sheet under your name.

This is how it works. It never needed them to act. It needed someone to stop waiting on them. Your trust has been trying to leave their hand for months, and it couldn't get across while everyone in this was waiting for somebody else to move. So no contact's needed, and it comes back when you read it. They don't have to do a thing, and they won't.

"What if they do?" You'll hear that start up in your head. When it does, finish it out loud: they won't. Then get on with whatever you were doing.

### The eleventh card: the Star

What was left unsaid comes down to one sentence, Diane. *You were right to believe me. I was the one who wasn't true.*

And you don't answer it. Not in writing, and not in your head either. You don't need to. By the time you've read it, it's already back.

A woman with nothing on kneels at the edge of a pool with one foot on the water. She's got a jug in each hand and she's emptying both of them, one into the pool and one onto the grass, where it runs off in little streams. One huge star hangs over her with seven small ones round it, and a bird's sitting in a tree behind.

It's the same water. On the sixth card the angel kept it going from cup to cup and never lost a drop. Here it's going out, both jugs, and she isn't keeping any of it.

The same thing was happening at my table. Once the first line was down the rest came fast, and that sentence came ahead of any sorry. You've had plenty of sorry. You've written it for them yourself.

You never got that answer, so you've been after it in those old messages, late at night, hunting for the place you got it wrong. There isn't one. They've known that all along and they've never said it.

You'll want to reply. Anyone would. There's nothing to reply to, because nothing's been sent. If this person hurt you, you owe them no reply, not one word, not now, not ever. You don't forgive them. You don't have to think well of them. I'm saying it again here because you're about to read their words.

And you've been writing their half a long time, Diane. Some nights they were sorry. Some nights they were a monster. Either way it was you holding the pen. You're off that job now. The next time a line starts up in your head in their voice, say "that's been said" and go and do something else.

### The twelfth card: Strength

What you've got to stop is going back. The one thing you've got to do is let someone do something for you and not check it. You'll get through what's coming on those two, Diane, and there isn't a third.

A woman in a white dress with a belt of flowers has her bare hands on a big red lion. She's easing its mouth shut. There's no chain on it and no sword anywhere near. She isn't even frowning. Its tail's gone down between its legs, and over her head there's a figure of eight lying on its side.

That lion's the pull. I showed you the chain in my first letter and told you it lifts off. This is what it looks like once it has. You've still got a lion. You've also got your hands on it.

I told you the pull's strongest when the message gets close, and that it shows itself inside five to seven days. That means now, this week. It says nothing about them turning up. You'll have their name in your search bar. It'll feel like a sign. It'll be the lion.

**Stop going back.** The rereading, looking them up, starting a message to clear the air: one thing with three names. Your trust is back when you read that letter and the need to go looking goes with it, but your thumb won't know that yet. So when the pull comes, you put your phone in another room, you set the oven timer for twenty minutes, and you get your hands in something. The dishes will do. You don't argue with it. A pull rises and falls by itself, like anything else in a body, and if nobody feeds it, it's mostly over inside twenty minutes. All this time you've gone and looked right at the top of it. That taught it that it works, so it keeps coming. Every one you let go past makes the next one smaller. You'll do this more than once.

**The one thing to do.** Seven days, one a day. You let one person do one small thing for you, and you don't check it.

Let someone else book the table. Ask a neighbour to take in a parcel. Hand over a job you'd normally do yourself. Pick people who've never given you cause. You don't call to confirm, and you don't go behind them and do it again. Nobody from your past comes into this, least of all them.

Here's why it works. Nobody thinks their way back to trusting, and reading won't do it, not even this. You've got one big example in your head and you've gone over it so often that your head has nothing else to go on. Trust gets built out of small things that came good. Someone says they'll come and they come, a few thousand times over. Yours is back in your hand now and it wants using. Seven days gives your head seven new things to go on. And if somebody drops one, good. It's a parcel. You'll find out you're still standing.

It won't feel like much, dear. You'll know it's working by what stops: you'll get to the end of a day and realise you never looked. How soon that comes is down to the twenty minutes and the seven days, so it's down to you. It's got nothing to do with me, or them.

And that word of yours, careful. Keep it for stairs and ladders. Take it off people.

Then the next time someone reaches for you, there's someone there.

It was full light when I laid that card down. I took the Hermit, Judgement and the Devil off the windowsill and put them back in the deck. Then I wrote out the letter at the same table, with them sat across from me till the last line. I've changed nothing. I don't get a say in it.

Here it is. Read it once, slowly, to the end. Some of it's hard. You don't have to do anything about it.

## The letter they never sent

Diane,

I'm never going to send this. You should know that before anything else. I'm alive, I get up in the mornings, I get on with my day, and nobody who knows me knows a thing about this.

You believed me. That's what I've got to say, and I've gone round it for a very long time. You believed me all the way down and I knew you did. I could see it. You stopped checking. You took me at my word so completely that I could have said anything, and after a while I did.

There was a point where what I was telling you had stopped being true. I knew it and you didn't. I could have told you then. I looked at you, so sure of me, and I let you go on being sure, because that was easier than watching your face change. I did that for longer than I'd admit to anyone. That's what I'd undo. The end was only what came of it.

It wasn't you. You'll have gone back through all of it looking for what you did. I know you. Stop. There's nothing in there. You didn't miss a sign, you weren't stupid, and you didn't give too much. You did what a decent person does when someone tells them something. You were right to believe me. I was the one who wasn't true.

I saw what you were early on. Someone who does what she says. Someone who stays. I'd never had that, I knew what it was, and I leaned on it because it held. When it was over I told myself you'd be fine. You looked fine. I said it so many times that I got to thinking I'd done you no real harm. That's how I've got through. She's strong, she'll get over it. It stopped working a while ago. I don't sleep the way I did.

I kept it because I couldn't stand to give it up. I'll say that too. As long as you'd never heard any of this, I was still someone you'd believed. Nobody else has ever believed me the way you did, and I've never earned it from anyone. Without that I'm just the person who did this to you, and I couldn't look at that. So I held on to what wasn't mine.

I don't want anything from you. I'm not asking you to forgive me. I'm not asking you to think well of me. I'm not asking to see you. Don't write. Don't look for me. The person you believed in was never here, and there's nothing here for you to come back to.

You gave me your trust thinking I was someone else. It was never mine.

So here it is. Your trust. I've had it too long and I never did one good thing with it. Take it. Give it to someone who'll do what they say.

I'm sorry. I know that's no use to you. It's true anyway.

---

That's all of it, Diane.

When I'd written the last line I looked up, and the chair was just a chair.

It's back. What you do with it is on you.

Your friend, even when you'd rather I wasn't,

— Evelyn
