# Donna's verdict, SU5 (A = "freedom", B = "softness")

I read the free letter first. It got me. "Then how do you still know the last message word for word?" I did not like how true that was. So I paid. Here's what I thought of each one, reading on my phone after supper.

---

## Reading A ("They took your freedom")

**1. Where my attention dropped**

Twice. The first time was early: "Your freedom is the part of you that goes where you like without asking anybody first..." That's a definition, like a pamphlet. And my first thought was: I live alone. Who exactly am I asking? It lost me right there, and I had to talk myself back in.

The second time was Card 8: "The page you tore out of a magazine and put in a drawer..." It's a long list, and by then I was skimming, thumb going.

**2. Where I felt seen**
- "At nine o'clock you say you'd better get home, and nothing's waiting there but the porch light you left on." That one hurt. It's true.
- "You turn down an invitation before your friend has finished asking." Yes, I do that.
- "The friend who used to ask you along and gave up asking." There is a friend like that.

**3. Where it lost me**
- **About some other woman.** The whole "freedom" thing reads like it was written for a wife whose husband wouldn't let her out of the house. That's not my story. He didn't stop me going anywhere. He left. The letter says "I made that hard... I let you think staying home was your own idea." I kept thinking, we didn't live like that.
- **It made my age into a wound.** "You slowed down. You'd done your running around. You like your own bed." I'm 61. Liking my own bed is not damage someone did to me. "What they're praising is a woman who quit going anywhere" and "the same few streets" felt like being told off for having a quiet life.
- **Blaming.** "That's your half." Then Card 9: "part of you is tired enough to want the one who made it small to do the deciding again." I bristled at that. I don't want anybody deciding for me. That's close to calling me weak, and she even says "it isn't weak" right after, which tells me she knew it sounded that way.
- **Odd.** "You tell the cashier why you're buying it." What? I don't. That one felt made up.
- **Repetitive.** "The sheet still had nothing on it but your name" came around again and again. I got the idea the first time.

**4. The cards**

Some of them helped. The Fool mid-step was good, and so was the man hanging upside down who looks perfectly content until you look at his ankle. The Wheel "hangs in the sky and nothing's holding it up" and the red bird in the tree felt like she was reaching to make the picture say what she wanted. By the middle I was skipping the picture paragraphs to get to what they meant about me.

**5. The letter at the end**

It's nicely written, but it isn't him. He'd never say "I let you think staying home was your own idea," because that never happened. "I kept the mind-reading" is a strange thing for a person to say. "Once would be enough" was the only line that sounded like a real man. It didn't land, because it's an apology for something he didn't do to me.

## Reading B ("They took your softness")

**1. Where my attention dropped**

Late, at Card 8: "I got up from the table at that point... I went up to the upstairs window." Evelyn's night gets too long there. "The lamp needed trimming and I let it smoke" made me stop. What lamp, in a kitchen? I wanted her to get back to me. The Card 10 paragraph about "your softness needed their signature" also went in circles a bit.

**2. Where I felt seen**
- "noticed one day that you hadn't cried in front of anybody in a very long time, and you were a little proud of yourself for it." That was me.
- "Every cry you saved until the house was empty, then did with the faucet running." I put the phone down for a minute after that one.
- "I told myself you were too much." He said that. Not those exact words, but he said it.

**3. Where it lost me**
- **A little preachy about "strong."** "They're admiring a door you shut." I've been proud of being strong, and it stung to have that taken off me. But it's honest, and Strength at the end gives the word back to me in a better shape, so I forgave it.
- **The theatrics.** "Swore at my own hands," the presence sitting in the chair across from her, the cat, the lamp. Some of it is atmosphere, but it starts to feel like a performance put on to make the reading seem worth more.
- **Repetitive.** Like A, "the sheet was still blank under your name" comes back again and again.
- **Small glitch in the letter.** "At least write me back." Write you *back*? I never wrote to him. It pulled me out for a second.
- "Years of birthdays where you smiled for the photo and went home and sat in the car" is close to a stock line, but it's close enough to true that I let it go.

**4. The cards**

These helped more. The Fool holding the white rose loose, "anybody who walked up could lift it out of his fingers": that's exactly how I was. The armour under the Emperor's robe. The Lovers, where she's looking past him. The Moon with nobody on the path. The Sun with the horse already past the wall. I looked at those pictures in my head. I only skimmed the Temperance one.

**5. The letter at the end**

It's closer. "When you opened up or needed something, I made you sorry for it" and "you were easier to be around when you weren't asking me for anything." I could hear him say that, or at least think it. "You weren't weak. I made it expensive" is the line I'd want from him. I still know Evelyn wrote it, and the line "I'm not stupid" sounds more like her trying to be gruff than like a real person. But it landed. I'd read it again.

## Which I'd rather have received

B, and it isn't close. The free letter told me the part of me someone decent reaches for isn't there anymore, and B named it: I stopped letting things in and stopped letting things show. That matches my life. I'm fine, I'm strong, I cry in the car. A told me I'd lost my freedom. I live alone and come and go as I please, so it felt like a reading for a woman with a controlling husband, and it made my ordinary quiet life at 61 sound like a sickness I'm half to blame for. B's homework is also something I'd actually do: tell a friend I missed her, in those words. A's homework (take a different road home) is fine, but it's a little like a self-help column.

**Gut on what felt off or not quite human:** In both, the long story of Evelyn's night (the tea, the presence at her shoulder, the blank sheet coming back again and again, the chair across the table) felt padded, like it was there to make the price look earned. Both letters open with the same tidy, articulate confession. Real men who hurt you don't write that cleanly. And in A, a couple of details (the cashier, the bird) felt invented to fit a pattern rather than seen.
