# Rhonda's verdict: Reading A vs Reading B

I read the free letter first. It got me with the late-night rereading and the "by Thursday you've found the fault" line. That's why I paid. Then I read both of these on my phone, one after the other, all the way through.

---

## Reading A ("They took your softness")

**1. Where my attention dropped**
- "I got up from the table at that point, which I don't do in the middle of a spread. I went up to the upstairs window..." (Card 8). I wanted to know about ME, and here's Evelyn trimming her lamp and catching her breath. One of these kitchen bits is nice. By the fourth or fifth one I was skimming them to find my part again.
- "The Sun shows a child riding bareback on a white horse..." (Card 10). By then I'd been reading a long time and the "turn" took a lot of words. I had to go back and read it twice to get the point, which was: quit waiting for him to admit it.

**2. Where I felt seen**
- "you hadn't cried in front of anybody in a very long time, and you were a little proud of yourself for it." That's me. I'm still a little proud of it.
- "did with the faucet running so even the walls wouldn't hear." I put the phone down for a minute after that one.
- "Years of birthdays where you smiled for the photo and went home and sat in the car." Too close.

Also the "She was too much" line. Somebody said that about me, in those words.

**3. Where it lost me**
- "Their regret isn't about wanting you back... You'll want to read it as love. It isn't." and Card 9's "There's no love in it." I understand she's being straight with me. But I paid $35 after a letter that said this person was full of regret and trying to reach me, and the paid part keeps telling me they don't care about me at all and just want to be rid of me. It stung, and a small part of me felt the free letter dangled one thing and the paid one took it back.
- She calls him "they" and "this person" all the way through. I know exactly who it is. It's a man. Saying "they" over and over made it feel like a form letter that has to work for anybody.
- "They kept the use of you, the part that caught what they missed" (Card 3). I didn't follow that. Caught what? I read it twice.
- The ending, "What you do with it is on you." After all that gentleness it felt a little cold, like being shown the door.
- It's long. Some lists go on one item too many.

**4. The cards**
They helped, mostly. The Empress sitting back on the cushions and the Tower with no door are pictures I can actually see, and she tells me what's on the card before what it means, which I like. Strength at the end, soft hands on the lion, was lovely, and it took the word "strong" back from me in a way I didn't mind. The Hierophant and the Temperance ones I skimmed. They didn't add anything.

**5. The letter at the end**
Mostly yes. "Some of the time I was glad of it, because you were easier to be around when you weren't asking me for anything." That sounds like him. That's the kind of thing he'd never say but would think. "You weren't weak. I made it expensive" is a bit too neat. It sounds more like Evelyn than like him. And "when you went soft on me" is a funny way for a man to talk. But it landed. I read it twice.

## Reading B ("They took your nerve")

**1. Where my attention dropped**
- "Sensible looks like this. You let somebody else pick the restaurant. You eat the meal that came out wrong." (Card 2). That's not me. I send food back. Right there I started thinking this was for some other woman.
- "Here's what it's been charging you. Every class you meant to sign up for and didn't..." (Card 8, Death). A long list of classes, trips and job applications. Some of it is fair, but it read like a list about being timid in general, not about him.

**2. Where I felt seen**
- "you're going over your side of it in the car again." Yes. I have the speech ready. I've had it for years.
- "That was the one place you kept your nerve... you saved the whole of it for that one talk." That one surprised me and it was true.
- "Going quiet isn't the same as saying yes" (from the letter). That one I needed to hear.

**3. Where it lost me**
- "They had a line that let them off, and the line was 'She went along with it.'" I don't like where that goes. Even though she says it's not on me, the picture is of a woman who rolled over. I bristled. I'm not a doormat, and I don't want to pay someone to tell me I was one.
- "Their regret is that they got you to back down, and they liked it... It's how much they enjoyed it." That's creepy. I don't want to picture him enjoying it. It made the whole thing darker than it needed to be.
- The homework, "Send back the order that came out wrong... Sign up for the thing," felt like a self-help book. It's for a shy woman. I'm not shy, I'm hurt.
- Same "they" problem as A, and the same "no love in it" sting.
- Small thing, but it bugged me: she says "past midnight, six cards down" when she's on card eight. And the Chariot comes before she's said anything about me, then the Magician turns up after the story bit at Card 11 like it got dropped there. It felt pasted together in places.

**4. The cards**
Honestly the pictures in this one were cleverer. The Hanged Man with the free leg crossed behind the tied one, and then the World dancer with the same crossed leg at the end. I liked noticing that. The Star woman with her foot on the water was beautiful. But clever pictures don't help much if what they're pointing at isn't me. The Chariot and the Wheel of Fortune I skipped.

**5. The letter at the end**
Partly. "I saw that. I liked it. Things were easier with you not arguing. I got my way more" sounds like a real man. But "You could have stood up to me, and I made sure you didn't" sounds like something written to make me feel better, not something he'd ever say. "I'm not good at this kind of thing, so it won't come out right" was a nice human touch. It didn't land as hard as A's.

## Which I'd rather have received

A. The free letter got me because of the going quiet when somebody gets close and the finding fault by Thursday, and A picks that up and explains it: I shut the door, both ways, and I've been calling it strong. That's what actually happened to me. The crying with the faucet running, the being proud of not crying, "she was too much," all of that is mine. B is about a woman who can't send her soup back. I'm not her, and the "she went along with it" and "they liked it" parts made me feel like I was being told I was a pushover who got pushed over. A made me cry a little. B made me a little defensive. I'd still say both cost me something I wasn't expecting. I paid because the free letter said he regretted it and was trying to reach me, and both paid readings spend pages telling me he doesn't love me and only wants me off his hands. I can take the truth, but it felt like being sold one thing and handed another.

## Did anything feel off or not quite human? (gut)

- The "they" for a man I both know is a man. A real woman reading for me would say "he."
- Evelyn's own night in the kitchen gets as many words as I do. The pot of tea, the window, the lamp smoking, cursing at her hands. It's like a story she's telling about herself. One or two of those beats would be spooky. Five or six feels staged.
- The two readings share whole paragraphs word for word (Card 7, bits of the letter, "And the next time someone reaches for you, there's someone there"). I wouldn't know that if I'd only got one. But some of the lines already felt like stock lines, even in one reading.
- The sentences sometimes hit a rhythm, short, short, short, then a turn, that sounds more written than said.
- B's card count slip (six cards down at card eight) made me think nobody read it over.
