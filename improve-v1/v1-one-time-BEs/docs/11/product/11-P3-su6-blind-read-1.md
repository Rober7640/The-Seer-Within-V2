# Cheryl's verdict, SU6

I read the free letter first, then A, then B, all of each, on the couch.

---

## Reading A ("your belief that you'd be looked after")

**1. Where my attention dropped**

- "They'd moved by then. After my question they left my shoulder..." (Card 5). By now I'd had the ghost at her neck, the pen picked up and put down, the empty sheet. The play-by-play about where the spirit is standing in her kitchen started feeling like stage directions. I skimmed until she got back to me.
- "When it went wrong with them, you changed the scenery. Different place, different people..." (Card 7). I didn't up and move. I've been in the same town for years. This part was about some other woman, and I drifted.

**2. Where I felt seen**

- "You'd sooner stand on a kitchen chair at eleven at night to change the smoke alarm battery" (yes. Last winter.)
- "the dish goes back the next day washed, with something baked in it, so nothing's owed" (that's me exactly, and I'd never have thought someone would notice it)
- "If a doctor's office has ever told you that you'd need someone to drive you home afterward" (my colonoscopy. I took a cab and lied about it.)

**3. Where it lost me**

- Card 7, the moving away and "you're itching to go the minute things start to feel ordinary." Not me. Felt pasted in.
- "You aren't sparing anybody. You're keeping the old rule going... making it true one more time." This tipped into a lecture. I know she keeps saying it's not my fault, but this part felt like it was.
- "Fine on my own was you carrying everybody's load and calling it a choice." It's a little preachy. I picked being on my own. Don't tell me I didn't.
- "If this person hurt you, you owe them no reply" comes up again and again. The first time it was kind. By the third time it sounded like something a lawyer told her to put in.
- The all-night routine: midnight, the back step, three in the morning, the window going grey. It's a nice story, but it's there to make me feel the $35 was a big effort. I could tell.

**4. The cards**

The pictures mostly helped, because she tied each one to me. The Chariot with no reins, "Nothing runs from him to anybody." Temperance, with the water running down and "Nothing runs back up." That one I got right away. I skimmed Justice and the Star. They felt like she needed a card there, not like the card was saying anything.

**5. The letter**

Yes, I could hear him in it. "You'd see I was worn out and you'd take care of it... I was busy, or I said you were better at that kind of thing than me." That's how it went, the man who let me do everything. "Someone should have looked after you, and I knew it" is plain enough to sound like a real person. It made my throat go tight. "Don't write, and don't come looking for me" stung, but it's honest. Then "What you do with it is on you" at the very end is cold. It felt like being shown the door.

## Reading B ("your softness")

**1. Where my attention dropped**

- "Next came the Emperor, sitting square on a stone throne..." (Card 2). It was short, but "a door you shut" in a speech about being strong felt like a greeting card. I started skimming here.
- "I got up from the table at that point... I went up to the upstairs window... The lamp needed trimming and I let it smoke." (Card 8). This is where I really lost her. Who has an oil lamp in her kitchen? It sounded made up, and I stopped believing the night happened.

**2. Where I felt seen**

- "patting her back twice and moving off" (I do that. My daughter's even teased me about it.)
- "Every cry you saved until the house was empty, then did with the faucet running" (that one got me)
- "You cut a story short at dinner in case it runs on." (yes, at my sister's)

**3. Where it lost me**

- "Softness." It's a therapist's word. Nobody I know talks like that. It made the whole thing feel like something I'd scroll past on Facebook.
- "She was too much." I've been called that. But B leans hard on him punishing me, "I made you sorry for it," and that didn't fit my man. Mine wasn't cruel. He just took and took. B turned him into a meaner person than he was.
- "They're admiring a door you shut." Felt like blaming me for being strong. I worked for that.
- The same Card 7 moving-away paragraph, and it fits me no better here.
- "I cursed my own hands out loud," the lamp, the "Nine cards down and three to go." Too much theater. A reader writing it up to make it sound like an ordeal.
- The disclaimers ("you owe them no reply" and so on) are repeated here just like in A.

**4. The cards**

Pretty, but more decoration than in A. The Fool with "a white rose held loose... Anybody who walked up could lift it" was lovely and got me. The Tower with "no door drawn anywhere on it" was good too. Strength at the end was the best card in either reading: soft hands on the lion. The High Priestess and the Hierophant I skipped. They felt like filler.

**5. The letter**

Partly. "Some of the time I was glad of it, because you were easier to be around when you weren't asking me for anything" is chilling and real, and "I'm not stupid" sounds like a man talking. But "You weren't weak. I made it expensive" sounds written. It's a line off a poster. No man I've known talks like that. And because it made him meaner than mine was, I couldn't fully believe it was him.

## Which I'd rather have received

A, and it isn't close. B is about feelings, like crying, hugging and being "soft," and any woman could read that and nod. A is about my actual life: the groceries in one trip, the smoke alarm, the casserole dish going back full, the cab home from the doctor, saying no when there's no milk in the house. That's being 61 and living alone, and nobody's said it to me out loud before. The milk-from-a-friend homework is something I'd actually do. A's letter sounds like the man I had, a taker and not a monster. B's letter made him crueler and gave him a slogan to say. Both go on too long about Evelyn's night in her kitchen, and both have the same paragraph about me moving away, which I didn't do.

## Gut: what felt off or not quite human

- Both are long, and the ghost moving around her kitchen card by card feels made up to pad the reading and prove she earned the money.
- The safety lines ("this is someone living," "you owe them no reply," "nobody from your past") show up too often. They read like a form, not a friend.
- B's oil lamp, and "You weren't weak. I made it expensive." Both felt written, not lived.
- Both end on "What you do with it is on you." After all that warmth, it's a cold last line. I'd have liked her to say goodbye to me herself.
