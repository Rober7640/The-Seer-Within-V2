# Verdict: Wanda reads A and B

I read them both in bed on my phone, one a night. Here's what I think.

---

## Reading A: "They took your softness"

**1. Where my attention dropped**

"I got up from the table at that point, which I don't do..." (Card 8). She'd just given me that long list, the faucet running so the walls won't hear and the people who quit asking. That one hurt and I needed a minute. And then she switches to herself going upstairs to look at the dark street, and a lamp that "needed trimming and I let it smoke." Who has an oil lamp? I skimmed down to where it was about me again. Same thing at Card 11, "Look at what it says. It names the price." I got it the first time, Evelyn. You don't have to explain the sentence to me.

**2. Where I felt seen**

- "you hadn't cried in front of anybody in a very long time, and you were a little proud of yourself for it." That's me, and I didn't like how fast I said so.
- "Someone thanks you and you make a joke of it so it can't land."
- "You cut a story short at dinner in case it runs on. You say sorry before you ask for anything."

**3. Where it lost me**

- **The bill.** "every one of those years went on your bill... Some of them would have stayed for the real one." She keeps saying it's on them, and then she hands me a bill for my whole life. That stung, and not in a good way. It came close to blaming me.
- **It doesn't match the free letter.** The free one said "You thought you were easy to walk away from. You weren't." This one says "They don't regret losing you, and they don't lie awake over it." Well, which is it? I paid $35 because of the first one. That feels like bait and switch, even if the second one is probably closer to the truth.
- **Card 3, "a woman who noticed things other people walked right past."** That's a compliment you could pay anybody. It's the old flattery I've been burned by.
- **Preachy at the end.** "That's what strong was supposed to mean... You can put that word down now." Don't tell me what my words mean.
- **Too long for a phone.** The ghost walking off at 3 a.m. and "I didn't know if I'd get it at all" is a bit of theatre. I could tell I was being kept in suspense on purpose.

**4. The cards**

I mostly liked them. The Empress leaning back into the cushions, the Fool holding the rose loose so anybody could take it, the horse already past the wall on the Sun. I could see those, and they meant something. The Emperor and the Hierophant I skipped. The Tower with "no door drawn anywhere on it" was good.

**5. The letter**

Parts of it sounded like a real man I could picture. "you were easier to be around when you weren't asking me for anything" made me put the phone down. So did "It was the best thing about you, and I acted like it was costing me." But "You weren't weak. I made it expensive." Nobody I've ever known talks like that. That's Evelyn's word. And then this part: "Something upsets you and you go make yourself useful to somebody. Everybody gets your time but you." How would *he* know what I do now? He's been gone for years. That paragraph broke it for me. It still mostly landed, though. I read it twice.

## Reading B: "They took your sense of being safe"

**1. Where my attention dropped**

Card 2: "You call that being alert. You read the mood of a room... Your phone sits face up wherever you are. You hear every car door on the street. You lock up, then go back and check the lock again." No, I don't. That's a woman who was scared of a man in her own house. It ended badly with me, but it wasn't that. From there on I read it as somebody else's mail. Card 8 lost me again: "Years of sleeping with one ear open... Every time someone came up behind you and you jumped." That's not me either.

**2. Where I felt seen**

- "Something feels wrong, and the first thing you do is talk yourself out of it." Yes, that one's true.
- "You'd say something felt off and hear that you were too sensitive." I've heard "too sensitive."
- "I don't know when you stopped turning your back on me. I didn't notice it go." That's in the letter, and it was the best line in either one.

**3. Where it lost me**

- **It makes me a victim.** Checking locks, sitting facing the door, jumping when someone comes up behind me. It paints me as a woman who was abused, and I'm not going to wear that.
- **"If you aren't safe where you are, that comes first... Tell someone who can help."** All of a sudden I'm reading a pamphlet at the doctor's office. It broke the spell, and it made me feel like she thinks I'm in danger.
- **The Death card.** She tells me right away that nobody dies, but I'm 61. I didn't love seeing that one turn up near the end.
- **The same bait and switch as A.** "they don't" miss you, after the free letter told me I wasn't easy to walk away from.
- **"you kept on trial so long that they gave up and went, and you took their going as proof."** Blaming again, dressed up as insight.
- **Same flattery in Card 3**, "a woman who noticed things."
- Evelyn's night in the kitchen felt like a show again: the clock ticking too loud, swearing at the deck.

**4. The cards**

The pictures were nice to look at. The sleeping moon with the dog and wolf howling at it was lovely. So was the woman dancing alone in the wreath on the World. But they were pinned to a story that wasn't mine, so mostly I skimmed them.

**5. The letter**

It's actually better written than A's. "you were just the jumpy one" sounds like a real person being nasty, and "I didn't notice it go. I only noticed it was gone" sounds like a real person being honest. "You were right to feel what you felt. It was never in your head" would knock some women flat. It didn't knock me flat, because the whole reading was about being afraid of him, and I wasn't. It has the same wrong paragraph as A, where he knows how I run around after everybody now. And the bit near the end, "Walking into a room and sitting down. Not looking over your shoulder. Sleeping through," is Evelyn's list again, coming out of his mouth.

## Which I'd rather have received

A, and it isn't close. Plenty of women my age shut down after someone made them feel like too much. Crying in the car, waving off help, saying sorry before asking for anything: that's my life and probably my friend Carol's too. B decided I'd been living in fear, checking locks and sitting with my back to the wall. When a reading guesses wrong that big that early, you stop believing the rest, even the good parts. And B's letter has some of the best lines in either one. A also gave me something I could actually do: tell a friend I missed her, in those words. I'd try that. I'll give them both credit for one thing. Neither tried to sell me anything else at the end, and I was waiting for it. And they told me flat out he isn't coming back to explain. I didn't want to hear that, but I believed it more than if they'd promised he would.

## What felt off

My gut says it was the lists. "Every time you... Every time you... Years of... Years of..." Real people don't talk in lists that long. Evelyn's night in the kitchen read like a script, and so did her own "I'm not waiting on you anymore" moment, which was a bit too neat a match for my lesson. The biggest thing, in both: the man in the letter knows what I do *now*, years after he left. That's when I remembered somebody wrote this, and it wasn't him.
