# Testina's verdict: Reading A vs Reading B

I read the free letter first, then both readings, on my phone, one after the other, at night. Here's how each one went for me.

---

## Reading A

### 1. Where my attention dropped

- **"Because whoever holds it gets to stay the one whose word counts."** (Card 4, the Hierophant.) The first three cards moved along fine. This was the first one I had to read twice to get. It's an idea, not a picture, and it's said in a roundabout way.
- **"It was midnight when I laid the High Priestess down, and your Inspector showed up in the cards."** By this point I'd lost the thread. My Inspector showed up *in the cards*? With notes *in the margin*? Cards don't have margins. I skimmed to the next card.
- In the letter, around **"I trusted your judgment over my own."** That paragraph and the "you'd go real quiet" one after it felt dropped in from somewhere else. They didn't follow from what he'd just said. I skimmed those two.

### 2. Where I felt seen

- "You bring a dish to every get-together and say it didn't turn out before anybody's tasted it." (Okay. That's me. Every Thanksgiving.)
- "You've gone up it alone for every bulb and every high shelf, knee against the rail." (I live alone. I do that.)
- "I said, 'Look after yourself,' and pulled the door shut behind me." (That's how it ends with a man like that. Something polite and nothing else.)

### 3. Where it lost me

- **The Inspector.** The first time I liked it. Something that checks everything before anybody else can find fault: fair enough. But it comes back over and over, and then she tells me "the one line the Inspector lives by". That's her telling me what goes on in my own head. It started to feel like a counselor's word, not a reader's. I don't need my head explained to me.
- **The bullet list on Card 5.** Five things in a row. The dish hit. The "anybody could have done it" hit. The text "dots" one didn't, since I don't fuss over texts that way. A list like that feels like throwing five darts and hoping two stick.
- **Card 11 gives the big line away.** "You were always good enough. I just never said it." Then I get to the letter and the big moment is something I've already read. It took some of the shine off.
- **The ask gets mentioned three times** (Card 9, Card 12, and after the letter). I heard you the first time.
- **"Your birthday, November 5, starts a new year for you. It's a year for clearing out."** That's the horoscope in the back of the paper. It could be anybody's birthday.
- **The stepladder ending**: "call somebody over to hold the bottom." It's sweet and I liked it, but it's a little set up. I'd have to ask my neighbor to come over for a light bulb. I might do it, though.

### 4. The cards

They helped. They're short, so I didn't skip any. The best part was the figure eight over the Magician turning up again over the woman on Strength. "What left with your first card is back by your last." I liked that. It made it feel planned for me. The crayfish crawling out of the pool, the Hanged Man ("whatever reaches him has to come the whole way up"), and the crown off on Death all made sense. The Fool and the Star were thin, and on the Tower she left out the lightning, which is the one thing I know is on that card.

### 5. The letter

Mostly, yes, it could be him. "It's fine" without looking up. The hand on the door. "Lucky them, whoever's with you next. They'll get to sleep through a crisis for once." That made me smile and it hurt a little. Then "I'd like to see you. If you won't, write to me. Just once." I'd been warned it was coming, and I still felt the chill when I hit it. That's when I believed her about the trap.

What doesn't fit is that he's more honest about himself than any man who ever walked out on me. "I liked being the one you looked to, and I'm ashamed of that now." Nobody I know talks like that, never mind a man. It landed anyway, mostly because of the door.

## Reading B

### 1. Where my attention dropped

- **"Here's what your twelve cards answer, in order."** Right at the start, before anything has happened, there's a whole list of twelve cards and twelve questions. It looks like the contents page of a booklet. I scrolled straight past it. I just wanted her to start.
- **The letter itself, around "I did love you. Whatever else you think of me, that part was real."** By then I'd realized I'd already read most of this letter, one piece per card, in italics. From there I was skimming to find the parts I hadn't seen yet.

### 2. Where I felt seen

- "You can fix the leak, sort the paperwork and drive yourself home from the hospital." (I have. Twice. I had to put my phone down for a minute.)
- "There's something in a drawer at your house you started once and never showed anybody." (There is. I'm not saying what.)
- "Every November 5 I think about picking up the phone, and every November 5 I put it back down." (The best line in either reading. My birthday.)

### 3. Where it lost me

- **"An old friend, a brother or sister, one of your kids said it once."** That's every person I know. It's the oldest trick there is: name everybody and one of them will be right. I nearly stopped trusting her right there on Card 1.
- **Every card has a piece of the letter in italics, and then the letter repeats all of them.** I see what she's doing, since the first letter said it comes in twelve parts. But it means I read the letter twice. The second time it felt like a re-run.
- **The flattery**: "That part of you was a gift... Plenty of people go their whole lives without it." "Every group of people needs one like you." Laid on a bit thick. That's what the cheap readers do.
- **"if you don't need anybody, why are you still checking every single thing you do?"** That one felt like a jab. Like being caught out. I bristled.
- **"It was never theirs to give back."** Wait, what? The whole thing, both letters, has been about them giving it back. Now it was never theirs? That confused me.
- **Card 1, the Chariot.** "He's the part they left with." I didn't see why a man in a chariot is my confidence. The Empress has a lovely description (the stars, the heart shield) and then nothing is done with it.
- **The letter jumps around**: "I kept the handy part. And when I left, I took more than that." Then suddenly it's about trusting my judgment, then "I saw all of that too, and I didn't do one thing about it." That doesn't follow. It reads like pieces put back together.
- **The ending is thin.** After all that, "Will I be happy again?" gets a short paragraph and a "six months or so". The practical things were on Card 12, before the letter, and I'd half-forgotten them by the end.

### 4. The cards

This is where B is better than A. The middle cards were the best part of B. The two cups, one held higher, and "it's being the higher cup": I got that straight away. The Lovers, one looking at the other and the other looking past: "You look past them, for a verdict from higher up." The dog and the wolf on the Moon, "you could take one for the other", answering the question from the first letter. The woman with her foot on the water and nobody telling her it would hold. Those pictures did the work. The Chariot and the Empress at the start didn't connect, and I nearly skipped them.

### 5. The letter

It has the two best moments of all: "That was the last thing you ever asked me to look at" and the November 5 phone. Those sound like a real person. If I'd come to them fresh they'd have flattened me.

But I hadn't come to them fresh. I'd read about two-thirds of the letter already, in bits, so it didn't feel like a letter arriving. It felt like the bits glued back together. It's long, and by the end I was looking for what was new. "It's a bill, and nobody owes it, you least of all" was a good last word from Evelyn, though.

## Which I'd rather have received

A. It reads like one woman sitting up with my cards through one night: midnight, three in the morning, the window gone gray, full light. I can picture her doing it, and that's what I paid for. It has one thing I'll remember, the stepladder, and it comes back at the end as something I can actually do. The letter came to me mostly new, so when he put his hand on the door and said "Look after yourself," it hit me like a letter should. B had the lines that got under my skin the most, the hospital, the drawer, November 5, and B kept its word to the first letter better: it calls back the trap, the window, the twelve parts. But B gave me the letter twice, and the second time I was skimming. A real letter you only get to open once. The Inspector in A wore on me, and I'd cut it in half. I'd still take A.

## What felt off, gut only

- In both, the ex writes like he's had ten years of therapy. Too self-aware for a man who left with "Look after yourself."
- In both, the "your name and date told me" paragraph (sorted by suppertime, hard to sell you a story, clammed up and chewed on it) reads like a form filled in. Then the same traits show up again in his letter almost word for word. That's when it stops sounding like him and starts sounding like the reading talking.
- B's letter feels assembled. A's Inspector "showing up in the cards with notes in the margin" feels made up.
- Neither one tried to sell me anything else at the end. Good. If either had, I'd have been gone.
