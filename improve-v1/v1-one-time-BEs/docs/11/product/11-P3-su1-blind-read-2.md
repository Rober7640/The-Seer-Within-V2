# Judith's verdict: Reading A vs Reading B

I read the free letter first. It got me. The bit about finding the fault by Thursday, and knowing the last message word for word, that was me. So I paid. I wanted to know what they took, whether they're giving it back or trying to get me back, and I wanted the letter.

---

## Reading A ("They took your nerve")

**1. Where my attention dropped**
"You were like that, before them. If you wanted to try something, you signed up..." That paragraph goes on and on: the clerk, cutting in line, driving with nothing but the directions, asking the shy question. After the third one I was skimming. It reads like somebody listing every brave thing a woman could do, hoping one sticks. It came again at Card 8, "Here's what it's been charging you. Every time you knew you were right..." Another long list. By then I was scrolling to find the next bold line.

**2. Where I felt seen**
- "You go over what you'll say in the car, and when the moment comes, you keep the peace instead."
- "You ask 'is that okay?' about plans that are yours to make."
- "Every speech you went over in the car and never gave."

The car bit is true. I do that.

**3. Where it lost me**
- "Someone made sure that every time you stood up to them, you paid for it." That's a big thing to tell me about my own life. It makes him a bully and me the woman who got pushed around for years. Maybe some of that's true, but I don't like being told I was managed like a dog. I'm not a victim and I bristled.
- The letter: "I'm not going to pretend I didn't know. I knew." Then two pages earlier she says "They don't know they're doing any of it." Which is it? It pulled me out.
- The clerk and the line-cutting are about any woman. It felt like a magazine quiz.
- "Before you go on, do one thing for me... quit asking what's wrong with you." and "once a day, do one thing you'd normally talk yourself out of" feel like a self-help book. I didn't pay $35 for homework.
- "Sensible" never really landed. Nobody calls me sensible like it's a wound. It's just a word.
- Evelyn swearing at the deck, the clock ticking too loud, the front step... it's a lot of her and her kitchen.

**4. The cards**
Some helped. The Hanged Man with the free leg folded up, and then the World with the same crossed leg dancing: I liked that, it tied up. The Star woman with her foot on the water was nice. The Hierophant and the Wheel I skipped. The Tower crown bit was fine but by then I was tired.

**5. The letter**
Half of it I could hear him saying. "I'm too proud. I've never once gone to anybody hat in hand." Yes, that's a man. "You could have stood up to me, and I made sure you didn't" landed, but it landed cold. It made him sound like a man who sat down and planned to break me, and I don't believe that's who he was. "I kept the handy part" is good. The ending ask, "I want you back. If you won't do that, then answer me, even one line," gave me a little jolt, which I suppose is the point.

## Reading B ("They took your softness")

**1. Where my attention dropped**
"I got up from the table at that point, which I don't do in the middle of a spread. I went up to the upstairs window..." The upstairs window, the lamp smoking, her breathing settling. I don't need Evelyn's evening, I need mine. That's where I skimmed. It drifted a little at Card 6 too (the Hierophant), which was short and didn't tell me much.

**2. Where I felt seen**
- "You just noticed one day that you hadn't cried in front of anybody in a very long time, and you were a little proud"
- "You keep a bad week to one line in a text."
- "Years of birthdays where you smiled for the photo and went home and sat in the car."

The birthday one I had to put the phone down for a minute. And the crying in the shower with the faucet running. How would she know that.

**3. Where it lost me**
- "Someone made sure it cost you" again assumes he was punishing me on purpose. Softer than A, but still there.
- "let one thing show to someone safe" — "someone safe" is a therapist word. Nobody I know talks like that.
- The bit about taking care of everybody in sight after it ended. That's every woman I know. Generic.
- Evelyn's night is too staged in this one: mint tea, shoulder, by the door, three in the morning, window going grey, "swore at my own hands." Too neat, like a script.
- "She was too much." Could be right, could be wrong. I'll be honest, I've heard it, so it stung in a good way, but if I hadn't, I'd have thought, that's not him.

**4. The cards**
These helped more. The Emperor with armor under his robe was good. Being strong as a door. The Tower with no door, built so nobody could get in and no thought about getting out: that's me, that's the house. The Fool holding the rose loose so anybody could lift it out of his fingers made me sad, in the right way. Strength at the end, soft hands on the lion, "that's what strong was supposed to mean," that turned the word around for me. I skipped the High Priestess and Temperance.

**5. The letter**
More like a real man. "I'm not saying I planned it. I don't think I did. But it happened, and I let it." That's how they talk when they finally own something. Half-way. "I'm not stupid" too, that's him being defensive, I believed it. "I told myself you were too much... I still say it in my head sometimes and I can't get myself to buy it" — that's a real person. The one line that didn't sound like him is "You weren't weak. I made it expensive." Men don't say "made it expensive." That's somebody writing. But the line before it about the best thing about me, I'll keep. "Come back. Or if you won't, at least write me back" — short, needy, believable.

## Which one I'd rather have received

B. A told me I used to be a firecracker who argued with clerks and he ground me down, and I sat there thinking, that's somebody else, and also, don't tell me I got bullied. B told me I stopped letting people in and started calling it being strong, and that I'm a little proud of not crying in front of anybody. That's true, and nobody's said it to me. The birthday photo and the car, the bad week in one line of a text, the Tower with no door: I felt those in my chest. B's letter also sounds more like a man who half-owns something, which is what they actually do, instead of a man confessing he did it on purpose. A had better bones in places (the crossed leg, the car speeches) but it kept piling up lists and it kept telling me what to do.

## What felt off or not quite human (gut)

Both of them narrate Evelyn's night in the same tidy steps (at my shoulder, over by the door, three in the morning, the window goes grey, they sit in the chair) and it feels like a play, not a woman at a table. Both have these long lists of "every time you..." that sound like they were made to cover everybody. Both end on "It's back. What you do with it is on you," which is a cold last line after everything. And both letters start word for word the same way, which I only noticed because I read two. If I'd only got one, I'd never know. But having read both, I trust the whole thing a bit less.
