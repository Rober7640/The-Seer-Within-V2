# Carol's verdict: Reading A vs Reading B

I read the free letter first, then A, then B, on my phone, in the evening. The free letter got me. The "you go quiet too, you say you're tired" part and the "by Thursday you've found the fault" part were me, and I wasn't happy about that. So I paid. Here's how each of the two readings went down.

---

## Reading A ("They took your softness")

**1. Where my attention dropped**

- **"When it went wrong with them, you took care of everybody in sight..." (Card 7).** Up to then it had been about me shutting down. All of a sudden I'm a woman who ran herself ragged looking after everybody. I do some of that, but it felt like a paragraph pasted in from somebody else's reading. I skimmed it.
- **"I got up from the table at that point... The lamp needed trimming and I let it smoke" (Card 8).** I don't need Evelyn's midnight walk to the upstairs window. A lamp that needs trimming? Who has one of those? It felt like a costume. Same with the 3 a.m. part where the presence walks out and she "didn't know if I'd get it at all." I've already paid. Don't make me sweat over whether I get what I paid for.

**2. Where I felt seen**

- "You hadn't cried in front of anybody in a very long time, and you were a little proud of yourself for it."
- "You cut a story short at dinner in case it runs on. You say sorry before you ask for anything."
- "Every cry you saved until the house was empty, then did with the faucet running"

The faucet one stopped me. I put the phone down for a minute.

**3. Where it lost me**

- **The bill (Card 8).** "Years of birthdays where you smiled for the photo and went home and sat in the car... You've been the one paying it." I'm 61. I know the years don't come back, and I didn't pay $35 to have them added up for me. It says the bill is theirs, and then it hands it to me anyway. That's the part that came closest to blaming me.
- **"They're admiring a door you shut."** Being strong is how I got through. Calling it a shut door, to a woman who's proud she held it together, is a bit of a lecture. To be fair, it did back off later with "you came by it honestly," and that helped.
- **"You still do their work for them"** stung. It's true, but it's said in a way that pokes.
- **Card 7 again.** The caretaking felt like it was about some other woman.
- **"They wouldn't [give anything to have you back]... It isn't [love]."** That hurt, but I respect it. That's not a complaint, just a note. A vague reading would never have said it.

**4. The cards**

I read them, and they helped. They're short and they mean something. The Fool holding the rose loose so anyone could lift it is exactly how I was, and it made me feel less stupid. The Emperor with armor under his robe, the Lovers with her looking past him, the Tower with no door all made sense to me. The Sun horse already past the wall was a bit of a stretch, but I went along with it.

**5. The letter at the end**

Mostly yes, it could be from him. "Some of the time I was glad of it, because you were easier to be around when you weren't asking me for anything." That sounds exactly like a person who'd do that, and it's mean in the way real people are mean. "I'm not stupid" sounded like him too. "You weren't weak. I made it expensive" landed hard. "I kept the sparkle" sounded odd, like nobody actually talks that way. The paragraph about me making myself useful to everybody felt tacked on. But overall it got to me.

## Reading B ("They took your freedom")

**1. Where my attention dropped**

- **"When it went wrong with them, you took care of everybody in sight..." (Card 7).** This one fit even worse here. What does looking after everybody have to do with freedom? Then the Wheel of Fortune "nothing holding it up" bit felt like a reach.
- **"But back then, they knew enough to let things stay the way they were" (Card 5).** I read it twice and still wasn't sure what it meant.
- **Card 9's Tower, "Nobody's coming out of there to sit down with you and explain."** I lost the thread there. The picture didn't match the point.

**2. Where I felt seen**

- "A woman who doesn't ask for more looks a lot like a woman who doesn't want more"
- "There's a trip you've been meaning to take, and every spring you say next year."
- "It's more like a light left on in a room you don't use"

**3. Where it lost me**

- **It's guessing about my life, and some of the guesses miss.** "You don't go out to eat by yourself, not ever." "You couldn't remember the last time you'd gone somewhere you hadn't been before." "At nine o'clock you say you'd better get home, and nothing's waiting there but the porch light you left on." I work, I've got kids and friends, and I'm not a shut-in. Some of it's me. But a lot of it reads like a lonely old lady in general. That's the "could be anybody my age" feeling I was afraid of.
- **It felt a little pitying.** The porch light, the movie I didn't see "because there was nobody to go with." Ouch. It's not blaming me. It's feeling sorry for me, and I don't like that either.
- **"Settled" is just what happens at my age.** Telling me my quiet life comes from a man years ago was flattering in a way, since it's not my fault. But part of me thought, honey, I'm 61, I like my own bed.
- **The homework.** "Take a different road home." That's fine, but it's small. It felt like a magazine tip.
- **Evelyn's kitchen theater again.** Nettle tea, a cat on the step, covering the card with her hand, the presence leaving at 3 a.m. The cat was nicer than the smoking lamp, but it's the same staged cliffhanger.

**4. The cards**

I read them, and some were really good. The keys lying on the floor with nobody holding them (Hierophant) is the best picture in either reading. "Nothing was ever locked" gave me a chill. Death walking with no scythe ("it went at a walk") was clever and true to how things slip away. The Wheel and the Tower felt forced, and by the end I was skimming the descriptions.

**5. The letter at the end**

It's softer, and I believed it less. "Your world got smaller around me, a little at a time, and I let it" sounds like a nice man who was a bit lazy. It doesn't sound like somebody I still think about because it ended badly. "You'd make up your mind in the morning and be doing it by afternoon" was a lovely line about who I used to be. "You were always allowed to go, and I made you think you weren't" is good, but it didn't hit like A's line did. It read more like a reflection than a confession.

## Which one I'd rather have received

Reading A. The free letter talked about going quiet when someone gets close and finding a fault by Thursday. A picked that up and told me why: I learned to shut it all down because somebody made me pay for being open. The not crying in front of people, the saying sorry before I ask, the faucet. I didn't want to admit those things, so they felt found out rather than guessed. The letter in A sounds like the real person, mean streak and all, and "you weren't weak, I made it expensive" is the sentence I'd have wanted. B had a couple of beautiful pictures, the keys on the floor most of all. But it mostly described a lonely woman my age who doesn't get out much. That could be anybody's aunt, and some of it just isn't me. A hurt more in the middle, with the years-on-the-bill part, but it felt like it was about *me*.

**Gut: what felt off or not quite human in both.** Evelyn's night of drama is the same in both: the tea, getting up from the table, the presence wandering off at 3 a.m., "I didn't know if I'd get it at all," then dawn and the pen moves. Once I'd noticed it, it felt like a template. The Card 7 caretaking paragraph is nearly word for word the same in both, and it doesn't really belong in either. Both readings also keep saying "I told you..." to point back at the free letter. That's a bit much, like being shown the receipt.
