# Karen's verdict: Reading A vs Reading B

I read the free letter first, then A, then B, all on my phone, all the way through. Here's what I honestly thought.

---

## Reading A ("They took your softness")

**1. Where my attention dropped**
"— Card 6 — Here's their regret, plain..." Card 4 through Card 6 got short and samey. Two ministers kneeling, a woman with a scroll. I could tell the cards were there because she needed a card, not because the picture told me anything. I started skimming until Card 7, and then Card 7 opened with "you changed the scenery. Different place, different people." I didn't do that. I stayed right where I was. That one was about some other woman.

**2. Where I felt seen**
- "you leaned into it instead of patting her back twice and moving off" — I do that. The two pats. Exactly.
- "Every cry you saved until the house was empty, then did with the faucet running" — I had to put the phone down.
- "Years of birthdays where you smiled for the photo and went home and sat in the car." That's me.

**3. Where it lost me**
- **Lecturing / blaming.** "The warmth you've refused everybody decent is still ready to go to them." and "every one of those years went on your bill." She keeps saying it's on them, then spends a whole page telling me I've been cold to every good person for years. I don't need a 2am reading to tell me I've been shutting people out. It started to feel like I was being told off.
- **"You were a little proud of yourself for it."** True, maybe, but it stung in a way that didn't help.
- **The Moon card.** "Nobody's coming down that path toward you, and nobody was ever going to." I know she meant *him*, but a woman my age who lives alone reads that line at 10pm and it lands as "nobody's coming for you, period." That was cruel even if it wasn't meant to be.
- **Evelyn's long night got theatrical.** Going upstairs to the window, the lamp needing trimming and letting it smoke, "swore at my own hands." Once is atmosphere. By the third time I felt like I was being *shown* how hard she worked for my $35.
- **Confusing.** Card 3: "They kept the part of you that made them look good, and they took the warmth that came with it. You went on giving them that after you'd stopped being soft with them." I read it twice and still don't know what I gave whom.
- **Stock lines.** "a woman who lit up a room and made them feel like somebody." That's what they say to everybody.

**4. The cards**
The Empress sinking into her cushions helped, I could see it. The Tower with no door was the best one in either reading, and it really did land. The Fool's loose rose was nice. The Hierophant and High Priestess I skipped; they were filler.

**5. The letter**
Parts of it, yes. "I told myself you were too much. I said it to other people too." He said exactly that about me to people, so that hit. "You weren't weak. I made it expensive." is a strong line, but it sounds like something a therapist wrote, not him. He'd never say "expensive" like that. And "When you went soft on me... I made you sorry for it. Some of the time I was glad of it" makes him sound like a real piece of work, colder than I remember him. It landed, but more as a hard slap than a release. The "Come back, or at least write to me" ask and Evelyn calling it a bill: that was good. I liked being told not to answer.

## Reading B ("They took your plans")

**1. Where my attention dropped**
"Justice sits between two pillars in a long red robe, and one foot shows under the hem." Card 4 to Card 6 again. A foot under a hem, a wheel with nothing holding it up. These felt like she was squinting at the card to find something. I skimmed. Then the same "you changed the scenery" paragraph as the other one, which wasn't me either.

**2. Where I felt seen**
- "Whatever goes on the calendar goes in pencil." Oh, that one got me.
- "Someone asks if you'd like to do a thing you'd love to do, and what comes out is 'We'll see.'" I said "we'll see" to my daughter on Sunday.
- "I never promised her anything." That's his line. Word for word, near enough.

**3. Where it lost me**
- **Thin card pictures in the middle.** Justice's foot under the hem, the Wheel of Fortune "nothing's holding it up," and Temperance with "one bare foot in a pool... You can tell which one is taking the weight." I couldn't. I didn't know what that last one meant at all.
- **Same stock bits as A.** "lit up a room," "changed the scenery," "halfway out the door before anybody knew." The door line doesn't even fit *this* story. What does running away have to do with plans?
- **A touch of the "you did it to yourself."** "Years of good people who'd have gone somewhere with you, if you'd only given them a date." It stung, but gentler than A, and right after she says the blame is theirs. I could take it.
- **Smaller worry.** At first "plans" felt smaller than "softness," like they took my calendar instead of my heart. She fixed that with "I mean the future itself... A lantern is for seeing the road in front of you." That tied back to the Hermit and I believed it.

**4. The cards**
Better than A on the ones that worked: the Empress with wheat not cut yet, the Fool with "nothing packed for further on," the Tower with two people falling ("You've already been up there with them once" gave me a chill), and the sunflowers over the wall. Strength with the gray hill and "nothing between you and it but the walk" was lovely. The middle three I skipped.

**5. The letter**
Yes. This could be him. "I nodded, or I said sounds good, or I didn't say anything, and you took that for a yes. I knew you did." That's how he was. He wasn't a monster, he just let me do all the building and said nothing. "You'd talk about next spring like it was a sure thing, and with you it was." I cried a bit there. "I let you build on me, and I knew it wouldn't hold" sounds like something a plain man could actually say. It landed softer than A but it felt *truer*, and I felt let off the hook for being a fool instead of told off.

## Which I'd rather have received

B. A had a couple of moments that cut deeper, like the two pats on the back and the faucet running, but A spent too long telling me I've been cold to every decent person for twenty years. By the end I felt like I'd paid $35 to be scolded, and the letter made him crueler than he was. B named something I hadn't put words to: the pencil, the "we'll see," never looking forward to anything until it's on top of me. The letter sounded like the man I actually knew, the one who nodded along and let me build. And the homework (lunch three weeks out, written in pen) is something I'd really do this week. I'd forward B to my friend Linda. I'd keep A to myself.

## Gut: anything that felt off or not quite human

- Both readings are clearly built on the same bones. Several paragraphs are almost word for word the same ("changed the scenery," "lit up a room," "halfway out the door," "There's someone there"). If I ever saw a friend's reading I'd recognize the template, and I'd feel cheated.
- Evelyn's night (tea, the one at her shoulder, midnight, 3am, window going grey, full light) is laid on thick. In A especially it reads like a performance of effort. One or two beats would be plenty.
- The middle cards in both feel like the card was picked first and a meaning was squeezed out of one detail (a foot, a hem, a wheel). That's where I stop believing a real reader is looking at a real spread.
- A few lines are too neat to be his: "I made it expensive." A real man doesn't talk like a greeting card.
