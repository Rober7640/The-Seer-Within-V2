# Testina's verdict: Reading A vs Reading B

I read the free letter first, on the couch, the way I read everything. It got me. The bit about finding the fault by Thursday, and knowing the last message word for word. So I paid. Here's what I thought of the two versions of what I paid for.

---

## Reading A

**1. Where my attention dropped**

"Before I turned a card, your name and date told me who I was laying them for. Hand you a mess and you'll have it sorted by suppertime..." That's a horoscope. It's nice, and it's sort of true, but it's true of every woman I know over fifty. Then right after it came a list of thirteen questions with card names next to them. On my phone that's a menu. I scrolled past it to get to the first card. It picked up again at the Empress.

It dropped a second time inside the letter, around "I told myself you could look after yourself." I'd already read that line under the Emperor, word for word. From there on I was skimming the letter for anything new.

**2. Where I felt seen**

- "a little money put by, a spare key nobody else knows about." Yes. There's a key. Nobody knows.
- "you've said 'Fine, I've got it' before they've finished asking" That's me at work, at my daughter's, everywhere.
- "the best of your life isn't behind you." At my age you want somebody to say that and mean it. She said it plainly, and I liked that.

**3. Where it lost me**

- "You hear a car slow down out front before anybody else in the house does." I live alone. There's nobody else in the house. "When you were at ease, the whole house was" is the same mistake. That was written for a woman with a husband snoring next to her.
- The letter's version of him: "I was one way one week and another the next," "you started listening for my car," "how the door shut." That makes him a man I tiptoed around and makes me the poor thing who learned to read his moods. That's not how it was, and I don't care for being made the victim of it. It's some other woman's story.
- Repetition. Nearly every card had a line in italics "from them," and then the letter used every one of those lines again. "I took the part of you that could rest easy. I knew it when I went." I'd read it. "Why did I hang on to it?" I'd read it. By the end the letter felt like a reprint.
- "Every November 5 I think about picking up the phone." For one second that got me. Then I thought: I typed my birthday into that form two days ago. It felt like my own form being handed back to me.
- "Lucky them, whoever's with you next. They'll get to sleep through a crisis..." I read it twice and still don't know if it's sweet or snide.
- The High Priestess bit, "they lost the one who kept watch for both of you, so they never had to." I had to go over it twice, and I'm still not sure what she meant.
- "A year for clearing out. Old papers, old habits, old hurts." That's straight out of the newspaper horoscope.

**4. The cards**

They helped, mostly because they were short. The Fool not looking at the ground, and the Lovers standing there with neither of them moving, both made her point. The Empress on her cushions I skimmed. I couldn't tell you now what was on the Justice card.

**5. The letter**

Could it be from him? Parts of it. The nap in the car with my head against the window, and sleeping through a thunderstorm, I loved. But it's long, the middle drags, and the moody-man part isn't him. Because I'd already read every big line under the cards, the ending didn't hit. "Come and see me" had already been quoted at the Lovers, so it was no surprise. The line after it, "It's a bill, and nobody owes it," was the best thing in the whole reading.

## Reading B

**1. Where my attention dropped**

Card 2: "Here's how I know: your name and date told me who I was laying them for. Hand you a mess..." I asked whether he loved me, and she answers by telling me I'm organized? That didn't follow. It's the same horoscope paragraph as the other one, just dropped in a stranger place.

It dropped again at the Wheel of Fortune: "a sphinx sits on top of the wheel, a sword held across it, and a dog-headed figure comes up the right side." She lost me there. And near the end, at "Signs to watch for," I could feel a list coming and started skimming.

**2. Where I felt seen**

- "When the phone rings after nine, you answer 'What's wrong?' instead of hello." I do this. My son teases me about it.
- "you got yourself through every night of it with nobody watching your back but you." Yes, that's living alone.
- "I'm sorry. Not for ending it. I'm sorry for the way I did it, all at once." That's the apology I'd actually want.

Honorable mention: "You weren't jumpy. You were right." And "There's one thing I can't do: promise you nothing will ever go wrong again." I trusted her more for saying what she can't do.

**3. Where it lost me**

- The Watchman. Naming it once was fine. By the eighth or ninth time it felt like a character from a self-help workbook.
- Justice: "Only one foot shows under the hem... nobody looked under the hem." That's reaching.
- The bullet list of five things I do. Some hit hard (the phone after nine, "I'll meet you there"). But a bulleted list in a psychic reading looks like a magazine quiz.
- "You said you'd always been a light sleeper. You hadn't." Telling me what I said and then correcting me was a bit cheeky. It also stung because it's true.
- The ending. After the letter I wanted to sit with it. Instead I got predictions, then a list of signs, then "Three of those inside a month means it's settling," then three tasks, then a lightbulb. "Get a bulb with a motion sensor in it. They fit the same socket." I laughed. It's sensible, it's what my sister would say, but it's a lot of homework all at once, and it assumes I've got a porch light burning all night.
- The same "Lucky them, whoever's with you next" line is in this one too. Still don't get it.
- The same "year for clearing out" horoscope bit.

**4. The cards**

These did more work. The Tower one was the best use of a card in either reading: if none of it was real, there'd have been no crown up there to knock off. That changed how I saw it. The "4 at its heart" bit about my birth name made me feel the cards were picked for me and not just pulled out of a hat. The Wheel was too busy and I didn't follow it. On Death, "Nobody's died. Here Death means something's finished." Thank you. At my age you need to hear that first.

**5. The letter**

This one could be him. "Near the very end there was one minute when I could have told you straight. One plain sentence." That's exactly how it goes when someone leaves badly. They knew, and they let you find out. It had lines I hadn't already read: the flashlight when the power went out, naps on other people's couches. Because she hadn't quoted the ask under the card, "Come and see me. And if you won't, write back" hit me fresh at the end, and I did feel the little lift she warned me about. Then "a hand held out from the top of the wheel. You don't have to climb." That landed.

I also liked her night: midnight at Temperance, three in the morning when the letter stopped coming, the sky gray at Death, the sun up when she finished. It made me feel she'd actually sat up with this. A small voice did say "she didn't really sit up all night for $35," but I let her have it.

## Which one I'd rather have received

Reading B. It felt more like it was about me, a woman on her own: nobody else in the house, answering the phone with "What's wrong?", getting myself through the nights. It didn't turn him into a monster or me into a victim. It said he left without warning and knew he was doing it, and that's the version I can take. The letter in B felt like a real letter, not a rerun of lines I'd just read, and the apology ("Not for ending it") is one I'd believe from a real person. A had some lovely bits, the spare key and "it's a bill," but it kept talking to a woman with a full house and a moody husband, and by the letter I'd heard it all already. B overdoes the ending with the lists and the lightbulb, and the Watchman wore thin, but I'd still take it and I'd still keep it.

## Gut: what felt off or not quite human

- Reading them side by side, whole paragraphs were word for word the same in both: "Hand you a mess and you'll have it sorted by suppertime," "When it went wrong with them, you clammed up, chewed on it," the letter opening "When I first knew you, you were the one who handled things," and "Lucky them." If I ever compared readings with a friend and found the same paragraph in hers, I'd want my money back. That's the thing that would make me stop trusting Evelyn.
- A: the italic "their words" under every card, then the same words in the letter, felt assembled, like a kit put together from parts. The "Every November 5" line felt like my own form being read back to me.
- B: the Watchman, the bullet lists and the 30-day plan read a bit like a self-help book in places. Evelyn's all-night story felt human. The lists didn't.
