# Norma's verdict — SU 7, Reading A vs Reading B

I read the free letter first, sitting up in bed with my phone. It got me, I won't pretend it didn't. "You say you're over it. Then how do you still know the last message word for word?" Yes. So I paid. Here's what I thought of each one.

---

## Reading A — "They took your faith in your own judgment"

### 1. Where my attention dropped
At Card 5, around **"On the Wheel of Fortune, a wheel hangs in the sky..."** I'd already had the "imagining things" part and it was good. Then came a wheel with nothing holding it up, and "the months I told you about are that wheel coming around to where it started, and that part they haven't noticed yet." I read it twice and still don't know what it means. After that I skimmed Card 6 and only woke up again at Card 8.

Earlier than that, I nearly put it down at Card 1: **"You can stand in front of two coffee makers for a quarter of an hour."** No, I can't. I'll come back to that.

### 2. Where I felt seen
- "I'm probably wrong, but. I might be imagining it, but." I say that. I say it at work all the time.
- "When somebody remembers it differently, you give way, even when you're the one who was there."
- "You've been waiting for them to say you were right. That's a second opinion too, the biggest one you ever asked for." That one stopped me. It's true, and I'd never put it like that.

### 3. Where it lost me
- **About some other woman.** The coffee makers, holding up two sweaters and asking a stranger, reading a text out loud to someone before I send it, changing my order after hearing everyone else's. I live alone. I raised kids. I make a hundred decisions a day and nobody's around to ask. That isn't me. It reads like a nervous little woman who can't pick a sandwich, and I didn't like being painted that way.
- **"When somebody asks what you'd like for dinner"** and **"birthday dinners where somebody else picked the place."** Who's asking me what I want for dinner? I eat alone most nights.
- **Repetitive.** "You got a little proud of it" in Card 1, then "you're a little proud of it" again in Card 2. Once was a point. Twice was a formula.
- **A bit preachy.** "Keep asking people the things they'd actually know, like the way to somewhere you've never been." Thank you, I know how to ask for directions.
- **Too much Evelyn.** Pen down, cat on the step, back door in the cold, three in the morning, the window going grey. I liked a little of it. By the fourth time she told me the sheet was still blank I felt like she was telling me how hard she'd worked so I'd feel I got my money's worth.
- The idea underneath, that he told me I was imagining things, did ring true. It just got buried under examples that weren't mine.

### 4. The cards
Some helped. The Chariot with no reins in his hands, I got that right away. The Tower with the crown knocked off and still in the air was the best one: "your say over your own life," up there ever since. The Moon, the dog and the wolf sounding the same, tied right back to the free letter and I liked that. But the High Priestess "in no doubt which is which," Justice's sword, and the Wheel felt like she was making the picture fit. By the middle I was skimming the card descriptions to get to what they said about me.

### 5. The letter
Parts of it could be him. "I'm not saying I sat down and planned it. I don't think I did. But I did it, and I knew." That sounds like a real person squirming. "You saw it, you were right, and I told you that you weren't" is plain and I could hear a man say it. "I told myself you were imagining things. I said it to other people too." That hurt, in a way I think was the point. The ask at the end, "If you won't, then write to me. Just once," that's exactly what he'd do, and Evelyn calling it a bill was satisfying. It landed, maybe 7 out of 10. It would land harder if the reading before it had sounded more like my life.

## Reading B — "They took your softness"

### 1. Where my attention dropped
At Card 4, **"The High Priestess holds a scroll with writing on it..."** The scroll part didn't mean much to me and the paragraph after it went in circles ("if it was never a weakness, then you were right, all along"). Card 6, the Hierophant, was thin too. It picked back up at Card 7 and I didn't drift again until Card 11's **"On Temperance, a winged figure pours water..."** Water going across "on a slant"? So what?

### 2. Where I felt seen
- "Every cry you saved until the house was empty, then did with the faucet running so even the walls wouldn't hear." I had to put the phone down after that one.
- "You don't call it a gap. You call it being strong." That's what I call it. I'd have said those exact words to her face.
- "Every piece of bad news you sat on until it was over, so nobody could fuss, and then told it later like a funny story." My daughter has said this to me nearly word for word.

### 3. Where it lost me
- **Generic underneath.** "You've put up walls" is the oldest line any psychic has. I've been told it at a fair for twenty dollars. If B had stopped at "you're guarded" I'd have rolled my eyes. It's the details that save it: the car, the shower, the faucet, patting a friend's back twice and moving off.
- **"She was too much."** Most women have heard that. It didn't feel like he said it to me in particular.
- **Stung a bit too hard.** "Nobody's coming down that path toward you, and nobody was ever going to." I know she means him. At eleven at night, a woman who lives alone reads that as "nobody, ever." She should have said "he isn't coming" and left it there.
- **Close to blaming.** "Some part of you still wants to be soft toward the one person who made it cost," then "the warmth you've refused everybody decent." I bristled at that. She did say it was on him, more than once, so I let it go. Just.
- **"I swore at my own hands," "the lamp needed trimming and I let it smoke."** What lamp? It's a kitchen. Same as A, too much of Evelyn's long night, though here it bothered me a little less because the reading itself was holding me.

### 4. The cards
Better than A's. The Empress sitting back in the cushions, letting them hold her, was me before. The Emperor with armor under his robe, "he never takes the second thing off," was me now. The Tower with no door, built so nobody could get in and nobody thought about getting out, was the best card in either reading. And Strength at the end, soft hands on the lion, "that's what strong was supposed to mean," turned my own word around on me in a way I didn't mind. The Fool's rose was pretty. Temperance and the Hierophant I skipped.

### 5. The letter
Mostly yes. "Some of the time I was glad of it, because you were easier to be around when you weren't asking me for anything." That's cruel and true, and it's how he'd say it. "I'm not stupid" sounds like a real man getting defensive in his own letter. "Come back. Or if you won't, at least write me back." Him all over. But the big line, **"You weren't weak. I made it expensive,"** doesn't sound like a person. Nobody talks like that. It sounds like something off a greeting card or out of a therapist's mouth, and it's the line the whole thing builds up to. A's big line sounded more like a man. B's letter around it sounded more like mine.

## Which I'd rather have received

B. A was the cleverer idea, and the bit about waiting for him to say I was right was the single truest thing in either one. But A kept describing a woman who can't choose a coffee maker or order dinner without asking the table, and I'm not her. I've been on my own a long time and I decide everything. B described the woman I actually am now: the one who says "fine," cries in the car, turns her bad news into a joke, and calls all of it being strong. It didn't make me feel feeble. It made me feel like someone had noticed what it cost. I'd have kept B and read it again. I'd probably have deleted A after a day.

## Anything that felt off or not quite human (gut only)

Both had the same rhythm: every card gives you four or five little "you do this, you do that" examples in a row, then "Here's what..." and "That's the danger, right there." After a few cards I could feel a pattern, like a form being filled in. Evelyn's all-night vigil, the presence moving from her shoulder to the door to the chair, was a nice idea in small doses, but it showed up after nearly every card and started to feel like a performance put on to prove I'd got my money's worth. And in B, "I made it expensive" was the one line I'd swear a person didn't write.
