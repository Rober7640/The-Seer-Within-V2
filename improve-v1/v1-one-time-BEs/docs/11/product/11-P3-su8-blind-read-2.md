# Brenda's verdict, Readings A and B

I read the free letter first, then both readings top to bottom, on my phone, after supper. Here's what I honestly thought.

The free letter got me. I knew who it meant before I finished the first line, the way she said I would. So I came to both of these wanting to know two things: what they took, and whether he's giving it back or trying to get me back. Both readings answer both questions, and I'll give them that.

---

## Reading A: "They took your sense of your own worth"

### 1. Where my attention dropped
- **"They've kept it because giving it back means saying what they did."** (Card 4, Justice.) The scales paragraph went abstract on me: "everything you were to them on one side, and what they gave you back on the other." I read it twice and still had to work out what she meant. That's where I started skimming.
- **"When it went wrong with them, you started reading into everything."** (Card 7.) It felt like the subject had changed. The whole reading was about me wanting too little, and suddenly it was about me watching for trouble. It's true enough, but it didn't seem to belong here.
- The long **"Years of letting other people pick the restaurant..."** list in Card 8. By then I'd got the point.

### 2. Where I felt seen
- "You drink out of the chipped mug and put the good ones out for company."
- "told everybody not to make a fuss over your birthday, and then been a little hurt when nobody did."
- "When someone does something nice for you, you're already working out how to make it up to them"

That birthday line made me put the phone down for a minute.

### 3. Where it lost me
- **Generic.** "Sense of your own worth" is the first thing any reader would guess about a woman my age who lives alone. It's true of me, but it's true of my sister and half my friends too. When I first saw the word "worth" I thought, well, that's a safe bet.
- **Preachy, and a little blaming.** "Nobody has to hand you the smaller piece anymore. You reach for it yourself." And "you got the smaller piece, without anybody meaning you harm... because you never once let on." She keeps saying it's on them, then keeps telling me what I do wrong. Card 8 reads like a lecture about my habits.
- **Repetitive.** It says "you're a little proud of it" in Card 1 and again in Card 2, back to back. The cake, the end piece of the loaf, the pie, the smaller slice: it's the same food picture four times.
- **Muddled.** At the start she says some cards will "look like they disagree with each other." None of them did, so I kept waiting for that.
- The ghost in my chair at the end. It's a bit much, but I went along with it.

### 4. The cards
Mixed. The Empress with her whole weight in the seat helped, and so did the Tower with the crown knocked off ("You never put the crown back up there"). That one landed. The Hierophant in Card 2 is described and then just left there, and I couldn't see what two kneeling men had to do with me taking the end piece of bread. The Wheel of Fortune and Justice I mostly skimmed.

### 5. The letter
Parts of it sound like him. "I'm not much good at writing things like this, so I'll just say what I did." That's a man's voice, and it's plain. "I kept the handy part" stung. But the big line, "I knew what I was paying," is odd. It sounds like a bill at a hardware store. And there's a paragraph about me "hunting for what it meant" that wasn't really what this reading was about, so it felt stuck on. It landed about halfway. I believed he could have written it, but I didn't cry.

## Reading B: "They took your softness"

### 1. Where my attention dropped
- **"I got up from the table at that point... I went up to the upstairs window"** (Card 8). The lamp that "needed trimming" and the smoke. Who trims a lamp? It felt like stage scenery. I skipped to the next card.
- **Card 9 runs long.** Evelyn's bit about swearing at her own hands comes *after* she's already explained the card, so I lost track of the order things happened in.
- Card 11's Temperance picture: "the stream crosses the gap on a slant." I didn't follow it, and I didn't care to.

### 2. Where I felt seen
- "When a friend put an arm around you, you leaned into it instead of patting her back twice"
- "Every cry you saved until the house was empty, then did with the faucet running"
- "the line was 'She was too much.'"

The faucet one. I've done exactly that. And "too much" is what I was told, more or less word for word. That's when I stopped reading it as a reading.

### 3. Where it lost me
- **A little presumptuous.** "Someone made sure it cost you," and in the letter, "I'd make you pay one way or another." It's close to the truth, but it nudges toward making him a monster and me a victim, and I don't care for that word on me. It stayed just on the right side of the line for me.
- **One line read wrong at first.** "Nobody's coming down that path toward you, and nobody was ever going to." On the first read I thought she meant nobody at all, ever. That's a cruel thing to read alone at night. Then I worked out she meant him.
- **The props.** The lamp, the upstairs window, "swore at my own hands." It's dressed up. The tea and the cat-type details in A felt more ordinary than this.
- The same "some of these cards will look like they're arguing" warning at the top, and again none of them argued.
- The ghost in the chair. Same as A. It's a bit creepy, but I let it go.

### 4. The cards
These helped. I actually looked at the pictures in my head. The Emperor with armor under his robe ("he never takes the second thing off") is me, and it's exactly what "being strong" is. The Fool holding the rose loose so anyone could lift it, the Lovers with her looking past him, the Tower with no door, the horse already past the wall on the Sun: every one of them meant something, and she told me what. I liked that Card 1 went back to the old man's lantern from the free letter, so it felt like one story.

### 5. The letter
Yes. This could be him. "Some of the time I was glad of it, because you were easier to be around when you weren't asking me for anything." That's an ugly, honest thing to say, and it's what makes me believe it. "I'm not stupid" is just how he'd talk, defensive right up to the end. And "You weren't weak. I made it expensive." I read that three times. It landed. "Come back. Or if you won't, at least write to me" is exactly the kind of ask he'd make, and I'm glad she warned me about it first.

## Which I'd rather have received

B. A is true of me, and the birthday and chipped-mug lines are as sharp as anything in B, but "worth" is what you'd say to any woman over sixty. It spends a lot of its time telling me what I keep doing wrong, and that wore on me. B is about the thing that actually happened between him and me. I'd open up, I'd pay for it, and I'd quit. And the letter says the one sentence I've wanted from him for years, in words I can hear him saying. The things B asks me to do (tell a friend I missed her, say I'm worn out when I'm worn out) are things I could actually do this week. I'd have paid $35 for B and felt fine about it. With A I'd have felt like I got a good horoscope.

## Did anything feel off or not quite human?

- Both of them are built the same way, card after card: a bold line, the picture, "you still do their work for them," a little task. By the middle I could feel the pattern, and A felt it more.
- A says "you're a little proud of it" twice in a row. A real person wouldn't do that.
- The all-night story is the same in both: tea, him at her shoulder, three in the morning, the window going grey, him in the chair, the first line of the letter. In B it's laid on thicker (the lamp, swearing at her hands), and that part felt written rather than lived.
- Both open by warning me that the cards will seem to contradict each other, and they never do. It reads like a line from a template.
- "I knew what I was paying" in A's letter doesn't sound like a person talking.
