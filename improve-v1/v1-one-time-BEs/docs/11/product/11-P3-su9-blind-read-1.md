# Testina's verdict

(I'm 61 and I read these on my phone after supper, one each night. Here's how they went down.)

## Reading A

### 1. Where my attention dropped
The first card. "The Magician stands at a table. His right hand holds a white wand straight up..." It went on and on: a cup, a coin, a sword, a snake for a belt, a figure eight, roses, lilies. Then she never told me what any of it had to do with me. I skimmed it, and after that I skimmed every card picture. By card 7 ("Eight letters run around its rim, two alphabets alternating") my thumb was just scrolling.

### 2. Where I felt seen
- "You make a joke when a kind word gets too close."
- "You wait until you're alone to let the hurt show."
- "Being cared for by you is what they're missing... That's not the same as missing you."

### 3. Where it lost me
- **Every card ends with an order.** "Catch yourself..." "Stop asking..." "Stop going over..." "Stop treating..." "Stop composing..." By the sixth one I felt like I was being handed homework by a school nurse.
- **Repeats itself word for word.** "Shame stops them. They can't stand to be looked at by the one person who knows what they did." That's in card 9 AND card 10, same words. I noticed. When something's that copied, it stops feeling like she's talking to me.
- **Stiff, odd phrasing.** "You call the gap 'strong.'" I never called anything a gap. "They saw someone who could run things and make them happen" sounds like a job reference, not like me.
- **Confusing bits.** "I swear at my own hands. They've come right up to the table." Who came up? Her hands? Them? I had to read it twice. And card 8 opens "Nobody's died." Out of nowhere. Gave me a jolt for no reason.
- **"Set both feet flat on the floor."** What? Why? It just sits there.
- **The pictures and the meaning don't meet.** She describes the Sun card in detail and then talks about something else. I couldn't tell why that card came up.
- The bit where she writes down my "homework" on paper ("Write the name of the last decent person you found a fault with, and the fault beside it") felt a bit like therapy worksheets. I didn't pay $35 for a worksheet.

### 4. The cards
Skipped them after the first one. They read like a museum catalogue. Long lists of what's on the card, with nothing telling me why it matters to me. Worse than no pictures, honestly, because they broke up the parts I did care about.

### 5. The letter at the end
It had one good line: "You weren't weak. I made it expensive." That one hit. But the rest sounded like the reading read back to me in the first person. "I taught you that going soft costs you." Nobody talks like that. He'd never say "going soft." It felt like Evelyn summarising herself and signing his name. "Please answer me" at the end was a little chilling, which I think was the point, but I didn't believe he wrote the lines before it.

## Reading B

### 1. Where my attention dropped
Card 12, around "Here's why the timer works. An urge climbs, tops out and falls away..." It turned into a self-help column there. I'd already had my moment by then, and the oven-timer advice felt like a magazine tip. I also drifted a little in card 11 at "It gets from one to the other without going to waste." The water-cup thing was a stretch.

### 2. Where I felt seen
- "Every cry you saved until the house was empty, then did with the faucet running"
- "Years of birthdays where you smiled for the photo and went home and sat in the car."
- "patting her back twice and moving off" (that's exactly what I do, and I hated reading it)

### 3. Where it lost me
- **Card 7, "Now your half, and you won't like it."** I bristled. Then "You've been charging it to everybody since." Okay, it's true, but I don't love being told I'm the one who did the damage after paying for help. She did soften it ("That was fair. You'd been hurt.") so I stayed.
- **Card 9.** "catch the small lift it gave you, the warm drop low in your stomach." That felt a touch pushy, like she was telling me what my body did. It did, a bit, which is worse. But "it's the lowest point in this whole reading" made me feel judged.
- **Card 8, "the door you left off the tower, that was you."** Again, close to blaming. She keeps "That part is theirs, every bit of it" right next to it, so it just about held.
- **The order got muddled in card 5.** Halfway through she goes back to what happened after card 4 ("When I'd asked my question out loud, the fourth card answered it..."). I lost track of where we were in her night.
- Crying at a sad movie, leaning into a hug, picking up the phone: some of that list could be any woman. But it was specific enough that I didn't mind.
- "one thing with three names." Cute line. Sounds a bit written.

### 4. The cards
These helped. Each one had one thing to look at, and she told me why it was me. The woman sinking into cushions. The man with armour under his robe. The rose held loose so anyone could lift it. The tower with no door. The path nobody's coming down. The horse already past the wall. I could picture them, and I'd remember them. I'd have liked her to say the card names, though. "The woman on this card" every time made me wonder which card it was.

### 5. The letter at the end
This one I believed more. "Some of the time I was glad of it, because you were easier to be around when you weren't asking me for anything." That's a thing a real man would think and never say. "I said it to other people too." That stung, because of course he did. "I'm not good at this and I'm not going to get better at it." That sounds like a person, not a card. "You weren't weak. I made it expensive." landed again. The ask at the end ("I'd like you to come back. Or answer this, if you can't do that.") felt like him, and I was glad she flagged it as a bill nobody owes. I sat with my phone face-down for a minute after.

## Which one would I rather have received?

B, and it isn't close. A felt like someone filling out a form about me: describe the card, say a thing, give an order, next. I skimmed most of it and only a few lines stuck. B felt like a woman sitting at her kitchen table with me in mind. It told me things about my own life I'd never said out loud, the faucet, the car after the birthday party, the patting-on-the-back hug, and it told them plainly, not dressed up. It was hard on me in the middle, and I didn't enjoy card 7 or card 9, but it was hard on him too, and the letter at the end sounded like a man I could have known. I'd pay $35 for B. I'd want my money back on A.

## Did either feel off or not quite human?

**A, yes.** The long card descriptions that go nowhere, the same sentence pasted into two cards, every card ending in an instruction, and odd lines like "Set both feet flat on the floor" and "Nobody's died." It felt assembled. The letter read like the reading said back in his voice.

**B, a little.** Mostly the phrase "someone who could run things and make them happen", which shows up in both readings and in both letters and doesn't sound like how anyone talks about a person. And the timer-and-laundry advice at the end slipped into self-help-column voice. Otherwise it felt like a person wrote it.
