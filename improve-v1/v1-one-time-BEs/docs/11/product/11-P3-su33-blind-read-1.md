# Diane's verdict: Reading A vs Reading B

I read the free letter first. It got me. I knew who it meant before the end of the first line, and the bit about finding the fault "by Thursday" was a little too close for comfort. So I paid. Below is how the two paid readings landed, read on my phone in the evening, the same as the first one.

---

## Reading A ("They took your wanting")

**1. Where my attention dropped**
"You keep the heat where everybody else likes it..." (Card 2). I live alone. The heat's where I put it. After that line I started reading with one eyebrow up. It dropped again at Card 7, "When it went wrong with them, you changed the scenery. Different place, different people." I didn't move anywhere. That read like it was about some other woman. And from about Card 5 to Card 8 it felt like the same point being made over and over: I say "I don't mind."

**2. Where I felt seen**
- "When the dessert menu comes around, you wait to see whether anybody else is getting something" (OK, yes. Guilty.)
- "you say 'either one's fine,' and then you watch their face" (that's me with my daughter, honestly)
- "You've said it was fine and then minded the whole way home" (this one stung, in a good way)

**3. Where it lost me**
- **About some other woman:** all the restaurants, dessert menus and "what to do this weekend". It pictures a woman who's always out with a group, or married to someone who picks. I eat supper alone most nights, and I pick what's in it. The "wanting" idea fits a wife more than it fits me now.
- **Repetitive:** "I don't mind" must come up a dozen times. By Card 8 I got it.
- **A bit like a diagnosis:** "You got slow at knowing it." "Your mind goes blank." I didn't love being told my own mind's gone slow. That's close to calling me a pushover, and I'm not one.
- **Same as B (see below):** the long story of Evelyn's night ("the sheet still blank under your name", said about four times). At first it was nice. By the third time I skimmed it.

**4. The cards**
Some helped. The Magician with his hand up "the way a hand goes up... who wants the last piece" was good, I could see it. The Chariot with no reins made sense. The Hierophant's fingers and the High Priestess felt like they were reaching to fit the point. By the end I was skimming the card bits to get to what it meant for me.

**5. The letter**
Mostly believable. "You'd start to say what you wanted and stop halfway, and look at me first" sounds like something he could have noticed. The key line, "You were allowed to want things, and I made that too much to ask," is gentle and I liked "allowed". But it's a bit too tidy and polite for him. He'd never say "allowed". The ending, "Come back. Or if you won't, at least write me back," does sound like him, and Evelyn telling me to leave it alone helped.

**6. Scores**
- Kept me reading: **6**
- Felt written for me: **5**
- Worth the $35: **6**
- I trust Evelyn: **6**
- I'd tell a friend about her: **5**

## Reading B ("They took your softness")

**1. Where my attention dropped**
The same "you changed the scenery. Different place, different people" paragraph in Card 7. It's word for word what A says, and it's not me either. Then in Card 8: "The lamp needed trimming and I let it smoke." A lamp that needs trimming? Who has one of those? It felt like set dressing, and I lost the thread for a second.

**2. Where I felt seen**
- "You don't call it a gap. You call it being strong." (this is where I put the phone down for a minute)
- "patting her back twice and moving off" (I do exactly that and didn't know anyone could see it)
- "Every cry you saved until the house was empty, then did with the faucet running" (too close. That's not something you guess.)

**3. Where it lost me**
- **Laid on thick:** Card 8 is a long list of everything I lost: birthdays in the car, people who stopped asking, and on and on. Every item hit, but all together it started to feel like being told my life's been a waste. I nearly bristled. It's saved by "Strong worked, Diane, and you came by it honestly", but only just.
- **A bit too sure about him:** "They went for it on purpose." Maybe. But Evelyn can't know that, and it's the kind of thing a reader says to make you angry on your own behalf.
- **Confusing:** in Card 3, "You went on giving them that after you'd stopped being soft with them." I read it twice and still wasn't sure what it meant.
- **Same as A:** the night-at-the-kitchen-table story, with the sheet still blank and the clock times, goes on too long. "Three in the morning" and "full light" read like padding.

**4. The cards**
These helped more than A's. The Emperor with armor under his robe is me, plain as day. The Tower with no door drawn on it gave me a chill. And the Moon, with "Nobody's coming down that path toward you, and nobody was ever going to," was hard to read, but I believed it. Strength at the end, a woman with soft hands on a lion, turned the word "strong" around for me, and I liked that a lot. I didn't skip the cards in this one.

**5. The letter**
More like him than A. "Some of the time I was glad of it, because you were easier to be around when you weren't asking me for anything." That's an ugly thing to admit, which is why I believed it. "I'm not stupid" sounds like him too. The big line, "You weren't weak. I made it expensive," landed, but "expensive" is a writer's word, not his. He'd have said "I made you pay for it." The ask at the end, "at least write to me," feels like him, and I liked Evelyn calling it "a bill".

**6. Scores**
- Kept me reading: **8**
- Felt written for me: **7**
- Worth the $35: **7**
- I trust Evelyn: **7**
- I'd tell a friend about her: **6**

## Which I'd rather have received

B. I'm 61, I live alone, and the thing people say about me is that I'm strong. Nobody says I'm easygoing. B took the word I'm proudest of and showed me what it's been costing me without calling me a victim, and then the Strength card handed the word back to me in a better shape. That's the part I'd remember. A was about a woman who can't pick a restaurant, and some of it was me (the dessert menu, watching faces), but a lot of it pictured someone with a husband and a busy social table, and it said "I don't mind" so many times it started to feel like a lecture. B went on too long in the middle, and the misery list in Card 8 nearly tipped over, but it cut deeper, and the letter sounded more like him.

**What felt off in either (gut only):** both use the identical "you changed the scenery, different place, different people" paragraph, and both say "halfway out the door". If two readings say the same sentence about me, it's not really about me. Evelyn's night (the tea, the blank sheet, the clock going midnight, three, full light) is the same story in both with the props swapped, and it reads like a template. Both also repeat the same phrases ("stop asking what's wrong with you", "they won't take one step", "on those two"). The one-thing-to-do homework at the end was the most human part of both. The oven timer and "say what you'd like, even if it's only soup" felt like advice from a real woman.
