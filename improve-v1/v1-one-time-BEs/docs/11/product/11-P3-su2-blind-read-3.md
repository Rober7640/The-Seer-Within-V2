# Which reading I'd rather have got (Testina)

Read on my phone in the evening, a few days after the free letter.

---

## Reading A

**1. Where my attention dropped**

The first drop was the list near the top: "Card 1, the Chariot: What did they take from me?" and twelve more like it. That's thirteen lines of questions before anything happens. I scrolled past it. It reads like the contents page of a manual.

The bigger drop was the letter itself: "When I first knew you, you were the one who handled things." By then I'd already read most of it in little italic bits under every card. "I did love you," I'd read that. "None of this was your fault," read that. "What I regret is...," read that. "Give me one more chance," read that. The letter was the thing I paid for, and it felt like a rerun. I started skimming for anything new.

**2. Where I felt seen**

- "the part of you that could hear 'I'll be there at six' and stop thinking about it."
- "when someone kind asks how you are, you say 'fine' and hand them nothing else."
- "you asked me straight out if everything was all right between us... I said yes."

**3. Where it lost me**

- It repeats itself. Apart from the letter, the bit about me going quiet comes three times: "You'll go quiet, dig until...", then "you clammed up, chewed on it, and wouldn't move till you were sure", then "you'd go real quiet. You'd turn it over in your head for days." Once and I feel seen. Three times and it's a horoscope for my sign.
- "Hand you a mess and you'll have it sorted by suppertime, the bills paid, the paperwork filed." That's flattering, and it could be about any woman over fifty who's held a house together. Then the letter says it twice more, in two paragraphs back to back.
- Card 2: "Did they ever really love me? Yes." Then the reason he loved me is that I could "walk into a mess and straighten it out." So he loved me because I was handy? That's not much of a yes.
- "Lucky them, whoever's with you next. They'll get to sleep through a crisis for once." Nobody who'd hurt me would write that. It makes me sound like a good secretary.
- Card 8: "They got that right, and I'll say it myself." Who's "they"? I had to go back and check.
- "Every November 5 I think about picking up the phone." Part of me liked that. Part of me thought, you just plugged my birthday in.
- "Your birthday, November 5... The change starts well before then." Depending on when this reaches me, my birthday could be this week or already gone, so "well before then" made me stop and count.

**4. The cards**

Some helped. The Emperor with armor under his robe is me being "careful." The dog and the wolf on the Moon worked. The Strength lady with the chain of flowers, "nobody checking the knot," I liked. Others I skipped: the Empress's shield, the Lovers looking up at the angel, the Chariot with no reins. I couldn't see what any of those had to do with what she was telling me. Each one was a picture and then a point, with nothing joining them.

**5. The letter**

In places it could be him. The night I asked if we were all right and he said yes got me. "Home by six" with dinner on at five-thirty, yes. But I'd already read so much of it, and the rest was a list of my good points, in an order a guilty man wouldn't use. Evelyn's line afterwards, "It's a bill, and nobody owes it," landed better than the letter did.

## Reading B

**1. Where my attention dropped**

Card 2: "Here's how I know: your name and date told me who I was laying them for." Then comes the bit about me being organized. How does that prove he loved me? It didn't follow, and I lost the thread for a minute. I had to read "It was the dog" twice too.

Then near the end came "Signs to watch for:", with a bullet list and "If three of these turn up inside a month." That's a magazine quiz, and my eyes slid right over it.

**2. Where I felt seen**

- "When somebody says something nice about you, you laugh and say, 'What do you want?'"
- "If you were ready for me not to show, I didn't have to feel bad when I didn't."
- "I knew when I said it that I wasn't going to call. I walked out and let you wait."

**3. Where it lost me**

- The second paragraph, before a single card: "You call it careful. It's waiting to be let down." She hadn't earned that yet, and it stung. In A it came after she'd shown me my habits, and I took it better there.
- "The Lookout." The first couple of times it was a good idea, a way of saying this isn't my fault. But it comes up about a dozen times, and by the end it's a character in a self-help book. And "the Lookout spoke up" at her table at midnight: is that my Lookout, at her house? That was confusing and a little creepy.
- Same handy-woman problem on Card 2: he loved me because I "could walk into a mess and straighten it out." And "Lucky them... sleep through a crisis" turns up in the letter again.
- The going-quiet line repeats here too ("clammed up, chewed on it"), same as in A.
- The overnight clock: midnight, three in the morning, the window going gray, full light. I liked it as a story. But a little voice said she wants me to know she stayed up all night, so I'll feel I got my money's worth.
- The ending turns into homework. There are signs to watch for and three tasks for thirty days. Then I'm told to dig out my spare key, give it to a neighbor and "don't ask for it back." I live alone. I'm not handing out my house key because a card reader said so. "By tonight it'll hang on somebody else's ring" is deciding for me.

**4. The cards**

I read nearly all of them, because the pictures were doing the talking. The High Priestess has her back to the veil and never checks behind her, which is exactly the trust I used to have. The Emperor's hands are both full, so he can't hand anything back. The Hanged Man keeps his hands behind his back, so his sorry can't reach me. The Tower has no door. Those made the point for her. I skipped the Wheel.

**5. The letter**

This one could be him. "The checking let me off." "Nobody's fool, that one," said like it was a favor he'd done me. The door, and the call that never came: that's the answer I never got, from the free letter. "I put you at that window." "I won't call. You can take my word on that one" is exactly the sour little joke he'd make. And the ask at the end, "I'd like to see you, so you can see I've changed," is what he'd say. Most of it was new to me when I got there, so it hit. I put the phone down for a minute.

## Which I'd rather have received

B. It felt like one story from start to finish: the spare key in the drawer at the top, the dog and the wolf, Evelyn sitting up through the night, the key again at the end. When I got to the letter it was new, and it sounded like a real man who knew exactly what he'd done and was still angling a little. A told me everything twice. By the time I reached the letter, which is what I really paid for, I'd already read the best lines under the cards, so it felt pieced together. A was tidier, and I loved the picture of me six months out, still looking for my shoes at five to seven. But B got to me and A didn't quite. I'd just cut B's ending short. Stop after the letter and the one thing to do, and leave my house key alone.

## What felt off (gut only)

- **Both:** the "sorted by suppertime" paragraph and "clammed up, chewed on it, and wouldn't move till you were sure" read like someone filled in a form about me. Nobody talks like that.
- **Both:** "he loved you because you were handy" is a strange comfort.
- **Both:** the birthday-as-a-new-year bit, "Old papers, old habits, old hurts," could be in any Sunday paper horoscope.
- **A:** the letter felt stitched together from the card quotes, like it was built backwards.
- **B:** the all-night clock and "the Lookout" are a little staged. The key homework is the one place it felt like someone had a plan for me, not a reading of me.
