# Testina's verdict, A vs B

I read the free letter first, then A, then B, on my phone after supper, the way I would have. The two readings are close to the same reading. It's the same cards and the same story, and most of the sentences are the same. So most of what I say below goes for both, and I've marked where they differ.

---

## Reading A

**1. Where my attention dropped**
At Card 5: *"Getting on for midnight. When I turned this card, they were standing over by the door..."* By then I'd had Evelyn's kitchen at Card 1, again at the end of Card 3 ("So I did something I don't do. I asked out loud, in my empty kitchen..."), and now again. Then there's another one at Card 7 ("Past midnight. Seven cards down"). I started skimming those bits to get back to me. I paid for my reading, not her night.

**2. Where I felt seen**
- "you were a little proud of yourself for it." Yes. That one stung, and it was true.
- "patting her back twice and moving off." I do that exact thing.
- "did with the faucet running so even the walls wouldn't hear." I had to put the phone down for a minute.

**3. Where it lost me**
- **Blaming:** *"Some of what's happened since is yours."* Then *"You've been charging it to everybody since"* and *"it's more than they ever took."* I know she says it's on them, and she says it more than once. But Cards 7 and 8 together felt like being sat down and told off. I paid $35 and two cards out of twelve are about what I did wrong. I bristled.
- **About some other woman:** *"a woman who could take charge and get things done."* That's every woman over 50 who ever ran a house. *"If somebody's beside you now"* tells me she doesn't know whether I'm with anyone, and a reader should know that. *"by the end of the week you've found what's wrong with them"* is fine, but I'm 61. I'm not out turning down dates every week.
- **Not human / clinical:** In Card 12, *"Left alone, an urge climbs, peaks and dies down. Looking when it's at its worst teaches it to come back,"* and *"Your head has one huge example of what happens when you go soft."* That's my daughter's therapist talking, not a psychic at her kitchen table.
- **Repetitive:** cost, price, bill, debt, expensive, door, strong. I got the idea by Card 5, and it kept coming.
- **Fake / theatrical:** *"swore at my own hands," "the lamp needed trimming and I let it smoke."* Who has an oil lamp in the kitchen? The more she tells me how hard her night was, the less I believe it happened.

**4. The cards**
The pictures helped, mostly. The Emperor with armor under the robe, the Fool holding the rose loose, and the Lovers with her looking past him all made me see the point. The Sun with the horse already past the wall was the best one. The Tower confused me. I thought that was the one with the lightning and people falling out, and she describes a sealed tower with no door. I let it go, but it made me wonder. I skipped the Hierophant.

**5. The letter**
It lands in the middle with *"You weren't weak. I made it expensive."* That line is good. But the rest is too well spoken for him. *"I treated it like a cost so I wouldn't have to meet it." "I had no right to the use of it." "You can let things reach you again. You can let people see you."* No man who did that to me ever talked like that. It reads like Evelyn wrote it and put his name on it. *"Some of the time I was glad of it, because you were easier to be around"* is the one line that sounded like him. *"I'd like you to come back"* is too polite for the man the reading just described.

## Reading B

**1. Where my attention dropped**
At Card 8: *"I told you it makes no difference to whether they've still got it, ten years or thirty..."* Card 8 is where B puts all of Evelyn's midnight business in one lump (the window, the lamp, him by the door), and right after it comes the long bill. That was the slog. Before that I didn't skim at all. B moves along faster than A in the first half.

**2. Where I felt seen**
- "You cut a story short at dinner in case it runs on. You say sorry before you ask for anything." That's me exactly, and it isn't in A.
- "You keep a bad week to one line in a text." I did that this month.
- "You've run the whole talk over the dishes and lying awake... and come out of it more tired." Yes.

**3. Where it lost me**
- **Blaming:** the same Cards 7 and 8 as in A: *"Some of what's happened since is yours," "more than they ever took."* It's the same problem and the same bristle.
- **Generic:** the same *"take charge and get things done,"* and the same *"If somebody's beside you now."*
- **Repetitive:** the same cost/bill/door words, though B felt a bit leaner.
- **Fake:** the same lamp and the same *"swore at my own hands,"* but there's less of it spread around, so it bothered me less.
- **Letter parrots the reading:** *"You clam up, you chew on it, and you won't move till you're sure"* is word for word what Evelyn said in Card 7. A does this too. When he uses her exact words, it stops sounding like him.

**4. The cards**
Same as A. They helped, the Sun most of all, and the Tower puzzled me. *"Fold laundry. Wash a pan."* isn't a card, but it's the kind of plain thing I can actually do at 11pm, and I'd rather have that than A's lecture about urges.

**5. The letter**
This one I could believe came from him, mostly. *"I'm not saying I planned it. I don't think I did. But it happened, and I let it."* That's how he'd squirm. *"I'm not stupid."* That's him, defensive even when he's owning up. *"you were the one who handled things. Something needed doing, you did it, no fuss"* is how a man would put it. *"Come back. Or if you won't, at least write me back."* is pushy and needy, and that's exactly him. It made Evelyn's point about the ask being a bill. I'd have cried at *"You weren't weak. I made it expensive."* in either one, but in B I believed he was the one saying it.

## Which I'd rather have received

B. They're nearly the same reading, so it comes down to a few things, and B wins each one. B has the line about cutting my story short at dinner and saying sorry before I ask for anything, and that line is the reason I'd say "she knows me." A doesn't have it. B's letter sounds like a man, a bit clumsy and defensive and still asking for something at the end. A's letter sounds like a nice woman wrote it for him. B also keeps Evelyn's kitchen drama in one place instead of interrupting me every two cards, and its advice for the bad moments is "fold laundry, wash a pan," not a paragraph about how urges peak. In both, Cards 7 and 8 are where I nearly closed it. I don't need two cards telling me I've been making decent people pay. She gets away with it only because the next card is about him trying to get me back, and that pulls me in again.

## Gut: what felt off or not quite human

- The way Evelyn tells her night (mint tea, a lamp she let smoke, swearing at her own hands, the window going grey, the chair turning back into "only furniture") feels staged. One mention would be atmosphere. Five is a performance, and A has more of it.
- In both letters he says Evelyn's own phrases back word for word ("clam up, chew on it," "too much," "made it expensive"). A real man wouldn't use her exact words. It makes me think the letter was built from the reading instead of being his.
- A's Card 12 sounds like a pamphlet from a therapist's waiting room.
- The Tower without its lightning made me wonder whether she was bending the card to fit what she wanted to say.
- In both, Evelyn knows exactly what he took and what he regrets, but doesn't know if I'm with anyone ("If somebody's beside you now"). That gap gave me a moment of doubt.
