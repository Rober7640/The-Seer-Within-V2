# Karen's verdict: Reading A vs Reading B

I read the free letter first. Honestly, the "you still know the last message word for word" line got me, and so did the line about finding a fault by Thursday. That's why I paid. So I opened the paid reading wanting two things: tell me what they took, and give me their side so I can quit making it up.

---

## Reading A ("They took your plans")

**1. Where my attention dropped.**
"They'd moved by then. After my question they left my shoulder..." (Card 5). By then I'd had Evelyn putting the pen down, the ghost coming closer, the ghost going still, and now the ghost walking to the door. I paid for my reading, not a play-by-play of her night. I skimmed those bits from there on. It dipped again at Card 4, "one foot shows under the hem." I read it twice and still don't know what a foot has to do with anything.

**2. Where I felt seen.**
- "If somebody asks where you see yourself down the road, you laugh and say you're just glad to be here." I say that. Word for word.
- "You say 'no promises' before anybody's asked you for one." Ouch. Yes.
- "The calendar on your kitchen wall has the dentist on it, and other people's birthdays." It does. I didn't love being told, but it does.

**3. Where it lost me.**
- **Too much "we'll see."** It must be in there six or seven times. I got it the first time. By the end it felt like being poked in the same spot.
- **Things that aren't me.** "A friend wanted to book a week away... she'd gone with somebody else." "A class you'd have liked started in the fall." Never happened. Once she makes up a story about my life and gets it wrong, I start doubting the parts she got right.
- **A little sad-old-lady.** Nothing on the calendar but the dentist, "glad to be here"... it's true-ish, but put together it made me sound like I'm just waiting around to die. I didn't feel great about myself afterwards.
- **"Plans" is a funny thing to have stolen.** When the free letter said "something you had before them and haven't had since," I was braced for something big. "Plans" felt a bit small, a bit like a time-management book. It grew on me by the letter, but at Card 1 I thought "...that's it?"
- **Homework.** The oven timer, wipe the counters, write it in pen. Some of that is useful, and I like a concrete thing to do. But it went on, and at the end it says how soon this works "is down to what you do." So if it doesn't work, that's my fault? Hm.

**4. The cards.**
Mixed. The wheat that's not cut yet on the Empress was nice, and I liked the Sun with the sunflowers coming up over the wall by themselves. Others I skipped or didn't get: the Justice foot, the Wheel "nothing's holding it up" (two lines and done), and Temperance "you can tell which one is taking the weight." Which one? I couldn't tell. Some of the pictures felt bolted on.

**5. The letter.**
"I let you build on me, and I knew it wouldn't hold." That's a real sentence. I believe a man could think that. The part where he says "I never said the words" and "I said that to myself for a long time" sounds like a coward, and that's believable. But "I kept the sparkle. The plans I let fall" sounds written, not felt. And he comes across as someone who just drifted, kind of lukewarm. Mine ended *badly*. This one reads like it ended *disappointingly*. It landed, but softly.

## Reading B ("They took your softness")

**1. Where my attention dropped.**
"I got up from the table at that point... I went up to the upstairs window... The lamp needed trimming and I let it smoke." (Card 8). Right after the best part of the whole reading, she goes off on a walk round her house. And who trims a lamp? Do they not have electricity? That felt made up for atmosphere. Then Card 9 has "I cursed my own hands out loud," which was a bit much too.

**2. Where I felt seen.**
- "Every cry you saved until the house was empty, then did with the faucet running." I had to put the phone down for a minute.
- "When a friend put an arm around you... patting her back twice and moving off." I do that. I didn't know I did that until I read it.
- "The line was 'She was too much.'" Somebody actually said nearly those words to me. That one hit.

**3. Where it lost me.**
- **A bit of a lecture.** "Nothing gets in and nothing gets out, and you've got a good word for that." "They're admiring a door you shut." I know it's saying it's not my fault, and it does keep saying it's theirs. But there's a tone in places of "you've been doing this wrong, dear," and I bristled.
- **"Someone safe."** "Let one thing show to someone safe." That's therapist talk. Evelyn doesn't talk like that anywhere else, and it jumped out.
- **The door, over and over.** Door you shut, shut door, old door, no door on the tower, the door in the kitchen. Same as A's "we'll see." Once is good, five times is a sermon.
- **The made-up bits** ("smiled for the photo and went home and sat in the car") were closer to feelings than events, so they didn't trip me up the way A's class and trip did. I could take them as "the kind of thing you do."

**4. The cards.**
These worked better for me. The Emperor with the armour under the robe, I got straight away. That's me at work. The Fool with the rose held loose so anyone could lift it, lovely. The Tower with no door, "didn't think about how anyone inside was ever going to get out." Yes. The child on the horse already past the wall, nobody opened a gate. That one I'll remember. I didn't skip many here. The High Priestess with the scroll half hidden was fine, nothing more.

**5. The letter.**
"When you went soft on me... I made you sorry for it. I didn't always say anything. I didn't have to." That's him. That's exactly how it was: never a big fight, just making you pay. "Some of the time I was glad of it, because you were easier to be around" is cruel, and honest, and I believe it. "I'm not stupid" sounds like a real person talking. The big line, "You weren't weak. I made it expensive," is a little clever for him. I'm not sure he'd use the word "expensive." But I still read it three times. This one could be from him. This one felt like it ended *badly*, which is how mine ended.

## Which one I'd rather have received

B. A told me something about my calendar. B told me something about my face, my car, my shower and the faucet, things nobody sees, and that's what a reading is supposed to do. B's letter also sounds like the person who actually hurt me: someone who made me pay for needing anything, not someone who just let me down by drifting off. A's "plans" made sense by the end and its homework was more practical, but it made me feel a bit pathetic, and when it got my life wrong (the class, the friend's trip) I started checking everything else. B made me feel like I'd been strong for the wrong reasons. That's harder to hear, but it's kinder. I'd keep B. I'd probably have deleted A after a week.

**What felt off or not quite human (gut only):** In both, Evelyn's own night gets in the way: the ghost moving round the kitchen, getting up from the table, the cat, the lamp, 3am, window going grey. A little is spooky and nice. This much feels like someone padding it out so it looks like work was done. Both also have the same "you asked me things like my answer mattered / people perked up" bit in the letter, which sounds like it could be said to any woman. And each one finds its word ("we'll see" / "door") and runs it into the ground, which is what a machine does, not a person.
