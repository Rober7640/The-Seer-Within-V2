# Bonnie's verdict: SU 11, Reading A vs Reading B

I'm Bonnie, 61. I read the free letter, then both readings on my phone in the evening, all the way through.

---

## Reading A: "They took your softness"

**1. Where my attention dropped**

- Card 8, second half: "I told you it makes no difference to whether they've still got it…" By then she'd already said "the bill" and "paying" several times. It started to feel like being walked round the same block, and I skimmed down to the next card.
- Card 5, a smaller dip: "You were exactly as much as a woman who lets things reach her." I read that twice and still don't know what it means.

**2. Where I felt seen**

- "you hadn't cried in front of anybody in a very long time, and you were a little proud of yourself for it." That one went right through me.
- "Every cry you saved until the house was empty, then did with the faucet running." I've done that. I live alone and I've still done that.
- "Years of being the one other people lean on, and leaning on nobody."

**3. Where it lost me**

- **The list in Card 1** ("You cried at the sad part of a movie… You took a compliment and kept it… You let people do things for you…") is too long. Some of it fit me and some didn't. Eight examples in a row feels like casting a wide net to see what I'll grab.
- **Card 3 got muddled.** "They kept the part of you that made them look good, and they took the warmth that came with it. You went on giving them that after you'd stopped being soft with them." Which part is it? I lost the thread.
- **Card 9 felt like being told off for my own feelings.** "You'd find yourself being gentle about them in your head before you'd been gentle with one living soul in your own life." "The warmth you've refused everybody decent." Refused? That's a hard word to hand a woman, and it felt like blame dressed up as insight.
- **Card 8 stung in a way I didn't like.** "Some of them would have stayed for the real one." Maybe true, but it's a mean thing to leave a 61-year-old with. She says it's their bill, and then she spends a page on what I've paid.
- **Evelyn got theatrical.** She swore at her own hands, went to the upstairs window to get her breath back, let the lamp smoke. One of those is atmosphere. All of them together and I start to feel I'm being performed at.

**4. The cards**

Mostly they helped, and I didn't skip them.

- The Tower with no door drawn on it was the best picture in either reading. I could see it.
- The Emperor with armour under his robe and the Lovers not touching also worked.
- The Hanged Man in Card 5 felt bolted on after the point had already been made.
- The Fool's rose was pretty, but I didn't need it.

**5. The letter**

It mostly sounds like a person. "You were easier to be around when you weren't asking me for anything" is a cold, true thing for someone to admit, and that one landed.

The big line, "You weren't weak. I made it expensive.", doesn't sound like anybody I've known. Nobody says "I made it expensive." It sounds like it was written to be the big line.

The ask at the end ("Come back. Or if you won't, at least write to me.") worked, because she'd warned me it was coming and told me what it was.

## Reading B: "They took your voice"

**1. Where my attention dropped**

- Card 7: "When it went wrong with them, you put your head down…" It felt like a detour from the voice idea into a different idea about handling everything alone, and then the Hanged Man came in. I drifted for a paragraph.
- Card 12, the World card, "No ground under her feet." I didn't understand why no ground was a good thing, and I skimmed to the instructions.

**2. Where I felt seen**

- "You ask 'Does that make sense?' right after you've made perfect sense." I actually laughed, and it wasn't a happy laugh.
- "The right thing to say turns up in the car on the way home, word for word, twenty minutes too late."
- "You did say. You said it more than once, and it got talked over." I didn't know how much I wanted somebody to say that.

**3. Where it lost me**

- **Card 8 had a blaming line.** "What you built after it ended has taken more from you than they ever did." That's a sharp thing to say, and I bristled. To be fair, she softened it right away with "It spared you some things, too… I won't pretend that was nothing," and that made it bearable. A never gave me that.
- **The Death card came in cold** at the top of Card 8. At my age you see "Death rides through" and your stomach drops before she explains it means slow years. She should have eased me in.
- **"Easy-going" and "you pick" are a little risky.** I'm not a pushover, and if I'd been in a prickly mood I might have said "that's not me." The details were specific enough that they mostly won me over.
- **Same telling-me-what-I-feel move in Card 9** ("part of you is glad"). But here it came with a picture I believed: rehearsing the speech at the sink, and him cutting in halfway through like he used to. That felt like a warning, not a scolding.

**4. The cards**

These helped more than A's, and I didn't skip any.

- The Chariot with no reins was perfect. That's exactly what going along feels like.
- The Justice scales hanging level, "On its face it sounds fair," set up the "it was never even" turn really well.
- The Moon with its eyes closed was better than A's empty path.
- Death was jarring, as I said above.
- The World I didn't get.

**5. The letter**

This one I believed. "I'd see you give up on a sentence halfway through, and I'd keep right on going." That's a real person confessing a real, small, ugly thing. The big line, "You were right to say it, and I stopped listening on purpose.", is something a person could actually say. "Most of it I needed to hear" got me.

The ask at the end ("write to me. Just once.") sounded like him, and Evelyn's warning about it made sense.

## Which I'd rather have received

B, but it's close.

A has the lines that hit hardest: crying with the faucet running, being proud I hadn't cried in front of anyone, the Tower with no door. Some nights that's what I'd want.

But A leans on me more. "Refused everybody decent," the bill, "some of them would have stayed for the real one." And Evelyn's drama (swearing at her hands, the smoking lamp, the upstairs window) made her feel like she was putting on a show. A's letter has one line that sounds written rather than said.

B was calmer and more practical. "Mexican or Italian, and leave off the 'but I'm easy'" is something I could actually do on Tuesday. B also gave me the one thing I didn't know I needed: "You did say." And B's letter sounds like a man I could picture at a kitchen table. If I'm paying $35 for the words he never sent, I want them to sound like him and not like a greeting card. B's do.

**Gut on anything off:**

- **Too much the same.** Both readings were plainly built on the same bones. Card 7's opening paragraph is nearly word for word, and Card 3's "a woman who lit up a room and made them feel like somebody" feels like it could go to anyone.
- **The night is a bit scripted.** Midnight, then three in the morning, then the window going grey, the ghost moving from shoulder to door to chair. I went along with it, but by the third time stamp I could feel the clock being wound.
- **The "I told you…" callbacks.** There are a lot of them in both, and after a while they read like someone ticking boxes from the first letter.
