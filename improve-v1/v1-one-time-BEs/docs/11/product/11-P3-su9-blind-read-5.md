# Verdict: Faketta reads her two readings

I read the free letter first, then A, then B, all the way through on my phone, the way I'd read it after dinner. I'll be straight with you: most of the two readings are word for word the same. Where they split, they split on the thing I care about most, which is whether he still wants something from me.

---

## Reading A

**1. Where my attention dropped**

"Handing your softness back would mean admitting it was never a weakness..." (Card 4). Up to then it was me, my face, my car, my shower. Here it turned into an explanation of *him*, what he gets to keep believing. That's a puzzle and not a picture. I skimmed the High Priestess and most of the Hierophant. It came back hard at Card 7 ("you clammed up, chewed on it") and stayed with me through the Tower.

I also skimmed Evelyn's kitchen bits the second time round ("The lamp needed trimming and I let it smoke"). Who trims a lamp? It reads like a movie set.

**2. Where I felt seen**

- "you noticed one day that you hadn't cried in front of anybody in a very long time, and you were a little proud"
- "Every cry you saved until the house was empty, then did with the faucet running"
- "You'd find yourself being gentle about them in your head before you'd been gentle with one living soul"

Also "patting her back twice and moving off." I do that. I did it last week.

**3. Where it lost me**

- **"the line was 'She was too much.'"** That's a line I've heard about other women, but I'm not sure it was ever said about me. I've been told I was hard to read, not too much. For me it was the one spot that felt like it was about some other woman.
- **"Their letter ends with an ask... I've left it in. Do nothing with it."** She tells me the pull is strongest in the next five to seven days, and then she hands me the one sentence that would set it off. Leaving it in so I'd "see it for what it is" felt like being handed a lit match and told not to drop it. A bit creepy, honestly.
- **Card 9, the ghost coming "right up to the table... closer than they'd been all night."** Too much. Dramatic for the sake of it.
- **Repetitive.** "Nobody... Nobody..." and the shut door shows up again and again. By Card 8 I'd got the door.
- No lecture, no blame. She was careful about that and I noticed. "That was never your fault" landed fine.

**4. The cards**

The pictures helped when they were short and did one job: the Empress leaning back into the cushions, the Fool holding the rose loose, the Tower with no door, the horse already past the wall. Those I'll remember. I skipped the Hierophant and the Hanged Man; I couldn't tell you what they meant now.

**5. The letter**

Yes, it could be him. That's what's uncomfortable. "Some of the time I was glad of it, because you were easier to be around when you weren't asking me for anything." That one went straight in. "You weren't weak. I made it expensive" is a line I'd keep.

Then "Come back. Or if you won't, at least write me back." That is *exactly* the kind of man who hurts you, and it's exactly the line that would keep me up. It landed, but it landed like a hook, not like closure. I'd reread that line at eleven at night, which is the thing she just told me to stop doing.

## Reading B

**1. Where my attention dropped**

Same place, Card 4, "Handing your softness back would mean admitting..." Same reason. But Card 5 pulled me back faster here, because the examples were mine (see below). Second drop at Card 9, "That path is the day you've kept free." I had to read that sentence twice to understand what she meant.

**2. Where I felt seen**

- "When you're sick, you tell people not to come, that it's only a cold."
- "You end bad news with 'but I'm okay.'"
- "You'd tell anyone who asked that you quit hoping for that a long time ago, and you'd believe it"

That last one. I'd have said I quit hoping. I'd have meant it.

Plus the same ones from A: the faucet running, the birthdays sitting in the car, patting her back twice.

**3. Where it lost me**

- **"The regret where they miss you... is one you've been writing for them, and they've never had it."** That stung, and part of me bristled. It's close to being told I made up the part where he cares. It fits with "stop writing their half," and it's probably true, but it's hard to read at 61, alone, in the evening.
- **"under all that anger you left one small spot soft."** What anger? Nothing before that said I was angry. That came from nowhere and felt like it was about some other woman.
- **"Once it's back with you, they can stop thinking about you."** Cold. Honest, I think, but cold.
- Same repetition as A, same stagey kitchen and lamp.
- No sales trick in it, and nothing preachy. The "she'll be fine" bit could have tipped into blame and it didn't: "Counting on that was their choice."

**4. The cards**

Same as A: the Empress, the Fool, the Tower and the Sun earned their place. The Moon is better here: "There's nobody on it, coming or going," and then "That path is the day you've kept free." Once I got it, it was the strongest picture in the reading. Hierophant and Hanged Man I skipped again.

**5. The letter**

Mostly the same as A, and those parts still land. "I kept the get-up-and-go" sounds a little less like him than "I kept the handy part." "Handy" was a nasty little word, and it was right.

The ending is different: "I'm not asking you for anything. Not to forgive me, not to see me, not an answer. Don't write to me, and don't go looking for me." Could he say that? Maybe. "Don't go looking for me" sounds a touch like Evelyn talking through him, because it's her own advice word for word. But it *closes*. I'd put the phone down after this one. And "There won't be one word after it" earlier in the reading makes that ending feel earned, not tacked on.

## Which I'd rather have received

B. A is the more exciting read: the ghost creeping closer, him wanting me back. Some vain old part of me liked hearing he still wants something. But that's the whole problem. A tells me the next week is when the pull is worst and then ends on "Come back, or at least write me back." I paid $35 to be done with him, and A leaves the door open a crack with his hand on it. B hurts more in the middle. "They don't regret losing you" and "the day isn't coming" are hard things to read. But it's the kind of hard that lets you finish. "She'll be fine," "only a cold" and "but I'm okay" are my life. "She was too much" isn't. B is the one I'd read once, cry over a little, and actually put down. That's what I paid for.

**Gut on what felt off or not quite human (both):** every card follows the same pattern: a bold line, then the picture, then "That's how it goes with you." By Card 6 I could feel the machine. Evelyn's night (window, smoking lamp, swearing at her hands, the ghost moving around the kitchen) reads staged, like she's acting out being moved. "Faketta" comes up so often it starts to sound like a mail merge. The lists of "you used to..." have seven items each, and seven is a lot; a couple read like they were put there to catch whoever's reading. And in B, "under all that anger" is the one line that felt like it slipped in from someone else's reading.
