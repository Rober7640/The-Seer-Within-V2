# Rhonda's verdict: Reading A vs Reading B

I'm reading this on my phone after supper. The free letter got me with "Someone decent asks you out and by Thursday you've found the fault." That's why I paid. So what I wanted from the full reading was simple: tell me what they took, and why nobody new gets through.

---

## Reading A ("They took your softness")

**1. Where my attention dropped**
- Card 8, "I got up from the table at that point, which I don't do..." I don't need Evelyn's trip to the upstairs window or the lamp smoking. I paid to hear about me, not her night. I skimmed down to "Here's the deal."
- Card 3, "And they didn't take all of you. They kept the use of you..." I read it twice and still couldn't tell you what it means. I lost the thread for a minute.

**2. Where I felt seen**
- "you leaned into it instead of patting her back twice and moving off" — I do that. Exactly that.
- "hadn't cried in front of anybody in a very long time, and you were a little proud of yourself" — yes, and I didn't like being caught.
- "Years of birthdays where you smiled for the photo and went home and sat in the car." That one got me.

**3. Where it lost me**
- "People tell you they admire it. They have no idea what they're praising. They're admiring a door you shut." That's a slap at the one thing I'm proud of. I bristled at it.
- There's a lot of crying in this one: in the car, in the shower, "with the faucet running so even the walls wouldn't hear." Some of it's me. Laid on that thick, though, it starts to sound like a sadder woman than I am. I'm not a victim.
- Card 5, "Nobody has to shrink you now. You do it yourself, and you get there first." Fair, but preachy. Same with "they go find somebody who'll let them." That stings, and it's partly blaming me, even though she keeps saying it's on them.
- "Softness" comes up so many times it starts to sound like a word off a greeting card.
- Evelyn's night (cursing her own hands, 3 a.m., the chair going empty) is theatre. I liked it the first time and got tired of it by the third.

**4. The cards**
They helped, mostly. The Sun, with the horse already past the wall and nobody opening a gate, is the best one. I understood it right away and I'll remember it. The Lovers card, him looking at her and her looking past him, made sense too. The Tower with no door fit. The Hierophant I just skipped.

**5. The letter**
Mostly yes. "Some of the time I was glad of it, because you were easier to be around when you weren't asking me for anything" is cruel and honest, and it sounds like him. "I'm too proud" sounds like him. But "You weren't weak. I made it expensive." is not how a proud man talks. That's a line somebody wrote. It's the big line and it rang a bit hollow. And "What you do with it is on you" at the very end felt cold, like a door closing on me.

## Reading B ("They took your nerve")

**1. Where my attention dropped**
- Card 6, "On the Wheel of Fortune, a sphinx lies along the top of the wheel with a sword..." I had no idea what the snake or the sphinx had to do with me. I skipped down to the regret part.
- Card 5 opens with Evelyn moving across her kitchen to the door, "getting on for midnight." Again, her night, not mine.

**2. Where I felt seen**
- "you think of exactly what you'd have said, every word of it, and you say it to the windshield." That is me. I laughed out loud.
- "you say 'it's fine, it doesn't matter,' about a thing that does." Guilty.
- "It's the only place you've still been brave, and you've been brave there for nobody." That one sat me back.

**3. Where it lost me**
- "Somebody had to make the decisions... about money and plans and how the days went." That sounds like a controlling husband of twenty years. That isn't necessarily my story. This is the part where it felt like it was about some other woman.
- The free letter told me nobody new can reach me and I find the fault by Thursday. "Nerve" doesn't really explain that. Speaking up at dinner isn't why the nice ones don't get through. It answered a question I didn't ask.
- "It suited them" is in here over and over. "It suited them, and they let it stay that way." I noticed it four or five times. That's when it starts feeling like a form letter.
- "Patience isn't what it is. It's waiting to be told something's worth the trouble." It's a little insulting, though it did give me credit first ("You should be, some"), and I appreciated that.
- Death card: a woman my age doesn't love seeing "Death rides a white horse" in her reading, even with the explanation.

**4. The cards**
Mixed. The Star, with her foot on the water and her knee still on the grass, is lovely. I got it, and it doesn't make me sound reckless. The Hanged Man's folded free leg coming back as the World's dancing leg is clever, but it felt like something a teacher would point out. The Chariot with no reins worked. The Wheel of Fortune and Justice I skipped.

**5. The letter**
This one sounds more like a real person. "I'm not good at this kind of thing, so it won't come out right." "I'm sorry for that. I don't say it much." That's a proud person trying. But "You could have stood up to me, and I made sure you didn't" clashes with "I didn't plan any of it" two paragraphs up. "Made sure" means he planned it. And it has the same cold last line, "What you do with it is on you."

## Which I'd rather have received

A, but it's close. The free letter promised to tell me why nobody new can reach me, and A actually answers that. The part they reach for isn't there because I shut it, both ways, coming in and going out. That's what I paid for. And the Tower bill, the birthdays, the people who quit asking, is the strongest stretch in either one. B has better single lines ("say it to the windshield," "brave there for nobody"), and its letter sounds more like a real man. But it drifted into a story about being bossed around in a marriage, and it never connected that back to why I push decent people away. I'd still have liked A more if it had laid off the crying a bit and not called my strength a shut door.

**Gut, on what felt off or not quite human:** in both, Evelyn's long night (the tea, the one at her shoulder, cursing at 3 a.m., the window going grey, the chair turning back into furniture) felt staged, like a script she runs for everyone. And both letters end on "What you do with it is on you." No real person who'd just apologized would sign off like that. It reads like Evelyn talking, not him. The way both readings keep saying "I told you..." to point back at the free letter felt a bit like being walked through a checklist.
