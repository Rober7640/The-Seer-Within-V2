# Gail's verdict: Reading A vs Reading B

I read the free letter first. It got me. "You already know who I mean" was true, I did. So I paid. Then I sat with both of these on my phone after supper, one a night.

---

## Reading A ("They took your softness")

**1. Where my attention dropped**

- *"The High Priestess holds a scroll with writing on it..."* (Card 4). Short and abstract, and I couldn't see me in it. I skimmed.
- *"I got up from the table at that point, which I don't do..."* (Card 8). The upstairs window, the lamp needing trimming, letting it smoke. Who has an oil lamp? That's about Evelyn's night, not mine. I skimmed it to get back to me.
- By Card 11 I had read "if this person hurt you, you owe them no reply" for the third time. I know. You said.

**2. Where I felt seen**

- "you leaned into it instead of patting her back twice and moving off." That's me at every funeral for ten years.
- "you hadn't cried in front of anybody in a very long time, and you were a little proud of yourself"
- "Every cry you saved until the house was empty, then did with the faucet running"

And from the letter: "you were easier to be around when you weren't asking me for anything." I had to put the phone down after that one.

**3. Where it lost me**

- **The money talk piles up.** "Cost," "the bill," "expensive," "charged," "the price." Once is fine. By the fifth time it sounds like someone working a theme, not talking to me.
- **Card 7 could be about any woman.** "You took care of everybody in sight... you ran yourself ragged." Lots of women my age did that, and plenty didn't. It's a safe guess.
- **"A woman who knew what they needed before they'd said a word."** Flattering, and I've had readers flatter me before. I didn't trust it.
- **A little preachy.** In Card 5 ("You still do their work for them... you get there first") and Card 8 ("every one of those years went on your bill"), I felt told off. It did say it wasn't my fault, but it said it right before lecturing me, so it didn't help much.
- **"They kept the use of you, the part that kept track of their moods."** I read that twice and still don't fully know what it means.
- **The ghost.** It sat at her shoulder, went to the door, left, then sat in "the chair where you'd sit." A bit much, and a bit creepy. It kept me reading, though, I'll admit it.

**4. The cards**

They mostly helped. The Fool with the rose held loose, so anybody could lift it, is the one picture I still remember. The child on the horse already past the wall was good too. The Lovers with her looking past him worked. The High Priestess and the Hierophant I skimmed.

**5. The letter**

Mostly yes. "I made you sorry for it. I didn't always say anything. I didn't have to." That sounds like a real person, a proud, cold one. "I told myself you were too much. I said it to other people too" hurt, because that word got said about me. The big line, "You weren't weak. I made it expensive," doesn't sound like him. He'd never say "expensive" like that. It sounds like Evelyn wrote it, which she did, but I wasn't supposed to feel that. Still, it landed. The ending, "the chair... was only furniture again," gave me a chill.

## Reading B ("They took your trust")

**1. Where my attention dropped**

- *"On Justice, a figure holds up a pair of scales..."* (Card 4). "Put it on the scale... the pans stay level." Too clever, so I skimmed.
- *"Every kind thing somebody said to you that you turned over looking for the hook..."* (Card 8). A long list, and half of it wasn't me ("every paper you read twice, fine print and all, and still didn't sign"?). My eyes slid down it.
- The Evelyn-at-the-back-door part. Same as A, I skipped ahead.

**2. Where I felt seen**

- "You let other people drive, book the table, read the map and settle the check"
- "People tell you you're nobody's fool, and they mean it kindly."
- "The night before plans, you text to make sure they're still on." Guilty.

**3. Where it lost me**

- **It hedged.** "Whether it came as one big lie or a hundred small ones, you know which it was." That's exactly what the cheap readers do: give you both and let you pick. It's the moment I thought "oh, here we go."
- **Some details belonged to another woman.** "There's a spare key to your place that nobody else knows about." No there isn't. Also "took a picture of what somebody wrote you" and reading the fine print and not signing. Those are someone else.
- **Trust felt like the obvious guess.** Evelyn told me most women guess wrong. Trust is the first thing I'd have guessed, so being told it was trust felt a bit flat. It confirmed my guess instead of surprising me.
- **The sting in Card 9:** "you were saving the believing for the one who'd earned it least." True, maybe, but it felt like a scolding.
- **"Wasn't true"** is an odd way to talk about a person. Old-fashioned.
- The same "you owe them no reply" repeats as in A.

**4. The cards**

They were good in places. The Strength card with the chain of flowers the lion could break and doesn't was the best card in either reading, honestly. The dog and the wolf making the same noise was clever and true. The Emperor with both hands full, passing nothing, was good. The Justice scales and the Star I skimmed.

**5. The letter**

It's softer and vaguer than A's. "I didn't always tell you the whole truth" is about what? It never says. A real person who lied to me would have one thing in mind, and this letter talks around it. "It was easier to let you keep believing than to be what you believed I was" is a good line, and I'll give it that. "You were right to believe me" is something I've wanted to hear. But it reads like an apology written to cover any liar, not mine. It landed softer than A's.

## Which I'd rather have received

A, though not by a mile. They're the same reading underneath. Same order, same ghost, same Card 7 almost word for word, same homework shape. What made the difference was the thing they said was taken, and the letter. B said trust, which is what I'd have guessed, and it hedged on how I lost it ("one big lie or a hundred small ones"). That's the oldest trick there is, and it made me feel read *at*. A said softness. I wouldn't have guessed that, and then it described me patting a friend's back twice and moving off, and crying with the faucet running. That's when I stopped feeling like a customer. A's letter also sounds like a real cold man, not a sorry-card. Where A loses me is all the "cost" and "bill" talk and a couple of spots that felt like being told off. B's flower-chain lion is the prettiest thing in either one, and its homework (let a friend pick the restaurant, don't look up the menu) is something I'd actually do. If I could, I'd take A with B's Strength card.

**Gut, on anything that felt not quite human:** In both, the same phrases keep coming back ("it only comes back whole," "you owe them no reply," "here's the deal, and I want you to have it exactly"), like a form with my name dropped in. The middle of the night is too neatly staged: midnight, three in the morning, the window going grey, full light. A real woman's night isn't timed like a play. In A, "I made it expensive" is a writer's line in a proud man's mouth. In B, the spare key and the fine print sound like a list of guesses.
