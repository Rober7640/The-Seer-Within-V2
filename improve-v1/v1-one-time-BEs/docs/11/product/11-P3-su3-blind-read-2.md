# Testina's verdict: Reading A vs Reading B

I read the free letter first, then both readings, start to finish, on my phone after supper.

---

## Reading A

**1. Where my attention dropped**

- A small dip early on, at "Here's how I know: your name and date told me who I was laying them for." That paragraph about sorting out a mess by suppertime reads like a horoscope. It also doesn't explain how being organized proves somebody loved me. It's flattering, and I don't trust flattering.
- The real drop came after the letter, at "Signs to watch for:". I'd just had my moment with the letter, and then it was predictions, a list of signs, three things to do and a fourth thing on top. It turned into homework. The radio at the very end pulled me back in.

**2. Where I felt seen**

- "Since then, whenever anybody asks, you've said you're fine, and you are. You just haven't been happy since." That's the first thing I read and it stopped me. That's exactly it. Nobody would call me unhappy. I'm fine.
- "At a birthday or a cookout, you're the one taking the pictures, so you're hardly in any." My daughter said that to me last Thanksgiving.
- "You've clapped at weddings and held new babies and meant it." That one made me put the phone down for a minute.

**3. Where it lost me**

- Card 5's answer, "Because 'I'm fine' let them off." So it's taken this long because of me? The next line softens it ("it's true"), but the bold part reads like blame, and I bristled.
- "It was midnight when I laid the Wheel down, and the Chaperone started in." Wait, the Chaperone is supposed to be *my* thing. Why is it tutting at Evelyn's kitchen table? It confused me, and it felt like a writer being clever.
- "I can read it out to you, and I have." Except I hadn't seen the letter yet at that point. Small, but I noticed.
- "When somebody asks you along to the dancing." Nobody's asked me to "the dancing" in twenty years, and that isn't how we talk here. That line was about some other woman.
- The bullet lists (what people see, signs to watch for) feel like a magazine quiz. Each item is good on its own, but a list doesn't sound like a person talking to me.
- The Tower: "there's no door drawn on it anywhere." I know the Tower. It's the one with lightning and people falling. Mentioning only the door felt picked to fit the point.

**4. The cards**

They mostly helped, and I didn't skip them. The Empress with the water falling "for nobody in particular," the Hierophant ("From behind, anybody kneeling that neatly looks fine"), the snake sliding down with nothing reaching out to stop it, and the sun coming up behind the bishop on Death all stuck with me. The line "your birth name carries a 3 at its heart" made me believe the first card wasn't random. The Tower and the World were thin. Honestly, the radio did more work than any card.

**5. The letter**

Could it be from him? Partly. "I'd say, 'Huh,' and go back to what I was doing" and "I said, 'That's nice,' and went to find my coat" are things I can see him doing. "The smile you'd give a stranger in a checkout line" landed hard. "Happy the way some people are tall" is something he'd never have said out loud, but this is the letter he never sent, so I'll allow it.

What doesn't fit: it says "You know what I did," and then what he confesses is being flat and tired with me. It ended worse than that for me, and the letter never goes near the actual ending. I respect that it didn't guess, but the big wound is left alone. And "Lucky them... sleep through a crisis for once" turns me back into the handy one, the woman who files the paperwork. That's a strange note to end on after saying the happy part was the best thing about me.

The ask at the end ("Come and see me") worked. Evelyn had warned me, so I read it as the trap, and that made me trust her. "A request for an encore" is a bit cute, though.

## Reading B

**1. Where my attention dropped**

- Right away, at "This reading settles it. It names what they took, answers the questions..." That reads like the back of a box. Then came "Here are the questions your twelve cards answer, in the order I turned them," a contents page with twelve card names. I scrolled straight past it.
- The worst drop was inside the letter, around "None of it was your fault. You being happy was never the problem. I was." I'd already read that line, in italics, under card 3. By then I recognized nearly every paragraph and started skimming the letter, which is the one part I paid for.

**2. Where I felt seen**

- "You were pleased about them, the polite way, and went on to the next thing." Yes. That's how I take good news now.
- "Every November 5 I think about picking up the phone, and every November 5 I put it back down." That got me. It's the one line that made me think of a real person on a real day.
- "They don't want you. They want somebody that glad to see them again." Blunt, and true.

**3. Where it lost me**

- An italic quote from the letter sits under every single card. After three or four it felt like a formula, like slots being filled. Worse, it used the letter up before I got to it.
- "'You used to be so happy.' Or, 'Where'd you go?'" Two options so one of them hits. I've had readers do that to me before. It's fishing.
- "People drifted toward it at parties and in waiting rooms. A dull errand turned into a good afternoon because you came along." Lovely, but it could be said to any woman alive. Flattery.
- "So stop asking what's wrong with you." Don't tell me what to stop. That's a lecture.
- Card 5 has the same "it let them off" as A. It still reads like it's my fault it's taken this long, though "you were proud of that, and you'd earned it" helps.
- The letter feels stitched together: "I kept the handy part..." then "I trusted your judgment..." then "I saw all that, and I let it go on." The paragraphs jump around like pieces put back in a row.
- "Not fine, not getting by. Happy." came round three times. I got it the first time.

**4. The cards**

B used the pictures better than A. The Fool holding the rose loosely, "for real, but loosely," the High Priestess's scroll half covered, the Emperor who won't turn his head, the Hanged Man with his hands behind his back, and the Strength woman closing the lion's mouth "gently and with both hands" all meant something. But the italic quote under each picture pulled my eye off it, and by the middle I was skipping the pictures to read the quotes.

**5. The letter**

Same man as A, more of him. The new bits are the best bits: November 5, "the way you know you've left a light on in an empty house," "I liked being the one you told first," "I saw your face drop." Those made him more real than in A.

But I'd read most of it already, one piece per card, so it felt like a recap and not a letter. The moment I was waiting for, the whole thing coming back to me, didn't feel like a moment. "It's a bill, and nobody owes it" was clearer than A's "encore," and I liked "In my first letter I said one of these two ways was a trap. This is the trap." Evelyn kept her word there. The ending, "the light comes through the kitchen window... 'Well, look at you,'" was lovely and short.

## Which I'd rather have received

A. The radio stuck on the talk station is the thing I'll remember. I'd tell my friend Carol about that first, because my kitchen radio *is* on the talk station, and the house *is* quiet at night. A opened on me, not on a list of what I was about to get. It had the little true things (taking the pictures, clapping at weddings, humming then stopping myself), and Evelyn sitting up all night from midnight to full light made it feel like she'd actually done twenty-four hours of work for my $35. Most of all, A saved the letter. I read it fresh, and the coat moment hit me cold. B used the cards better and kept the free letter's promises more tightly, and its letter has the two best lines of either one (November 5, the light left on). But B gave the letter away a spoonful at a time, so by the end I was skimming the one part I'd paid for.

## What felt off or not quite human (gut only)

- **A:** The Chaperone showing up at Evelyn's table to tut at her. "The dancing." The checklists after the letter, which read like a printout and not a woman talking.
- **B:** The quote-under-every-card pattern felt machine-made. It made me wonder whether the letter came first and the reading was cut up out of it, which gives the trick away a little. Then there's the contents page, and the "Or, 'Where'd you go?'" hedge.
- **Both:** The "sorted by suppertime" paragraph is word for word the same in both and reads like a horoscope. "Lucky them... sleep through a crisis" makes me sound like a secretary. And in both, the letter says "you know what I did" but only confesses to being flat with me, which is smaller than how it really ended.
