# Sharon's verdict, SU 8 (A = "softness", B = "worth")

I read both on my phone, in bed, the same night. The free letter got me because of the "found the fault by Thursday" part and the rereading late at night. I wouldn't tell anybody it got me. So that's what I was hoping the paid one would carry on from.

---

## Reading A ("They took your softness")

**1. Where my attention dropped.**
"I got up from the table at that point, which I don't do in the middle of a spread..." (Card 8). Up to there I was hooked. Then Evelyn's going to the upstairs window, the lamp "needed trimming and I let it smoke," the shadow went over by the door. Who has a lamp that needs trimming? It felt like a show put on for me. By Card 9 she's cursing her own hands out loud at three in the morning, and I started skimming her parts and reading only the parts about me.

Card 3 lost me for a minute too (the Fool, more on that below).

**2. Where I felt seen.**
- "you noticed one day that you hadn't cried in front of anybody in a very long time, and you were a little proud" — that one sat me up. I *am* proud of it. I didn't know that was a thing to notice.
- "patting her back twice and moving off" — that's me exactly when a friend hugs me.
- "then did with the faucet running so even the walls wouldn't hear" / "told it later like a funny story" — I do both. My daughter thinks my bad news always comes out as a funny story.

Close behind: "She was too much." I've been told that, near enough word for word. And "You cut a story short at dinner in case it runs on."

**3. Where it lost me.**
- **Confusing:** Card 3, the Fool. "A young man standing at the edge of a cliff with a white rose..." and then "You held yours like that. And they didn't take all of you. They kept the use of you..." I read it twice and I'm still not sure what "the use of you" means. Also, a young man on a cliff is me?
- **Generic flattery:** "a woman who lit up a room and made them feel like somebody." Every reader says I lit up a room. That's the vague stuff I've been burned on.
- **Fake/staged:** the Evelyn diary bits get bigger and bigger. Mint tea, the shoulder, the window, the lamp, cursing her hands, 3am, then dawn and "I'm not waiting on you anymore" out loud to her kitchen. One or two of those I'd have bought. That many and I could see somebody building it up on purpose.
- **A little preachy:** "You still do their work for them... You do it yourself, and you get there first." That stung. She does say "that was never your fault," so I let it go, but I felt told off for a second.
- **Repetitive:** every card does the same thing. Describe the picture, "that's you," then "the next time someone... notice..." By Card 7 I could see it coming.
- The Card 7 caretaking part ("you took care of everybody in sight") felt like it could be about any woman my age. My sister would say the same about herself.

**4. The cards.**
The first few helped. The Empress leaning back in the cushions, the Emperor with armor under the robe ("never takes the second thing off"). I got those. The Tower with no door was good: "didn't think about how anyone inside was ever going to get out." The Fool, the Hierophant and the Hanged Man I skimmed. They felt like she had to fit a card in. The Sun with the horse already past the wall: that one I looked at twice, and I liked it.

**5. The letter.**
Mostly yes. "Some of the time I was glad of it, because you were easier to be around when you weren't asking me for anything." That's the meanest, truest thing in either reading. It's exactly the kind of thing he'd have thought and never said. "I'm not stupid" sounds like him too. "You weren't weak. I made it expensive" is a good line, but "made it expensive" sounds more like Evelyn than a man. And "I kept the sparkle" — what sparkle? Nobody I know says sparkle. The ending ("Don't write to me, and don't go looking for me") hurt, but I think it was meant to. It landed. I'd read it again.

The very last line, "What you do with it is on you," felt a bit cold after all that.

## Reading B ("They took your sense of your own worth")

**1. Where my attention dropped.**
"The Wheel of Fortune hangs in the sky with nothing holding it up..." (Card 7). I didn't follow what a wheel in the sky had to do with me. After that, every card was money. Price, bill, paid short, marked down, settle up, paid in full, kept the difference. Around Card 8 ("They ran up the bill. You've been the one paying it off") I thought: all right, I get it, it's a bill.

**2. Where I felt seen.**
- "You drink out of the chipped mug and put the good ones out for company. You take the end piece of the loaf." — I laughed out loud, and not happily. I do that.
- "told everybody not to make a fuss over your birthday, and then been a little hurt when nobody did" — ouch. Last March.
- "You'd be gracious about it. You'd let them finish." — I *have* practised what I'd say back. That one was private, and she got it.

Also good: the nice dress kept in the closet "for an occasion that never comes up," and leaving the tags on something a week and taking it back.

**3. Where it lost me.**
- **Generic in a different way:** the details are good, but they're details about "a woman my age who doesn't ask for much." Reading down the prices on the menu, "oh, this old thing, it was on sale." Half my friends do that. It felt less like it was about *him and me* and more like a magazine quiz on being a pushover.
- **Bit of blame/lecture:** "You've already marked yourself down." And Card 12's job is "If your order comes out wrong, say 'This isn't what I ordered.'" I paid $35 to be told to send my soup back? That's a self-help column. It made the whole thing feel smaller.
- **Repetitive:** the money talk on every card, plus the same "next time someone offers... watch your hand" ending as A.
- **Confusing order:** Card 9 jumps from the Moon drops, to Evelyn with her palm on the card and the empty doorway, then back to "You know the day I mean." I lost the thread and had to scroll back.
- **Staged, but less:** the cat that wouldn't come in was a nice, ordinary touch. I believed that more than A's lamp. Still a lot of shoulder/door/window business, though.

**4. The cards.**
The Empress with the crown of stars "like nobody ever had to tell her she could wear it" — lovely. The Hierophant ministers taking "the low place, nobody had to push them down there" — got it. The Tower with the crown knocked off, "it hasn't landed anywhere" — good. The sunflowers over the wall — I liked that better than the horse, honestly. Strength with the "whole wreath, not one flower" — that made me smile. The Wheel and the Hanged Man with his hands behind his back I skipped. The pictures were a bit better in B than in A.

**5. The letter.**
Less than A. "You never once sent me a bill" and "I knew what I was paying" — he wasn't an accountant. That's Evelyn's money talk coming out of his mouth. The big line, "You were worth more than I gave, and I knew what I was paying," I had to read twice to get, and a line that's supposed to hit you shouldn't need that. "I'm not much good at writing things like this" was a nice human touch. "It got easier to say the more I said it" sounded real. But overall it felt like a summary of the reading, not a man finally talking.

## Which I'd rather have received

A. B had the better little everyday pictures, the chipped mug and the birthday and the tags. It made me wince more often. But A went deeper, and it's the one that would have made me cry. The not crying in front of anybody and being proud of it, the "too much," the faucet running. That's the actual damage he did, not the restaurant habits. And A's letter sounds like a man who was glad I stopped asking him for things. That's the ugly true thing I've always suspected and never heard. B made me feel like a pushover who needs to send her soup back. A made me feel like something real was taken. For $35 I want the second one.

## What felt off or not quite human (gut)

- Both are built on the same frame. I didn't know that reading them, but by the middle of each I could feel it: describe the picture, "that's you," "the next time... notice...," card after card. Real people don't talk in such even steps.
- Evelyn's night (tea, shoulder, getting up, the door, 3am, dawn, saying it out loud) is the same in both. In A it's turned up so loud it starts to feel like a performance.
- Lines like "She isn't perched on the edge... she's letting the cushions hold her" and "He isn't gripping it or hiding it" — that "she isn't this, she's that" pattern keeps coming back, and it sounds like writing, not a woman talking to me.
- "I kept the sparkle" is in both letters, and nobody talks like that.
- "A woman who lit up a room" is in both. It's the one line where I thought: this could be anybody's.
