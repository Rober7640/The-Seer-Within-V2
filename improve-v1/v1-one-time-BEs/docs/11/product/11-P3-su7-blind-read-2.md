# Peggy's verdict: Reading A vs Reading B

I read the free letter first, sitting up in bed. It got me. "You already know who I mean" was true, and so was the line about knowing the last message word for word. That's why I paid. So I came to both of these wanting to be told *what* they took, and wanting it to sound like him.

---

## Reading A ("They took your softness")

**1. Where my attention dropped**
At Card 4: "Handing your softness back would mean admitting it was never a weakness. And if it was never a weakness, then you were right..." I went back over that twice and it still went in a circle. Right about what? It read like it was there to fill the slot for "why they kept it." I skimmed to Card 5.

I drifted again in the middle of Card 9, at "I'd sat back down to turn this one, and when it came up I cursed my own hands out loud." By then I'd had a lot of Evelyn walking around her house at midnight. I wanted my reading, not her night.

**2. Where I felt seen**
- "you leaned into it instead of patting her back twice and moving off"
- "then did with the faucet running so even the walls wouldn't hear"
- "Years of birthdays where you smiled for the photo and went home and sat in the car."

Also "You say sorry before you ask for anything." I do that. And the Moon part about keeping "one small spot soft, just in case. It was for them" made me put the phone down for a minute.

**3. Where it lost me**
- **Generic:** "You cried at the sad part of a movie with other people in the room..." The whole "you used to be like her" list could be any woman who ever got hurt. Softness is what everybody loses. That's the vague-reading feeling I've been burned by. It got better once it moved on to what I do *now*.
- **A bit blaming:** "You still do their work for them... Nobody has to shrink you now. You do it yourself." I know she says it's not my fault right before. It still stung. Then Card 8 says "you've been the one paying it," and Card 7 says the decent men "go home with it, and you're left with the door." That's a lot of "you" in a row.
- **Theatrical:** The upstairs window, the lamp that "needed trimming and I let it smoke" (who has an oil lamp?), cursing her own hands, the ghost going over by the door. One of those would be atmosphere. Four starts to feel staged.
- **Preachy:** "That's what strong was supposed to mean. Soft hands on something fierce." That's a greeting card.
- **Repetition:** "strong" gets explained in Card 2, Card 8 and Card 12. I got it the first time.

**4. The cards**
They helped, mostly. The Emperor with armor under his robe was a good picture. So was the Tower with no door, and the horse already past the wall on the Sun. That one made the point better than the words did. The Fool holding the rose loose was nice. The Hanged Man and the Hierophant I skimmed. They felt hung on.

**5. The letter**
Parts of it could be him. "Some of the time I was glad of it, because you were easier to be around when you weren't asking me for anything." That's exactly the kind of thing he'd think and never say. It hurt in the right way. "I'm not stupid" sounded like him too.

But "You weren't weak. I made it expensive." No man I've known talks like that. That's a writer's sentence, and it's the one the whole reading builds up to, so it stood out. "When you went soft on me" also sounded off coming from him. It landed about two-thirds of the way.

## Reading B ("They took your faith in your own judgment")

**1. Where my attention dropped**
Cards 2, 3 and 4 in a row: the Hierophant "between two stone pillars," the High Priestess "between two pillars," then Justice "sits between two pillars." By the third set of pillars they'd blurred together. Card 4 ("Give it back and the sword comes down") went by without me taking it in.

**2. Where I felt seen**
- "'Am I crazy, or was that strange?' ... You still don't quite believe it until she's said it twice."
- "I'm probably wrong, but. I might be imagining it, but."
- "most of the time you were right, and that got to be a problem for me." (in the letter)

Also the friend who cancels three times, where "you knew by the second time how this would go." That one's real.

**3. Where it lost me**
- **Didn't fit the free letter:** The free letter said I find something wrong with every decent man by Thursday, that I'm too picky. This one says I don't trust my own read on people. Card 7 tries to make them fit ("you hand the call to anybody but yourself, and somebody always has a reason"), but I could feel it straining. Which is it, Evelyn? Am I too sure about men, or not sure enough?
- **Slow start:** Card 1 tells me I'm "the fair one" and "a little proud of that" for a long while before it gets anywhere.
- **Thin where it should hurt:** In Card 8, the "bill" is one story about one person I had a bad feeling about. Reading A's version of this card had me in tears. This one I just nodded at.
- **Slightly talked down to:** "Keep asking people the things they'd actually know, like the way to somewhere you've never been." I'm 61. I know how to ask directions.
- **Preachy:** "If anybody's going to say out loud that you were right, it'll have to be you." Poster line.
- **Same lines as a form:** "Whatever you gave, you gave honestly. Everything done with it after that is on them." and "Here's the deal, and I want you to have it exactly." Those sound like stock phrases, the kind she says to everybody.

**4. The cards**
Mixed. The Chariot with no reins was clever and I looked at it twice. The crown knocked off the Tower, "still in the air, tipping," was the best picture in either reading. The dog and wolf howling on the Moon tied straight back to the free letter's "they sound the same," which I liked. But the three pillar cards in a row made me start skipping the card descriptions.

**5. The letter**
This one sounded more like a real person. "You saw it, you were right, and I told you that you weren't." Plain words, the way a man actually talks. "What I didn't like was how sure you were" was cold enough to be true. I believed this letter more than A's.

One catch: he says "I'm too proud" in the first paragraph and "I'm not proud of any of it" a few lines down. That made me blink.

## Which I'd rather have received

Reading A, but not by much. It moved me. The birthdays in the car, the faucet running, the one soft spot I kept "just in case" for him: I'd read those parts again. And the thing it asks me to do (tell a friend I missed her, in those words) is something I could actually do this week and would want to. Reading B was sharper in places, and its letter sounded much more like a real man. "You saw it, you were right" is what I'd want to hear. But B didn't match what the free letter told me about myself, and the middle cards blurred together, so it felt more like a clever idea than like my life. A's weak spot is that "softness" could be any hurt woman's story. It got away with that because the small details were so true.

**What felt off or not quite human (gut only):** In both, Evelyn's all-night story (the ghost moving round the kitchen, leaving at 3 a.m., coming back at dawn when she "stopped waiting") felt like a play put on for me. A laid it on thicker. Both had the same tidy stock lines a real person wouldn't repeat so neatly. And A's big sentence, "I made it expensive," is a sentence nobody's ex has ever said.
