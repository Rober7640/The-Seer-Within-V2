# Wanda's verdict: Reading A vs Reading B

I read the free letter first, on the couch, phone in hand. It got me. I knew who she meant before I finished the first line, and that's why I paid. So I came to both of these wanting three things: what did they take, are they giving it back or fishing, and what do I do about the pull. I got all three in both. Here's how each one sat with me.

---

## Reading A ("They took your softness")

### 1. Where my attention dropped
"I got up from the table at that point, which I don't do in the middle of a spread. I went up to the upstairs window..." (Card 8). I was right in the middle of the hardest part, about what it's cost me, and suddenly I'm reading about Evelyn's stairs and her lamp smoking. A lamp? In a kitchen? I skimmed the rest of that paragraph to get back to me. Same thing again at Card 9 with "I cursed my own hands out loud." Once was atmosphere. By the third or fourth time it felt like a play.

### 2. Where I felt seen
- "you hadn't cried in front of anybody in a very long time, and you were a little proud of yourself for it." That's me, and I've never said it to anybody.
- "Every cry you saved until the house was empty, then did with the faucet running" I had to put the phone down after that one.
- "you leaned into it instead of patting her back twice and moving off." My daughter does the hugging. I do the pat.

Honorable mention: "When you cried, it was a scene. When you needed something, you were needy." I've heard that, almost word for word.

### 3. Where it lost me
- **Confusing:** "They kept the use of you, the part that caught what they missed, and they took the warmth that came with it." I read it three times. I still don't know what "kept the use of you" means. It sounds like a sentence somebody wrote quickly.
- **Close to preachy or blaming:** "Nobody has to shrink you now. You do it yourself, and you get there first." and the bill business in Card 8 ("every one of those years went on your bill"). She does say it's on them, more than once, and that helps. But I got the feeling of being told off gently, and I don't love that.
- **A little generic:** Card 7, "you took care of everybody in sight." Maybe. That's every woman my age. It didn't sting the way the crying lines did.
- **Theatrical:** the ghost goes to the door, leaves, comes back, sits in my chair. It's too much stage business. I believed the cards more than I believed the ghost.

### 4. The cards
They helped, mostly. The Fool with the rose "held loose in his lowered hand" so anybody could take it. I got that right away, and it made me sad in a good way. The Tower with "no door drawn anywhere on it" was the best one. Strength at the end, "soft hands on something fierce", turned the word "strong" around for me. The High Priestess and the Hierophant I skimmed. They were short and didn't add much.

### 5. The letter at the end
Yes, mostly. "Some of the time I was glad of it, because you were easier to be around when you weren't asking me for anything." That's an ugly thing to say, and it's exactly the kind of thing he'd think and never say. It made the letter feel real instead of nice. "I'm not stupid" sounded like a person. "You weren't weak. I made it expensive." landed. I read it twice. The line I'd have cut is "It was the best thing about you." It's a bit greeting-card. He'd never say that.

## Reading B ("They took your sense of being safe")

### 1. Where my attention dropped
"Near midnight they left my shoulder and went to stand by the hall door." It drops in by itself between two cards and I lost the thread. Then again at Card 8, "the clock behind me ticking too loud." Less of this than A, but it still pulled me out each time. I also slowed down at the start of Card 1, where she explains what being safe "is" ("It runs two ways..."). That part read like a lesson.

### 2. Where I felt seen
- "You keep a plan B for nearly everything, and a little money put by, just in case." Guilty.
- "When the phone rings at an odd hour, you're braced before you've said hello." Every time. I live alone.
- "Relief isn't the same thing as rest." That one's going to stay with me.

Honorable mention: "you'd stopped looking forward to things, in case they fell through."

### 3. Where it lost me
- **About some other woman:** the letter says "I'd be one way one week and another the next" and "You'd ask twice about everything, just to be sure." That's a very particular kind of man, the hot-and-cold kind who tells you it's in your head. If that wasn't how it was with him, half of this reading is about somebody else. Card 1 even hedges: "It doesn't much matter what shape it took with them." That told me she didn't know.
- **"She worries too much":** I see where it was going. But part of me heard "you're an anxious old woman," and I bristled before she turned it around.
- **Felt like a disclaimer:** "If you're not safe where you are right now, put these pages down and tell someone who can help first." I'm sure it's meant kindly. It read like small print, and it broke the spell right at the end.
- **The Death card:** I know she explains it. I still flinched when I saw it.
- **Same confusing line:** "They kept the use of you, the part that caught what they missed." Still no idea.

### 4. The cards
Good, and a couple were better than A's. The Chariot, where "everybody on that card is resting except him", is a picture I won't forget. The Moon with its eyes closed while the dogs howl all night was me at 3am. The Wheel of Fortune as "a day with them" worked. Justice and the Magician I skimmed. The Magician explanation, "his two arms make one line through him", was a stretch.

### 5. The letter at the end
It's well written, and "You were right to feel what you felt. It was never in your head." is a sentence a lot of women would cry at. But this letter is the one that described a specific man in detail, and I'm not sure it was mine. "I could see what it was doing to you" was good, and "It's late for it" sounded human. Overall it landed softer than A's. It felt more like Evelyn writing in his voice than him.

## Which one I'd rather have received

A, but not by a mile. A said things about me that only someone watching would know: being proud that I don't cry in front of people, the faucet running, the pat on the back instead of a hug. And it blamed him for those things without making me feel pathetic. Its letter had one mean, honest line in it, and that's why I believed the rest. B was steadier and better organized. The plan B and the phone ringing at odd hours were dead on for a woman living alone. "Relief isn't rest" is the single best line in either one. But B described a particular kind of man, and every time it did, I thought "was he like that, though?" A described what I've become, and I couldn't argue with that. A would make me cry. B would make me nod. For $35 I'd rather cry.

## Gut: what felt off or not quite human

- **Evelyn's night of drama** is in both, in pieces between the cards: getting up, swearing, the ghost leaving and coming back to sit in "the chair where you'd sit." It's too staged, and it's the part that made me think "sales." It's worse in A.
- **"I told you..."** shows up again and again in both ("I told you most women guess wrong," "I told you about the old man," "I told you ten years or thirty..."). It felt like she was ticking off promises from the free letter, not talking to me.
- **Lawyer-sounding lines** in both: "Everything done with it after that is on them." and "kept the use of you." Those read like they were written by a form, not a woman at her kitchen table.
- **The ending.** Both end "It's back. What you do with it is on you." That's a bit cold for a last line after all that. I wanted her to say goodnight.
