## Orientation

Shot from directly above. The **rim** is the outer edge of the white circle; the **base** is the
centre. The **handle points to the lower right, about half past four** on a clock face. Positions
below are clock positions as you look down. A pale brown **tea stain** washes down the right-hand
wall between about one and three o'clock. Loose specks of leaf are scattered across the base, and a
thick dark band of leaf runs along the lower wall from about five round to seven o'clock.

## The reading conventions that already exist in the letter and readings

- **The handle is her.** Near the handle is close to her; across the cup is far from her.
- **Height is time.** At the rim is now and the next few days; further down the wall is further
  back; the base is what's buried, older than anything she has noticed.

So, true of this cup and usable in every reading:

- **Nearest the handle:** the tree (3), then the heart (2), then the lighthouse (6). These are close to her.
- **Far side:** the bird (1) and the boat (4).
- **Highest (soonest):** the butterfly (7) on the rim, then the bridge (5) and the lighthouse (6) just under it.
- **Lowest (oldest):** the heart (2) and the bird's tail (1).
- **The bird's age** is read from its tail, which reaches about halfway to the base: **two months**.
  Its head is up under the rim: he is still going now.

## What NOT to say

⛔ Anything not in the photo: no "fine line running round under the lip", no "leaf lying edge-on",
no "a leaf at the very bottom", no cup-specific layout. The three readings describe the **same
physical cup**. The lighthouse is NOT opposite the handle; it is high on the handle's side. Don't
claim the shapes are crisp pictures. They are loose clumps a reader interprets, and the crops show
exactly that, so describe what she can see and then what it means.
