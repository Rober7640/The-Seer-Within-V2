## The one problem

Not poverty. She'd know what to do with poverty. This is stranger, and it's the highest-value
theme in the entire money bucket for exactly this reason — the VOC data calls it **"the invisible
block,"** 1,208 real concerns, buy-index 1.32, **$14,083 per 1,000** — and it wasn't written for
this offer, it was found first and the offer built to fit it. Real words, from women who never
saw this bracelet:

> "I keep trying to get a head in life and no matter what I do nothing seems to work out it's
> like something is holding me back"

> "I sometimes feel I am close to love, happiness, money only to have it vanish."

> "Sometimes I see light and when I am suppose to get a breakthrough then all of sudden its gone
> what makes to be like that"

> "Everything appears to be reaching me but doesn't."

Read those again. Not one of them says *nothing is coming*. Every one of them says *something
comes, and then it doesn't stay*. That's the whole game. She doesn't have an attraction problem.
She has a **containment** problem, and nobody has ever sold her a container.

**For copy:** *"reaching me but doesn't"* is hers. Steal it back to her, word for word, early.

---

## The big idea

**She doesn't need something that pulls money toward her. She already has that — it keeps
reaching her. What she needs is something that seals shut once it's inside.**

Not a magnet. A one-way valve. That's the whole reframe, and it's the sentence every other
decision in this doc has to survive contact with.

---

## The mechanism

Here's the myth, and I'm not dressing it up, because the real one is stranger and better than
anything invented for this deck so far.

**Pixiu** sat in the Jade Emperor's court — dragon head, lion's body — and one day it ate the
treasury. Not stole from it. Ate it. Jewels, gold, silver, straight down. When the Emperor found
out, he didn't kill it. He sealed it. He closed the one place all of that would eventually have
come back out.

So Pixiu just kept eating. It has been swallowing wealth for several thousand years and it has
never once let go of a single piece of it — not out of loyalty, not out of generosity. It
*physically cannot let go.* That is the least metaphorical piece of folklore in this entire deck.
It isn't a story about attracting anything. It's a story about a **body with no exit.**

Worn against her skin, that becomes a rule for whatever reaches her too: once it's in, it stays
in. The **black agate** underneath does the other half of the job — it's a grounding stone,
which in practice means it *holds* a charge instead of losing it to the room, the same way she
loses money to a bill she forgot, a subscription she stopped noticing, an "opportunity" that was
actually just a hole in the floor. Two materials. One job. **Seal it shut, both ways.**

The **mantra engraved into the beads** — *Om Mani Padme Hum* — isn't decoration either, and don't
let it get written as decoration: say what it is (a compassion mantra, worn as a working part,
not a spiritual sticker) and move on. Evelyn doesn't do incense-shop mysticism.

### The one instruction that isn't decoration

**It goes on the left wrist.** The left hand is the receiving hand — nearer the heart, the hand
that takes rather than gives. Worn on the right, she's offering the seal to what she's sending
*out*, which is the one way to take an object built to stop a leak and make it feed one instead.
It's a small instruction. It's also the only one in this whole reading she could get backwards —
which is exactly why it's the one worth building the section around, per the framework's own
rule: *every section needs a warning that names a behaviour, not a feeling.*

---

## The solution — what she actually does

The bracelet ships with a real capsule built into it — gold-plated, hexagonal, sealed at both
ends, not a design flourish. Before she puts it on:

1. **She names the specific one.** Not "more money" — the deal, the client, the check that was
   supposed to clear, the raise that keeps not arriving. The near-miss, not the wish.
2. **She writes it on the wish paper it ships with**, one sentence, and seals it inside the
   capsule — the same seal Pixiu carries, the same seal the agate carries. Third repetition of
   the one idea, and repetition is the point: she should not be able to *not* notice the pattern.
3. **Left wrist. Doesn't come off.** Not for a shower, not to lend it, not as a gift to someone
   else mid-working — every one of those breaks the seal she just built, and a broken seal is a
   worse story than never having sealed anything.

Every section needs something to *do*, not just something to feel. This is it.

---

## What it can't do — the honest limit

**Pixiu seals a container. It doesn't fill an empty one.** If nothing has come near her in a long
while — no near-miss, no almost, nothing that got close and then didn't land — this isn't the
offer that starts that. It's the offer for the money she's already watched slip through her
fingers, more than once, recently enough to still sting.

Say that limit *out loud*, in the copy, not just here. It's the same move 02 and 04 make with
their own mechanisms, and it does two jobs at once: it's honest, and it's the single cheapest
insurance against the refund from the woman who wore it for three weeks and "nothing happened,"
because nothing was ever going to reach her in the first place — that was never what this fixes.

## Why this, and not another money theme

The invisible block theme was picked over debt, retirement, or bereavement on purpose:

- **It already converts best of any theme that doesn't carry a compliance flag** — retirement and
  bereavement rank higher per 1,000 but both are harder to sell an object into (retirement wants
  security, bereavement is the one theme in the whole bucket that "most deserves a deliberate
  decision rather than a default yes").
- **It's the one theme that literally is an object's mechanism.** A block is a thing to be undone,
  and a bracelet-you-wear is the single most concrete "thing you carry" fix for a problem that
  every other money theme would need an abstraction to solve.
- **It has zero third-party or financial-outcome exposure.** Nobody else's fortune is invoked
  (unlike 05), and no specific dollar figure or timeline gets promised (unlike the lottery/windfall
  theme, flagged as the sharpest legal edge in the bucket). This is the cleanest theme in the deck
  to sell a physical object against.
