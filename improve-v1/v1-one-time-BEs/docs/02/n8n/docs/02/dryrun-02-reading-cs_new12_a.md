I laid your twelve last night, dear, after the house went quiet. I did not get up again until the light outside had changed.

One of them kept me sitting long after the rest were read — the twelfth. The card standing there looks like a card of rest, and I have watched readers hand that one over as peace and be wrong. I would not write to you about it until I was sure which it was.

I pulled these from the trumps only, as I said I would. A Majors twelve costs me a whole night and takes something out of me — I keep it for the draws that ask, and yours asked.

Now the shape of it, flat, because you have paid and suspense is not the product. This is a strong draw. It is not a soft one.

The window for all twelve is the next two months. Your next stretch happens in three rooms above the others — your second, your fifth, your eighth.

Those same rooms settle what was left open. The second and the eighth carry the two windfalls and the doors they come through. The fifth decides which reading of the Lovers is yours. The twelfth names the person. That is what you paid for.

There was nothing to be ashamed of in asking. Wanting to see the ground before you walk on it is not weakness.

Some of this will look as though it argues with itself — a card giving in one room and asking work of you in the next. Read all twelve before you decide what they say. The arrangement is where the meaning sits, and no life is consistent either.

[1 · yourself · the Magician]
His lower hand points at the ground while the wand goes straight up in the other, so one line runs through him from the air to the floor. He is not asking for anything. He is passing something through.

That is the first thing I want you to see.

Look at his table: a cup, a coin, a sword, a wand, all set out before he began. The lilies at his knees are already open. Nothing is waiting to be delivered to him.

That is you at the start of this stretch, dear. The means are in the house.

What the card asks in exchange is small and unromantic — one thing begun this month with your own hand, in the ordinary way, on a weekday.

Leave it as it stands and the table stays laid. The luck comes, and it finds a woman with her hands still down at her sides.

[2 · what you own · the Nameless One]
The bishop stands square in the horse's path, hands folded, hat tall, and he does not move. He is not stopping the rider. He is meeting him — and the horse walks on past at the same pace it came.

That is your money in this stretch. It comes through something being wound up. An arrangement you are already inside gets closed out, and the first windfall is what falls loose when it closes.

You will know it because it will not feel like luck. It arrives as paperwork with a final date on it, wanting your name, and your first response will be to put it down again.

Justice reversed in your eighth says it from the other side: what is owed you sits in someone else's hands, held past its time. So the sum is released to you. Not given.

Count the flag, dear. One rose, five petals. It comes in pieces, and the first piece is the smallest of them.

Sign what reaches you the week it reaches you, and the ending pays you out; leave it lying on the table and it pays the person still holding it.

[3 · what you say, and who you say it to · the Hierophant (reversed)]

There was a way to say it properly. Out loud, to the face, with other people present. That way was open to you and it did not happen, and now you say it in your head instead, in better words each time, to no one.

You are outside the form, dear. You are still being measured by it.

Judgment in your seventh is what changes this. The one you are bound to asks you outright, and you answer — plainly, in your own words, nothing rehearsed. That is the conversation that counts, and it will not look proper while it happens.

So say the unsent thing first, this month, before you are asked it.

Look at the two keys crossed on the floor at his feet. He has not handed them down. They lie there, in reach of anyone who kneels.

[4 · home, and what you came from · the Chariot (reversed)]

That is the house you came from. Two ways of doing a thing, both facing forward, both handed to you, neither one hitched to the other.

So the force in you goes in and the ground under you does not move.

You know this already. You have watched yourself out-work everyone around you and finish where you started, and you decided the fault was in your character. It is not. It is the steering, and the steering was fitted before you were old enough to be asked.

Look at the walled town behind him, small now, well back. He is not going to it.

Neither are you, dear. You settled that a long while ago, and you settled it right.

So the question in front of you is not whether you can work. You have proved that twice over and it moved nothing. The next room asks something else of you entirely — whether you can recognise a good thing on a day when you are not braced for it.

[5 · romance, and what delights you · the Devil (reversed)]
The horned figure crouches on his half-block, one hand up and one hand down, and the lowered hand grips a lit torch aimed at the floor. He is not burning anything with it. He is holding heat where nobody standing there can feel it.

That is what your pleasure has been doing. The thing you get something real out of still stands, and it has quietly stopped giving it back — the grip went slack without you deciding anything.

So there is no renewal here to hand you. What has gone slack in that hand does not tighten again on its own.

Your answer is the arrival, dear. A twin flame, and not a stranger — the Sun in your eleventh puts him already inside your own circle.

The third week from this one. I can date this room and no other because an arrival is an event, and events land on a day; the rest of your twelve is weather.

He will be mid-errand when it happens, on his way somewhere, easy with people he owes nothing to. He is not looking for you. You are on the way, and he stops.

A twin flame arriving and a twin flame recognised are two separate days. Women brace for the second and let the first walk past.

Read as freedom, that slack becomes somewhere to rest — and a woman resting in it hears him as pleasant company and lets him go by.

[6 · work, health, the daily grind · the High Priestess (reversed)]

[7 · who you are bound to · Judgment]

So do not prepare an answer. When you are asked, say the true thing in the first breath.

[8 · what is hidden, and what comes through others · Justice (reversed)]

Here is your second windfall, dear. It comes through the hands of whoever administers the thing that still carries two names — a solicitor, an officer, someone paid to be slow. [9 · what you believe · the Wheel of Fortune]

The Chariot reversed in your fourth is where that got fitted — you out-worked the room, finished where you began, and filed the result against yourself.

[10 · your standing · the Hanged Man]
