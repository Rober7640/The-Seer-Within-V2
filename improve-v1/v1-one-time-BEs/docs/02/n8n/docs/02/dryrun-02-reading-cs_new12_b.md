Sarah,

I laid your twelve last night and I was still at that table when the light came up. I pull these from the trumps only, as I said I would. It is the heaviest reading I do, it costs me a whole night, and I keep it for the women whose first draw asks for one — yours asked.

Here is the first thing I noticed, and it is a finding, not a kindness. The Devil did not come. Neither did the Moon. Those two are what I watch for when a woman is being made to pay for something, and neither is anywhere on this table. So whatever you had to admit to yourself before you sent for this, none of these twelve cards is charging you for it. That is answered, and I will not keep answering it.

It is a good draw. It is not an easy one. The window for all twelve rooms is the next two months, and the weight of it sits in three: your second, your fifth, your eighth. Money, a man, and a thing said about you in a room you were not in.

Four of the twelve came reversed. Not one of the four is a card of harm. Every one of them is a card of something held, spent, or done too early.

What the letter left open is what you paid to have closed. Which door the money comes through is your second. Which of the two Lovers readings is the true one is your fifth, and that room has a week on it. What comes through somebody else is your eighth. The person in the Tower is named in your twelfth.

One warning before you begin. Two of these rooms will look as though they argue — one hands you something, the next asks you to work for it. Read all twelve before you decide what the reading says. The sense is in the arrangement, and no woman's life is consistent either.

[1 · yourself · Justice (reversed)]
She is seated between two pillars, with a purple cloth hung behind her, and there is no window in it. Nothing reaches her from outside. No weather, no news, no sight of the road the answer has to come down.

That has been your position. It was the first thing the table gave up last night: you are waiting on somebody else's answer, and you have no way to hurry it.

Her scales hang level in the left hand. Neither pan down — so nothing has gone against you. It is simply undecided, and it has taken far longer than it should.

The sword, though, is in her own right hand, straight up, the point above the crown. Yours has stayed in your lap.

Taken as good news and left there, this card lets the matter be settled by whoever spoke to them last, and the answer comes to you secondhand.

Put your claim in front of the one who actually decides it, this month, and in writing.

Left as it stands, this room keeps you a bystander to your own business, dear.

Hold on to that phrase — somebody else's hands. The next room says it again, and there it has a figure attached.

[2 · what you own · the Empress (reversed)]
Twelve stars ring her head in a circle, not a row — each one small, each one lit on its own, and the crown only holds because none of them has gone out.

Yours have been going out one at a time. What you have put into other people has left faster than anything has come back.

This card does not charge you with being careless with money. It shows you keeping more than one household on one purse.

The door is this one, dear. The first windfall is money already yours, sitting in somebody else's hands — a repayment, a settlement, a share of an estate or a policy.

The Hanged Man in your eighth confirms it: held, not lost, and released late rather than never. You will recognise it as paperwork before you recognise it as money.

The wheat in front of her is high enough to cut. This room will hand you a harvest, and it wants the first cut of it put back into your own ground — before anyone else asks.

[3 · what you say, and who you say it to · the High Priestess]
Her crown is two horns with a round moon held up between them. The horns do not touch it. They carry it in the gap and leave it plainly in view — and it is her own head bearing the weight of it.

That is the shape your speech has taken. Holding a thing steady, in sight, rather than handing it to anybody.

Point at three of them, dear. The answer you give when somebody asks how you are. The message you wrote out properly and never sent. The one person you used to tell everything to, and what you tell them instead now.

You have not lied in any of the three. You have edited — and the editing has cost you help that was yours to have.

Say the whole of it to one person this week. Aloud, not in writing.

The scroll in her lap is rolled, but part of the writing shows past the edge. She is waiting to be asked.

[4 · home, and what you came from · Strength]
One grey hill sits a long way off behind her, and the ground between is bare — no house, no fence, no road drawn towards it.

You were raised in that country. Nothing built between you and the distance, and nobody further along the path than you.

So you became the shelter yourself. The one who keeps the peace, keeps the temper down, keeps a hand on the thing in the house that could go badly.

Her flowers run in a chain from her waist and loop over the lion's neck. She is tied to what she manages, dear. It stays in the room with her.

This room gives you real authority in your own home — provided you stop doing the holding in secret. Tell one person in the family, this month, that you are the one who has been holding it. Plainly, and without apologising for it.

You decided a long while ago that your house would not be run the way yours was run.

[5 · romance, and what delights you · the Magician]
Four things lie on the table in front of him — a cup, a coin, a sword, a wand. Laid out flat, all four inside the reach of a man who has not begun yet, and none of them borrowed.

Four, dear. Feeling, money, judgment, will. Nothing has to be sent for.

So the second reading is the true one, and here is why. Temperance reversed in your seventh shows what you pour going one way only. There is no renewal there to be quickened.

It is a twin flame, and he comes through your circle rather than out of nowhere.

The second week of next month. I can date this room because an arrival is an event, and events land on a day; the other eleven are weather.

He will be mid-errand, brief, courteous, going somewhere. He stops.

Arriving and being recognised are two different weeks. Which man you already know would you never have counted?

He comes past you, then. The next room is about whether you are anywhere a man can come past.

[6 · work, health, the daily grind · the Hermit (reversed)]
He is above the snowline, hooded, and only one side of his face is given to you. Nobody is climbing after him. The sky behind is empty — no track cut up to that peak, and none cut down off it.

You went up there on purpose. After the years of being the one holding the house steady — Strength in your fourth — quiet was the right medicine, and it did what medicine does.

What has not happened since is the second choosing. It stopped being a rest and became the address.

It shows in the working day. The task that could be handed over, you take to the far end of the table. Help offered gets thanked and refused. Your body is not tired from labour, dear. It is tired from carrying alone.

Pick one job this week and do it in company, badly if need be.

His staff is held clear of the snow, not leaned on. The strength is spare, and it is going into staying still.

[7 · who you are bound to · Temperance (reversed)]
A gold circle sits on her forehead, and on her breast a square with a triangle set inside it. Both are worn openly, where anyone could read them. She is marked with the proportions before she pours a single drop.

You have been pouring without the marks.

The two cups are held at unequal heights, and the water crosses the gap in a stream that does not fall straight down. That crossing takes a season. You have been trying to finish it in a fortnight — a stretch of giving far too much, then a stretch of giving nothing, then back.

This is not news to you. You watched that swing in yourself and told yourself you were the one being unfair.

You were not being unfair, dear. You were being early.

One bare foot stands in the pool, the other flat on the bank, and the yellow flowers are growing by the standing one.

For the rest of this month, add nothing new to that tie. No fixing, no gesture, no putting-it-right conversation.

Nothing new into that tie, then. What comes next arrives from a direction you are not watching, and it is the room that finishes the fifth.

[8 · what is hidden, and what comes through others · the Hanged Man]
The ring of yellow light sits around his head, as wide as his shoulders, and it is burning while he hangs upside down and goes nowhere. The payment is there. Not in the beam, not in the rope.

What is being kept from you in this room is not money. It is something said about you, warmly, in a room you were not standing in.

It reaches you through a woman in your circle — the Chariot in your eleventh is the thing that carries it to your door. She will hand it over sideways, half as gossip, not knowing what she has.

That is your second windfall, dear, and it moves your love life rather than your purse because it names which man to count. The Magician in your fifth cannot be read without it.

Taken for a piece of luck and left to ripen, it arrives after he has drawn his own conclusion from your quiet.

Left hanging, this room costs you the year.

[9 · what you believe · the Wheel of Fortune]
The wheel hangs in open sky with nothing under it. No post, no axle, no hand on the rim — only air beneath, and it turns regardless.

You do not believe in that. Not aloud. But you have run your life on the reverse: that anything good has to be dragged in by whoever wants it, and that what comes free comes with a bill walking behind it.

That creed is the same one holding you above the snowline in your sixth. Carry it alone and at least you know it is carried.

This card is not calling your thinking superstitious, and it is not telling you your faith is thin. It is telling you your rule is about to be broken by something that never asked your permission first.

The sphinx sits at the top with its sword laid flat. Steady, while the whole thing moves under it.

So when the good thing arrives with no reason attached, take it in both hands, dear — this room gives you a turn you did not work for, and wants only that you stop auditing it for the catch.

[10 · your standing · the Nameless One]
Two towers stand far back on that card, and the sun is coming up in the gap between them. The light is behind the rider, not ahead of him — it arrives after he has already gone by. That is the order your name has been getting. Credited late, and to somebody else first.

Something in how you are known is finishing. It comes at a walk, so you can watch it come the whole way in.

Three places to point, dear. The way you describe your work when a stranger asks what you do. The room where you go quiet and let another voice take it. The thing that went out under a name that was not yours, and was thanked.

None of the three survives in its present shape. Say your own part in it aloud this month, to the people who do the counting.

The bishop stands in the horse's path with his hands folded and does not move. The horse walks on.

[11 · your circle · the Chariot]
A crescent moon rides each shoulder, and there is a small face set inside each one — two watchers, one at either side, looking out where the man himself cannot look.

Your circle is those two faces. Not the crowd. Two women who see for you at the flanks.

He holds no reins. Nothing runs from his hands to the sphinxes lying in front of him, and the thing moves regardless.

So the people who arrive when your luck turns are already chosen, dear, and you will not be steering them. What this room gives you is carriage — your name and your good news travelling by mouth to the right ear without you arranging any of it.

It asks one thing back: be reachable this month. Say yes to the invitation you would ordinarily let go past you.

And the ones who have gone quiet — you stopped chasing them a while ago. That was your decision, and you were right to make it.

Those two women see for you at the flanks. One of them has already seen what the twelfth room is about to tell you.

[12 · what is being kept from you · Judgment]
Three grey figures stand up out of open boxes on the water — a man, a woman, a child, the child in the middle with its back to us, arms straight out from the shoulders. Nobody hauls them up. They rise on their own, in the open, where the rest of the water can see them.

What you are braced for is not somebody coming back. It is somebody still here who has already started going.

Temperance reversed in your seventh is the working: what you pour crosses one way and nothing crosses back. That has been true long enough that he has stopped intending to return it.

Count the figures. Three stand close, and more stand further off, smaller. Three people near you can already see this. Others further out have heard it said.

So it is not shape any more, dear. It is visible.

Ask him plainly, this month, whether he means to stay.

Who was the last person to change the subject when you spoke about him?

Add the twelve together and they say one thing. Everything owed to you is sitting in other people's keeping, and it is all being let go at once.

The order is what matters. Your second moves first, and it moves on paper. Your eighth has to reach you before the fifth is worth anything — that is the condition the other two hang on. Your eleventh is the carriage for it, which is why being reachable this month is not a nicety. If the eighth comes late, he reads your quiet his own way, and the year in your twelfth simply repeats itself.

The way news gets to you changes now. You hear it while it is still being decided, from the person deciding it.

The first sign, inside five to seven days: a woman in your circle repeats to you something a third person said about you, lightly, in passing, as though it were nothing.

I have dated one room and one only. The fifth, because an arrival is an event and events land on a day. The other eleven I have not dated and will not pretend to. Two months is when the movement starts, not when all of it finishes; some rooms will have opened inside a fortnight, and the slow ones are slow because of the cards standing in them.

This week, do the third room. Say the whole of it out loud, to the one person, and do not write it down.
