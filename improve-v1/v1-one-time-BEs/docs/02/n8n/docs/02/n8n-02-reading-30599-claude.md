I laid your twelve last night, dear, with the lamp lit and the rest of the house asleep.

I would have bet on the fifth. That is where your question lives, and I expected it to be the room that kept me at the table.

It was the twelfth. What stands in your twelfth kept pulling my eye back to it, and I sat a long while before I would write it down.

Now the shape of it, flat, because you have paid for flat. This is a good draw with one hard room in it, and the hard thing there is something you have been kept from knowing.

The next two months are the window. Every one of the twelve begins inside that stretch.

And inside it, your life happens in three rooms — the second, the fifth and the twelfth. Money, an arrival, and a man's decision.

Twelve trumps and no minors; that was my instruction, not the deck's doing. It is the heaviest reading I lay, it costs me a night I do not get back, and your first draw asked for it.

What the letter left standing is settled here. Not in this opening — in the rooms themselves, each answer in the room it belongs to.

These are twelve rooms of one life. Not four answers with filler around them.

One thing before you start. You had to admit something to yourself to send me that money — that you could not work this out alone, and that you had stopped being able to ask anyone close to you. That admission is not an accusation, and nothing in these twelve cards holds it against you. It is the reason the reading is any use at all.

Read all twelve before you decide what they say. One card will promise you ease and the next will ask you for work, and they are not contradicting each other. A life does not run consistent either. The meaning is in how they sit together.

[1 · yourself · the Hermit]
Behind the old man there is nothing but sky. No track worn up to where he stands, and none going down the far side — the painter gave him no way back and no way onward. The climb is over. He arrived.

So you are not stuck, and I will not let you call it that. You are standing where you spent years getting to.

Here is the thing I think you have never said out loud. You write the long answer, read it twice, then send the short one — and you carry the long one around with you all day.

The staff in his left hand is held upright, not leaned on. You do not need propping. Notice that the Chariot sits in your seventh: with him you move at his speed, and alone you move at yours.

The lamp lights one step. Send the long version once this month, to one person, and it will keep lighting them.

Nothing on that hill was given to you. The next card is about a thing that was.

[2 · what you own · the High Priestess]
Two pillars, one black and one white, and each has a single letter cut into it — B and J. Not words. Letters, standing for something longer that nobody has spelled out for her.

Your money is written down somewhere, in full, and you have not been told the whole figure.

That is the door, dear, and I promised you a door. It is paper, and a stranger's desk. A letter or a statement with a sum on it that is already in your name.

Temperance in your eighth pours from one cup into another without spilling, and it is somebody else's hands doing the pouring. So a clerk checks, and the number read back to you is bigger than the one you have been living on.

You kept the papers when throwing them out would have been easier. That was right, and it is the thing that pays you. Ask in writing this month.

The scroll is in her hands, half of it under her mantle. She is waiting to be asked.

[3 · what you say, and who you say it to · the Empress]
The sceptre is up in her hand, not resting on anything — a small globe at the top of it, held out over the field in front of her. She is not planting. She is standing over what is already up.

Warm words of yours are written and true already, dear. They are sitting where nobody can read them.

The corn is ripe and not one stalk has been cut. Someone has to walk out and bring it in.

In your ninth the Fool steps off with nothing packed, on a quiet belief you have never once defended out loud: that a person who wants to hear from you will ask first. That belief is what leaves your field standing.

I won't pretend this card hurries. It feeds what you feed, at the speed of a crop.

A woman who reads this as good news and waits hears his news second-hand, from somebody else's mouth.

You decided a long while back that you would not go cold on anyone to make a point. You chose right.

[4 · home, and what you came from · the Magician]
The wand stands straight up in his right hand, held high, while his left hand points down at the bed of lilies. One line runs through the man from sky to soil. He is asking nobody for anything.

Whoever ran the house you were raised in ran it alone, and you learned the whole trade by watching them do it.

That is not a charge of stubbornness, dear, and I will not let you read it as one. What the card shows is a table already set with the cup, the coin, the sword and the wand. Nothing has to be sent for.

Look at what sits in your tenth, though. The Hanged Man hangs still while your home runs like clockwork — the private room has been paid for out of the public one.

So carry this out with you. Of the things you do at home, which did you choose, and which did you simply inherit?

You have been waiting on the next room since the letter came, and I know it. What stands in it settles which of the two readings was yours. Read it slowly. I did not write it quickly.

[5 · romance, and what delights you · the Nameless One]
Far off on the skyline two pillars stand, and the sun rests low in the gap between them. The light is past the ride, not in it.

What you hold now is what this card has been sent to finish. I won't soften that, dear.

So nothing is being renewed for you. Something arrives instead — your twin flame, and the words are exact.

Judgment in your eleventh shows people standing up out of their places. He is already among the ones you know.

The fourth week from the day you read this. I can put a week on this room and on no other, because an arrival is an event, and the rest of your reading is weather.

He is going somewhere with his mind on the errand, and he stops. Arriving and being recognised are two different days, and women miss him braced for the wrong one.

Point at the message you reread and leave unanswered. The evening you keep clear for nobody. The name that comes up, and you turn the talk.

He is not braced for it either.

[6 · work, health, the daily grind · Strength]
The flowers at her waist are not a belt. The chain runs on off her hip and loops over the lion's neck — she is leading him by a garland, and it holds.

So what you keep down all week is not going anywhere, and I won't pretend it should. It gets held. By hand. Again tomorrow morning.

Your week is going into that holding, not into the tasks. You keep your voice level with the one who decides, and then you are awake at two.

Look at your twelfth. That wheel is turning on something nobody has told you yet, and the steadiness you spend at work is what buys the turn. You are paying in sleep.

This card will carry you, provided you stop spending it twice. Hold your tongue in the room, then say the whole thing aloud to one friend that same evening.

Do that the first day it happens again this week.

[7 · who you are bound to · the Chariot]
He is standing in that stone chariot, not seated — feet planted on the floor of it, armour flat across his chest, no hand out to steady himself. The thing is moving and he is not braced.

Two sphinxes are harnessed in front, one black, one white. Not one strap runs from his hands to either of them.

So count the hands on the steering of this. One pair. Dear, they are not yours.

He set the road before you were up in the seat with him, and you have kept pace since.

The Hermit in your first put you on a hill you walked up alone. Nobody drove you there. In this room you are being driven, and I won't pretend it stops on its own.

The walled town behind him is already small.

Leave the seat as it stands and you arrive. Somewhere you never picked, on time, with the long answer still unsent in your pocket.

That is the pair of hands on your life, and it is worth knowing before the next card, because the next card takes weight off you. Not from him. From a woman.

[8 · what is hidden, and what comes through others · Temperance]
Above the two low hills there is a crown inside the light, faint enough to take for weather. It is not the sun. The narrow path leaves the water and goes toward it.

The second good thing owed to you arrives in this room, dear, and it is not money.

Somebody who has been letting you carry all of it starts carrying half. A woman, and there is no conversation about it — she simply begins doing the part you always did.

In your sixth you are gripping that garland by hand every morning, all week, both hands full. That grip is what gets poured off here, cup to cup, without you being asked.

What comes back to you is the evening, and your temper at nine o'clock. That is why this changes your love life and not your bank balance — the man in your fifth meets a woman with something left in her.

Let her do it badly the first time and say nothing, and she goes on doing it.

[9 · what you believe · the Fool]
The wallet swinging off the end of that wand has marks stitched into it, dim ones, and not one of them spells anything. He is carrying a record that cannot be read back. No receipts, no letters, nothing he could hold up as evidence that any of this has ever worked before.

You believe that if you have to ask for a thing, it does not count. That being wanted means being sought first.

You have caught yourself at this already and argued yourself out of it by lunchtime. I will not let you do that again — you were right the first time.

That rule is what leaves the Empress standing in your third with the corn uncut. The private belief is charging the field.

Ask for something this month with no proof it will be given.

The little dog is up on its back legs at his heel with its mouth open, making noise, and he steps anyway.

[10 · your standing · the Hanged Man]
His free leg is hooked back behind the tied one, knee pushed out, holding the shape of a four. A man in agony does not cross his legs like that.

Nobody strung him up. He knotted his own ankle to the beam, and the rope has not slipped since.

So your name is not moving, dear, and the hand on the rope is yours.

Your fourth has a man running a house single-handed, and every hour that goes into yours is an hour nobody outside watches you work. That is what is paying for this stillness.

Here is the part you have never said aloud. When the wrong name gets thanked in the room, you nod and pass the plate along — and you never mention it afterwards, not even at home.

I won't pretend the credit comes while you are still hanging there.

Green leaves are pushing out of that beam. What holds you is alive.

You decided long ago that the house came first. You chose that.

[11 · your circle · Judgment]
Three of them stand straight up out of open coffins on the water — a man on the left, a woman on the right, a child between them with its back to us, arms out from the shoulders. Nobody hauls them up. They stand because they heard something.

The trumpet sounds once. So a name out of your past comes back into your circle in the open, in front of the ones who stayed, and it does not come back a second time.

That name wants an answer from you, dear, and answering is your part in it — you are not on trial in front of these people.

You kept answering someone the rest of them had quietly given up on. That was right, and it is why this room has anybody standing in it at all.

One of them goes quiet when your luck turns. I won't pretend that is a loss.

When the good news lands, which of them do you tell first?

Hold that question one page longer. Before you can answer it you need the thing nobody in that circle has said to you, and it has been standing in your last room all along.

[12 · what is being kept from you · the Wheel of Fortune]
Eight letters run round the rim of that wheel, two alphabets taking turns, and there is no gap in them and no place where the reading starts. So you cannot tell where in the turn you are standing.

That is the whole of this room, dear.

The man you are bound to has already begun leaving. Not somebody out of your past coming back — the one in your seventh, feet planted, both reins in his own hands. He has started stepping down, and nobody around you can see it yet.

I won't pretend he tells you first. His half is decided.

Strength in your sixth is where it has been hiding. You hold your voice level with him, then lie awake at two, and you have been calling that tiredness.

A woman who takes a turning wheel for plain good luck and sits waiting hears this from a friend of his.

Ask him plainly, to his face, this week. Once.

The snake goes down the left side head first. On the right, at the same moment, the dog-headed figure is coming up.

Put the twelve beside each other and an order comes out of them.

The money in your second comes first, and the rest of it leans there. It is also what the Wheel in your twelfth has been sitting on — the paper and the man are one question, not two.

The hands taken off you in the eighth come second. If that is slow, the arrival in your fifth meets a tired woman instead of a rested one. Nothing else here is spoiled by being late.

What changes for you is the order you hear things in. You have been told last, at home and outside it. Now you are the one asking — the Fool in your ninth is where that habit turns over.

The first sign, inside five to seven days: paper with a figure on it, or a call about paper, and the figure is larger than the one in your head.

I have dated one room. The fifth, and no other — an arrival lands on a day, the rest of this moves like weather, and I will not pretend to date weather for you, dear.

Two months is when the movement starts, not when it is finished. Some of it will have opened inside a fortnight. The slow rooms are slow because of the card standing in them, and a woman who calls slow a failure gives up weeks short of it.

This week, write and ask for the whole figure. Keep a copy of what you send.
