## House 3: B, with a source-grounding reservation

B makes the teaching easier to use. “The Magician asks what work the words are actually meant to do” turns its categories into one recognizable question. “A sentence need not carry every feeling behind it in order to be honest” sounds like something a person could retain and apply. The sequence from available instruments to audience to the purpose of speech earns the application. Its conditional situations allow recognition without requiring a supposed personal incident. The explicit boundary, “None of these possibilities establishes a particular conversation in your life,” reinforces that restraint, although it briefly sounds like an editorial qualification.

A has the stronger trust performance. “Communication is not proved successful merely because it produces compliance” establishes a valuable limit, and “A polished sentence is not automatically a true one” develops it without inventing biography. Its teaching is coherent, but “Relational speech creates recognition rather than agreement” and “Your communication field is governed by capacity rather than scarcity” keep the voice at a classroom distance. The number of named categories and abstract restatements makes it harder to feel personally addressed. Its examples make sense; they do not quite overcome that distance.

B's most consequential surviving defect is the turn from clarification toward insinuating indecision. “Check whether you lack instead the decision to use the ones already sitting on the table” risks making a reader's genuine difficulty finding language sound like unwillingness. It also says, “The roses above and lilies beneath the Magician,” whereas the supplied printed source places both beneath: “Beneath are roses and lilies.” The supplied text does not establish roses above, but it also does not rule them out. Without seeing the card image, this is an unsupported image detail, not a verified visual inaccuracy. The white color of the wand is likewise not established by the supplied text. These are minor grounding reservations; the trust difference rests principally on B’s final attribution of difficulty to indecision.

Contract coverage: no substantive omission. Neither candidate owes personal biography, and neither needs to fabricate it. B wins narrowly for readable, humane application; A remains stronger on epistemic care.

Fix mechanisms: trim duplicate classifications in A and move sooner to the practical contrasts. In B, ground image details in the available source and preserve several possible causes of silence instead of resolving the final beat toward indecision.

## House 5: A

A offers a more proportionate experience of the forecast. Its invitation is “offered in a way that leaves genuine room for your answer,” and the explicit limit “It is not a deadline placed on you” helps a timed prediction avoid becoming a task for the reader. “House 7 shows what may be sustained if both people later choose to sustain it” makes the Empress connection useful while preserving mutual choice. The known person and third week are forecast content, not an invented account of the reader's past.

B has an appealing opening and useful agency language: “intensity alone need not be promoted into obligation.” It also gives the promised known man's manner and activity clearly enough to picture. But “the heaviest reading I do” inflates authority rather than earning the claim through the card explanation, and “The branch chosen here is ARRIVAL” exposes a specification-like scaffold. The capitalization of “TWIN FLAME” adds sales emphasis to an already substantial required prediction. I do not penalize naming the twin flame itself, which the contract requires for arrival.

A's teaching mostly earns its application: dormant figures answering a summons support renewal, and the Empress supplies a distinct idea of sustained care. It becomes less convincing when the domestic rule and work-pace sections give way to “Reclaiming that single judgment is what leaves an evening free when the trumpet sounds.” That closing causal link makes a non-romantic decision sound like preparation for the romantic invitation, though no actual scheduling conflict has been established. “That is a forecast of an event with a day” is also an awkward justification for precision; being an event does not explain why this reading can identify its week. B repeats essentially the same problem.

B's clearest contract weakness is timing recognition. It dates arrival with “The arrival falls in the third week from now,” then expressly distinguishes “recognition is the separate instant when you understand what place he may hold.” Its later “When recognition occurs” gives manner and activity but no relative week for that separate recognition. The contract asks for a relative week for recognition, so this is at least a material ambiguity and, on a strict reading, an omission. A dates renewed attention and immediately identifies its first sign, making its recognition window sufficiently clear.

Both settle confinement at home rather than shared power, and both identify daily work/restoration pacing as the surrendered non-romantic choice. B's “You stopped choosing” states that surrender more accusingly than necessary. A still generalizes from inherited standards to overextension, but its qualifications provide more room for the reader. B's final book/music instruction is low stakes yet somewhat arbitrary; the lesson does not specifically earn that homework.

Most consequential surviving defect in A: the unsupported bridge from reclaiming daily judgment to freeing an evening for romance, plus the explanation that treats eventhood as warrant for week-level precision. There is no clear substantive contract omission in A. B meets the other arrival requirements but leaves recognition timing unresolved.

Fix mechanisms: keep the non-romantic surrender's consequence independent of assumed romantic logistics; remove the false rationale for predictive precision; in B, attach the required week unambiguously to recognition, soften accusatory attribution, and remove authority inflation and branch-label presentation.

## House 12: B

B is the more controlled explanation. “The card settles direction, not motive” states the crucial distinction cleanly. “She distinguishes what has not yet been spoken from deliberate deceit” gives the High Priestess an actual reasoning function, and “You can leave the reasons unresolved” makes the application less demanding than a requirement to achieve emotional closure. The discussion of delayed replies, tone and unfinished exchanges supplies recognizable examples without claiming those exact events occurred.

A also has a useful boundary in “this card does not give you his motives,” but repeatedly moves from interpretation into asserted knowledge of the reader. “The consequence first, because it is what you are living inside” claims experiential access before earning recognition. “Once you face it directly” can make disagreement look like avoidance. Its concluding opposition between “building his reasons or building your own next room” is memorable but risks making normal reflection after a loss seem like the wrong use of an hour. The metaphors also obscure the otherwise practical point about unchanged information.

Both fulfill the required branch and stage: a present person is leaving, the transition is already underway, and the decision has passed into enactment. Both use the High Priestess, distinguish withdrawal from fear's interpretation, and explain why repeatedly interpreting unchanged ambiguity produces more possible motives rather than new information. No substantive required item is absent.

The major surviving defect in both is the conflation of the forecast with observed evidence. A says, “the evidence has already yielded the one fact it can yield: the connection is moving toward an ending.” Ambiguous evidence has not independently established that fact within the excerpt. B is somewhat better at locating direction in the card, yet still says, “you possess enough information to recognize the direction,” and closes with “when one more ambiguous detail appears, it changes none of the evidence already visible.” That last claim moves beyond rereading unchanged evidence: a newly appearing detail may be new evidence even when ambiguous. The contract requires explaining the futility of rereading the same evidence, not declaring in advance that additional observations cannot matter.

The image teaching supports an interpretation of settled passage within the genre. It does not earn the additional assertions about what the reader has observed or what future details can establish. B also opens with “this deliberately weighty, Majors-only table,” an unnecessary authority cue. Neither the mandated departure prediction nor the settled stage is itself the basis for the trust deductions; the added evidence claims and pressure are.

Fix mechanisms: keep the required forecast attributed to the card, distinguish hypothetical observations from known facts, limit the unchanged-evidence argument to genuinely unchanged material, and remove language that equates acceptance with facing reality. Retain the separation of direction from unverified motive without prescribing how the reader must process the loss.

## Verdict, confidence and limits

House winners are B, A, B, respectively. The strongest recurring quality is teaching that leads to a recognizable distinction while leaving personal history open. The recurring weakness is explanatory certainty that exceeds the forecast commitment, especially when ambiguous evidence is said to prove the prediction.

Confidence is moderate: all six excerpts were read in full under the same anchors, but the House 3 choice is close and prioritizes conversational readability over A's stronger trust. The House 5 recognition-timing issue is clearest under a strict distinction between arrival and recognition, which B itself introduces. These judgments concern a bounded three-house writing screen. They are not customer NPS, measured recommendation behavior, predictive validation, a writer-identity assessment, or proof of unseen whole-report quality.
