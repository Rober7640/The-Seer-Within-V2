DRAFT
There is a particular kind of arrangement that everyone recognizes once it is pointed at: the one that works. Not the disaster, not the thing friends warn you about — the situation that pays out reliably, meets a real need, and asks in exchange only that you not look too hard at the terms. It is comfortable in the way a chair with one short leg becomes comfortable, because you have learned exactly how to sit in it. That is the stance this reading opens on, and since I have laid twelve Majors here, every room after this one is going to be weighed against it.

The picture printed above you is usually read as a picture of capture, and it isn't. Look at what is actually drawn: the chains at the two figures' necks pass down to a ring on the altar, and they are slack — wide enough to lift over the head without help, without permission, without anyone's hand being pried off them. Waite says plainly that the figure raised above them is not their master forever. The horned thing on the black cube does not hold the chains. It holds a torch, and holds it pointing down at the ground, which is a strange way to carry a light if illumination were the purpose. Whatever is being lit is not being lit for the benefit of the two below.

In the first house, this is not a description of your character and not a judgment on your appetites. The first house is stance: where your weight is at the start, and what part of the situation is genuinely in your hands. So the reading is narrower and more useful than a moral one. Something in your life at this moment is stable, and its stability is doing real work for you. You get something out of it that you would have to replace if it went. That is the honest half of the card, and it is the half most readers skip in a hurry to call the arrangement a trap. An arrangement you gain nothing from is easy to leave. This is not that kind.

What the house puts in your hands is smaller than a decision and more consequential than a mood: the accuracy of your account of the trade. If you privately describe the situation as something imposed on you, then every plan you make will be a plan about someone else's behavior, and it will stall, because the ring is not the part that moves. If instead you can name what you receive — security, ease, an identity that requires no defense, the relief of not having to ask a question — then the terms become visible, and visible terms can be revised. Notice which of those two descriptions is nearer to hand when you think about it.

One more thing about the drawing bears on your stance. The two figures have grown small horns of their own. The likeness is not accidental. Long participation in an arrangement makes you resemble it, and the tell is usually in vocabulary rather than in behavior: the phrases you use to explain the situation to people who ask, the sentence that arrives already finished. If you know, without hunting, which sentence that is — you know precisely where your hand is on the chain.
