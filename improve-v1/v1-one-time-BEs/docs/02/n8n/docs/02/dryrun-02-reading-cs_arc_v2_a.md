Dear Sarah,

I laid your twelve last night at the kitchen table, long after the rest of the house had gone quiet. Majors only, no minors, the way I told you I would do yours — it is the heaviest work I do, it costs me a full night, and I keep it for the women whose first draw asks for it. Yours asked twice.

It is a good draw. Not a soft one. The next two months are the window for all twelve of these, and inside those two months the real movement of your life happens in three rooms: your second, where money is; your fifth, where a man is; your twelfth, where the man already beside you is.

The one I sat with longest was your fifth. I could not begin writing it until nearly two in the morning, because the card standing there will not let me hand you renewal. It shuts a door I believe you have been holding open with your foot. Once I was certain, I wrote it out in full.

Everything the two letters left hanging is settled below. Both windfalls. Which of the two loves it is. Who the person is, and which direction he is walking. Each answer stands in the room that owns it, and I have not carried any of it up here to be admired first.

You had to say something true to yourself before you could send for this. It was not a confession, dear, and I am not going to raise it again.

One warning before you start. Parts of this will look as though they argue with each other — a card handing you ease in one room and asking you for work in the next. Read all twelve before you decide what they say. The meaning is in how they stand together, and no woman's life is consistent either.

[1 · yourself · the Nameless One]
The rider comes at a walk. Black armour, a white horse, no hurry in it — and not a scythe or a blade anywhere in those hands. He carries a flag instead, black, with one white rose on it.

Something in your life is finished, and you knew it before anybody told you. You said it to yourself plainly and did not take it back. I will give you that, because most women I read for spend a year refusing to.

Strength sits opposite you, in your seventh — you with your hands on something that will not settle, holding it quiet. You have been doing that holding on both sides. Only one side is yours.

The bishop in the horse's path stands with his hands together and does not move, and the rider comes on anyway.

So stop tidying it. This week, say the thing is over to the one person who will not argue you back out of it.

[2 · what you own · the Wheel of Fortune]
On top of the wheel a sphinx lies still with a sword laid flat across it. It is not turning anything. It rests above the wheel and keeps its blade quiet while the snake slides down one side and the dog-headed one climbs the other.

Your money moves without being pushed by you, and it moves upward.

The first windfall arrives as money already owed — a sum sitting in someone else's hands until a person you have not met signs it off. A settlement, a back-payment, a claim finally paid. The Fool in your eighth says the same thing from the other end: it comes through a stranger's decision, not your effort.

Which jug is being filled, dear? The one you keep. Money that is still there at the end of the month.

Read the wheel as luck and the letter goes unanswered, and the amount shrinks.

Leave this room and the money arrives and drains straight out again, unaccounted.

[3 · what you say, and who you say it to · the Sun]
A low grey wall runs right across the card, and the horse is already out in front of it, standing on open ground. Nobody climbed that wall. The child is simply past it, bareback, both arms open, a red flag streaming out sideways.

You are being heard better than you believe you are. The one you keep drafting messages to has already understood you.

This card does not accuse you of hiding, and it does not say your quiet has cost you anything with anyone. It says what you put into words now lands the way you meant it — nothing to decode, nothing between the lines.

Judgment stands opposite in your ninth: the thing you hold and would not defend out loud is precisely the thing that survives being said flat.

So send the short one. Not the softened draft — and I won't pretend the softened one reads better, dear, because it does not.

Daylight is free in this room, and it stays only while you go on saying things the first time.

[4 · home, and what you came from · the Hierophant]
The staff in his left hand carries three crossbars, the top one shortest — a rule passed down through three hands before it ever reached the floor of that room. He holds it. He did not make it.

Your home runs on somebody else's rule, and you have never once voted on it.

I mean something plain. The order you do things in on a Sunday. The way your voice changes when a certain relative telephones. The one room in the house nobody sits in.

None of that came from you. You kept it because keeping it was easier than being the one who stopped it.

The Empress in your tenth is growing something outward, in public, and she is being paid for out of this room. Standing kept up, home kept quiet.

I won't call that peace, dear.

The two keys lie crossed on the floor at his feet, one gold, one silver. Neither of the kneeling men reaches for them.

So the fourth is a rule you inherited and never got a vote on. Now the one room in this spread where somebody walks in from outside it, asking nobody's permission at all.

[5 · romance, and what delights you · the Magician]
One hand points down at the ground while the other holds the white wand straight up, so the man runs as one line from the lilies at his feet to the air above him. Nothing on that table is ornament. Cup, coin, sword, wand — laid out, his, before he starts.

Renewal is not what you are being handed, and I won't dress it up for you. The High Priestess in your twelfth has the man already beside you halfway out of the door. There is nothing standing there to be made new.

So it is arrival. Your twin flame, a man you already know, in the second week from the day you read this. I can put a week on this room because a meeting is an event, and events land on a day. The other eleven rooms are weather.

He is not looking for you. He is halfway through an errand, already late for something, and he stops anyway — then stays past the point he meant to. Braced for lightning, you will walk straight past a man who simply does not leave.

Temperance in your eleventh says he comes to you through the people already around you. Provided you say yes this month to the one invitation you would normally decline.

And the structure you stand inside is not one you built, dear. You are being kept in it, and it sits in your fourth, where a man holds up a staff he did not make. Where you stopped choosing is your second house, what you own — you handed over deciding where your own money goes.

You already chose to stop waiting for the old one to soften. That was your decision, and you made it.

[6 · work, health, the daily grind · the Chariot]
The walled town sits behind him, small already, and he has not once turned his head toward it. He left. Whatever he settled there, he settled by leaving.

Your week does not need pushing. It is already moving, and it is moving faster than you can stop it now.

Count the harness — two sphinxes, one black, one white, and no rein running from his hands to either of them. Two animals pulling, nothing steering them. Your job and your body are both under way, and you pointed them so long ago that you no longer check where.

I won't call that a virtue, dear.

The High Priestess sits still in your twelfth, between her two pillars. Your days run at this speed because you are not looking at what she is keeping.

So stop one thing this week. Not the biggest — the one nobody will notice has gone.

Where were you going, when you first pointed yourself this way?

[7 · who you are bound to · Strength]
One grey hill sits far behind her, and the ground between her and it is bare — no road, no house, nobody walking toward her. She is doing this on her own.

The man you are bound to is not improving. You are the one keeping him level.

You drop your voice before he has raised his. You answer the same question again without saying it is the second time. You read the room he is in from the doorway, before you step into it.

That is work, dear, and it comes round again in the morning.

The rider in your first house walks on while the bishop stands in his path with his hands pressed together, and you told me the thing was finished. Your hands are still on the jaws. The Magician in your fifth is bringing a man toward you, so understand what you are holding when he arrives.

The flower chain round her waist runs on and loops over the lion's neck.

Everything up to here you have been holding up yourself. The eighth is the one room where the good thing is being carried toward you by somebody else's hands.

[8 · what is hidden, and what comes through others · the Fool]
The marks worked into that wallet never make a word. Someone stitched them there and left them unreadable — a bag carrying something with no paperwork behind it.

I promised you two windfalls. This is the second, and I am paying it here.

It is not the settlement sitting in your second house, signed off by a stranger and dropped into an account. This one comes through a woman at the edge of your circle who has been saying your name in rooms you were not standing in. Temperance in your eleventh agrees: what is good this season reaches you through people already near you.

It changes your love life because nobody in your fourth house gets to ask where it came from. It pays for a door.

You have already noticed her doing it, dear. You decided you were flattering yourself.

Thank her plainly this week, and let her tell you what she has been arranging.

[9 · what you believe · Judgment]
A square of white cloth hangs off that trumpet, a red cross laid flat across it, and nothing about it is blowing — there is no wind anywhere on this card. It is a claim being made, not a flag being waved.

Something you settled about yourself a long time ago is standing up out of the water now and waiting for you to say it in the open.

You believe good things have to be paid for.

So you send the message and then go back over it. You add the line apologising for the length. You accept help and then find something to do for the person before they can call it a favour.

I won't call that good manners, dear.

The Sun in your third has you being understood the first time you speak. That is bought out of this room — you say it flat, then settle up afterwards, privately.

The child between those two coffins has its arms straight out and asks nobody's leave.

Leave this belief where it stands and the money coming and the man who stays get charged to your account. You will find a way to owe for both.

So ask what you have been quietly paying for. Then look at what your name is already worth outside your own front door.

[10 · your standing · the Empress]
Her arm is raised with the globe on the end of that sceptre, held up over an open field where nobody is standing to watch her do it. She holds it up anyway.

The people who decide things about you have already decided well. What has not happened is one of them saying it to your face.

You do what you said you would do, on the day you said it, and you have never once traded on that. The corn in front of her is ripe because of it — and not one stalk of it is cut yet.

The Hierophant in your fourth is the bill for this field. Your name goes up outside while at home you go quiet.

I won't have you keep paying it that way, dear.

So this month tell one person in that house, plainly, what you were thanked for. This field feeds you, and it wants the harvest said out loud where you sleep.

[11 · your circle · Temperance]
The winged one is not filling a cup. The same water goes out of the high cup into the low one and comes back, over and over, and it crosses the gap on a slant no falling water takes.

Nobody goes quiet on you this season. What happens instead is slower and better: two people who know you and do not know each other end up in the same room.

The Magician in your fifth is standing at the end of that. He does not arrive out of nowhere — he arrives down a road your friends are laying without telling you, one introduction at a time.

I won't pretend it pours itself, dear. If she takes the good news and lets three invitations go unanswered, the two cups stay separate, and the man on his errand walks a street she is never standing on.

Yellow flowers grow at the water's edge, right beside the foot already in the pool.

Your circle is walking a man toward you. Your twelfth is where you find out what has to be set down before he gets to the door.

[12 · what is being kept from you · the High Priestess]
Two pillars, one black, one white, a single letter cut into each — B on the dark, J on the pale — and she sits between them saying nothing to either side.

What is being kept from you is not being kept by him. You have it.

The person is the one still here, and he has already begun leaving. Not somebody from before, walking back in. Other people can see it now; that is how far along it is.

The piece you keep talking yourself out of is the true one. He decided a while ago. The rest — the reasons you build out of his quiet — is paint you put on yourself, and staring longer into that quiet only adds more of it.

This card does not call you foolish for missing it. Half that scroll is under her mantle by choice.

Strength in your seventh has you keeping him level with both hands. Put them down this week, dear.

You already decided this was over. You did not take it back.

Now what the twelve say together, which no single one of them said.

Money, work and love all move toward you from outside, and not one of them is waiting on effort. The only labour asked of you in this whole spread is letting go of something you are holding up with both arms.

The twelfth comes first, and it is the condition of the other two. Your first house has the rider walking on past a man who will not move — you said it was finished before I did. Put your hands down and the fifth arrives on schedule and the money in your second stays where it lands. Hold on, and the man in your fifth still comes, but he finds you occupied, and the money passes through you.

And you stop hearing things last. Twice in this spread, people are deciding well about you in rooms you are not in. From here you find out while it is still being arranged, not afterwards.

The first sign is small and countable. Inside five to seven days, someone will tell you your name came up — that a woman mentioned you to somebody, favourably, without your asking. Mark the day.

I have dated one room, your fifth, because a meeting is a thing that happens on an afternoon. The other eleven I have not dated and will not pretend to. The two months are when the movement starts, not a promise it is all finished by then. Some of this will be open inside a fortnight. The slow rooms are slow because of the cards standing in them, and women lose these things by reading slow as no.

This week: the invitation you were going to decline politely. Accept it.
