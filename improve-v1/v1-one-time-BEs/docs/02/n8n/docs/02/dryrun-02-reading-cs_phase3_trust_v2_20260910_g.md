Sarah,

I have laid all twelve and sat with them a while before writing, and the first thing worth telling you is how the table is weighted. Twelve Majors and no lesser cards — the reading works only in the register of governing forces, the ones that move whether or not you attend to them. That is the instrument you asked for, and it answers in kind.

The second thing is a pattern in the arrangement itself. The rooms that describe you speak of holding: something known and not yet said, something waiting under a roof, something climbed and earned and then guarded. The rooms that describe what comes toward you speak of motion authored elsewhere: things delivered, things blown once and not repeated, things already decided in places you were not standing. That division is not accidental, and it is the key to how the houses qualify one another. When a room tells you the pace of your own hand, read it against the rooms that arrive on their own clock, because your tempo and theirs are not the same, and several confusions in this reading come from measuring one by the other.

You will also find rooms that seem to contradict until you set them side by side. A house that says something concludes sits near a house that says something returns, and only together do they tell you the shape of the space that opens. A house that says your standing is understated sits near a house that says your circle is quietly densifying, and each explains the other's timing. So do not read these as twelve separate verdicts. Read them as one argument with twelve entrances, in which the later rooms keep adjusting the weight of the earlier ones.

I have not softened anything to make it sit easier, and I have not reached past what the cards will carry. Where a thing is settled I have said so plainly. Where a thing remains genuinely open, I have left it open rather than dress a guess as a certainty. Begin at the first house and read them in order; the order is doing work.

[1 · yourself · the High Priestess]
There is a particular kind of quiet that isn't emptiness. You have sat in a meeting knowing the decision before it was announced, or held a conclusion about someone whole and finished while you kept nodding along to the conversation on the surface. This is the state you begin from — not confusion, not searching, but knowing something clearly and having said none of it out loud, not even fully to yourself. That gap between what you have concluded and what you have spoken is where this whole stretch starts, and it starts in your hands, because the withholding is yours to keep or release.

The figure that stands for you here sits between two pillars, one black and one white, a single cut letter on each. She is placed exactly between the plain answers. That placement is the first true thing about you now: you are not a person who lacks a verdict. You are a person holding one and declining, for the moment, to state it. The scroll rests in her hands with writing already on it — half of it covered by the fall of her mantle. The knowledge is complete. What varies is how much shows.

Consider the categories that quiet could belong to, because they are not the same and the difference matters to what you do next. There is the knowing you are protecting because speaking it changes something you're not ready to change. There is the knowing you have set aside as merely a mood, discounting your own reading because it arrived without proof. And there is the knowing you are still testing against the facts, honestly, waiting for one more confirmation before you commit. Only you can say which of these your silence is. The card does not flatter the first by calling it patience, nor scold the third by calling it delay. It only insists that the material is already in you, finished, and that the question is disclosure rather than discovery.

The cost is worth naming plainly. This way of moving — slow, holding the full text under the cloth — has a real strength. Nothing is said too early; nothing is spent that cannot be recovered. But a scroll kept entirely covered instructs no one and settles nothing, and a woman who mistakes every genuine conclusion for a passing feeling eventually stops trusting the one instrument that has been right. The waiting serves you only up to the point where it becomes a way of never being accountable to what you already believe.

What is in your hands at the start, then, is not information. You have that. It is the decision about how much of it to bring into the light, and to whom, and in what order — and the first move is entirely private. Read your own scroll before anyone else does. Sit with the thing you already know and refuse, this once, to file it under intuition or nerves. Name it to yourself in a single flat sentence, the way you would state a fact you had checked.

That is the honest starting position: you are the one who has the answer and has not spoken it, and the next stretch turns on your relationship to your own certainty. Not on new evidence arriving. On whether you will let yourself count what you already hold as knowledge rather than weather.

[2 · what you own · the Magician]
Look at what is laid out in front of him: a cup, a coin with a star cut into it, a sword, a wand. Four things, all present, none of them fetched during the act. The figure has not gone shopping. He raised one hand and pointed the other and the whole apparatus of the work was already within arm's reach when he began. That is the printed argument of this card, and in the house of what you own it is not a metaphor about potential. It is a statement about your inventory.

So the first windfall has a door, and the door is your own competence. The money comes to you for something you already know how to do — a skill you possess now, not one you would have to acquire, and not a sum handed over out of anyone's generosity. It arrives as payment. Something you can already make, already judge, already fix or arrange or carry, gets converted into a figure larger than the labour appears to justify. That disproportion is how you will recognize it. A gift feels like weather; this will feel like a bill you finally sent yourself. You will be able to trace it, without effort, back to one specific capacity of yours and name the capacity out loud.

The Empress in house 10 changes how the door opens. Standing, in that house, is fertile and visible — you are more legible from outside than you assume you are. Which means the windfall does not require you to build something new to be paid for. The thing you already do is already known, and the approach comes toward an existing reputation rather than an announcement. Read together, these two cards say the sum arrives through the ordinary channel of your competence being recognized where it already operates. You will not have to become someone else to receive it.

Now the replenishment, because this house holds it too. What is being restored is what you keep, not what you give out. This is the distinction that matters, and the card decides it plainly: those four objects are stock, not gifts. They sit on the table as reserve — the store a person draws from before anything leaves their hands. Your outflow has not been the problem. Your reserve was thin, and it is filling again.

You will notice it in the residue at the end of a day. A task that used to consume everything you had will cost less than it did, and something will be left over — not much at first, but unmistakably yours and unspent. That leftover is the evidence. Watch for the day you finish what was required and find you still have appetite for something of your own choosing; that surplus is the replenishment arriving in a form you can measure. It has already begun. The refilling started before you noticed the level rising, which is ordinarily how reserves behave.

What has not changed is the speed. This card moves exactly as fast as the hand that holds the wand — no faster, and it grants no one else the job. Nothing on that table lifts itself. The serpent at his waist closes its own circle: your means and your effort feed each other, and neither arrives from outside the loop. So the sum is coming through your capability, and the pace of the whole business will match the pace you set.

Which leaves you with a piece of housekeeping rather than a labour. Take honest stock of what you can presently do — not what you intend to learn, what your hands know now. Write the list if it helps, or simply count it. The value of that inventory is about to be established by someone else's money, and it will be worth more than your private estimate of it.

[3 · what you say, and who you say it to · Justice]
House three is the house of ordinary talk — the message typed at midnight, the phrase you try out on a friend before you try it on the person it concerns, the sentence that has been sitting in your mouth so long it has worn smooth. Justice does not belong to that register at all. Justice is what gets said once, in a room where it is written down. So the first thing this card tells you about your own voice is that it has been operating in the wrong register for the thing you are actually trying to accomplish, and the effort has been going into rehearsal rather than into record.

Look at where the eyes are. She is not watching the scales; she is looking straight out, at whoever came to be answered. The weighing is not the interesting part — the weighing is arithmetic and it happens whether or not anyone attends to it. What matters is that a person is being looked at and told the result. That is the shape your speech is being pulled toward now: fewer words, delivered face-on, about outcomes rather than feelings about outcomes.

The comparison that helps here is the difference between an argument and a receipt. An argument runs on intention: I meant well, you took it the wrong way, if you understood why I did it you would forgive it. A receipt runs on event: this was given, this was taken, this remains. Where the comparison stops mattering is in the matter of warmth. A receipt is cold and you are not; you have been softening your sentences because you would rather be understood than be right, and that instinct is not a fault. But Justice measures the act and not the motive behind it, and no amount of explanatory preamble changes which pan the thing lands in. The preamble is what you have been rehearsing. The act is what has already been recorded.

There is a consequence to this, and it cuts in both directions, as the sword held upright rather than pointed at anyone is meant to indicate. If you keep the unsent version unsent, the ledger stays as it stands, and you go on paying interest on a sentence you have already composed. If you say it plainly — the version with the justification stripped out, three lines instead of thirty — you enter a process that has a decision at the end of it, and decisions get dated and filed and are not easily reopened. Both are real costs. Only one of them ends.

Waite takes trouble in the note above to separate these pillars from another set that opens onto a different world entirely: one threshold leads to what is known inwardly, the other to what is answered for publicly. Your speech this season is standing at the second.

For the rest of the table, this changes the grain of everything. Several rooms in this spread deal in things that arrive, things that are given, things you are told. This house is the one where you are the one speaking, and Justice sets the standard: what you say is going to be assessed on its accuracy, not its diplomacy. Read your own unsent draft back and mark which sentences describe what happened and which defend why. That division is the whole instruction.

[4 · home, and what you came from · the Hanged Man]
House four asks two things at once and expects one answer: the roof you live under now, and the household that taught you what a roof is for. The card that answers here shows a man who is not falling. He is tied by one ankle to a beam, and everything else about him — the crossed leg tucked behind the bound one, the elbows angled out where his hands are hidden — is arrangement, not accident. Whatever holds him, he stepped into it.

That is the friction in this room. A fourth house deals in what came to you unchosen: the tone of voice used for money, the assumption about who absorbs inconvenience, the definition of a good week. The Hanged Man deals in consent. Put them together and you get the plain reading, which is this — the pattern of waiting in your home was inherited, but the current version of it is one you agreed to. Somewhere along the way you took a habit you were handed and signed it, and it has been running since on your signature rather than anyone else's.

What is being paid here is time. That is the whole price of this card and it is not a small one. The trade is that time buys a change of view, and the view is genuinely different at the end — this is not endurance for its own sake, and it is not punishment. Look at the face in the picture: wide awake, absorbed, no strain anywhere in it. That is the correct description of your domestic situation right now. Suspended, not stalled. Nothing in it is dying.

I want to be careful about what I am and am not saying. I am not saying you are trapped, and I am not saying someone is holding you. The beam is living wood with new leaves coming out of it — the thing you are tied to is still growing, which means the arrangement has a future and is not a dead structure you are propping up out of loyalty. But I am also not going to pretend the hold has lifted. It hasn't. The mechanism of this card is that nothing moves while the hanging lasts, and the stillness is not a failure of the process; it is the process.

The inheritance itself is worth naming precisely. What you are still running on is the assumption that patience is the same thing as competence — that the person who can wait longest is the person handling it best. In the house you were made in, that may have been true, and it may have been the only available form of strength. In the house you live in now it functions differently: it makes you the one who absorbs the delay, quietly, without anyone having to ask. Someone raised in a household where announcing a need was expensive learns to convert need into patience automatically. Whether that describes yours is yours to check, but check it against the specific rooms you move through rather than against the idea of your childhood.

Here is what I would actually do. Choose one thing in your own space that is on hold pending nothing — a decision waiting for a permission that no longer needs granting — and change it. A small one. Rearrange the room you wait in most. Then watch what argues back inside you, because that objection is the inherited part speaking, and hearing it clearly is what the waiting is buying.

[5 · romance, and what delights you · the Wheel of Fortune]
The Wheel is read badly more often than any other card in the deck, and always in the same direction: as luck. As a coin flip with better graphics. Waite is explicit that the picture contains the denial of chance, and the thing he sets against chance is not your merit — it is intention operating outside you. That distinction matters enormously here, because the flattering misreading and the honest reading make the same prediction and give you completely different instructions. Luck says wait and hope. This card says something is already in motion whose axle is not in your hands, and your only real work is to be awake on the day it arrives.

Look at where the wheel hangs: in open sky, with nothing under it and nothing holding it. Nobody is turning it. That is the whole argument of this room. House five is romance as beginning and pursuit — the appetite, the first look, the pleasure of it — and it is not house seven, where things are bound. The Wheel in the house of beginnings means the beginning is not something you will construct out of effort or availability. It will be delivered. It will move fast, it will move once, and it will change your circumstances rather than your character; you will be recognisably yourself on the other side of it, in a different situation.

So the branch is arrival, not renewal. A renewal would be a slow warming of something already in your hands — a wheel you turn yourself. This card cannot describe that. What it describes is a **twin flame** arriving, and the word arrival needs separating from the word recognition, because they are not the same event and they will not happen on the same day. He has already arrived. He is a man you already know — known well enough to name, not well enough to have wondered about. The arrival is finished. What is dated is the recognition.

Temperance in house 11 tells me where he stands. Your circle is the room in which two things that had been kept in separate vessels get poured together, and that is exactly the mechanism the Wheel needs to complete its turn: not a stranger from nowhere, but a rearrangement among people already on your list. He comes through the shared water, not through the door.

The recognition falls in the fourth week from now. I can be that precise because a meeting is an event with a day attached, unlike a mood or a healing, which smear across months. When it happens he will be occupied — mid-task, sorting out some practical detail on behalf of a group of people, something not strictly his job that he has picked up because it needed doing. His manner will be unhurried and faintly amused, attention on the work rather than on you, and that is precisely why you will see him properly for the first time. Nothing will be performed at you. If you were expecting the recognition to feel like an introduction, it will not; it will feel like a correction, as though a name you had been mispronouncing for a long time has just been said aloud.

There is a second thing this room owes you, and it can be settled plainly. The structure around you is confinement, not support, and it sits in house four — home and what you came from — where the Hanged Man shows a hold you consented to and then stopped noticing you had consented to. It is not cruel. It is simply not chosen any more. And the place where you stopped choosing is house six, the daily grind, where the Hierophant sits: you surrendered the decision about how your ordinary week is shaped — what your days are arranged around, what hours belong to your work and what is left for your body — to an approved way of doing things that you did not design and have not reviewed. Not money. Money you have kept your hand on. Time and health you handed over.

The connection between those two facts and the fourth week is not mystical. A schedule you did not choose is a schedule you cannot easily interrupt.

Watch for the moment you feel mild irritation at a man for being competent about something dull. That is the tell, and it will arrive before you have any language for it.

[6 · work, health, the daily grind · the Hierophant]
At the end of an ordinary week you are more tired than the work accounts for. That is the finding of this house, and the arithmetic behind it is not mysterious: a sizeable share of your daily effort is not going into the task at all. It is going into the presentation of the task — the correct sequence, the accepted format, the version that will survive being looked at. That second layer of labour is invisible on any list of what you did, which is exactly why the fatigue reads as inexplicable.

The card that governs the sixth house here is the one of sanctioned form, and the detail that settles it is the two ministers kneeling below the seat with their backs to us. They are not depicted working. They are depicted in position. Their whole visible activity is being properly placed in front of the authority that certifies them, and the picture treats that as their occupation, because within an institution it genuinely is part of the occupation. This is the ruling power of the outward and manifest side of things, all that is righteous and sacred where it can be witnessed — and the price of that standing is the doing of things in the agreed manner, at the agreed pace, which is the pace of an institution rather than of a person who can see the shorter road.

So the honest division of your week is roughly this: the work, and the overhead of being seen to do the work correctly. You will underestimate the second because it doesn't feel like a separate activity. It feels like conscientiousness. But consider the difference between two ways a day can end badly. If you finish having done something difficult, the tiredness has a shape and it recedes. If you finish having spent yourself on rehearsal, deference, restating what was already settled, or bringing something into the accepted form when the substance was already sound, the tiredness has no shape and it does not recede. It carries into the sleep and out the other side. Notice which of those two your ordinary evening resembles, over several evenings rather than one, because a single bad day proves nothing.

The body is where the overhead gets stored. There is no separate health question in this house; there is the same question, asked of the part of you that keeps the account. Belonging properly to a structure costs a steady, low tariff — not a crisis, a tariff — and it is paid in the shoulders, the jaw, the appetite, the hour you meant to have to yourself and gave back.

You can spend less on the presentation layer without leaving the structure. Where the form is genuinely load-bearing, pay it and stop resenting it, because resentment is a second charge on the same bill. Where you are performing correctness for no witness who requires it, that is your own money, and it is retrievable inside your own routine.

What is not available is doing it your own way and keeping the approval. The card holds the crossed keys at its feet and does not lend them; the standing comes with the method attached. Your energy will keep going where it is going for as long as you want the sanction that it buys — and wanting that sanction is a legitimate choice, provided you make it knowing the rate.

[7 · who you are bound to · the Nameless One]
Two misreadings sit on this card, and they cancel each other out. The first is dread — the reader who goes quiet, the buyer who stops breathing. The second is the polite correction that follows: *it only means change*, said so softly that it means nothing at all. Both are wrong, and the second is the more expensive error, because a reading that means nothing cannot be acted on.

What the card actually states is narrower and much harder. Something has been decided. Not proposed, not risked, not hanging on anyone's next move — decided, and now being carried out. The horseman comes at a walk. That pace is the entire argument: a thing moving at walking speed across open ground can be watched approaching for a long time before it arrives, and watching it does not slow it. Whatever is ending in this house has been visible for a while. Not necessarily named. Visible.

The pictured world confirms which kind of ending this is. The rider carries no weapon of any sort — no blade, no instrument of harm. What he carries is a black banner with a single white rose on it, five petals, the flower that stands for life rather than its cessation. And past the falling figures, on the skyline, two pillars with the sun standing in the gap between them. The old commentary is right to insist this is a vision of passage rather than a reaper's errand: the transit itself is a form of progress. This is not damage. It is completion.

House seven holds the person you are bound to, whether or not the binding has a name that either of you uses out loud. Here is where I want you to be precise with yourself, because the card is precise. The ending is of a form, not of a person. Look at the king on the ground: his crown has come off and is lying apart from him. The office is finished. The man is still there. A bond can lose its arrangement — its terms, its ranking, the assumptions about who does what and who is owed what — while the two people in it remain entirely present to each other. That version of the ending is available. The version where the arrangement survives untouched is not.

Set this against house five, where the Wheel sits. The Wheel's motion comes from outside and does not ask permission; it does not restore a previous position. Two cards agreeing on that point settle a question you have been holding open. What you have does not resume in its current shape. Whatever your romance house delivers, it will not be the old configuration handed back intact.

What stays constant is more than you may expect. Affection is not on the table here. Neither is history, nor the parts of your obligation to this person that you would keep by choice. Nothing in this card removes your capacity to act well toward someone whose position in your life is being rewritten.

So the tension in this room resolves cleanly: you are bound, genuinely and still, and the binding as presently arranged is concluding. Watch the walk rather than bracing for a gallop. What arrives at that speed can be met standing up.

[8 · what is hidden, and what comes through others · Judgment]
House eight is the quiet house. It deals in what is withheld, what travels sideways, what is decided in a room you were not in. And the card that has come to stand in it is the loudest thing in the deck — a trumpet leaning out of cloud, a flag squared off against the sky, figures standing up out of the water with their arms in the air. Nothing about that image is discreet.

The tension resolves the moment you stop treating hidden and secret as the same word. This is not a concealment. It is a public event happening out of earshot. Somewhere at a remove from your daily life, something belonging to your own past has been raised, spoken about, and answered in the affirmative by people who did not consult you, because consulting you was never the mechanism. The commentary printed above is exact about the direction of the summons: it is heard and answered from within. What comes toward you has already had its answer given elsewhere. Your part is not to be judged. Your part is to respond.

What reaches you is money, and it reaches you through a person, not an institution: someone out of your earlier life, standing as the hand for a decision made further off. That is the role — the intermediary who already knows you, who carries what a third party has settled. Note what the card does with its three risen figures. They come up separately, out of separate coffins, and they are as one in the astonishment. The money and the messenger arrive as a single fact. You will not be able to take the sum and leave the person at the door.

That is precisely why this belongs to your love life rather than only to your accounts. A windfall carried by a stranger changes what you can afford. A windfall carried by someone with a history in your life changes who is currently in it. The transfer reopens a channel that had gone still, and the reopening is the real inheritance. It gives a name a legitimate reason to be in your week again, and legitimate reasons are how affection re-enters a life that has learned to keep its doors polite.

Read this next to the Nameless One in house seven, which says the bond you are presently held by ends in its current form. That changes the shape of what house eight delivers. If seven had promised continuity, this returning figure would arrive into an occupied room and would function as pressure, a complication to be managed. It does not. The seat is vacating. So what comes through another's hands lands in space rather than against resistance, and the person carrying it is not competing with anything — which is exactly the condition under which an old acquaintance stops being an old acquaintance.

Two things about the manner of it. It arrives suddenly; there is no approach, no softening, no series of hints that lets you rehearse. And it happens once. This is not a recurring stream you can afford to mishandle the first time and correct later. A trumpet is blown, not repeated.

What I would watch, in the meantime, is your own instinct to check the story before you accept the sum — to want the whole provenance laid out before you will let the thing be good. That instinct is not wrong, but it is slow, and it will be operating on a matter that was concluded before it reached you.

Across the table, this is the room that turns the reading outward. Houses one through six have been about what you hold, say, tolerate and pay for; they are interior arithmetic. Here the spread admits that the next movement in your life is not authored by you. Something already decided is coming in, attached to a face you know, and it will change the romantic weather rather than merely the household budget.

[9 · what you believe · the Hermit]
The consequence shows up first as a delay. Something is put to you, you have a response ready in the first second, and you do not give it — not out of caution about the person, but because an answer that arrived that fast does not yet feel like it has been paid for. So you sit with it. You turn it over until the turning has cost you something, and then you speak, and what you say is nearly always what you had in the first second. The interval was not thinking. It was a toll.

What collects that toll is a belief about how understanding is made: that it is not real until it has been carried up some distance on your own, and that anything handed over — a shortcut, a plain explanation, someone else's conclusion offered whole — is a lesser grade of knowing. You would not defend this out loud. Asked directly whether you think people can learn from each other, you would say yes, obviously, and mean it. The belief operates below the level where you would ever have to argue for it. It shows in what you accept as settled, which is only ever the thing you worked out yourself.

This card is the reason. House nine is not the house of opinions you hold; it is the house of the frame those opinions sit inside, and the frame here is a figure who climbed on purpose and stopped when he got there. He is not looking for anything. Waite is emphatic that this is attainment rather than search, and takes trouble to strip the picture of the isolation people insist on reading into it. The staff makes the point without help: it is held, not leaned on. He does not need it to stand. It is what he used on the way up.

The house does something specific to that image, though. In the ninth position, a card of attainment stops describing an event and becomes a standard — the measure you apply to every claim on your attention, including your own. That is where the cost sits. As a description of one climb, the Hermit is admirable. As a permanent condition of belief, it means each new question has to be climbed from the bottom again, alone, before you will grant that you know the answer. Efficiency has nothing to do with it. The point is the earning.

Consider what follows if the belief stays exactly as it is. You would go on being unusually accurate and unusually slow, and the slowness would keep looking like rigour, because a good deal of the time it is. But it would also keep taxing the ordinary business of being told something true. If, on the other hand, you loosened it into the version this card actually supports, very little of the accuracy would go. His cloak falls entirely clear of the glass; not a fold of it shades the light. Nothing about him is arranged so the star burns for him only. The lamp says that where he stands, another person may stand too — which means the height was never the private property of the one who climbed it. You can hold to the belief that understanding must be earned and still drop the clause that says only your own earning counts.

Notice too how far that lamp reaches. One step of ground, illuminated at a time. That is not a limitation on the light; it is the pace he chose, and it is your pace, and it is worth defending against anyone who mistakes it for hesitation. What is not worth defending is the toll.

So here is the small thing, and it is reversible in an afternoon. The next few times you catch yourself holding an answer you already have, note the reason — silently, for yourself. Sometimes it will be genuine: something is missing, a consequence hasn't been traced. Sometimes it will be that the answer came too easily to feel legitimate. Keep those two apart in your own accounting. You are not being asked to speed up, or to trust anyone you have reason to doubt. Only to stop charging yourself for the distance twice.

[10 · your standing · the Empress]
In most working groups there is a gap between the person who is trusted and the person who is quoted. They are often not the same person. Trust accumulates quietly, in the small evidence of things having gone well, and it attaches to whoever was actually present when they went well. Quotation attaches to whoever spoke last, or loudest, or in the room where the decision was minuted. The gap is not usually malice. It is a difference in the speed of two processes.

The Empress in the house of standing is entirely on the side of the first process. Waite's figure sits back among cushions in an open field, a heart-shaped shield with Venus cut into it resting beside her rather than raised, and in front of her a field of corn ripening — none of it cut. That last detail carries the room. The abundance is real, it is material, it has a body and a weight, and it is not yet harvested. She holds a sceptre topped with a globe and wears twelve stars gathered in a cluster; the authority is not in question. What is unfinished is the collection.

Think of an orchard three seasons in. The trees are the asset. Anyone walking past can see they are healthy, and anyone who knows fruit can tell they will bear. But nothing has come off them yet, so there is no crate with a name stencilled on the side, and the reputation of the orchard is currently held in the private judgment of the few people who have walked the rows.

The comparison has a limit worth naming. Fruit, once picked, becomes anonymous — it goes to market and nobody asks whose hands pruned it. What you have growing is not like that, because it is not detachable from you. Its character is yours. [12 · what is being kept from you · the Sun]
House twelve is usually read as a house of secrets, and that reading is lazy. Not-knowing comes in at least four kinds, and it is worth separating them before you decide which one you are living in. There is the fact someone is actively holding back from you. There is the fact nobody has thought to say, because to them it is unremarkable. There is the fact you were told plainly and filed as an exaggeration. And there is the fact standing out in the open on the far side of something you happen to be standing behind — not concealed by anyone, simply outside your angle. The card in this house names the fourth.

Look at the wall in the picture. It is low, grey, and runs the whole width of the card, and the horse is already out in front of it. Nothing about that scene is dim. The light is direct rather than reflected, which is exactly the distinction the source is drawing when it separates the sun of this world from what goes before the aspiring heart: this is not a light you interpret, it is a light you stand in. So whatever is being kept from you is not being kept by anyone's craft. It is a plain thing at a plain hour, and the only reason it has not reached you is where your feet are.

That changes what the man in your reading is. You have been trying to sort what you observed from what you supplied, and the sorting is possible. The real part is the movement. He has begun to go. That is not an inference you built; it is behaviour, and behaviour is the part that stands in daylight, visible to anyone standing where the light falls. The unreal part is everything you constructed to explain the movement — the account of why, the assignment of cause, the interior narration in which you are either the reason or the thing being deceived. Fear did not invent the distance. Fear supplied the story that made the distance mean something about your worth.

This is why going back over the same evidence has yielded nothing. Ambiguity is a property of the material, not a deficiency in your attention. A gesture that can be read two ways will read two ways on the ninth pass exactly as it did on the first; what accumulates is not information but interpretation, and interpretation compounds without ever converting into fact. You could study it indefinitely and know precisely what you know now.

So the answer to the question you asked about a coming break: it is not someone from your earlier life reappearing at an inconvenient hour. It is the person already in the room, and he has started leaving. As to how close — this has passed out of the internal stage, where a departure exists only as a private reconsideration, and into the behavioural one, where it shows in the ordinary texture of days. It has not reached the stage of announcement. That is the whole of the distance remaining, and it is not long ground.

The Nameless One in house seven alters the weight of that considerably. Seven says the bond ends in its present form; twelve says the ending is not being assembled somewhere out of your sight. Together they mean you are not about to be ambushed. You are standing behind a low wall, watching four sunflower heads clear the top of it, having decided that what you cannot see must be worse than what you can.

Facing the right direction is the only work here, and it is smaller than you fear and more expensive than it looks. You lose the version of him that your narration maintained, which was at least a complete character, and you lose the peculiar shelter of ambiguity, because while the evidence stays unreadable both futures remain technically alive and you are not required to grieve either. Clarity takes that shelter away. It gives you back the time you were spending on decipherment, and it charges you the comfort of not yet knowing.
