## House 3 — B wins

B earns its application more carefully. “He does not promise control over the result” establishes a useful limit, while “It may be a mismatch between instrument and task” leaves room for an ordinary communication problem without diagnosing the reader. The practical distinction in “A precise explanation may fail if the listener needed acknowledgment first” makes the teaching usable and recognizable without requiring an invented biography. Its treatment of listening, exploratory speech, and silence also fits communication more broadly than A's preference for a committing sentence.

A has the stronger moments of cadence and imagery: “Capability, not charm” is memorable, and the instrument metaphor remains coherent. It also grants that delaying speech can have “reasons, not failings.” But the decisive inference is too absolute: “a stalled piece of communication cannot be explained by insufficient skill,” followed by “What remains, then, is willingness.” The picture does not earn that exhaustive diagnosis. A reader can have incomplete information, conflicting purposes, difficult circumstances, or a real need to learn a skill. The later generosity does not undo the initial blame risk.

B's most consequential surviving defect is its own universal capacity claim: “the words, tones and forms available to you are sufficient.” This is gentler than A's diagnosis but still goes beyond what is known. The long taxonomy and terms such as “communication field” also make it feel like an articulate lesson more than an intimate reading. Both are readable; neither is exceptional for natural conversational warmth. B offers the more useful paths to self-recognition.

Contract omissions: none apparent. This house owes no fixed personal biography, and lack of one was not penalized.

## House 5 — A wins, despite a material timing omission

A offers a humane account of attraction and retains the reader's choice. “the feeling is real as an experience without yet being a contract” and “intensity alone need not be promoted into obligation” are consequential strengths. The opening song analogy is accessible and earns the idea of something known becoming newly pleasurable. The Wheel of Fortune supplies intelligible reasoning for a known person changing position in a social circle. The further comparison of the Hierophant, High Priestess, Temperance, and Sun makes the non-romantic branch decisions more transparent than B's compressed account.

B has a distinctive voice and a strong explanation of recognition as the actual event. “The event is the recognition” followed by “the third week counted from the day this reading reaches you” meets the timing promise directly. Its description of the man performing an ordinary practical task is vivid. But it introduces an unsupported deadline beyond that commitment: “A recognition of this kind lasts about the length of one conversation. It does not repeat.” It then ties the forecast to feared failure: “the summons can sound in a room you are not in.” Together these turn a reading into pressure to reorganize one's evenings to avoid losing a singular opportunity. “Your part is the answer, which is the only part the card leaves to you” further restricts agency. These are larger trust defects than the presence of the required twin-flame forecast itself.

A's most consequential surviving defect is the pseudo-justification for timing: “That precision is possible because an arrival is an event.” Having a date does not establish which week an event will happen. Both candidates use this faulty rationale. A also speaks with unwarranted biographical certainty in “You stopped choosing in house 6”; the contract requires a surrendered decision, so the branch itself is not counted as an invented extra history, but the surrounding explanation does little to invite recognition or acknowledge how broad it is. The last suggestion about a book or music is harmless but only weakly connected to the main forecast. Capitalized branch labels and the twin-flame label make A feel partly assembled from product requirements.

Contract omissions: A specifies that “The arrival falls in the third week from now,” then says “recognition is the separate instant.” It never clearly dates that separate recognition. Because the contract specifically owes one relative week for recognition, this is a material omission, not safely repaired by inference. A otherwise settles arrival, already-known man/twin flame, manner and activity, confinement, domestic location, and the surrendered work-pacing decision, using another dealt card. B meets those branch and timing commitments, but its added one-chance conditions are unsupported and coercive. A wins on the writing experience with the omission still unresolved.

## House 12 — A wins

Both candidates settle the required departure branch and locate it between decision and complete outward acknowledgment. Both explain why repeating an interpretation of unchanged ambiguous evidence cannot produce new facts, and both bring in the High Priestess. A's teaching is more developed and places the ending within the printed image's treatment of passage rather than simply using the rider's speed as authority. “The card settles direction, not motive” is the clearest useful distinction, and “You can leave the reasons unresolved” lets the reader recognize uncertainty without having to manufacture a detailed history.

B's account of repeated interpretation is especially lucid: “it does not increase the number of facts in your possession. It only increases your confidence, which is not the same thing.” That is its best passage. Its opening, however, invents supporting facts: “attention and care and good conversation no longer change the temperature for longer than an evening” and “Reassurance is accepted and does not hold.” Those precise relational patterns are not required by the contract and may be false for the reader. Later, “not withheld out of cruelty” asserts another person's motive while the piece says motives are not knowable. “it is not yours to extract in pieces” also adds a scolding boundary that the reasoning does not earn.

A's most consequential surviving defect is that it can still sound as though the forecast independently proves an observation: “The real part is the withdrawal itself.” The forecast is required, but presenting its direction as the actual observed fact risks collapsing the observation/interpretation distinction it is meant to teach. Its closing “when one more ambiguous detail appears” also moves beyond rereading unchanged evidence; a genuinely new detail might matter, even if ambiguous. B has an even more categorical version in “Nothing you notice will convert into the missing sentence.” Neither should imply that all future observation is useless. A is also abstract and repeats the settled-direction claim enough to feel more declarative than emotionally attentive.

Contract omissions: none apparent in either excerpt. The present person is leaving; closeness/stage is specified; signal versus fear's explanation is addressed; unchanged ambiguous evidence is discussed; another dealt card contributes reasoning. The quality and epistemic limits of those answers remain concerns rather than missing answers.

## Confidence and limits

High confidence in these local preference directions; moderate confidence in exact numerical scores. B in house 3, A in house 5, and A in house 12 offer the preferable reading experiences. House 5's preferred candidate is not contract-complete. No label is treated as a consistent writer across houses, and no aggregate writer verdict is inferred.

This is a three-house excerpt screen, not customer NPS, customer testing, a full-report score, or proof of whole-report quality. Recommendation scores concern only the writing shown. No assumption was made that readers aged 55 or older share a single taste. No replacement prose is supplied.
