I laid your twelve last night, gone eleven, at the kitchen table with the lamp pulled down close and the rain going.

Two of them came down facing each other across the cloth, and I sat back when they did.

Temperance in your second house. The Fool in your eighth. On its own neither one tells me which door your money walks in by. Set opposite each other, they split it clean.

One of the two comes in through your own hands. The other arrives in somebody's mouth. That is the thing I told you I could not yet see, and I can see it now.

The draw is a good one, dear. I will say that flat, before you read a single line of it.

It is hard in one place only, and that place is your seventh house and your twelfth, which are the same man.

The next two months are the window. Every card below runs on it, so no room has to invent one of its own.

Inside those two months your life happens mainly in three rooms — the fifth, the seventh and the twelfth. The other nine are the ground those three stand on, and I have read every one of them the same way I read those.

All twelve are trumps because I said they would be. No minors for a first draw like yours. It is the heaviest reading I do, it took me the whole of the night, and I do not lay it for everyone who writes.

What the letter left open, these close. Each answer stands in the room that holds it, and I have not carried any of them up here to save you the walk.

One more thing before you begin.

You paid a stranger to tell you about your own life, and some part of you filed that under weakness. Take it back out of the file. Nothing on this table is an accusation, and wanting to know is not the same as being lost.

And read all twelve before you decide what they say. One room will promise you ease and the next will ask you for work. That is the arrangement, not a contradiction — no life reads consistent either.

[1 · yourself · the Sun]
Four sunflowers stand above a grey wall, heads up over the top of it — taller than that wall was ever built to allow. They came up where they were planted and got over it anyway. Warm, plain, easy to read, and further along than the people near you have bothered to notice.

The child on the white horse is already out in front of that wall, bareback, both arms open, the red flag streaming sideways. Nothing about you is hidden at the moment.

Which is why your seventh house matters here. Bound to someone, you go quiet — you hint, you wait to be asked, you let him guess. Alone you are in full daylight. Those are not the same woman, and I won't pretend they are.

So stop translating yourself. Say the plain sentence to him this month, unsoftened.

You already decided you were done pretending you didn't mind.

[2 · what you own · Temperance]
One foot is in the pool, ankle-deep, and the other stays flat on the bank beside the yellow flowers. She has not gone in and she has not stepped out. Both are held at once, deliberately.

Your money is being kept the same way. Two small amounts, apart — one you earned, one still owed to you.

That is the door the first windfall comes through, dear. Not a stranger, not a prize, not an inheritance. Money already in your name, in two places, added up and spent at last as one thing.

You will know it by the count. Two cups, one held higher than the other, and the water crossing between them — one sum pours into the second, and the total is larger than you have been assuming.

The Fool in your eighth stands barefoot with nothing arranged for him. No other hands are bringing you this one. Yours are.

Which of the two have you never written down?

[3 · what you say, and who you say it to · the Hanged Man]
His hands are behind his back, out of sight, elbows pushed out to either side. Nobody tied those. He put them there himself.

The message you have written and not sent is that same gesture. You are keeping your own hands out of it deliberately, and the quiet is costing you time you can feel going.

The Hierophant in your ninth is why. You hold a rule you have never said out loud — that whoever speaks first has handed something over. You would not defend that sentence to me, dear, but you obey it every time you close the phone.

So this is not shyness. It is a rule, and you are the one enforcing it on yourself.

What hangs there does not rot. The gallows is living wood, small green leaves pushing out of it, and you are being paid in a clearer view of him.

Nothing reaches him while you hang.

[4 · home, and what you came from · the Hermit]
The lamp is held out at shoulder height, well away from his body, a six-pointed star burning inside the glass. His cloak falls clear of it — not one fold of grey drifting near the flame. He is careful with that light, and he is careful with it by himself.

That is the house you came out of. Nothing was asked for in it. Things were handled quietly and reported afterwards, already done.

You have caught yourself at this and decided it was only competence. It is not. You inherited it, and you are still running your rooms on it.

The Empress in your tenth has you full-handed in public — feeding people, easy with them, generous where you are seen. Indoors you carry the whole thing alone and mention it to nobody. The public room is being paid for out of the private one.

I won't call that strength, dear.

This month, ask one person into your house and give them something to carry out of it.

Nothing arrives at a house run that way. It cannot get in the door. Which is what makes your fifth house so strange, because something arrives anyway, unasked for and unarranged.

[5 · romance, and what delights you · the Wheel of Fortune]
The sphinx lies flat along the top of the wheel, sword held level across its body, and it never moves with the rim. Under it a snake goes down one side and a dog-headed figure climbs the other. Everything turns except the thing on top.

The High Priestess sits veiled in your seventh, saying nothing. So there is no one there to grow warmer toward you, and I won't hand you a renewal that does not exist.

Yours arrives instead. Your twin flame, in the third week from the day you read this — I can date this one room because an arrival is an event, and events land on a day.

You already know him. The Devil in your eleventh keeps him inside your own circle. He will be mid-errand, on his way to somebody else, and he will stop longer than the errand allows.

Arriving and being known are two separate days, dear.

You wait until you are alone in the car to say the sentence out loud.

Leave this room as it stands and he finishes the errand, and you say it to the windscreen again.

[6 · work, health, the daily grind · the Magician]
The snake at his waist has no buckle. It holds itself shut by biting its own tail, and the man inside it is fed by nothing but his own hands.

Your working week runs the same way, and it always has. Cup, coin, sword, wand — four things laid out, and not one of them is missing from your table.

You carried someone else's part through a bad stretch and never once said whose it was. That was the right call and I will not let it go unsaid.

But Judgment stands in your twelfth. People are weighing you where you cannot hear it, and what they are weighing is this ordinary week.

So do what the picture does. One hand up, one hand down, the whole line visible.

Say out loud who did which piece this month — the table is already laid, dear, and nothing on it lifts itself.

[7 · who you are bound to · the High Priestess]
The crescent lies flat at her feet, horns up, and the hem of her robe has come down over part of it. Her own light is under her clothing, and she sits there anyway.

There is a price still running in this room. You are tied to a man who has never been told the plain thing, and you have named that waiting loyalty.

It is not loyalty. The scroll is in her hands the entire time — she knows, dear, and she has chosen the pace herself.

He is keeping something back as well. I won't guess at what, and I won't invent it to fill the space.

If the quiet here gets taken for patience and left alone, he says his plain thing to someone else first — and you hear it from her, kindly, long after.

Her robe runs down into the water at the bottom edge, and the join does not show. Nothing marks where she stops and the water starts.

So the seventh is the room where you are paying. The eighth is the room where you are paid, and not by him.

[8 · what is hidden, and what comes through others · the Fool]
The white rose hangs stem down from his lowered hand, loose enough to drop. He is not carrying it anywhere, and he did not pick it himself. Somebody gave him that.

I promised you a second windfall, and this eighth house is where it comes from. It is not money — Temperance in your second already counted the money out, two sums, both of them yours.

So this one is free to arrive as a person's word instead.

Somebody inside your own circle says your name to him, and says it well, without being asked to. Hands you did not arrange, on an errand that was never yours.

That is why it moves your love life and not your bank balance. Nobody hands over a purse here — they hand over a sentence about you.

The Fool is not charging you with being naive, dear, or with stepping out unready. It shows a gift given before you thought to want it.

You decided, a while ago, that you would never ask anyone to speak for you.

[9 · what you believe · the Hierophant]
Two men kneel below him in white robes, backs to us. We never see their faces. They are not being taught anything — they are waiting to be told they did it in the correct order.

You hold a belief you would deny if I said it plainly: that approval has to be granted to you by somebody with the standing to grant it. It cannot be taken. It has to be given, properly, in front of others.

Point at three things and you will find it. The person you check with before you decide anything that matters. The order you make yourself do things in when you want something. The reason you give for having waited.

That is not carefulness, dear. It is a rule, and it is slower than your life.

The two keys are crossed on the floor at his feet. He has not handed them down to anyone.

Whose approval are you still waiting to be given?

Then the tenth house arrives and makes that rule look expensive.

[10 · your standing · the Empress]
The corn stands ripe in front of her, and not one stalk of it has been taken. Full ears, bent with their own weight, still in the field.

You are already thought well of. That grew while you were busy, and it is further on than you think.

What has not happened is the cutting. Nobody has said your name aloud in the room where the decisions get made, and you have never said it there either.

The Hermit in your fourth is the same hand at work. Indoors you hand things over already finished, with no one attached — and outside you do it exactly the same way.

This card feeds what is grown, provided you stand where it is counted. So take the next piece of praise, dear, and stop there. Do not pass it along to whoever helped, and do not cut it shorter. Modesty has nothing to do with it.

The sceptre stays up in her hand the whole time.

[11 · your circle · the Devil]
The torch is lit and turned down, the flame pointed at the ground beside that black block. It burns nothing and lights nobody. All the warmth in that group of yours is being spent at the floor.

Count the chains. Two necks, two chains, one ring — the whole thing hangs off a single fixture, and the loops are wide enough to lift off over the head.

So your circle is not the crowd. It is two people keeping one thing in place, and one of them is you.

The Wheel in your fifth is what pays for this. He is inside this group, and that is why you keep saying yes — you stay near the people who cost you, to stay in the room with him.

I am not telling you to drop anyone, dear.

The next time the one who always organises asks you for something, say no. Out loud, this week.

Keep the two men apart as you read on. The one standing in your eleventh is the arrival. The one in your twelfth is the man you already have, and his is the harder page.

[12 · what is being kept from you · Judgment]
Further out on the water, past the three in front, more coffins stand open — small figures with their arms up, too far off for faces. The calling has already started out there, where you cannot see who is standing.

The man you are bound to has begun leaving. Not a face from before, returning at the wrong hour — the one still here, the one sitting behind the veil in your seventh.

It has begun in him and nowhere else yet. Nobody around you has seen it.

This stayed out of your sight for a plain reason. Your days run like the Magician in your sixth — four things on the table, all of them yours, both hands busy. A woman working that hard hears nothing coming.

Facing the right direction is narrow, dear. You ask him the direct question, and you refuse a warm answer that answers nothing.

Say nothing and the news reaches you last, out of another woman's mouth, long after everybody involved already knew.

Add the twelve up and they say one thing about you.

You run your entire life on not saying the plain sentence. Not to him. Not in the room where you are counted. Not to the woman who always asks you for the favour.

Every card here is charging you for that silence, and the price has just gone up.

The order matters now. The word somebody speaks for you comes first, and it comes without your help. The arrival in your fifth follows it. Neither of those waits on you.

The direct question in your twelfth does wait on you. That is the condition of the other two, and Judgment is standing there with the far coffins already open — the calling began before anyone told you. Ask him late, and you meet the man in your fifth with both hands still tied.

Here is what changes, dear. You will be early to your own news for once, hearing things while they are still being decided rather than after they are settled.

The first of it shows inside five to seven days. Someone in your circle repeats a good sentence about you that came out of somebody else's mouth. Mark the day it happens.

I have dated your fifth house and no other room in this reading. An arrival is an event and lands on a day; a man's slow leaving does not, and I am not going to invent days for the rest of it.

Two months is when the movement starts, not when it is all finished. Some of these rooms will have opened inside a fortnight. The Hermit and the Hierophant are slow cards by nature, and slow is not the same as failing — women stop a few weeks short of the thing coming right.

This week, write down the second sum, the one still owed to you. Put the figure somewhere you walk past every day.
