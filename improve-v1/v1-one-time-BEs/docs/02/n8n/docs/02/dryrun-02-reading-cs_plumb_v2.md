I laid your twelve last night, after the house had gone quiet, and I stayed at the table until every one of them had been written on.

The one I sat with longest was your first — a man hanging by one ankle with the light already lit around his head. I could not write a line of it until I looked across at your seventh, where the Hierophant sits. Until then I could not see why a woman who already knows the truth of her own life would hold so perfectly still.

It is a good draw, dear. I want that said before you read anything else.

The next stretch of your life happens in three of these rooms: your first, which is you on your own; your second, which is money; and your fifth, which answers what I could not settle about the Lovers. All twelve run on one window — the next two months. That is the horizon I am reading to, so no room below has to invent its own.

Every one of the twelve is a trump. A Majors-only twelve costs me a whole night and most of the day after, and I keep it for the draws that ask for it. Yours asked.

What my two letters left open is settled here. Which door the money comes through, which reading of the Lovers is yours, which of your two pourings is being filled, whether the structure you are standing inside is a floor or a shut room — none of it gets answered up here. Each answer is standing in the room that decides it, and several of the rooms below carry work my letters never touched at all.

Here is what I think you had to admit to yourself before you paid me. That you no longer trust your own read on your own situation. You do trust it. You have simply stopped saying it out loud, and a thing never said aloud begins to feel invented. That is all that has happened, and I will not raise it again.

One instruction before you go down. Read all twelve before you decide what they say. A card will hand you something easily in one room while another makes you work for the same thing, and it will look as though I have contradicted myself. I have not. The meaning is in the arrangement, and no woman's life is consistent either.

[1 · yourself · the Hanged Man]

Nothing else on that card is moving. The light is the only thing on it doing any work.

And here is the first thing it says. You are not in trouble in this card.

Look at how he got there. He is tied by one ankle to a gallows shaped like a T, and the wood is alive — small green leaves are coming out of it. He tied the rope himself.

So this is not a punishment and it is not a failure. It is a decision you made, and it has been costing you time, and the time has bought something.

That is the good news in this room, and I want it said plainly before anything else. The bill has largely been paid. What you have not done is collect.

Because the light is on. Somewhere in the last stretch of standing still you worked out what is actually true about your situation — not what you tell people, the real one. You know it. You have known it for a while.

His free leg is crossed behind the tied one, making the shape of a four. His hands are behind his back, elbows out. Nothing is reaching for anything.

Now, three places you can point at it, and you will know each one better than I do.

Second: what you say when somebody asks how things are, or what you are going to do. You have a phrase. You use it every time. It is a phrase that ends the conversation without telling anybody anything, and you did not always speak that way.

Third: the message, or the call, that you compose and do not send. You know which person is at the other end of it.

Those three are the rope. And you will notice the rope goes round one ankle only — you are not bound hand and foot in this card, dear.

Now, the reason you are hanging there rather than standing up, and this is where I have to bring in another card, because on its own the first house does not explain itself.
