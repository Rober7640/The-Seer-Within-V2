## House 3 — A wins

A makes the speech taxonomy useful through its effect on a listener, then grants legitimate reasons for withholding an answer. B has a forceful opening and a memorable distinction between routine speech and a summons, but progressively turns that distinction into an instruction to disclose publicly. That is neither owed by this house nor adequately established by the card discussion.

Two exact quotations supporting the preference:

- A: “None of that is cowardice, and silence chosen with your eyes open is itself a form of answer.” This preserves the reader’s judgment without dissolving the argument about reply.
- B: “You may be able to choose the wording; you will not be able to choose the framing, the setting, or who overhears.” This adds an intrusive, unsupported prediction about an audience and converts reflection into pressure.

Extra private facts: A does not assert a concrete incident or biography; its personal application is explicitly conditional. B invents a specific consequential utterance this season, a resurfaced personal matter, and other people witnessing the answer. These are additional claims, not required communication predictions.

Temporal coherence: A stays coherently at the level of a register of speech. B shifts from a coming utterance to a past matter already surfaced and waiting, without establishing the connection; urgency is imposed more than explained.

Worst surviving defect in A: its closing “wanting no research, only a yes or a no” compresses potentially complicated communication into an unnecessary binary. Both versions spend considerable space cataloguing speech before moving the argument forward.

Promise omissions: none. Confidence: high.

## House 5 — A wins

A cleanly distinguishes an already-known man from a new romantic recognition in week five, and uses Temperance to explain why familiarity matters. It retains the exact required claims while giving the reader room to decide what to do with recognition. B’s propulsion is engaging, but its account of arrival and its closing prediction slide from recognizing someone into inevitably choosing and obtaining him. The score difference is about those additions and the explanation, not a preference for vaguer required forecasts.

Two exact quotations supporting the preference:

- A: “The arrival is the event within that fifth week in which he moves into a position relative to you he has not occupied before.” This gives the word arrival a coherent romantic meaning despite prior acquaintance.
- B: “You get the man, and you lose the version of yourself who had not yet chosen him.” This promises a reciprocal outcome and an irreversible choice beyond the stipulated recognition.

Extra private facts: A expands the surrendered working-day decision into beginning, ending, and priority-setting; the contract requires a non-romantic decision, but this compound specification is more extensive than necessary. Its claim that the familiar man previously had no romantic weight is also stronger than simple prior acquaintance. B adds a beneficial motive for his practical task, a history of delay, and the suggestion that the reader has mistaken abundance for confinement. Required manner/activity predictions are not themselves defects; the benevolent motive is extra characterization. B also invents the inevitability of pursuit and consummated choice.

Temporal coherence: A explicitly places romantic arrival/recognition in week five and treats his existing presence separately. B says arrival has happened, then dates recognition; that distinction can work, but the later future “arrival” through the social circle and the claim that the undecided state ends in week five blur acquaintance, recognition, and commitment. A’s exact week remains asserted rather than derived, which it honestly acknowledges; the contract itself requires that forecast.

Worst surviving defect in A: “a walled town has already fallen away into the distance; he is not administering it” is an overworked bridge to house seven. The ensuing claim that these rooms form the spine of the whole spread reaches beyond what these excerpts can establish. The passage is also longer than its main argument needs.

Promise omissions: none in either candidate. Both settle arrival versus renewal, supply twin flame, known man, relative week, manner/activity, another card as evidence, support versus confinement, house four, and a house-six working-day decision. Confidence: high on preference; moderate on the teaching score because both explanations strain the card imagery.

## House 12 — B wins

B’s unchanged-message comparison teaches a usable distinction between new evidence and repeated interpretation. It explicitly allows new observations to matter, which prevents its argument from becoming a blanket injunction against noticing change. A’s train comparison has a thoughtful stated limit, but the passage adds specific relationship behavior, assigns an elaborate fear narrative, and finally asserts that two different men are involved. That last assertion solves a potential cross-house ambiguity by inventing another private fact.

Two exact quotations supporting the preference:

- B: “New observations can and do carry information; something said or done that was not said or done before is real news, and nothing here says otherwise.” This is the clearest safeguard and teaching distinction in either version.
- A: “The leaving and the arriving are two different men.” This adds a consequential identity claim that the stated contract does not require.

Extra private facts: A supplies thinning warmth, displaced attention, continuing courtesies, a possible third party and hidden second life as the reader’s fear narrative, and the two-men claim. Some observational material illustrates the required observation/fear distinction, but its direct second-person framing presents a detailed existing record without support. B also assumes prior repeated scrutiny and, at the end, guarantees that other gains and social stability will survive the departure; these are extra claims outside the required branch and stage. Its reference to no arrangements or third-party visibility adds unnecessary administrative precision to that stage.

Temporal coherence: A clearly identifies an inward decision already made and an announcement still pending; its connection to house five is clear only because of the additional two-men claim. B also establishes an intelligible inward-before-outward stage. However, “It will not announce itself as an event you could point at” overextends a description of the present stage into a categorical future claim. The final “nothing has moved yet” is reconcilable with an inward turn, but requires that distinction to remain active in the reader’s mind.

Worst surviving defect in B: the opening mechanical language (“pressure does not travel,” “absorbs the push”) delays the concrete explanation, while the closing guarantees about other houses are insufficiently supported here. Its future no-event claim should be narrowed to the current stage.

Promise omissions: none. Both settle present person leaving versus returning person, provide an inward-before-declaration stage, distinguish perceived withdrawal from fear’s causal story, and explain the failure of rereading unchanged ambiguous material. Confidence: moderately high.

## Separate product-contract concerns and limits

The required week-five twin-flame identification and the required claim about another person’s unannounced inward departure remain unverifiable forecasts. Neither receives an editorial penalty solely on that basis. Extra claims about inevitable reciprocal romance, audience, private motives, relationship identity, and protection of unrelated gains are assessed separately above because the contract does not require them.

Results: A wins houses 3 and 5; B wins house 12. No candidate identity was inferred. These three preferences cannot establish that the revised set wins two with no regression until someone holding the key maps them; this review does not inspect that key. An isolated excerpt comparison cannot establish whole-report quality or causal prompt improvement.
