I laid your twelve last night, late, when the house had gone quiet enough that I could hear each card come down on the wood. Your reading came out of the trumps alone. That is the heaviest work I do, it costs me a whole night, and I keep it for the women whose first draw asks for it — yours asked.

Two of them pull against each other, and I saw it while they were still going down. The Wheel of Fortune in your sixth says movement is already under way and not by your hand. The Hanged Man in your ninth says you believe holding still is the honest way to be paid. I am not going to settle that here. The arrangement below settles it, and it does not settle it in the Hanged Man's favour.

This is a good draw, dear. Not a gentle one in every room, but a good one. The next two months are the window for all twelve, and no room below invents a clock of its own. Your life actually moves in three of them — your second, your fifth, your sixth. Money through your own hands. An arrival. And the ordinary week that turns out to be the door for both.

Four rooms carry what you were owed. Your second and your eighth hold the two windfalls, and each names the door its own comes through. Your fifth separates the two readings I refused to choose between. Your twelfth names the person the Tower meant. That is what you paid for, and each answer stands in its own house below.

Whatever you had to admit to yourself before you paid me — set it down now. Nobody reads her own house from inside it.

One thing before you start. Some of this will look as though it argues with itself — ease offered in one room, work asked for in the next. Read all twelve before you decide what they say. The meaning is in the arrangement, and no life is consistent either.

This card is not calling you weak, and it is not calling you a fool. It is showing an arrangement you get something real out of. Comfort. Quiet. The relief of not having to decide. It holds because it pays you.

Between the horns sits a star set upside down, above the eyes. The thinking is inverted, dear. You have made the smaller life sound like the sensible one.
