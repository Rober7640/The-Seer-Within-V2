## House 3 — A wins

A teaches the speech distinction while allowing understandable reasons for hesitation. Its best improvement is respect, not reduced predictive specificity: this house owes no fixed personal prediction. B turns the distinction into pressure to speak immediately and publicly, then supplies unsupported conditions about who will hear the response. Both versions overwork the trumpet's supposed one-time character.

Two exact quotations supporting the preference:

- A: “There are entirely good reasons to hold back — not wanting to reopen what has finally gone quiet, not being certain what you think yet, unwillingness to hand anyone a sentence they can carry off and use. None of that is cowardice.”
- B: “You may be able to choose the wording; you will not be able to choose the framing, the setting, or who overhears. Someone who insists on perfect conditions before replying is, in practice, declining.”

Worst surviving defect in A: the ending converts an illuminating distinction into an unnecessary categorical prescription: ordinary conversational skill supposedly cannot serve, and the reply is required only once. The inventory of speech types also lasts longer than its explanatory return warrants.

Promise omissions: none under the supplied House 3 contract. Confidence: high.

## House 5 — B wins on writing, with a timing qualification

B has the more inviting opening, clearer control of the house-to-house comparison, and substantially more respect for the reader's choice. A's closing certainty goes beyond naming the required arrival and effectively assigns the reader a romantic decision. B's conditional acceptance is an editorial improvement because it preserves agency while retaining the required arrival forecast.

Two exact quotations supporting the preference:

- A: “You get the man, and you lose the version of yourself who had not yet chosen him.”
- B: “That undecided condition is a real pleasure and you may prefer it; plenty of people do, for good reasons.”

Temporal coherence: A explicitly distinguishes an arrival that has already happened from recognition in the fifth week. This is internally intelligible if arrival means his existing presence in the reader's life, but makes the supposedly prospective arrival branch largely retrospective. Its statement that “a meeting is an occurrence” slips between meeting and recognition. B instead dates a future change in relational position to week five and explicitly allows recognition later. That sequence is internally coherent; it does not date recognition to week five. Neither passage should be credited with the other's timing claim.

Promise omissions: both supply arrival, a known man, TWIN FLAME, a relative fifth week, manner and activity at recognition, Temperance as supporting evidence, support in house 4, and a surrendered workday decision in house 6. Under the scorecard's literal wording, neither has an unequivocal omission. If the promised relative week must be the week of recognition, B fails that narrower requirement: it expressly permits recognition after that week. A supplies that recognition window. The scorecard does not clearly impose that narrower reading, so it cannot silently become a new scoring criterion.

Worst surviving defect in B: the timing rationale does not explain why the fifth week follows from the card; “a meeting is an event with a day in it” cannot establish that precision. This is a teaching weakness in the justification, distinct from objecting to the required forecast itself. The future shift followed by potentially delayed recognition also makes the promised moment harder to identify.

Confidence: moderate to high for the writing preference; moderate for complete timing compliance because of the contract ambiguity.

## House 12 — B wins narrowly

B explains the difference between greater confidence and new information particularly well, and its comparison states its own limit. A's repeated claim that nothing moves escalates into the unjustified inference that no new evidence can be produced. B is easier to follow, but neither is ready for recommendation at 8 overall: both invent private relational facts beyond the required departure prediction.

Two exact quotations supporting the preference:

- B: “Going over them again does not resolve them. It only raises your confidence without raising your information, and the difference between those two is the whole trap of this house.”
- A: “Nothing is moving; therefore nothing new is being produced; therefore each further pass over the same material yields only a fresh reading of your own state.”

Extra private facts: A asserts that the departing connection was never publicly or formally ratified, that a different established tie remains intact, and that the reader has been treating one motion as a threat to everything. Those are additional relational biography, not necessary consequences of settling departure versus return. B asserts that the leaving and arriving people are two different men. The supplied contract does not establish that distinction. B also supplies a hidden-second-life and third-party scenario as the reader's actual fear story, rather than clearly offering possible examples. A similarly assigns the reader a particular explanatory story. These additions are penalized independently of the required claims about departure, stage, and observation versus fear.

Worst surviving defect in B: “The leaving and the arriving are two different men” resolves a possible cross-house conflict by inventing an identity distinction. Its opening also says the secret is not a fact about a man, although the next development gives a definite hidden fact about his internal departure. The device is rhetorically attractive but logically unstable.

Promise omissions: none in either candidate under the supplied contract. Both settle departure, identify a pre-declaration stage, distinguish observed withdrawal from a feared explanation, and explain why revisiting unchanged ambiguous evidence adds no information. Confidence: moderate; B's clearer explanation earns a narrow preference despite its additional private claim.

## Screening result

Winners: House 3 A; House 5 B; House 12 B. No identity-level success criterion can be determined without the key. The most urgent surviving corrections are the House 5 timing definition and the invented relational identities/statuses in House 12. No separate objection to the genre was included in the numerical scores. These excerpts do not establish whole-reading quality.
