**Decision reason:**

The underlying product experience is strong, but repeated unsupported certainty damages trust and can influence consequential choices. The problem appears in the sampled houses and becomes more pronounced in the full reading and close.

**The one change required before reconsideration, if any:**

Apply one evidence boundary throughout: rewrite unsupported predictions, third-party mental states, exact timing, and outcome-dependent instructions as conditional possibilities or reflective questions, while retaining only low-risk actions grounded in observable customer facts.

## Post-decision close review

The one-page synthesis is one of the more readable sections. It restores the whole-reading map and ends with a reversible action:

> “Move one arrangement in the place you live that you never actually chose…”

The four-page ledger practice is clearly organized and initially sounds useful. Recording observations without immediately interpreting them could support reflection. The practice then becomes a confirmation mechanism for the reading:

> “Your reading said the first sign shows itself inside five to seven days. It lands in this turn.”
>
> “If nothing happens / Then you run the twenty-eight again.”
>
> “You did. It will be on the page in your own handwriting, three weeks before it happened…”

This is self-sealing: a hit confirms the prediction, while a miss requires another cycle. Combined with the claims about a man withdrawing, it may encourage hypervigilance and reinterpret ordinary messages, silences, and social encounters as evidence. The close reduces trust and should be rewritten before release.

## Sparse bold or underlining assessment

Sparse bold would improve readability for an older customer, particularly on dense continuation pages. Underlining should not be introduced; it resembles hyperlinks or direct-response sales emphasis and would make the document feel cheaper.

The best structural placement is one short takeaway on the final prose page of each house, after the interpretation and before any optional action. Use a separate line of roughly 8–15 words. Do not bold predictions, dates, “TWIN FLAME,” money amounts, or claims about another person, because emphasis would increase their apparent authority.

On the final synthesis page, use three short bold labels for the three house relationships. The ledger already has enough bold hierarchy in its section headings and night-range labels; further inline emphasis there would be excessive.

A safe frequency is:

- At most one body takeaway per house.
- Roughly one emphasized body line every two pages.
- No more than two bold elements on a page, including headings.
- No consecutive bold paragraphs, colored callout boxes, all caps, or underlining.

This would add useful scan points, but it would not solve the two sparse spill pages or the unsupported-certainty problem.
