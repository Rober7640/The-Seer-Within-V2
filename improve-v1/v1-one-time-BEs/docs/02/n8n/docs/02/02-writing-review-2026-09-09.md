# 02 Twin Flame reading — writing review

## 1. VERDICT FIRST — blind

The operator is right: the writing is terrible for a paid keepsake. It is not mainly terrible because the cards are misdescribed, the promises are unpaid, or the voice lacks force. It is terrible because it delivers conclusions before it has created understanding. The buyer gets twelve compressed verdict packets: image detail, personal claim, cross-reference, instruction or warning, stop. She is repeatedly told what is true of her, but almost never taught what the card means in a way that lets her recognise herself and arrive at the conclusion with the reader.

That difference was visible before opening any prompt or spec. The two generated documents are 2,880 and 2,964 words including their openings and closes. The three gold texts are 8,011, 8,150, and 8,948 words for seven cards. The raw length comparison is not perfectly like-for-like, but the use of the space is decisive: the gold repeatedly moves from Waite to definition, analogy, several ordinary-life categories, normalisation, and only then to the reader. The generated readings jump from a selected object in the picture to a claim about Sarah. Length is therefore the dominant defect, but it is not the only one; the prose also bears the fingerprints of fixed rules imposed twelve times.

### 1 — The readings assert recognition instead of earning it

The most refund-prone move is the unsupported leap from card to intimate verdict.

- v1, house 1: “Bound to someone, you go quiet — you hint, you wait to be asked, you let him guess. Alone you are in full daylight.”
- v2, house 7: “The man you are bound to is not improving. You are the one keeping him level.”

Neither passage first establishes a usable meaning of the Sun or Strength, shows how that meaning behaves in ordinary life, or offers a range of ways it might manifest. The claim simply arrives with the reader’s authority attached.

The gold does the opposite. Its Temperance passage first extracts a governing idea: “Temperance in general indicates balance and a certain sense of one’s higher and lower natures being in harmony.” It then defines that through an everyday example: “For example, outside of Tarot, ‘temperance’ is predominantly associated with refraining from alcohol.” It widens the idea into “ethics,” “religion,” and “lifestyle,” and only after that removes shame: “Nearly all of us suffer from it to one degree or another.” By the time it asks the buyer to locate her own disharmony, the reader has supplied a ladder rather than a pronouncement.

### 2 — The prose is clipped, airless, and all payload

Nearly every sentence has to be a finding, a correction, a threat, or an instruction. There is no conversational slack in which a thought can be explored.

- v2, house 2: “Your money moves without being pushed by you, and it moves upward.”
- v2, house 2: “Read the wheel as luck and the letter goes unanswered, and the amount shrinks. Leave this room and the money arrives and drains straight out again, unaccounted.”
- v1, house 12: “The man you are bound to has begun leaving. Not a face from before, returning at the wrong hour — the one still here, the one sitting behind the veil in your seventh.”

The second v2 quotation is particularly damaging: it makes two large causal claims in two breaths, with no explanation of why reading the card incorrectly would shrink or drain actual money. Compression turns metaphor into arbitrary law.

The gold is not merely longer; it changes pace. On the Wheel it says, “The Wheel of Fortune, however, reminds us that very little is in our control. The universe is fluid, and human life is always in flux.” Then it takes a real digression: “Are you perusing this reading during your lunch break? Consider the wheat that grew, died, and was ground to create the bread of the sandwich that you are chewing on.” The analogy lets the reader experience the proposition before it is applied. Generated 02 never has room for that kind of thought.

### 3 — Evelyn has become a repeated refusal construction

The v2 grader correctly caught one surface version, but the buyer would feel it long before she could name it.

- v1, house 4: “I won’t call that strength, dear.”
- v2, house 4: “I won’t call that peace, dear.”
- v2, house 6: “I won’t call that a virtue, dear.”
- v2, house 9: “I won’t call that good manners, dear.”
- v2, house 10: “I won’t have you keep paying it that way, dear.”
- v2, house 11: “I won’t pretend it pours itself, dear.”

The first person is not functioning as a mind on the page. It is a rubber stamp meaning “reader present.” Six variants do not create six moments; they expose the form.

The gold’s first person expresses an actual relationship to the material. “I admit, I often viewed Four of Pentacles in a purely negative fashion, until I by chance discovered a different perspective.” Elsewhere, after considering Waite, it says: “Waite says they tend to retreat back into the waters—I’m not at all sure I agree. I do agree with his suggested course of treatment.” That is essayistic presence: memory, taste, disagreement, revision. It cannot be reduced to “I refuse to call this X.”

### 4 — Every room opens like an inventory, and the inventory is forced to become proof

The selected picture details are often accurate, but their compulsory placement makes the twelve passages mechanically identical. Worse, a small visual feature is made to carry an interpretation it cannot bear.

- v1, house 6: “The snake at his waist has no buckle. It holds itself shut by biting its own tail, and the man inside it is fed by nothing but his own hands.”
- v2, house 8: “The marks worked into that wallet never make a word. Someone stitched them there and left them unreadable — a bag carrying something with no paperwork behind it.”

“No paperwork behind it” is not a reading of the Fool; it is a clever bridge invented to service a windfall obligation. The picture has become a quarry for metaphors. Across twenty-four rooms, even good image work becomes monotonous because the reader always watches the same operation: object first, meaning second.

The gold separates authority from interpretation. Its Moon block gives the actual symbol — “The path between the towers is the issue into the unknown” — and the writer then starts a human argument elsewhere: “I’ve developed a heuristic about life: assume everything is more complicated than it seems.” A little later she defines the concept: “What is the subconscious? Loosely, it’s everything that isn’t current conscious thought.” The picture supports the essay; it is not forced to masquerade as evidence for a personal fact.

### 5 — The spread has been over-joined into a proof graph

Cross-reading is necessary in the promise rooms, but v2 drags another card into all twelve rooms. That makes every passage feel engineered and keeps interrupting the card the buyer is supposedly reading.

- v2, house 2: “The Fool in your eighth says the same thing from the other end: it comes through a stranger’s decision, not your effort.”
- v2, house 3: “Judgment stands opposite in your ninth: the thing you hold and would not defend out loud is precisely the thing that survives being said flat.”
- v2, house 6: “The High Priestess sits still in your twelfth, between her two pillars. Your days run at this speed because you are not looking at what she is keeping.”

The current metrics confirm the blind impression: v1 names another card in 8 of 12 passages; v2 does it in 12 of 12. A spread is not made coherent by making every paragraph cite another paragraph.

The gold makes connections when a real conceptual turn needs them. “Having taken the steps of adopting Temperance, The Wheel of Fortune serves as a slight course correction.” It then explains the relationship: “Temperance emphasizes self-control. The Wheel of Fortune, however, reminds us that very little is in our control.” The second card changes the first idea; it is not merely named as corroboration.

### 6 — The endings are a deck of gimmicks, not the end of a thought

Rooms stop on conspicuous devices: a carrying question, a picture echo, a cost, a command, a pre-decided choice. The rotation changes the gimmick, not the fact that a gimmick has arrived.

- v1, house 2: “Which of the two have you never written down?”
- v2, house 6: “Where were you going, when you first pointed yourself this way?”
- v1, house 5: “Leave this room as it stands and he finishes the errand, and you say it to the windscreen again.”

The questions have not been developed enough to remain with the reader “two rooms later”; they read like mandated cliffhangers. The windscreen ending is vivid but also repeats an invented fact and closes on a miniature threat.

The gold teaches before it acts: “Each tool is first described in general terms, so you understand how and when the concept should be used. Then, each tool includes a series of seven concrete action steps.” It also returns agency: “Feel free to modify them to better suit your particular circumstances.” Its action is the consequence of explanation, not the closing shape assigned to a 150-word slot.

### 7 — False intimacy is being mistaken for personalisation

Several details are specific enough to be falsifiable but have no support in the card or buyer data. These are not categories she fills in; they are little scenes the model has decided happened.

- v1, house 5: “You wait until you are alone in the car to say the sentence out loud.”
- v2, house 4: “The order you do things in on a Sunday. The way your voice changes when a certain relative telephones. The one room in the house nobody sits in.”
- v2, house 8: “You have already noticed her doing it, dear. You decided you were flattering yourself.”

A buyer who does not drive, has no Sunday routine, or has no such woman is ejected from the reading immediately. The claims are presented as observed facts, so she cannot treat them as illustrative possibilities.

The gold uses concrete examples while openly widening them. “It’s possible to apply this on a much broader scale. Disharmony can be found in ethics … religion … and … lifestyle.” In another passage it says, “So to help, try tackling things categorically,” then offers work, home, spirituality, family, friends, feelings, talents, and hobbies. A hundred women can each supply different particulars. Specificity belongs in the example space; it should not be counterfeited as knowledge of this buyer.

### 8 — The voice corrects and threatens more often than it understands

The readings frequently convert the buyer’s silence, caution, or attachment into a bill she is making herself pay. That is not bracing honesty; it is coercive causality.

- v1 close: “Every card here is charging you for that silence, and the price has just gone up.”
- v1, house 4: “Nothing arrives at a house run that way. It cannot get in the door.”
- v2 close: “Hold on, and the man in your fifth still comes, but he finds you occupied, and the money passes through you.”

The last line makes both love and money contingent on accepting the reader’s relationship verdict. It weaponises the promises. A paying reader can reasonably hear: obey this reading or lose both rewards.

The gold can be direct without making shame the engine. “This is natural for human beings, and nothing to be ashamed of—but neither is it optimal.” When the material becomes darker, it stops to say, “Here’s a comforting thought, though: you aren’t alone. Everyone has a shadow self.” The correction follows recognition of a shared human pattern. In generated 02, the correction usually arrives first and the human context never arrives.

### 9 — The joiner sounds like an index of verdicts, not a person finishing a sitting

The opening and close are structurally competent but administratively voiced. They tell the buyer which answers exist and how they depend on one another.

- v1 opening: “Inside those two months your life happens mainly in three rooms — the fifth, the seventh and the twelfth. The other nine are the ground those three stand on.”
- v2 opening: “Everything the two letters left hanging is settled below. Both windfalls. Which of the two loves it is. Who the person is, and which direction he is walking.”
- v2 close: “The twelfth comes first, and it is the condition of the other two.”

The v1 opening also quietly devalues nine of the twelve cards the buyer paid for. The v2 close continues to manage dependencies rather than deepen the meaning of the sitting.

The gold opens by creating shared human ground: “Or there is no spouse, or kids, or fast food. Maybe you’re just sitting in your chair at three in the morning Googling things like ‘Meaning of life,’ ‘Is God real,’ ‘Why am I so depressed,’ and ‘How to move to an island.’” Then: “I’ve experienced variations of all of these. You probably have, too.” Its conclusion begins, “I must say, I have high hopes for your restructuring!” before it recaps. Even where the gold is generic, a person is thinking with the reader. The generated joiner is routing her through an answer set.

## 2. CAUSE

### Cause of fault 1 — no instruction; starved by the word budget

The machinery contains no per-card instruction to build the gold ladder. Instead, `scripts/02-houses.json` sets `"target_words": 2600` and `"position_share": 0.7`. Twelve passages therefore divide roughly 1,820 words, or about 152 words each. `HOUSE_JS` makes that arithmetic the ask: “Then the passage. `${Math.round(j.position_words / j.total)} words, give or take.`”

The spec’s own note states the now-obsolete design decision: “This generated reading targets 2,600 for the twelve houses, which is what the letter’s promise needs and what twelve model calls can hold at quality.” The current handover says Phase 3 “is not started” and defines it as “a Waite block per card, then 500–800 words of interpretation … ~8,000 words, ~32 pages.” The pipeline is still built to the pre-Phase-3 budget.

The starvation is most extreme in v2 house 5. `obligations_v2["5"]` is 531 words and requires the love branch, timing, description, arrival/recognition distinction, another-card proof, Emperor floor/shut-room decision, its location in house 4 or 8, and the seat surrendered in house 2 or 6. It includes: “AND FROM HER SECOND LETTER — THE EMPEROR: FLOOR OR SHUT ROOM, AND THE SEAT.” The rendered prompt is 4,022 words, with a 2,408-word voice block, asking for 152 words back. There is literally no room left to teach.

### Cause of fault 2 — flatness and brevity are explicitly rewarded

`VOICE`, rule 1 says: “CLAIMS ARE STATED FLAT.” Rule 6 says: “ONE TRUE THING, NEVER THREE GUESSES.” Its rhythm block adds: “Paragraphs of one to three sentences. One idea per sentence. Nothing over 25 words.” Those rules can protect psychic confidence and clarity, but together with 152 words they produce a page of short declarations.

`HOUSE_JS` further requires an early verdict: “SHE KNOWS WHETHER THIS ROOM IS GOOD OR HARD BEFORE SHE HAS FINISHED THE PARAGRAPH.” It also requires a picture opener, a rotated move, a prescribed ending, one reader-presence move, concrete nouns/actions, timing discipline, and often cross-card proof. The prose is written around required payload and prohibitions. The gold’s analogy, digression, qualification of a general concept, and reflective transition are not banned by name; they are squeezed out by the combination of budget and mandatory jobs.

### Cause of fault 3 — `HOUSE_JS` tells every call to perform the same kind of first person

The direct cause is the fixed block: “YOU ARE IN THE ROOM. SAY SO, ONCE, IN YOUR OWN VOICE.” It then narrows presence to a small menu: “Take a side. Refuse something. Promise something. Correct her.” Most damagingly, it supplies liftable examples from the shipped product: “I mean that narrowly” · “I won’t pretend otherwise” · “I am not telling you to cut anyone off” · “I won’t let you go on to it unprepared.”

This is precisely the pipeline’s documented template failure: a worked example becomes output. The caveat “Once. Twice at the most” governs one room, not the twelve-call document. Across twelve identical calls, the model chooses the cheapest allowed proof of presence: “I won’t … dear.” Rubric line 13 can fail the finished document after the fact, but the prompt manufactures the repetition first.

### Cause of fault 4 — the picture-detail opener is mandatory three times over

`VOICE`, rule 7: “PICTURE BEFORE MEANING. The first sentence about a card names something physically drawn on it.” `HOUSE_JS` then selects one detail and says: “OPEN ON THIS DETAIL … THE FIRST SENTENCE IS ABOUT THIS ONE.” Near the output instruction it repeats: “Open on what is physically drawn on the card.”

Seeding changes which object opens the passage; it does not change the repeated rhetorical operation. Because the prose must pivot from that object to the buyer immediately, the writer turns small details into causal evidence: an unreadable embroidery becomes missing paperwork, a downward torch becomes a group spending warmth “at the floor.” The card-lore `drawn` list is an excellent accuracy fence, but the opener mandate misuses it as a style engine.

### Cause of fault 5 — the axis instruction is stronger than its restraint

Every house receives an opposite and the starred direction: “Read your room against that one … The two cards are answering it together.” A few lines later the prompt says most rooms are read alone and “You do not have to mention it if the pair genuinely says nothing,” but the positive, starred command is clearer than the opt-out. One rotated move also says: “READ IT AGAINST ANOTHER CARD.”

The result is measurable: today’s v2 reading names another card in 12 of 12 rooms. This has surpassed the test plan’s already-failed over-correction: “10 of 12 rooms were cross-referencing.” The prompt contains the right principle—cross-reference only where it changes the room—but applies an axis brief to every call, so the model finds a relation every time.

### Cause of fault 6 — `MOVES` and `ENDINGS` assign a visible device to every room

`HOUSE_JS` says: “THE MOVE THIS PASSAGE OWES HER. Do this one, here, and do it once.” It then says: “THIS IS HOW THIS ROOM ENDS. The last line or two, and no other shape.” `ENDINGS` contains seven fixed functions: instruction, cost, condition, picture, prior decision, question, or “do not end.”

Rotation prevents twelve identical endings, but it guarantees twelve deliberately shaped endings. The reader can feel the mechanism even when the surface forms differ. “END ON A QUESTION SHE CARRIES OUT OF THE ROOM” produces the orphaned questions; “END ON THE COST OF LEAVING IT” produces miniature threats. Variety of gimmick is not natural variation.

### Cause of fault 7 — invented intimacy is an explicit requirement

The `MOVES` entry called “THE RECOGNITION” says: “Name one private thing she does that she has never told anyone and has never put into words herself.” It permits universality but still requires the model to state an unknowable private act as fact. That instruction directly explains the car, the unsent drafts, the changed voice on a relative’s call, and “you decided you were flattering yourself.”

The adjacent “GIVE IT OBJECTS” move correctly asks for “CATEGORIES SHE FILLS IN,” but the reading shows how easily the stronger demand for a private behavioural hit defeats the safeguard. With no buyer facts, personalisation can only come from the draw and broadly recognisable categories; it cannot honestly come from a supposedly secret scene.

### Cause of fault 8 — the machinery pairs flat certainty with cost and suppresses local normalisation

Three fixed instructions combine here. `VOICE` requires flat claims. Its reader-presence block orders: “Take a side. Refuse something. Promise something. Correct her.” `MOVES` contains “SET THE CONDITION. What this card gives, and what it asks in exchange,” while `ENDINGS` contains “say what this room takes from her if she leaves it exactly as it stands.” Those are direct recipes for “do this or the money/man goes.”

At the same time `VOICE`, rule 10 says: “SHAME COMES OUT ONCE, AT THE TOP, AND THEN IT IS DONE” and “Do NOT answer it again in room after room.” That rule fixed a real repeated-absolution template, but it also prevents the gold-standard move of normalising a difficult human pattern at the point where it is being explained. The needed distinction is between repetitive absolution and card-specific normalisation. The current prompt bans both in practice.

### Cause of fault 9 — the joiner has a checklist too large for its budget

`JOIN_JS` requires the opening to do five things: presence plus a rotated observation, the whole-draw verdict and main rooms inside 150 words, the global time window, settlement of the letter’s debts, shame removal, and the contradiction inoculation. It explicitly says: “SAY THE SHAPE OF THE WHOLE DRAW, FLAT … and in which two or three rooms the next stretch of her life actually happens.” That is why the opening becomes a contents page.

The close is similarly over-specified: “ADD THEM UP,” return to the answers, identify their order/conditions, pay “she stops finding out last,” date the first sign, explain the one dated room, and end on an action. Its budget is calculated only from what is left under the 2,600-word target: `openW = budget * 0.45`, `closeW = budget * 0.30`. The joiner has no room to be an essayist because it is performing contract administration.

The separate `NOTICED` rotation adds another visible device to every opening. A seed changes which observation is mandated, but not the fact that the opening is built around satisfying a slot.

## 3. THE FIX PLAN

### 1 — Fund the locked product before tuning the voice

**Change →** Replace the 2,600-word regime with the locked Phase-3 budget: twelve new cards, each with the already-rendered Waite block and 500–800 words of generated interpretation. Budget the opening and close separately instead of taking whatever remains after the houses. Aim for roughly 8,000 buyer-visible words and about 32 pages. Keep Claude Opus 5 as the writer. Raise or validate node 4b and joiner ceilings only as needed; do not treat a larger ceiling as a substitute for lowering reasoning effort if thinking consumes the output.

**Expected effect →** The model has space to define, analogise, widen, normalise, and apply. It no longer has to compress a 531-word v2 obligation plus every global rule into 152 words. This is the largest single improvement and a prerequisite for judging any voice change fairly.

**How to measure →** Extend `preview-02-prompts.mjs --metrics` to report generated words per house and buyer-visible total including Waite. Require 12/12 interpretation passages between 500 and 800 words, median 600–700, and a final reading between 7,500 and 8,500 words. Render with `make-02-pdf.mjs`; target 30–34 pages with no stranded heading, broken image, or close trapped visually inside house 12.

### 2 — Make the gold ladder the per-card content architecture

**Change →** Give each house writer the card’s `waite` source and require a general-to-specific argument: select one relevant Waite proposition; explain it in plain language; develop it through an everyday analogy; widen it into several categories the buyer can fill in; normalise the difficult part without generic absolution; then apply it to this room, argue any promise verdict from the table, and end with what follows for her. Use mechanism-only instructions and the source text—no sample finished sentences. Seed which eligible Waite proposition and which analogy domain the writer receives so the same card does not always take the same route. Preserve the test write’s architecture, not its wording or borrowed Temperance content.

**Expected effect →** Claims become conclusions the reader has been led to, not psychic assertions she must accept. General examples remain concrete without pretending to know her possessions or private habits. The essayist voice gains something to think about.

**How to measure →** Add a human ladder audit to the reader-grade pass. For both candidate readings, require 12/12 rooms to show all six functional rungs and 0/12 to make a buyer-specific factual claim before the general explanation has done its work. Have the operator mark the point where each passage first turns to “you”; the target is after a recognisable definition, analogy, category widening, and normalisation—not a fixed percentage or canned transition.

### 3 — Remove fixed per-room performance requirements; rotate at document level

**Change →** Delete the rule that every house must prove reader presence once and the rule that every house must take exactly one `MOVE` and one `ENDING`. Build a seeded document-level allocation instead: a limited number of rooms receive a warning, credit, question, condition, or explicit first-person judgment; several receive none; no device is assigned as the last line. First-person presence should be a document-level range, with essayistic reflection allowed, not a required refusal/correction in every call. Remove all worked sentence examples from live prompts.

**Expected effect →** The document stops advertising its template. “I” can express thought, experience, or disagreement rather than six versions of refusal. Paragraphs end when their argument ends.

**How to measure →** Extend metrics to count first-person families and closing types. Across a reading require no more than two total uses of the `I won’t / I will not / I am not` refusal family; no identical four-word opening or closing skeleton in more than two rooms; no more than two question endings; at least three rooms with no explicit closing gesture; and roughly 6–10 meaningful first-person uses per 1,000 generated words. Rubric line 13 must pass on both distinct-order runs, followed by an operator read because n-gram cleanliness is not prose quality.

### 4 — Cut the live instruction load and make obligations structured

**Change →** Move the historical autopsies (“Measured: a real run…”) and their quoted bad examples out of `VOICE`/`HOUSE_JS` and into tests/docs. Reduce the live voice to a compact hierarchy: truth/offer invariants, tarot accuracy, prose architecture, and output contract. Represent each room’s debts as structured facts/checks instead of a 371- or 531-word rhetorical brief. Let `check-02-promises.mjs` and the grader police omissions after generation. Resolve conflicts by priority in the prompt: paid promise and card truth first, teaching ladder second, style last.

**Expected effect →** Claude spends attention on the reading rather than reconciling a 4,022-word legal brief. Fewer prompt phrases leak into buyer copy. v2 house 5 can explain its two-letter answer instead of enumerating it at speed.

**How to measure →** Use `preview-02-prompts.mjs --house 5 --arc v2 --seed <id>`. Reduce the current 4,022-word rendered prompt to at most 2,300 words for v2 house 5 and ordinary rooms to at most 1,800, excluding the supplied Waite source if counted separately. With a 600–700-word output target, keep live instruction-to-output ratio below 4:1. Run `check-02-promises.mjs`; all current promise and machinery assertions must remain green.

### 5 — Keep picture accuracy, stop forcing picture-led prose

**Change →** Keep `card_lore.drawn` as an accuracy fence and keep the static card image, plain line, and verbatim Waite block in node 10. Remove “the first sentence is about this detail” and the duplicate instruction to open on the picture. Let the interpretation begin where its argument begins; it may return to a drawn detail when that detail genuinely explains the room. If opener variation is still seeded, rotate rhetorical starting functions, not just card objects.

**Expected effect →** The twelve rooms stop sounding like inventory. Visual details cease being forced into pseudo-evidence such as “no paperwork.” Waite carries authority openly, while the generated prose can sound like an essay.

**How to measure →** In the first two readings, verify 12/12 correct Waite blocks and 0 invented card details. No more than 3/12 generated interpretations should open with object inventory, and no opening grammar should recur more than twice. Re-run the working-reader audit for card accuracy; every house must be sound or defensibly thin, with 0 invented meanings.

### 6 — Restore selective spread reading

**Change →** Continue handing every writer the whole table, but make cross-reading mandatory only in rooms 2/5/8/12 where a paid verdict needs corroboration. Elsewhere, allocate a small seeded set of genuine axes to develop and pass “no cross-reference” to the rest. A cross-reference must change the conclusion and receive explanation, not merely say another card agrees.

**Expected effect →** The reading remains a spread, not twelve shuffled meanings, without turning into a citation network. Promise answers retain visible evidence; ordinary rooms get enough space to develop their own card.

**How to measure →** Use `preview-02-prompts.mjs --metrics` on both different-order readings. Target 6–7 of 12 rooms naming another card, matching the post-audit range, never 10–12. Require all four promise rooms to show their table argument, no card used as evidence more than twice, and every non-promise cross-reference to pass a human “changes the conclusion” test.

### 7 — Rewrite the joiner contract around a human opening and thematic close

**Change →** Preserve the required facts—good/hard shape and two-month frame in the first 150 words, one-time shame removal, the contradiction inoculation, first sign, one dated room—but stop requiring a list of main rooms or debts. Give the opening enough separate budget to establish a sitting and a governing idea. In the close, require one synthesis and one useful action; move mechanical promise confirmation to the verifier rather than making the close recite it. Add an explicit close marker so node 10 can render the conclusion as its own section, fixing the known “close inside house 12” layout defect while Phase-3 pagination is already being changed.

**Expected effect →** The opening invites the buyer into a reading rather than directing her to an answer index. The close feels like the completion of an argument rather than a dependency report.

**How to measure →** Human audit both openings and closes: 0 openings enumerate three or more rooms/debts; 0 closes restate the four verdicts as a list; both still state good/hard and the global window inside 150 words, carry the inoculation, name the five-to-seven-day first sign, and explain exactly one dated room. Rendered PDF must show a separately headed close, not an apparent continuation of house 12.

### 8 — Add a prose gate that rewards teaching, not density

**Change →** Keep the promise checker and tarot reader audit, but add a blind prose review based on the gold ladder, readability, dignity, and willingness to continue reading. Remove “insight density” as a success proxy. The automated grader should reject repeated structures and missing ladder functions, but the operator’s eye remains the release gate.

**Expected effect →** A reading cannot pass merely because it is specific, forceful, and contract-complete. The evaluation becomes aligned with the buying experience.

**How to measure →** For two distinct-order runs, require: all existing deterministic promise/draw checks green; 20/20 on the existing tarot/copy rubric; both working-reader verdicts “would sign” and “books a second reading”; ladder audit 12/12; operator readability at least 8/10 for both and an explicit “send” decision. Also ask the operator to name the worst line in each; a remaining refund-trigger line blocks release even when every machine is green.

## 4. What NOT to change

1. **Do not add dates back to make the longer prose feel specific.** The test plan records six to ten dated rooms before the fix and concludes, “Twelve dates is one date’s worth of credit divided by twelve.” Keep one relative dated room—house 5—plus the close’s promised first sign inside five to seven days. Never use a calendar date. Longer interpretation should explain more, not predict in more directions.

2. **Do not turn reversals back on for variety.** Reversals are locked off, and the hand-written product used none. Preserve the reversal machinery and card-specific lore for possible future use, but do not import Waite’s archaic reversed keywords or restore the failed “turn the picture upside down” method. The recorded result was the Hanged Man reversed as “the waiting already ended” and Temperance delivering a deadline—both false to the cards.

3. **Do not undo the Majors fix.** Twelve Majors are a chosen deck, not a marvel or omen. At the same time, preserve one clause of weight in the opening: the second-pass audit says, “The Majors fix removed the meaning with the marvelling” and reduced the $35 rationale to stock-keeping. State the labour/weight once without pretending the selected deck surprised its selector.

4. **Do not remove `card_lore` or loosen picture accuracy.** It corrected roughly thirty visual details and fences card claims. The fix is to stop using the detail list as a mandatory opener, not to stop supplying it. Equally, do not make `engine` or `reversed` vivid again: the second pass found “card_lore’s own words were being printed in the product.” Keep those fields mechanical and non-liftable.

5. **Do not solve template prose with more worked examples.** The test plan measured example nouns, a quoted Majors sentence, vivid lore phrasing, and joiner-brief sentences reappearing verbatim. The mixed seed over order id + card + house must stay; the earlier arithmetic seed collided for opposite houses. New instructions should specify functions and tests, never offer a sentence the writer can paste. Always compare different `--order` ids.

6. **Do not remove the whole table or return to twelve isolated cards.** The original audit found an unsupported “Not renewal. Arrival.” because house 5 could not see the decisive card in house 7. Keep all twelve visible and keep corroboration mandatory for rooms 2/5/8/12. The correction is from today’s 8/12 and 12/12 cross-reference rates back toward the measured 6–7/12 range—not back to three, and not up to twelve.
