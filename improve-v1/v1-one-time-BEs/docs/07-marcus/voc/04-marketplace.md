# 07 VOC — marketplace: the words of people who paid

Field pull, 2026-09-05. **Source: marketplace review pages.** Every person quoted below spent
money on a reading and then wrote about it. That is the whole value of this file — these are not
people speculating about tarot, they are people describing a purchase.

⛔ **All reader, advisor and seller names are stripped** and replaced with `[the reader]`.
No review is reproduced whole. Short fragments only.

---

## ⚠ Read this before you trust a number

**Sample.** ~14 public review pages across 9 marketplaces — California Psychics, Kasamba, Keen,
Purple Garden, Psychic Source, Mysticsense, AskNow, PsychicOz, LifeReader, Oranum — plus a BBB
complaint file and Etsy/Fiverr listing copy reached through search. Roughly **200 fragments seen,
~120 kept.**

**This is a qualitative skim, not a sample.** Each page shows only the ~20 reviews the platform
chose to surface under that filter. Star-filtered pages are self-selecting by design. Nothing here
supports a percentage.

**⛔ The biggest gap: Etsy and Fiverr both block automated fetching (HTTP 403).** Those two are the
closest structural analogue to 07 — *written* readings, paid up front, delivered by message, no
live clock. So **every buyer quote below comes from a per-minute live-chat/phone marketplace**,
which is a different product with a different failure mode (the clock). Seller listing copy from
Etsy did come through, via search snippets, and is in §3. **Buyer reviews of written email
readings are missing and are the single most valuable thing still unpulled.** They would need a
browser session, not WebFetch.

**Fidelity caveat.** Pages were read through a fetch-and-extract step, so a handful of fragments
may be lightly clipped rather than character-exact. Confidence is high where the typos survived
(`didnt`, `physics`, `i`, `abd`) — those are untouched. Treat any fragment as *directionally*
verbatim; re-pull before putting one in a headline.

# 1 · What she says she asked

This is the money section. Grouped by what she actually wanted, in her words.

## 1.1 · The man — and the specific, small, embarrassed question

> ⭐ *"I asked her if I was calling too much because I didn't want to be a burden."*

That is the most useful single line in this entire pull. It is not "will he come back." It is a
woman checking whether she is too much. **Note also that she asked it more than once** — the same
reviewer, later: *"I periodically asked if I was calling too much."*

Others:

- *"I wanted to get my ex back."*
- *"what will happen between me and my unrequited love"*
- *"if someone will be back, will you hear, why questions"*
- *"what to do about a certain situation, feelings, intentions"*
- *"should you stay or go"*
- *"my POI had strong feelings for me and wanted a relationship"* — she went in to have that checked
- *"answers about the person. Their behavior"*
- *"I requested a love reading and explained what had happened."*
- *"distance between me and my poi"* and a timeline for contact
- *"situationship with a Gemini"*
- *"questions about my poi"*

⭐ **"POI" — person of interest — is the marketplace's native word for him.** It appears
unprompted across Kasamba, Keen, Purple Garden and LifeReader. It is what she calls a man who is
not yet hers and may never be. There is no better one-word evidence that the ambiguous,
undefined, unnameable relationship is the mass-market case.

## 1.2 · Timing — the question under the question

- *"when am I going to meet my Life patterner?"*
- *"who is my soulmate, when will I meet"*
- *"reveal my soul mate of what he looks like"*
- *"the date of a specific event"*
- *"predictions"* with *"specific timeframes"*

## 1.3 · Work and money

- *"do you see me getting a new job anytime soon?"*
- *"do you see me changing fields?"*
- *"After asking about a possible promotion, she replied, 'You're going to land the position and
  receive an offer letter tomorrow.'"*
- *"work, promotions, family members, exes"*
- *"work-related reading"* and understanding *"their boss's personality"*
- *"shadow work, clearing old emotional gunk, my career"*

## 1.4 · Herself — not a forecast, an audit

- *"clarity on things I've been wondering about"*
- *"talking about my life and concerns"*
- *"navigate my concerns as I continue to exist on the planet"*
- *"find my way back to myself, trust my intuition again"*
- *"connecting with my guides"*
- *"past life reading"*
- *"relationships, coworker, marriage issues, work problems, family members"*

⭐ *"navigate my concerns as I continue to exist on the planet"* is a woman describing ordinary
Tuesday dread in her own strange, exact phrasing. That register — plain, slightly bleak,
unliterary — is the register the emails are missing.

## 1.5 · The shape of the ask, not the topic

Two structural facts worth more than any single topic:

1. **She asks the same question repeatedly, of different readers, and knows she is doing it.**
   *"I asked 10 different psychics neutrally"* · *"I asked two different physics the same
   question"* · *"I have read with 4.9 and 4.8 people on the same situation"* · *"Did two readings
   with two different readers about someone."*
2. **She tests.** *"I did a code word with him so i can tell it real or not"* · *"tell me the code
   word of me and my grandad."* She arrives braced for a con.

# 2 · ⭐ What made a paid reading feel worth it — or worthless

**The section that should change the emails.** Complaint first, deliberately.

## 2.1 · Worthless — in her words

### (a) The moved timeline
The most repeated complaint in the entire pull, across every brand.

- *"they will give give you a time line and then keep changing it"*
- *"stated it would be between November 2 to November 11, which hasn't happened"*
- *"when predictions did not happen, the advisor just moved up the time frame"*
- *"change the time frame to a later date"*
- *"specific dates and times"* that *"be extended repeatedly"*
- *"3 months or 8 weeks, 2 weeks. And guess what nothing came to fusion"*
- *"she started changing her storyline and outcome of what will happen"*
- *"Half of the timelines are accurate abd some ask questions"*
- *"Years later, none of this has come to pass, and in reality, the situation has only deteriorated"*

### (b) She supplied the content and got it read back
The second-most-repeated, and the one an AI product is most at risk of.

- ⭐ *"had to tell her stuff and then she started giving me a reading from what I told her"*
- *"Instead of giving a real reading, they spent the entire time asking probing questions and
  guessing information."*
- *"she didn't appear to have any insight or information on her own."*
- *"she asked for my name, what I needed, then had me explain my entire situation."*
- *"digs for information from you and pretends to know things"*
- *"[the reader] kept asking me if I had questions"*
- readers who *"ask you too many questions up front"* — fishing, not feeling
- *"I felt like it was a bit of pulling teeth to get information"*
- *"picking on the energy I give out and never consistent with previous readings"*

### (c) It fit anyone
- ⭐ *"anyone could have said that"*
- *"all they can provide is generic answer, super vague, zero insights"*
- *"cold reading, or just generic verbatim script"*
- *"they tell you nothing specific and precise – only generic and vague content"*
- *"guidance quickly shifted to vague, generic phrases like 'be patient'"*
- *"It's so vague, her interpretation, but yet she claims she can know more"*
- recycled advice like *"you're a queen, trust the universe"*
- *"it was more of a tone deaf pep talk than anything else"*
- *"generic readings and then to top it off, they cut your time off"*
- *"The readings were almost identical"* across different readers
- *"Some of the psychics just blow hot air"*

### (d) It agreed with her — and she noticed
This is the failure she resents *most*, because it is the one she can prove.

- ⭐ *"I asked 10 different psychics neutrally… All 10 provided said that it will"*
- *"9 out of 10"* merely told her what she wanted to hear; *"only two being accurate but unwelcome"*
- *"The psychic only told me what I wanted to hear"*
- *"Almost will give you what you want to hear and then have you follow up."*
- *"they just tell you what you want to hear"*
- *"Filled my head with absolute lies"*
- *"give me false hope for 4 months"*
- *"they take advantage of desperation and broken hearts."*
- *"reading not confirming what you hoped"* — the flip side, from a reviewer who wanted the lie

### (e) It ended and she still had the question
The purest statement of failure. She paid, and the question is still in her head.

- ⭐ *"I feel more confused and have the same question in my head. Im like what did I pay 60 for 15
  min for????"*
- *"Sometimes you don't get the answers but you still pay."*
- *"you never get a clear answer is always what they think or they project there self on to you"*
- *"They left me with information that I wouldn't know what to do with"*
- *"They told me lots of things...I will never know they were true or not"*
- *"not even insightful. Not giving me much information nor advice"*
- *"Very minimal info despite the high fees. It doesn't worth it."*
- *"I wished for more than i received but it was not a bad experience"*
- *"She could only answer questions. Bummer"*

### (f) Charged for the reader's own slowness — a live-clock failure only
⚠ **07 is a written product with no clock. This whole family of complaint does not apply to us,
and that is a real competitive advantage worth naming in copy.**

- *"The advisor took 3-4 mins just to respond"*
- *"run the timer before answering the next question"*
- *"The waiting time whilst a quick response is being typed drains your minutes"*
- *"Some of the readers fill the air with dead space just to use up the mins"*
- *"designed to run up billable chat minutes"*
- *"took so long to type, answering only 1 question"*
- *"It's pricey to pay 5 dollars a minute yet in 5 minutes I had only received one question"*
- *"she cut me off when i was trying to talk, she didnt listen"*
- *"the introductory session I paid for was supposed to be 20 minutes, but she ended the call about
  10 minutes early."*
- *"Take a very long time to reply to messages while you are wasting your money"*

### (g) Suspected of being a machine
⚠ Directly relevant to 07's disclosure decision.

- *"you're increasingly chatting with an AI-generated bot"*
- *"took so much time before answering my questions...too smooth, too bold...I believe they use A.I"*
- praise, inverted: *"they have real psychics and not AI use"* — a reviewer listing "not AI" as a
  reason to be satisfied

⭐ *"too smooth, too bold"* is the tell she uses. **Polish reads as machine.** An email written to a
flawless rhythm is doing the thing that gets a reader accused of being a bot.

## 2.2 · Worth it — in her words

Kept separate, as briefed. Note how much shorter and vaguer the praise is than the complaint —
that is itself the finding. Satisfied buyers say *"spot on"*; dissatisfied buyers write essays.

### (a) She didn't have to explain
The single clearest paid-and-happy signal, and the exact inverse of failure (b).

- ⭐ *"[the reader] knows the situation and details without having to say a word"*
- ⭐ *"She knew things before i even voiced them"*
- *"picked up on things without me telling him"*
- *"details without me saying a thing"*
- *"picked up on several things that really surprised me"*
- *"timelines, names and details that nobody else would know"*
- *"She was able to give me very specific personality and character details"*

### (b) It named something she had not admitted
- ⭐ *"You've been apologizing for wanting something that's yours to want."*
- ⭐ she was asked to *"name the last moment you'd felt like yourself"*
- *"to stop chasing energy in men who breadcrumb"*
- *"I felt so seen, heard, and validated"*
- *"hold[s] up a mirror you didn't know you needed"*
- *"the part about their own feelings"* was what landed, not the part about him

⭐ These four are the highest-value fragments in the file for email copy. **Not one is a
prediction.** Every one is a sentence about *her*, delivered as a statement, that she could not
have written about herself. That is the product.

### (c) Honest and warm at the same time — not honest *instead of* warm
- ⭐ *"Bluntly honest answers. She was empathetic but genuine"*
- *"honest and real with you and also kind and understanding"*
- *"difficult truths"* delivered *"in a very reassuring and gentle way"*
- *"She knows me and cares about my concerns. She is honest"*
- *"compassionate towards my questions and reassured me"*
- *"I felt the person was genuinely trying to connect with me and be as honest as possible."*
- *"quick with answers and kind. It was like talking with an old friend"*
- *"connected with a very down-to-earth psychic"*
- *"zero empathy, zero capacity for listening"* — the complaint that proves the axis

⚠ **This confirms the finding already in `market-research.md` §2 and sharpens it.** Bluntness alone
is table stakes and is *not* what buyers praise. What they praise is the **pairing** — blunt *and*
empathetic, in the same sentence, about the same fact. Marcus's register currently buys the first
half only.

### (d) It gave her something to do
- ⭐ *"direct information that was actionable - with doable solutions"*
- *"insightful, relevant and straight to the point"*
- *"clear, direct...took her time"*
- *"sensible, realistic and relevant to the situation"*
- *"balanced and relatable to the issue"*
- *"helped guide me out of a profound mental and emotional fog"*

## 2.3 · ⭐ The three that most reliably make a paid reading worthless

Ranked by how often the complaint recurs and how directly 07 is exposed to it.

| # | The failure | Her words | 07's exposure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **She fed it the answer.** The reader asked, she explained, the "reading" was her own words returned | *"had to tell her stuff and then she started giving me a reading from what I told her"* | ⛔ **Severe.** 07 collects her question, then answers it. Every sentence traceable to her intake reads as this failure |
| **2** | **It fit anyone.** No detail that excludes another woman | *"anyone could have said that"* | ⛔ **Severe.** A daily email goes to the whole list. Generic is the default state, not a mistake |
| **3** | **The date moved.** A timeframe was given, it passed, and the next reading pushed it | *"they will give give you a time line and then keep changing it"* | ⚠ **Moderate.** A recurring daily programme is structurally a series of rolling predictions. The moved date is the exact scam signature she is scanning for |

Runner-up, close behind and cheap to fix: **it agreed with her** — *"I asked 10 different psychics
neutrally… All 10 provided said that it will."*

# 3 · What sellers promise, and what they refuse

## 3.1 · The refusal list is near-universal on written-reading listings

Four different Etsy sellers, verbatim:

- *"I DO NOT answer questions concerning: Pregnancy Legal Issues Health or Medical Time Frame
  predictions (Because of free will)"*
- *"I do not answer questions related to health, pregnancy, legal matters, or gambling, as these
  topics fall outside the scope of my services."*
- *"I do not answer health related questions or gender, pregnancy and legal questions."*
- *"I do not answer questions regarding health, pregnancy, legal outcomes, or lottery numbers."*

⭐ **Note what one of them refuses that the others don't: *"Time Frame predictions (Because of free
will)"*.** That seller has converted failure #3 into a stated principle *before* the sale. It is
the cheapest available defence against the moved-date complaint, and it costs nothing.

## 3.2 · What they promise

- *"All readings are written and sent 24 hours within booking."*
- delivered as *"a beautifully designed PDF within 24 hours via Etsy messages"*
- *"photographs of the cards pulled with clarification"*
- *"an image of the card drawn"* attached to the PDF
- *"pictures and details about what spirit is communicating through the cards"*
- Etsy's own rule forces the artefact: a reading must include *"a tangible good, such as photos of
  the tarot spread, audio/video of a reading, or text of the reading."*

⚠ **Confirms `market-research.md`: the photograph of the actual draw is not a nice-to-have, it is
the category's proof-of-work, and on Etsy it is mandatory.** 07 has spread photographs but they are
stock-per-day, not per-buyer.

## 3.3 · The disclaimers

- *"all readings are for entertainment purposes only, as per Etsy policy"*
- *"I do not offer refunds"* — the service's nature is the stated reason
- *"refunds or exchanges are not permitted"* — each reading is *"custom-made for the client"*

## 3.4 · How the live marketplaces phrase the same offer

Marketplace category copy, not a seller's:

- ⭐ *"Will he/she come back? Do they feel the same about you? Find out now!"*
- *"Guiding broken hearts to true love for 25 years"*
- *"DIRECT honest insight backed by 20+ years and 140k real readings"*
- *"Clairvoyant insights with compassion, truth, and practical wisdom"*
- *"Outcomes that happen!! Accuracy with honesty, compassion, clarity & Wisdom!"*

⚠ The countable-proof move (*"140k real readings"*) is doing the same job as swornandsealed's
five named systems: making "experienced" falsifiable. Marcus has no number.

# 5 · ⚠ Sensitive — flagged, quarantined

Not for use without a deliberate decision. Several of these are the emotional core of a real
review and are exactly the material that would make copy feel invasive.

**Bereavement / contacting the dead.** Recurs across at least four platforms.
- *"I went to physical source to try to talk to my mom to see if I could find her."*
- *"tell me the code word of me and my grandad"*
- a reader who *"kept asking for my friend who had passed's last name and DOB"* while repeating
  *"She's in a better place now"*
- *"sometimes spirit stay if they have unfinished business or family that they still need to attend to"*

**Health and capacity.** A BBB complaint describes being *"medically impaired"* during the period
charges were incurred and unable to *"exercise sound judgment"*; the platform *"refused to address
unauthorized incapacitation-period charges."* Sellers' refusal lists exclude health, medical,
pregnancy and gender-of-baby questions as a category.

**Money desperation.** *"spent thousands and none were accurate"* · *"$1,000+ weekly spending
patterns"* · *"I asked them to close the account and refund my money"* · a reviewer noting readings
cost more than *"licensed therapists"* · *"I wrote an email asking about possible lottery numbers"*
(gambling is on most sellers' refusal lists).

**Infidelity accusation as harm.** Two reviewers describe a reading accusing a partner of cheating
and the damage that followed — one *"contacted the phone company and obtained phone record which
proved he was not."* ⛔ Marcus must never volunteer an unprompted third-party betrayal claim.

**Acute distress.** A reviewer already in *"panic"* was left *"anxious"* by a reader's question; a
BBB complainant sought guidance during *"significant personal trauma"* and says she was *"led to a
worse direction."* One reviewer sought help with *"a haunting issue"*.

No self-harm content was found in this pull.

# 6 · What this changes for the daily emails

Five things the evidence above supports directly.

1. **Write the small, embarrassed question, not the epic one.** *"I asked her if I was calling too
   much because I didn't want to be a burden."* The category's own ad copy shouts *"Will he/she
   come back?"* — she pays to ask something quieter than that. Marcus's opening beat should sound
   like the second question, not the first.
2. **Borrow her vocabulary, including the ugly parts.** *POI.* *situationship.* *unrequited love.*
   *breadcrumb.* *"as I continue to exist on the planet."* None of these are literary. All are hers.
3. **Every generic sentence is an accusation.** She has a name for it — *"anyone could have said
   that"* — and she is scanning for it. The test for a line in a daily email is not *is it true* but
   *does it exclude anyone.*
4. **Blunt AND warm, in the same sentence.** The praise is unambiguous: *"Bluntly honest answers.
   She was empathetic but genuine."* Bluntness alone is what the whole crowded market already sells,
   and the reviews about it are the angry ones.
5. **Don't be too smooth.** *"too smooth, too bold...I believe they use A.I"* — polish is now a
   machine tell. And ⭐ **the no-clock advantage is unclaimed:** an entire family of complaint here is
   about being charged for a reader's silence. 07 charges for a finished thing. Nobody in this
   sample says that out loud.
