# TEMPLATE A · The long look

*The reading told in the order he found it, not the order it makes sense in.*

---

## What it is — 3 sentences max

Marcus cuts, looks at the card, and does not know yet — so the letter starts where his eye starts
and arrives where he arrived, including the ten minutes he spent on a reading he then threw out.
The tension is not *what does it say*; it is *how did he get there, and is he right*, which she can
only settle by checking the picture herself. §4 explains a finished reading; this one happens while
she watches.

---

## Whose morning it's for — and whose it isn't

**For her.** The woman everyone who loves her has told she is overthinking it. She has spent years
being handed conclusions and no working, and she trusts a man who shows the working over a man who
shows the verdict. Also for **week four**: once she has learned the retrospective shape she skims to
the bold line, and this shape has no line she can skim to, because the finding is unfinished until
she has been through the snag.

**Not for.** The morning she opens it in a bad state and needs the answer in ten seconds — this
shape makes her wait, on purpose, and some mornings that is a cost with no return. ⛔ **And not for
a cold list or a first contact.** A reader saying *I had this wrong* is spending trust, not building
it; that is the same reasoning that demoted the precedent to optional (profile §4) — trust accrues
across letters, so this shape only works on a list that already reads him.

⚠ **Two structural swaps from §4, both forced by the fiction.**

- **The open loop moves from routing (§4 beat 8) to the first look.** *"There's better news in this
  card than you'd guess"* is a promise only a man who has finished can make, so it is unavailable
  here. **The unplaceable detail replaces it** and is a stronger loop, because admitting he cannot
  place something is the one promise a man mid-reading can honestly make.
- ⛔ **Bank G is unavailable in this shape.** A precedent is a reader stopping his own live reading
  to tell a story about a stranger. Its default was already nothing (G5), so the loss is small — but
  it is a real loss of a tool and beat 9 has to carry the credibility instead.

⚠ **Production consequence: `[IMG-1]` changes.** He has not laid the cut yet — one card sits on the
cut, face up, and the rest is still a deck. So IMG-1 is **the card alone on the cloth**, and the
face-down cards appear for the first time at IMG-4, when he lays them at beat 14. That needs a new
photo brief. It is also truer to *one cut*.

### What's on it

`[IMG-2 — the RWS scan: the Six of Swords]`

A man stands at the back of a shallow boat, pushing it with a pole. Two people sit in front of him — one hooded and hunched, one small and close beside her. Grey water, a low bank a long way off.

And six swords standing straight up out of the floor of that boat.

Not laid along the bottom. Driven down through the boards, inside the hull.

I don't know yet what that's doing there. Hold on to it.

## What this does that the existing template can't

| | |
|---|---|
| **The withhold stops being a sales beat** | His hand stops because he hasn't got her question, not because there's something to sell. Same words, different cause — and cause is what she reads |
| **The correction buys what no precedent can** | Proof that costs him something, in the present tense, about the card in front of her. Bank G4 (*his own change of mind*) is the freshest proof in the profile and is capped at once in ten. This shape gets it **every morning**, for free, because the change of mind is the structure |
| **A second axis of variety** | §4's variety is one-dimensional — the card supplies the subject. Here the card supplies the subject **and the route** supplies the shape. Two cards can arrive by different snags; one card can arrive twice by different snags. That is what a thirty-morning programme is short of |
| **An honest loop** | See the swap above. §4's loop requires foreknowledge and this one requires the opposite |
| **It earns the whitespace** | The pause is real, so short standalone lines and fragments are structural here rather than decorative. §4 cannot afford them — it is dense by design |

## What it costs — be honest about the risk

- ⛔ **It costs the verdict at 6am.** §4's headline lands as a claim about her in one breath. This
  one lands as a conclusion she has not earned yet, so it is weaker on the scan and weaker in the
  inbox. That is the trade, and it is the expensive one.
- ⛔ **It brushes the profile's central rule** — *one symbol, one reading*. Two readings are alive
  for exactly one beat, beat 8, and one of them dies in it. If the obvious reading survives into
  beat 10 as *"it could be either"*, the letter has become the hedged reader the profile bans, and
  the whole template goes with it.
- **A staged mistake is worse than no mistake.** The snag must be one a competent reader would
  genuinely make. Write a wrong turn nobody would have taken and she does not read modesty, she
  reads theatre.
- **It spends words on the route.** Roughly 165 goes into beats 7–9. §4 spends that on texture,
  proof and a longer position read.
- **New photo brief** for IMG-1, and IMG-4 now has to introduce the face-down cards cold.

---

## Where it would break

⛔ **On the tenth letter, the wrong turn is a tic.** *Here's the obvious reading — no, wait* is a
form by the fourth and a mannerism by the tenth, and a reader who is wrong every single morning is
either not much of a reader or is performing, and both cost more than the beat earns. It is the
exact failure that killed five anonymous women in beat 12 (profile §4), arriving through the same
door.

The fix is not better wording. Beats 7–8 need their own bank of **snag types** — a detail that
contradicts, a figure looking the wrong way, a thing that should have happened in the picture and
hasn't — and ⭐ **at least one letter in four must arrive with no correction at all**: the obvious
reading confirmed, by a route she did not expect, with the surprise moved into beat 9.

⚠ **The second failure is quieter and worse.** The unplaceable detail in beat 6 will start being
*chosen* for its payoff instead of *noticed* — and the morning it is chosen, the letter is
retrospective again, wearing real-time clothes. The only defence is order of writing: find the
detail before you know what it means, and if you cannot, that card is not a Template A card today.
