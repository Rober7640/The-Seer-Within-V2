# TEMPLATE E · The sentence I cut on

## What it is

One line of a woman's own speech goes on the cloth, and Marcus cuts on top of it. He does not read
the line the way she meant it — he names the one small word in it she didn't mean to write, refuses
to treat it as a feeling, and then turns the card, which says what the sentence was actually
reporting. ⭐ **The card's job here is not to supply the subject. It is to overrule her filing of
it.**

## Whose morning it's for — and whose it isn't

**For the woman who has said the thing out loud once and been told she was making too much of it.**
That is the entire target of the reader prompt's *"she has been told by everyone who loves her that
she is overthinking it — she is not"*, and this is the only shape where that line is the argument
instead of a mood. It is at its best on the frames whose raw material already **is** her own
wording: **4 · am I asking for too much** · **5 · no sugar coating** · **9 · he's gone quiet** ·
**10 · where the week actually goes**.

⛔ **Not for frame 1 (just tell me what I need to know).** The whole promise there is that she told
him nothing, and opening on somebody's sentence hands the blind cut an input it must not have. ⭐ One
exception, and it's a good one: a quote that *withholds* — *"You tell me. You're the psychic."* — is
the only line that can open a blind cut, because it gives him nothing either.

⛔ **Not for frame 6 (someone near you is not what they seem).** A quoted line about a person in her
circle walks straight into the third-party guardrail before Marcus has said a word.

⚠ **Weak on frame 2 (the week ahead).** A week doesn't have a sentence. Nothing to cut on.

## The worked opening

Card: **the Eight of Pentacles.**

> *"I write long emails I will never send."*
>
> {{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }} — Marcus here.
>
> That line came in a while back and I still haven't put it down. I read it again before I cut this
> morning, which isn't my habit.
>
> Everybody who reads it goes to the same word. *Never.* They hear the sad part and stop there.
>
> The word I keep going back to is **long**.
>
> She didn't write a line she couldn't send. She wrote a long one. She did the opening, the bit in
> the middle where it finally gets said properly, the ending she'd have wanted — and then she did
> his half too, because you can't write to somebody at that length without settling how they'd take
> it. That isn't a woman who's stuck. She's rehearsing.
>
> There's a third word in that sentence I'm leaving alone until the end. It's the one that lets you
> off.

*(145 words. Stop.)*

## What this does that the existing template can't

| | §4 · the card routes the cut | ⭐ E · the sentence I cut on |
|---|---|---|
| **Where she lands on line one** | in Marcus's world — a deck, a cloth, a method | in her own world, in her own register, before he has claimed anything |
| **What supplies the subject** | the card's signal | her sentence — so the card is freed to do only the interesting half |
| **Her voice** | beat 10, mid-letter, two decorative variants after the claim | beat 4, load-bearing, before the claim. The decoration beat is deleted |
| **The recognition** | she recognises herself in the reading, at ~beat 9 | she recognises the *voice* at word one, which costs her nothing to grant |
| **The ask** | argue her into a well-formed question | she has just watched one being made out of a woman's line. *Send me yours* is the same act, not a new one |
| **A repeated card** | the same door every time — learnable, and by week four also predictable | a new sentence every morning, so the repeat is invisible where it matters |
| **"You are not overthinking it"** | a nice line | the structure. A quoted sentence is by definition a thing somebody made too much of |

## What it costs — be honest about the risk

1. ⛔ **It re-runs the precedent error, thirty times.** Five letters opening on *"years ago I read
   for a woman called…"* were boring by the third and implausible by the fifth. E opens **every**
   letter on an unnamed woman's words. The defence has to be structural, not stylistic: the quoted
   woman gets **no name, no story, no outcome**, and she is gone by beat 7. A precedent asks the
   reader to trust him because of what happened to a stranger. A quote asks for nothing — it's
   evidence on the table, and the reader is the one being read. ⚠ If a letter ever says *"she wrote
   back and told me…"*, this template has become bank G and it will die the same death.
2. ⛔ **The subject-chooser goes back to being a writer.** §2 replaced the old daily precisely
   because *"something has to supply the subject, and until now that was a writer choosing a
   wound."* E hands that back — a writer choosing a sentence. Mitigation: the sentence is drawn from
   a fixed pre-ranked bank keyed to the frame calendar, never to a writer's mood, and the **card
   still owns the finding**. But the *"free, forever, out of the deck"* property is genuinely
   weakened. That is the price.
3. ⚠ **Provenance is a small lie waiting to happen.** The best lines in the bank came off Mumsnet
   and Reddit, not off Marcus's table. *"Somebody wrote me this"* about a lifted line is a
   performance inside a letter whose only selling point is that he doesn't perform. See below.
4. ⚠ **The wrong-word beat is a skill, not a slot.** Pick the obvious emotional word and the beat
   collapses into flattery, and the letter's whole edge goes with it. There is no bank variant that
   rescues a bad find.
5. ⚠ **The supply is finite in a way a deck isn't.** 78 cards never run out. Usable sentences do,
   and the no-number / no-fact filter kills most of the 485 in `05-texture`.
6. ⚠ **The subject line risks reading as somebody else's problem.** A quote in the subject can look
   like a forwarded complaint. Opens are the exposed number.

## Where the quoted lines come from

⭐ **The rule that resolves both the honesty and the privacy problem at once: the line is written,
not lifted.** True to a real line's *behaviour*, never to its wording, and it must fail a search.
`05-texture.md`'s own instruction already says it — *"Do not paste a line into an email. Take the
behaviour and write it in Marcus's voice"* — and E only bends it by keeping the line in **her**
voice instead of his. The reason is not squeamishness: *"my twitchy texting finger has been pried
from the phone"* is googleable straight back to a named thread, and the woman who wrote it can find
herself quoted in a marketing email to 76,000 people.

**Provenance, as a rule.** How the line reached him in five words or fewer, never who she was, and
⭐ **at least one letter in five carries no provenance at all** — just the sentence, cold. Log the
provenance phrasing in `rotation.md` under the same *no variant twice inside seven letters* rule as
banks A–G, or *"somebody wrote me"* is a tic by letter ten.

## Where it would break

- ⛔ **The card agrees with her.** Then the letter has quoted a woman and told her she was right,
  which is a horoscope. **The card must reclassify the line, never confirm it.** This is E's version
  of §3's *reading two must not restate reading one*, and it is the same mechanical check: cover the
  quote, and the reading should still say something the quote didn't.
- ⛔ **Her sentence out-writes the letter.** If the best line in the email is hers, the card is
  decoration and the ask has nothing under it. Check: strip the quote — is the reading still worth
  having read?
- ⛔ **Box one fills with feelings instead of questions.** Beat 17 asks for a sentence, not a
  question. If that produces un-layable input, fulfilment breaks on `07-C5` rule 1 and it breaks
  quietly, downstream, after the money.
- ⚠ **The same behaviour twice inside seven letters.** The bank is heavy on phone-checking. Three
  phone lines in a fortnight and the list concludes Marcus knows one kind of woman.
- ⚠ **Frames 1 and 6**, per above. And any morning where the honest answer is that he cut first and
  went looking for a sentence afterwards — that letter will read backwards, and she'll feel it
  without being able to name it.
