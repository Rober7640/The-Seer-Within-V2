# TEMPLATE C · THE RULING

*The spread it lays is called **The Split**. The template is named for what Marcus does; the
container is named for what she's living in.*

---

## What it is — 3 sentences max

A cut leaves two cards showing — the underside of the half he lifts and the top of the half he sets
it back on — and most mornings they agree, so he reads one and lays Template A. This template is
the morning they don't: both faces go up, they contradict each other flat, and the letter is Marcus
working out which card is telling the truth about **her** and which is telling the truth about **the
room she's standing in**. He does not split them, he does not say both are true, and he does not
soften the one he rules against — he says what it is still owed, and that debt is the withhold.

---

## Whose morning it's for — and whose it isn't

**For her** on the morning she's being pulled two ways and both ways are defensible. She has a
reason to go and a reason to stay and neither one is stupid, which is precisely why she's been stuck
in it for months and why everybody who loves her has told her to just pick. She is not indecisive.
She was handed two true-looking things and told to choose between them, and nobody has ever offered
to check whether they were the same kind of thing.

**Not for her** on the morning she's already decided and wants it signed off. This shape adjudicates;
it cannot bless. It is also wrong for the woman who wants to know why she's like this — there's no
history in it, same as Template A.

⛔ **Both cards must pass gate 4 separately.** A narrow pair is twice as narrow as a narrow single.
If either picture only fits a woman with a specific life, the cut doesn't run.

### The container — **The Split**, five cards, two free and three paid

| # | Position | | The job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The pull toward** | free | read in the letter |
| 2 | **The pull away** | free | read in the letter |
| 3 | **What the losing card is owed** | paid | the true thing inside the one he ruled against, and what it wants from her |
| 4 | **The move that isn't a compromise** | paid | the one thing she can begin that doesn't split the difference |
| 5 | **What goes quiet** | paid | what stops arguing in her own week once she's put one of them down |

⭐ **The box sentence is structurally different from A's**, which is the point of having a second
container: **"Two things are pulling me about ___ and I can't tell which one is actually me."**
Paste-able, one split, and layable — it's about what she's doing, not what somebody will decide.

⛔ **Bank F (the signal correction) is OFF by default in this shape.** Two cards means two chances to
say "most people read this one as…", and twice in one letter is a tic on the first outing, not the
third.

## What this does that the existing template can't

**It has an event in it that isn't a lookup.** Template A's engine is card → signal → container,
which is a dictionary consulted quickly. Nothing is at stake for Marcus in it. Here he arrives at the
table with a problem, and she reads him working — which is the thing clients actually find
convincing, and it is unavailable to any one-card shape.

**It can wrong-foot her.** She knows what a contradictory draw produces, because every reader she has
ever paid has produced the same thing from it: both are true, hold the tension, trust the process.
Refusing that is a genuine surprise on beat 9, and Template A structurally cannot surprise her about
the reading — only about the card.

**The tension lives in the free half.** A's suspense is manufactured by withholding; C's is a real
open question posed on beat 7 and settled on beat 9, both free. That means the letter passes the bar
in the reader prompt more easily — strip the invitation and there is still an argument in it.

**It's a visibly different morning.** Two pictures in the preheader, before she's read a word. She
learns *"this is the one where he rules"*, and that is variety she can see rather than variety a
writer hopes she notices.

**It doesn't collapse into A's box.** A's sentence is always *"What do I do about…"*. C's names a
split. Two containers that produce the same paste-able sentence are one container with two headers.

## What it costs — be honest about the risk, including the commercial one

**It cannot be run inside the live product, and that's the real bill.** Two free cards against the
current one-free-two-paid split leaves a one-card withhold, which is not sellable. So C requires
**five cards, two free and three paid** — a second registry entry, a second fulfilment spread, and it
front-loads the widening the voice profile parks until "before it takes money at scale". In absolute
terms the withhold gets bigger, not smaller. In proportional terms she gets 40% of the cut free
instead of 33%, and that number only stays acceptable while the ruling is good enough to be worth it.

**The withhold changes kind, and the new kind is harder to want.** A withholds the move — easy,
everybody wants the move. C withholds *what the losing card is owed*, which is a subtler appetite. If
beat 9 lands as a verdict rather than a debt, she feels finished and closes the email satisfied and
free. Mitigation is structural: he must rule against a card he says out loud is still right about
something. If beat 14 can't name that, the letter is a Template A with an expensive prologue.

**~250 words of front-loaded overhead.** Two paintings and a fight before beat 9 says one thing about
her. If the ruling isn't the best sentence in the letter, the whole first third was setup for
nothing, and there is no version of this shape that's cheap.

**He sounds uncertain for a third of a letter.** A man whose entire appeal is that he doesn't hedge
spends beats 6–8 admitting a mess on his own cloth. Done badly, that's the hedged psychic the profile
bans, arriving through the front door with a good excuse.

**Reach halves twice as fast.** Both pictures have to be broad. A pair where one card only fits a
woman with a particular life fails the whole letter, and there are far more bad pairs than bad cards.

## Where it would break

**By the tenth letter, the ruling always goes the same way.** The pleasant card gets ruled "the room"
and the moving card gets ruled "her", every single time — because that ruling is emotionally
satisfying, it flatters her instinct to leave, and it sells a move. She learns the verdict from the
preheader. At that point it isn't adjudication, it's a man with an agenda dressed as a method, which
is the exact thing Marcus has contempt for in other readers. It also makes him a liar about his own
rule, because a table where one side always wins isn't a table.

⛔ **The fix has to be structural, not "remember to vary it."** Add a `winner` column to
`rotation.md` — *hope* or *movement* — and hold a hard floor: **the pleasant card wins at least two
in five.** He has to rule *for* the Ten of Cups sometimes and say the walking man is her fear
wearing boots, and the letter has to be exactly as sharp on that morning. If it can't be, the
template doesn't work and the ledger will show it by letter five instead of letter thirty.
