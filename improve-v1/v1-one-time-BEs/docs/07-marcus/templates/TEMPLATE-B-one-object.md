# TEMPLATE B · The thing nobody mentions

*Proposed 2026-09-08. Second shape for the daily letter. Governed by
`copy/07-marcus/marcus-voice-profile.md` §7 (voice), §11 (don'ts) and the fixed commercial facts —
one card free, two named and never read, no price, no dates, ~1,000 words. Only the ENGINE and the
ORDER differ from that file's §4.*

---

## What it is

Marcus says what the card means in two flat sentences, agrees with it, and then admits that this
morning he did not look at the card — he looked at one small thing painted on it, and the whole
letter is that one thing. The object is described until it is strange, then it becomes hers, and the
paid cards are the two that tell her what to do with it. Where template A reads a whole card in a
seat, B reads a bandage, a dog, four roofs, a rope with no knot in it.

---

## Whose morning it's for — and whose it isn't

**For her when the obvious reading of her own life is true and useless.** She knows the headline —
he went quiet, the marriage ended, the job is fine and she is not. Being told the headline again is
what everybody who loves her does. This shape moves her attention off the headline and onto a detail
she has walked past a hundred times, which is the only place a reading has ever done any work.

**Not for her on a morning when she does not know what is wrong.** B needs a story she is already
sure of, because the entire move is a demotion of it. A woman with no headline has nothing to
demote, and the letter will read as a man being clever about a picture.

⛔ **And not for a card without a real object.** About half the deck has one. The other half is
composition — figures, a sky, a throne — and forcing an object out of it produces the failure in the
last section of this file. Put the card back and use template A.

### The routing, in B, is by object class

⭐ **Four classes, and they land on the four containers already in the profile's §5.** This is what
keeps B learnable: she is not learning card → cut, she is learning what kind of detail opens which
cut, which is finer and holds across all 78 cards.

| The object is | Container |
|---|---|
| **Put there by somebody else** — a bandage, a carving on the bed panel, a rope wound by other hands | Past, Present, Future |
| **Still there, still near, still held out** — four roofs, a hand in a cloud, a lit window | Problem, Action, Outcome |
| **A gap** — no knot, no lock, an empty seat, a cup that isn't in the row | He's Gone Quiet |
| **Facing the wrong way** — eyes on the three cups, the dog behind his heels, feet pointed off the path | Should I Stay or Go |

⛔ Written as his practice: *"when what I notice is a thing somebody else put there, I lay it as a
past."* Never as the cards choosing.

### B's version of the two-readings rule

Reading one is beat 6 — the card, given straight and then set down. Reading two is beats 8–10 — the
object. ⛔ **If beat 8 arrives at the same sentence beat 6 already gave, there was no object and the
letter is template A with a longer description in it.** The check is mechanical: delete beat 6 and
beat 8 should lose its point.

## The worked opening

**Subject** — `{{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}, there are four roofs in this card and he can't see them`

**Preheader** — Ten staves held across his chest. Top right, past them, a village.

---

# {{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}, you've arranged the load so you can't see the end of it

{{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }} —

Marcus here.

I cut the deck before it gets light and read whatever's sitting on the cut. One cut, and that's the whole of my method. This morning it was the Ten of Wands.

I've laid this card more times than most of the deck and I know what it says. Somebody carrying more than her share, too far in to set any of it down. That reading is right. I've given it a hundred times and I'd give it again this morning to anybody who asked.

Only I didn't look at the man.

Top right of that picture, small enough to miss, there are four roofs. Pale walls, a tree beside them, the ground rising slightly toward them. It's near. Not over a horizon, and not a day's walk from where he's standing.

*(150 words — sample stops here.)*

## What this does that the existing template can't

| | |
|---|---|
| **She can check it herself** | The scan is in front of her. She looks at the corner, and the object is there. A position reading she has to take on trust; an object she verifies in two seconds — and verifying it once makes the rest of the letter true by association |
| **It buys credibility without a proof beat** | Beat 6 agrees with the popular reading before it leaves it. A man who says *"that reading is right and I've given it a hundred times"* is a man who knows the card. Bank G exists because A needs proof from somewhere; B makes it structural |
| **The box sentence gets its noun from HER** | A's conversion is near-mechanical and lands on the same *"What do I do about…"* every morning. In B she names the thing at beat 16, so the sentence is concrete by construction and different for every woman on the list |
| **A card is genuinely reusable** | A has to solve repeats by changing the finding in the same picture. B changes the OBJECT — the Ten of Wands is the roofs one morning and the staves across his eyes another, and those are two different letters, two different containers, no strain |
| **Shame comes off without addressing her** | Beat 11 lets her off by talking about a bandage. A's beat 11 has to say *you weren't stupid* to her face, which is the beat that will age fastest across thirty mornings |
| **It passes the forward test on its own** | Strip the ask and it is still a man showing her something in a famous picture she has looked at her whole life and never once seen. That is a forwardable object. A stripped A letter is a good reading with a hole in it |

## What it costs — be honest about the risk

- **One beat carries the whole letter.** 120 words of pure description at beat 7 is the biggest block in the shape and the highest-variance one. A bad object cannot be rescued later — an A letter with a dull card can still be saved by a sharp position reading, and B has nowhere to go.
- **The object has to actually be on the card.** No approximations, no half-remembered detail, no inventing a buckle. Every letter needs the real scan open while writing. She knows what the Devil looks like, and one stated detail that isn't there costs the programme more than a weak letter does.
- **The transfer is a leap and it can fail silently.** Object → her is one sentence's worth of load-bearing work at beat 10. When it doesn't hold, nothing looks broken — the letter just reads as art history, and she finishes it having learned about a picture instead of about herself.
- **Routing gets finer, which means it gets harder to learn.** A's promise is *the Hermit means this cut.* B's is *this kind of detail means this cut*, which is truer and slower to land. Mixing A and B in one week risks her seeing the same card open two different cuts and reading it as inconsistency rather than depth. If both shapes ship, B must never route a card A has already routed differently — check `daily/rotation.md` before choosing.
- **The supply is finite and front-loaded.** Maybe forty cards have a real unmentioned object, and the best six are obvious to any reader — the hand in the cloud, the rope with no knot, the dog. Spend them early and month two is scraping.
- **Bank F becomes dead weight.** The signal-correction bank is a one-line move; in B the correction has been promoted to a whole beat. Using both makes the letter say twice that most people read this card wrong.

## Where it would break

**On the tenth letter, the swerve becomes the form.** She has read nine of these. She reaches beat 6
already knowing that Marcus is about to agree with the obvious reading and then abandon it, so the
demotion stops being a discovery and becomes the bit before the good bit — and a letter she can
predict at the fourth paragraph is a form, which is the exact failure this shape was built to
escape.

⚠ **And it breaks worse in the same place, from the other side.** By ten, the good objects are gone,
so the tenth object is a buckle or the shape of a cloud, and the transfer has to reach further to
make it mean anything. Reaching is arbitrary. The tell is a transfer that would work just as well
attached to any other object on the card.

**The check, and it's mechanical.** Cover the object's name and read beat 10. If it still makes
sense, there is no object in this letter — only a reading with a picture in front of it. Put the
card back.

⭐ **The rule that follows from it:** if the object needs more than one sentence to connect to her,
it is the wrong object. And beat 6 must be varied at the sentence level like a bank entry — some
mornings he agrees with the standard reading, some mornings he says he gave it yesterday, some
mornings he gives it and says nothing about it at all. Log the beat-6 move in
`daily/rotation.md` alongside the object, or the tenth letter arrives on schedule.
