# BRIEF — one card, five templates

⭐ **The test design.** Same card, same container, five different letter shapes. The template is the
only variable, so whatever differs between the five letters is the shape and nothing else. ⛔ Do not
change the card and do not change the container to make your template more comfortable.

---

## The card on the cut — the Eight of Swords

🔴 **CORRECTED 2026-09-08, after the reading grade caught it.** The description below was
originally written from memory and **it was wrong about the most-described object on the card.** Two
graders traced imagery faults in two letters straight back to these lines. The version below has been
checked against `assets/tarot-rws/eight-of-swords.png` with the file open.

⛔ **What was wrong, so nobody reintroduces it:** the brief said *"a long red cloth is wound round her
body and arms."* **The cloth is white. The DRESS is red.** Letters A and E inherited that verbatim and
now contradict a picture the reader is looking at. B, C and D opened the scan and corrected it.

**What is actually drawn:**

A woman standing, head bowed. **Her dress is red**, full-length, to the ground. **A pale, whitish
cloth is wound round her body and upper arms**, and **a second pale cloth is tied over her eyes**.
Her feet are bare and visible below the hem.

Eight swords stand point-down in the ground around her — several to her left, several to her right,
some behind. **The front of the card is open**: they do not enclose her, and there is no sword
between her and the viewer.

She stands on wet, marshy ground. **Shallow water and puddles run across the whole foreground**, with
patches of pale bare earth between them. Behind and to her upper right the ground rises to a **rocky
crag with a small building on it, its roof picked out in red**. The sky is pale cream, not grey.

**Details that are true and that most readers walk past:**
- The swords are **in the ground**, not touching her, and they do **not** close the front.
- The pale cloth **is** touching her — it is the only thing in the picture that is. ⛔ Do not write
  "nothing in the picture touches her"; that is false, and it is the sort of line a woman with the
  card open will catch.
- The cloth is **wound**, not knotted to anything. Nothing ties her to the ground.
- Her **feet are bare**, and the water is shallow.
- The building on the crag is on **dry, high ground**, and she is below it and facing away.

⛔ **Open the scan yourself anyway.** This paragraph has been wrong once.

## The container — Problem, Action, Outcome. ⛔ Unchanged.

Three cards. One free, two paid. Count line: *Three cards. One turned over. Two still face down.*
Slug on every link: `problem-action-outcome`.

| # | Position | | The job, verbatim — this IS the reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The problem | free | What is actually in the way, named as one thing she could point at. Not the version she would give if somebody asked her, and not a feeling |
| 2 | The action | paid | The thing she can begin before this week is out, and what beginning it costs her. ⛔ Not permission, and not something she is waiting on anybody else to decide |
| 3 | The outcome | paid | What is different in her own week once she has begun it — in things she can see. ⛔ Never a date, and never another person's response |

## ⛔ Do not pick the frame

The card supplies the subject. That is the point of the structure. Whatever your template's own logic
finds in the Eight of Swords is the reading — five templates should find five different things in one
picture, and that is the result we are looking for.

## Precedent names, if your template uses one

⛔ No price. No delivery promise. No dates and no predictions about what another person will decide.
