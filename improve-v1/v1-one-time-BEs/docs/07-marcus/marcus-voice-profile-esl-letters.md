# MARCUS STONE — ESL VOICE PROFILE (backend letters, across divination methods)

How he sounds when he breaks his own pattern to write to one person — whatever object he used to
do the reading. Six sections, same shape as `07-marcus/marcus-voice-profile.md` — read 1, 2 and 3
and you can write a sibling offer's letter.

**Two layers, and they don't mix.** Everything below is either the *mechanism* (constant — Marcus's
actual voice, regardless of what he's reading) or *the words* (specific to one divination method's
own object, symbols, and reveal logic). **Twin Flame Tarot is the fully-worked example throughout**,
because it has the most proven source material. **04 Tea Reading is the second worked example**,
added in §2 to prove the mechanism actually transfers to an object that isn't a deck of cards at
all. Its raw source, `docs/04-Wiccan Watch - Tea Reading - ESL.md`, is a swipe file that gets the
*device* right (leaves forming shapes in a cup) and nearly everything else wrong — shouted claims,
manufactured busy-ness, an undifferentiated paid product. Its flaws are used below as evidence, not
its voice.

*Governs `02-E2`/`02-E3` (the free letters) and `02-P1`/`02-P3` (the paid twelve + its free gift).
Not the booking page or the booking chat — voice and commitment-ladder rules for those live in
`02-C1`. Ratified for group A (2026-08-04); siblings
03/04/05 inherit the mechanism, not the words — each must write its own precedent story and its own
device shapes; §2 now works through 04's device shape as the proof this actually holds. Where
Marcus's file and this one genuinely disagree, it's said plainly below. Neither overrides the other;
they are different formats for different lists.*

> A man who writes to everyone every evening, and just this once, writes back to one of them —
> because the deck would not let him put it away.
>
> Change the object and the sentence still has to hold: *because the leaves would not settle into
> anything ordinary.*

## 1 · THE READER

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | Prior V1 buyers only, behind a women-only gate (`S7`). Not the cold 76,000 — a narrower, already-paying segment |
| **Relationship** | She already gets Marcus's regular send (the reframe deck, 6:30pm SGT) most days. This letter's entire premise is that it is **not** that — the credibility engine is the broken pattern itself |

| **When** | ⛔ **Sends in the morning — corrected 2026-09-07.** The canonical example's own language ("I'm writing tonight," "an hour I don't send," "won't keep until morning") describes a *night* send and must not be copied into new letters; it now contradicts the actual delivery time. So: unlike Marcus, this format doesn't need to earn attention in two lines above the fold — it needs
the reveal to feel personal enough to justify a $35 ask five sections later, to a reader already
inclined to believe her.

**The first ask lands at ~62%, after the entire free read is complete.** Compare Marcus's first CTA,
which can land as early as beat 9 of 12 — this format spends nearly two-thirds of the letter on
pure content before asking for anything, because the reader is warmer but the ask (a paid twelve-card
reading) is heavier than three CTAs pointing at the same booking page.

**Role continuity is exact across a resend and must never vary:** blessing → blessing + fork →
threat, in that order, every time. v2 swaps every card (World→Star, Lovers→Emperor, Tower→Moon) but
never the *role* a position plays. The threat is always last, closest to the ask.

⛔ **The origin beat (6) depends on a fact about the sending programme** — that Marcus writes daily
to everybody but does not draw for individuals. The one-card daily tarot series this used to depend
on was retired 2026-07-26; if the sending programme changes again, re-check this beat before
anything ships. This is the single most fragile line in the whole letter, because the entire
credibility device rests on it being true.

### The shape without the cards — what has to be true regardless of the object

Strip the tarot specifics out and four questions remain, and every new method has to answer all
four before a letter can be written:

1. **The uninvited act.** Something happened that he didn't ask for, and that Marcus doesn't
   normally do for individuals — a card came away in his hand, leaves refused to settle into the
   usual shapes, a stick fell the wrong way twice. Name the object's own version of this. Never
   open on the object mid-ritual with no "this wasn't supposed to happen" beat first — that beat is
   what turns an ordinary Tuesday into a letter at all.
2. **Reveal some of it, withhold the rest — from the SAME reading.** Whatever the object is, some
   of what it showed gets interpreted now; some doesn't. The withheld part is named by the
   *question* it will answer once paid, never left as an unlabelled "more" — that's the swipe
   file's actual mistake, not a stylistic quibble (see the failure gallery in §6). The paid product
   is the rest of *this* reading, unless the offer has an explicit, deliberate escalation into a
   bigger, separate reading — the way Twin Flame Tarot escalates into "the twelve." If it does,
   that escalation has to be stated as its own deliberate design for that one offer. It never
   travels sideways into a different offer that hasn't earned it: no Major Arcana, no "houses," no
   "twelve new cards" in a tea reading, and no cup, rim, or leaves in a tarot letter.
3. **A precedent, once, distant.** One person, undated, a *change* in her, not a promised outcome
   in theirs. The name is spent the moment it's used — check the full cross-offer list in §4 before
   picking one, regardless of which method's letter you're writing.
4. **An authority beat that explains the object, not just names it.** Twin Flame Tarot justifies
   using only the Major Arcana. A different method needs its own version — why *this* object, why
   it's read *this* way — sourced from how the method actually works, never invented. See below for
   a worked example.

### Worked second example: 04 Tea Reading

The raw source (`docs/04-Wiccan Watch - Tea Reading - ESL.md`) gets the object right — tea leaves
forming shapes in a cup is a real, recognisable device — and almost everything else wrong. It
reveals all seven symbols in the free email with nothing held back, then sells an unlabelled "full
reading" with no stated difference from what she already read for free. That's not a withhold. It's
an ask with no product attached to it. Below is what actually applying this profile's rules to the
same object looks like instead.

| Beat | What it does | Tea Reading's own version |
|---|---|---|
| The uninvited act | Same job as the deck closing itself | The tea doesn't clear the way it usually does — the leaves gather into shapes instead of scattering, and Marcus notices before he's finished the cup |
| Stakes preview | Two goods, one threat, and a window | Same shape as tarot's — the object changes, the beat doesn't |
| Precedent | One name, distant, undated | A fresh name — not Ruth, Marisol, or any name already spent (§4) |
| The big idea, withheld | Name the structural gap before showing anything | A cup reads in zones by distance from the rim — near the rim is soon, near the bottom is further off, and whatever clings to the handle concerns her directly rather than someone else. **This is standard tasseography, not invented for this offer — verify against a real reference before it ships, the same way the tarot letter's symbolism was checked before writing, not assumed.** The free read stays near the rim; the bottom of the cup — the part that actually answers "how far off" and "who else is involved" — is what's withheld |
| Units — revealed | Described in words since there's no card art, meaning stated flat, one withheld detail per symbol | E.g. a shape near the rim reads as something arriving soon; withholds *what shape it takes once it lands*, answered only by reading further down toward the bottom |
| Authority flourish | Why this object, why read it this way | Why leaves and not cards: the shape a fresh pour makes never repeats, so what settled this time is his alone — not one of a fixed 78 possibilities being read differently each time |
| Close | Same discipline as tarot's | Refusal, then a promise about her life — never "you'll know everything," which is what the swipe file's undifferentiated "full reading" effectively promises even without saying so |

⛔ **Never borrow another method's product-specific language, in either direction.** Each method
designs its own withhold once, using the four questions above, and that design does not travel
sideways into a different object's letter just because both are Marcus offers.

## 3 · HOW HE SOUNDS

**Claims are stated flat. The withhold is never a hedge — it's the offer.** *Luck is going to favour
you.* *The person on your mind is going to become more attentive toward you than they have ever
been.* *It arrives as a person.* An early draft ran these through "usually / tends to / almost
never" and it read like a reader who didn't trust his own deck. But *"which of the two, I can't tell
you from a single card"* stays word for word — that withhold is the engine of the sale, not a
softener. **The rule is: hedge nothing you're claiming, withhold only what you're selling.**

**"Namaste" is load-bearing, not a tic to be trimmed.** 12 uses in v1, 10 in v2, spaced through every
section. ⚠ **This is the opposite of Marcus's file**, which bans "Namaste" outright. Neither rule is
wrong — Marcus writes to a cold, skeptical, phone-scanning reader who'd flinch at the address; here
he writes to a warm reader who already lets him in. Don't import one persona's ban into the other's
format on the assumption that "spoken and plain" means the same restrictions everywhere.

**Read the spread, not the card, and never the back catalogue.** *(operator correction, 2026-08-03)*
The World means luck-and-windfall here because of what it's sitting next to in *this* draw — not
because of what any earlier email said about the World. An early draft softened it to
"completion" for consistency with his published tarot emails; that cost the letter its best
blessing, because the craft doesn't actually work that way. This is Marcus's version of Marcus's
finding rule (§2 of his file: "the position's `job` string, answered — not the card") — same
principle, different mechanism: his is a spread contract, his is the pairing in front of him.
**The same rule holds for any object.** A shape in a teacup means what it means because of what
else is in the cup with it, not because of a fixed dictionary of tea-leaf symbols recited from
memory — a bird means one thing next to a broken heart and another thing entirely next to a
lighthouse. Read the reading, not the symbol in isolation, whatever the object is.

**No shouting.** No block capitals, no "WIN," no exclamation stacks — that register belongs to the
source material this was rewritten from, and a list that's had months of Marcus writing to it daily
would read a sudden loud letter as a different sender. The urgency lives in what he says (an
uninvited act, an off-hour, a threat named twice), never in how loud he says it. This matches
Marcus's blunt-not-loud instinct even though the two voices otherwise diverge. **04's own swipe file
makes the identical mistake independently** ("RIGHT COMMUNICATION," "HEAD OVER HEELS," "HOT and
COLD," all in block caps) — treat that as confirmation the ban is a property of Marcus's voice, not
a one-off fix for one bad tarot draft.

**Never present a guess as three guesses.** The swipe file reads a bird taking flight as "pulling
away, seeking freedom... or that he is unsure... He might even be looking at another woman" — three
unrelated explanations offered in place of one. That isn't nuance, it's a reader who doesn't know
what she's looking at. Pick the one true thing the symbol is saying in *this* reading and say it
flat, the same discipline §3 already requires of a tarot card.

**Calendar windows and flat predictions are allowed here — deliberately, and only here.** The
five-to-seven-day window appears twice in the free letter and gets a specific window again in the
paid twelve (weeks four and five). Two guardrails were explicitly *lifted* by the operator for this
format on 2026-08-03: "no calendar deadlines" and "tendencies, not promises." ⛔ **Don't assume this
generalises.** Marcus's file still bans a date on another person's decision. The difference isn't
sloppiness — it's the reader: a paid-before, self-selected buyer of a dated reading is asking for a
timetable, and a Marcus daily broadcast reader never asked for one.

**Em-dashes are his cadence, not an AI tell.** Roughly one per paragraph, measured against his live
tarot sends. Flag a paragraph only if it carries two or more.

**The threat is always a shape, never a locked fact — and that's already correct here, no fix
needed.** The Tower is *"either it's somebody from before... or... someone still very much here who
has already begun leaving."* Two named possibilities, neither asserted as certain outside the paid
reading. This is the same instinct behind Marcus's "feeling, not fact" rule for his Beat 2 — but
where his turn had to be *corrected* toward it, this threat unit was already built this way. Note
it as a pattern to protect, not a problem to fix.

⚠ **One place this format uses literal circumstance on purpose, and it's fine.** *"A Tuesday night
with her phone in her hand"* · *"deciding at two in the morning with a phone in her hand."* On a
Marcus daily broadcast to 76,000 mixed readers, a detail that specific would exclude everyone whose
situation differs. It doesn't fail here, because this list isn't a blanket broadcast — it's a
self-selected segment who already bought a reading about exactly this pattern (a twin flame, a man
she's decoding). A literal circumstance is exclusionary on a cold list and *confirming* on a
pre-qualified one. Don't port the "feeling, not fact" fix across formats without checking which kind
of list you're actually writing to.

## 4 · ROTATION AND THE BANKS

This format doesn't rotate through a bank of interchangeable lines the way Marcus's daily does —
there's no 30-day calendar to fill. What rotates is the **letter itself, across a two-part sequence
to the same woman**, and then across **sibling offers in the same corpus** (03/04/05, not yet
written). Two different scopes, two different rules.

**Within one woman's sequence (v1 → v2), must vary:**
the three cards · the headline (*"I drew three cards"* → *"I drew three MORE cards"*) · the
precedent (present in v1, dropped in v2 — proof is a first-contact device, spent once) · the origin
story (compressed to one line in v2; re-justifying an established relationship reads as a needier
sender) · the threat's register (v1's Tower comes at her from outside; v2's Moon is already in the
room — *"the threat escalates in intimacy, not volume"*, never in loudness).

**Within one woman's sequence, must never vary:**
role continuity (blessing → blessing+fork → threat, always in that order) · read-the-spread-not-the-
card · stated-flat claims with a genuine withhold · the CTA verb (permission · authorise · let me ·
draw · follow — never "buy") · "Namaste" as the address term · the deck-not-card-back-catalogue rule ·
the price and SLA staying completely absent from the letter.

**Across sibling offers (02/03/04/05), the standing rule is device variance:** any device used in
2+ offers must change in kind, not just in name. The precedent must be a genuinely different
*story* in each offer — "a woman I read years ago, same shape of draw" landing twice across two
letters the same buyer reads is the exact tell this rule exists to catch. The free-gift structure is
the same discipline: 02's is 28 nights in four 7-night turns; 03's is 9 nights; 05's is a 9-night
sealing practice built as four *parts of one night* rather than four weeks. No two siblings may share
a shape. **This now explicitly includes the object and its symbol vocabulary, not just the free-gift
shape** — cards, cup, sticks, or whatever 03/05 turn out to use are each their own closed set (§2's
four questions), and a symbol or structural device proven in one never gets reused in another just
because it worked.

⛔ **CTA ranges are reserved per send and never reused**, which is this format's version of Marcus's
`rotation.md` ledger — except here the ledger is the URL itself, not a separate file: `c=1–6` is v1,
`c=7` is v1's P.S. (now a permanent slot, not a challenger), `c=21–26` is v2, `c=27` is v2's P.S.,
`c=31–33` is reserved for abandon-nudges. A new send picks the next open range; it never borrows one
already spoken for, because `S11`/`S11`-style tracking depends on the range alone to tell sends
apart.

## 5 · THE SEND

**Merge token:** `{{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}` — Liquid, exactly as Marcus's file
specifies. The drafts still say `%FIRSTNAME%` as a placeholder; the legacy `{!firstname_fix}` token
some early copy claimed is wrong and must not ship. ⛔ **Two brace tokens live in the same file and
are not the same thing** — the Liquid name tag must reach AWeber intact, `{{BOOKING_URL}}` is a build
token that must be substituted before send. A blanket `/\{\{[^}]*\}\}/` sweep eats the name tag, the
same failure mode Marcus's file warns about independently — this deck hit it too.

⚠ **No fallback filter, and it's untested whether one would even parse.** A subscriber with no first
name on file gets a headline that opens on a bare comma and a salutation that degrades to a lone
em-dash. Putting the name tag in five body slots (not just the subject) multiplies where this shows
up. Worth a seed-list test of `| default: "Namaste"` before the next send; don't assume it works.

**Price and SLA never appear in the letter.** First mention of money is booking-page statement 6,
after five agreements — moving it earlier is not a style call, it's rebuilding the ladder from
scratch.

**Hero image ships face-down, never face-up.** The origin story says the cards came away *"together,
face-down"* — face-down IS the moment being described, and a face-up hero hands over the whole draw
before sentence one. Same principle as Marcus's `[IMG-1]` discipline, different mechanism: his is a
marker string the builder tests for, his is a literal card orientation the copy would otherwise
contradict. **The principle, not the literal orientation, is what transfers to a new method:** the
hero has to show the moment *before* meaning was assigned to it, whatever that looks like for the
object in question — a full, unread cup for a tea reading, not specifically a face-down card. Name
each `IMG-n` token for the symbol or unit it reveals, the way `{{IMG_THE_WORLD}}` is named for the
card it reveals, regardless of what the object actually is.

⛔ **The plain-text part is a separate file, not a derivation, and the generic tool corrupts it.**
AWeber stores `body_html` and `body_text` separately, and the live send path reads text from its own
file rather than generating it. The generic `aweber-broadcast.cjs` `htmlToText` used to turn every
`&#39;` into a space — *"I don t draw for people one at a time,"* 73 times in v2 — and stripped every
`<a>` tag, leaving a text-reader no way through to any CTA. Fixed, but derivation still drops links:
always generate the text part with `esl-to-text.cjs --text`, never let the broadcast tool derive it.

**Subjects are banked separately in `02-E1`**, not inline in the letter file — check there before
writing a new one rather than inventing on the spot.

## 6 · DON'TS, AND WHAT BREAKS MOST

**Never:** a price or an SLA inside the letter · a hedge word on a stated claim ("usually," "tends
to," "almost never") · the free card described as newly "falling" into a house in the paid delivery
(it isn't there — that would quietly break the "twelve **new** cards" promise) · a precedent story
reused across sibling offers, even under a different name · "buy" as a CTA verb · a threat resolved
to a named certainty outside the paid product · deriving the plain-text part from the HTML via the
generic broadcast tool · a symbol given three unrelated possible meanings instead of one flat
reading · a paid product left unlabelled after the free letter already showed everything it has ·
another offer's object-specific vocabulary (houses, horns, cup zones) borrowed into a letter it
doesn't belong to · "I'm writing tonight," "an hour I don't send," or "this won't keep until
morning" in a letter that sends in the morning.

**Avoid:** re-justifying the origin/credibility beat at full length on a resend (compress to one
line — a second letter that rebuilds everything reads as a different sender) · raising the threat's
*volume* on a resend when the brief calls for raising its *intimacy* instead · letting the manufactured-
agency device ("the cosmos has guided your choice") decay into repetition — the source's failure was
three consecutive "here's what it signifies" headers in a row.

### The editing pass, worked
**Before** *(an early pass of `02-P1`, house 4):* *"The Emperor fell here, in the house of your
home, and that tells you it's a floor and not a locked room."*

**After:** *"It is the house of your home. The card that came up in it is Temperance, and Temperance
does not keep anybody anywhere... So it is a floor, Namaste. Not a locked room."*

The verdict is identical. What changed is which card is doing the work — the free card (the Emperor)
never appears in the paid twelve at all, so it cannot "fall" anywhere. The *new* card standing in
that house is what earns the sentence. One misplaced verb quietly breaks the novelty promise the
whole product depends on.

### Before filing
Read the spread, not the card · claims flat, withhold specific · role order blessing→fork→threat,
never reordered · "Namaste" present, not trimmed · no price or SLA in the letter body · hero art
face-down · no "tonight" / "won't keep until morning" framing in a letter that sends in the morning
· CTA range not already spoken for by another send · text part generated by
`esl-to-text.cjs --text`, not derived · `node scripts/copy-check.cjs copy/02` green.

*These catch tells, not quality. There is no word count that makes a sentence believable — use §3.*
