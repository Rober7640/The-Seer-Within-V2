# Sarah, one card came up this morning and it sits on something you cut out of a message this week

*You wrote the whole thing. Then you read it back, took a piece off the end, and sent the shorter one.*

`[IMG-1 — the photograph: the cut, this morning. One card turned, six face down]`

*The cut, this morning — one turned, six not*

Sarah —

You asked somebody for something this week, and you made it smaller before it left you.

Not the whole of it. You kept the ask. You took the end off, or you put something on the front so it landed softer. *No rush.* *Only if you're free.* *Sorry, ignore me if you're busy.*

Then you sent the short one. Or you said it out loud with a laugh on the end, which does the same job.

And then you put the phone face down on the arm of the chair and went and did something else. The dishwasher. A drawer that didn't need doing.

Marcus here. I cut one deck most mornings before it's light, and I don't know whose name is going to open it when I do. No second cut. Nobody asked me for this one.

There's a reason I'm writing about this one and not some other card. The first thing I did with it was count what's in the picture, and the number turned out to be most of the reading.

Fourteen years at this, and I have a sense by now of which cards are worth writing about. This is one of them. What it points at is small enough to move.

Look at the picture before you read what I make of it. Count what's in it while you're there. I'd rather you got the number yourself.

Now, what a card like this can and can't do. I can see the size of what you've been asking for, Sarah. What I can't see from one card is what you take off it. The word, the sentence, the *only if*. Until you can name that bit, your hand goes on doing it and you do not feel it move.

Here is the part I have to get right.

What you have been asking for is small. It was small before you started trimming it. Every version he has ever heard was a cut-down of a modest thing. Nobody has had the full-size one put in front of them for years, and that includes him.

There is a piece of good news sitting in that, and I am holding it back until the end. Said now it sounds like a scolding, and it is the opposite of one.

### What you actually asked for: the Two of Cups

`[IMG-2 — the RWS scan]`

A young woman in a white gown and a young man in a yellow tunic, standing on flat ground, facing each other. Each of them holds a gold cup out towards the other. His free hand is coming forward for hers. In the air between their heads there's a tall staff with two snakes wound round it, and a winged lion's head above that. Behind them, up a green hill, a small house.

Count the cups. Two. One each.

The two cups are what you actually asked for, at the size it was before you got to work on it. Not a list of things. Two.

I don't know what yours is. Some women tell me it's a straight answer about Saturday instead of a maybe. Some tell me it's a phone call where they hear his actual voice, because it has been a month. Some tell me it's being told in advance rather than after.

Nothing in that painting says you were greedy. Cups are the feeling suit, and two is the smallest number the suit can make and still have two people in it. Nobody in the picture is kneeling. Nobody's hand is out empty. You are holding one as well, and that is the part that goes missing at four in the morning.

Then there's the lion. A winged lion's head, hanging in the air over two people making a small even swap. Big, gold, ceremonial, the whole weight of it up there. Down on the ground it is two cups and a hand coming forward.

Which is where the card stops being any use to me. I can see how big the ask is. I can't see the shape of the trim, and the trim is where all of this actually happens.

### The spread this belongs to is called Am I Asking For Too Much

Seven cards. One turned over. Six still face down.

I didn't pick seven to make a number. An ask has six things standing round it. What you take off it first, before it leaves your mouth. Who taught you it was expensive, because somebody did, and it wasn't him. What apologising first still buys you. What the small one saves him. What another year of asking small takes off you. And the ask made once, out loud, at full size. Plus the thing itself. That is your seven.

You have had one of them this morning, Sarah, and one card gives you the size and then stops.

The other six are not more description. This one told you the ask is small. The six tell you what comes off it, who taught you to take it off, and what you have been buying with the shortfall. One card has never handed me that yet.

Here they are, still face down.

**And what that has bought** · What the small ask saves him · What another year of asking small takes · The ask, unshrunk

*These 6 are still in my hand*

So [let me turn the other six for you]((booking link)?c=1&s=am-i-asking-for-too-much), and I'll tell you which word comes off first.

### Back to the card, and the thing I said I'd hold back

Nobody in that picture has stepped back.

Have another look at him. His cup is still out. His free hand is still coming forward. No arm up, no turned shoulder, nothing pulled in towards his chest. Whatever else is going on between those two, there is no refusal anywhere in the painting.

Here's what I held back. He has not said no to what you actually want. He has never had it put in front of him. He has been answering the trimmed one for years, and a trimmed ask is cheap to agree to. That is why he comes across as perfectly reasonable, and why you can't work out what you're upset about. What exactly it has spared him is the fifth card, and the fifth card is still face down.

So that is the piece I was holding back. Nobody has turned you down, because nobody has been asked the whole question yet.

If you take one thing out of this letter, take this. A yes to a small ask is not a yes to you. Nearly every woman I have read on this card had a stack of yeses behind her and nothing she wanted.

### The question you came with

Most people write to me with some version of *am I asking for too much*. I will not soften this one. No card settles that, because the question is not about size. It is about permission, and you are asking me to be the one who hands it over. I would be making that up.

Here's the one I'd rather have. **What did I take off it, and what did I think it would cost me to leave it on?**

That one can be answered. It will come out as one specific sentence, it will be slightly embarrassing to type, and that is how you'll know you have got it. It is sitting in the six I haven't turned over.

So [send me that question]((booking link)?c=2&s=am-i-asking-for-too-much), in your own words. Send me the message you actually wanted to send, if that's easier than explaining it.

So, Sarah. What did you ask for this week, before you took the end off it?

[Tell me the full-size one, and I'll lay the other six]((booking link)?c=3&s=am-i-asking-for-too-much).

Do that and the next time you want something, you'll catch yourself trimming it while your thumb is still moving, in time to leave it whole. You stop finding out years afterwards that you were being reasonable the entire way through.

— Marcus, at the table most mornings

# Sarah, a woman sent me one line about a man on her phone, and I cut on it this morning

*"I've been texting this guy but I'm not sure if he is for real"*

`[IMG-1 — the photograph: the cut, this morning. One card turned, six face down]`

*The cut, this morning — one turned, six not*

Sarah —

Marcus here.

The line up there is hers, not mine. A woman sent it to me a while back and I've kept it, spelling and all. Fourteen words. She'd been talking to him every day for months by the time she wrote it.

I cut the deck before it gets light. Whatever is sitting on top is what you get, and I don't lay it a second time to fetch something friendlier. Nobody commissioned this one.

I'll tell you why I kept her fourteen words, though. Look at what she didn't ask. She didn't ask whether he loves her. She asked whether there is a him.

Fourteen years at this and I get that question more often than anybody would guess. I want to say something about the women who bring it. They are not gullible, and it isn't a daft thing to wonder. You only arrive at that question after a long stretch of taking somebody at their word, which is the ordinary thing to do and the thing most of us were raised on.

Have a proper look at the picture before I put any words on it.

Now, here is the trouble with this card. I can see what you have of him. What I can't see from one card is how it started. Which of you moved first. How the approach actually ran, as against the way it gets told now. Until that is on the table the rest of it doesn't settle, and that is not a rule I invented. It has held in every reading I have done on a question shaped like this one.

The next bit I want to go slowly on. At six in the morning it can land like an accusation, and it is not one.

Everything you have of him came from him. Every message, every photograph, every account of where he is and why he can't be here yet — all of it arrived from the same one place. That is not a claim about the man. It is a fact about the distance, and the distance is what I want to look at with you.

There's a piece of good news sitting inside that, and I'll come back for it before the end.

### What you have of him: the Eight of Wands

`[IMG-2 — the RWS scan]`

Eight long wooden staves, up in the open sky at a slant, all eight pointing the same way. Leaves still growing on them. Underneath there's a green field, a river running across it, and a low hill a long way below with a small house on it.

And nobody. Not one person anywhere in that painting.

Look at the rest of a deck sometime. There's a figure in nearly every card. This one gives you eight things in flight and no one at either end of the throw.

So the card sits where it does for a reason. Things sent. Every one of those eight left a hand somewhere out of frame, and not one of them has come down yet.

I don't know what yours looks like. Some women tell me it is the first message of the morning and the last one at night, every day, for a year. Some tell me they've got more hours of his voice on their phone than they have of anyone they've actually lived with. Some tell me their friends said their piece months ago and they couldn't take it in.

I want to be plain about something here, because this is where people go quiet on me. There is no fool in that picture. There is nobody in it at all. Wands are the fire suit — wanting, and moving fast — and every one of those eight is moving because somebody meant it. You did not misread anything. You read a long run of a man being consistent, and consistent is the one thing every single one of us is taught to trust.

And here is the edge of what one card does. I can see what you're holding. Nothing in it shows me what any of it cost to send, and that is the whole business. From far enough away, a message costs a man about four seconds.

Six cards behind this one go looking for exactly that. One card on its own can only show you the pile.

### The spread it belongs to is called Is He Who He Says He Is

Seven cards. One turned over. Six still face down.

Seven isn't a number I picked to look thorough. You can't check him. That's the whole position you're in, so the cut has to work outward from the only material there is, which is what you already hold.

Six things sit behind the pile. How it started, and which of you moved first. The thing that has never once happened, however many times it was nearly arranged. What the distance is doing for him. What this has already taken from you, and I don't mean money. What believing him still hands you every morning. And the one thing you can ask him for that costs him nothing at all if he is real.

Plus the pile itself. That is your seven.

You have one of the seven this morning, Sarah, and one is the pile and nothing else.

The six are not a longer version of the same thing. This card told you what's in your hands. The six tell you where it came from, what the distance is buying, and what you can ask for. I have never once got any of that out of one card.

Here they are, still face down.

`[IMG-3 — the photograph, cropped to the six face-down cards]`

**Where it came from** · How it started, and who moved first · The thing that has never once happened · What the distance is doing for him
**And what's yours** · What it has already taken from you · What you'd lose if he turned out to be nothing · The one thing you can ask for

*These 6 are still in my hand*

So [let me turn the other six for you]((booking link)?c=1&s=is-he-who-he-says-he-is), and I'll start where it started — with which of you moved first.

### One more look at the card, and the good news in it

Nothing in that picture has landed.

Go and look again. Eight staves still up in the sky, all of them going, and the field underneath is empty. Not one of them is down. Nothing in that painting is finished.

There's the good news I said was coming. What is still in the air can still be answered. You are not looking at a verdict this morning. You are looking at a pile of post at one address, and nobody yet standing at the door.

And I'll say the hard part flat, because I'd rather you had it from me than worked out later that I stepped round it. I am not going to tell you what he is. Not in this letter and not in the other six. A deck on a table cannot do that, and I'd want you to walk away from any reader who tells you theirs can.

What the cards do is lay out what you have and what the distance is buying. You draw the conclusion. It has to be yours, or you won't still believe it in a fortnight.

If you take one thing out of this letter, take this. A man can be completely consistent from a place you have never been.

### The question you'd send me

The question that comes in on this card is nearly always the same one. Is he telling me the truth. I'll answer that honestly rather than gently. I can't settle it from a table in another country, and neither can anybody else who has never stood next to him.

There's a better question, and it's the one I'd want in front of me. **What have I got of him that didn't come from him?**

You'll have an answer to that inside about ten seconds. It'll be a very short list or a surprisingly long one, and either way it's the first solid ground you've had in this. And it points straight at the six I haven't turned.

So [put that to me]((booking link)?c=2&s=is-he-who-he-says-he-is), in whatever words you've got. It doesn't have to be tidy and you don't have to defend him in it.

So, Sarah. What have you actually got of him?

[Tell me what you've got, and I'll lay the other six]((booking link)?c=3&s=is-he-who-he-says-he-is).

Do that and you stop being the only one in this doing the work of believing. And whatever he turns out to be, you get your evenings back off a man who has never had to be anywhere.

— Marcus, at the table most mornings

*Tomorrow I cut before I've looked at a single name, and I'll tell you who it isn't for.*

# Sarah, I cut this before I knew a single name, and what came up is about asking

*A man on a stone seat with one hand raised. Two keys crossed on the floor by his feet. And two people on their knees in front of him, backs turned, waiting to be told.*

`[IMG-1 — the photograph: the cut, this morning. One card turned, five face down]`

*The cut, this morning — one turned, five not*

Sarah —

Marcus here.

I cut this at ten to six with the lamp on, before I had looked at a single name. That is the method, all of it. No question in front of me. Nobody's situation in my hands.

I'll tell you why I like it that way round. If you have told me nothing, I cannot hand you your own words back with a candle behind them. Fourteen years at this and that is the trick half the trade runs on.

Before I go on, who this morning is not for.

If you've kept this to yourself — if there's not one person alive who has heard you say it out loud — today's card isn't yours. Read it if you like. It won't fit, and I'd sooner say so than watch you bend it until it does.

Still here? Then you've asked somebody. And I do not think you asked once.

Sit with the picture a second before you read what I make of it.

Here's what one card, cut blind, can honestly do. It can name the question. I can see the one you have been carrying round the houses this year, and I'll name it in a minute.

Where it stops is what the cut put in front of that question. This morning the deck did not answer it. It changed the subject on you.

I want to go slowly on the next part, because at speed it sounds like a telling-off.

The answers you have been given have mostly been fine. The trouble is the question you have been handing out. You picked one you could stand to hear the answer to, and everybody has been answering that one honestly.

Nothing about that makes you a fool. It is the best news on that card, and I will show you why before I sign off.

### The card that came up: the Hierophant

`[IMG-2 — the RWS scan]`

A man in red and white robes on a grey stone seat, between two plain stone pillars. A tall three-tiered crown, one hand up with two fingers raised, a long staff with a triple cross on the end. Two keys lying crossed on the floor by his feet.

And in front of him, backs to us, two men kneeling on the step in patterned robes.

Look at the two on the floor. Neither of them is speaking. They came for the answer and they are waiting to be handed it.

Nothing about that is stupid. Going to somebody who has seen more of this than you have is the sensible move.

Here's what the painting can't show you, so I'll say it plainly rather than point at it. There are two men on that floor, not one.

A queue only forms when the answer at the front did not hold. If the first one had gone in, the second man would have got up and gone home.

So here's what I'd put on card one. Not some question you have never asked. The question you have already asked everybody.

Your sister has heard it. Whoever you ring on the drive home has heard it. Somebody who does what I do has heard it, and I would not be shocked if it were two of us.

Some women tell me they have asked ten. One told me she took the same question to two readers in the same week to see whether the answers matched. They matched. She kept neither of them.

And here's the half nobody says out loud. You got your answers. They came, they were mostly honest, and not one of them stayed the night. By Thursday you needed to hear it again from somebody new.

I'm going to be plain here, because this is the sentence people flinch at. Nothing in that card says you are weak for asking. Asking is the whole subject of the picture.

You were not fobbed off and you have not wasted the year. You took a question round to people who love you, and people who love you answer the question they were asked.

Which is where one card stops. I can see what you have been asking. What I cannot see is what came up in its place, and that's card two, face down. You didn't bring it and nobody ever does.

### The spread this belongs to is called Just Tell Me What I Need to Know

Six cards. One face up. Five still face down.

How long it has been sitting there. What not looking at it keeps comfortable, and who else that suits. What it takes off you every week you look the other way. And the one bit of it that can't wait. Plus your question. That makes your six.

You have had one of them, Sarah, and one is the question you already knew you had.

The other five are not more description of your question. They tell you what the deck put there instead, and how long it has been standing in the corner while you asked about something else.

Here are the five, still face down.

`[IMG-3 — the photograph, cropped to the five face-down cards]`

**The change of subject** · What came up instead · How long it's been sitting there
**And what it costs** · What not looking at it keeps comfortable · What it's costing while you look elsewhere · The one thing that can't wait

*These 5 are still in my hand*

So [let me turn the other five]((booking link)?c=1&s=just-tell-me-what-i-need-to-know) and tell you what the cut put in front of your question.

### Back to the two on the floor, and the good news I promised

Have another look at them. No chains. Nobody standing over them.

They walked in on their own two feet and they can stand up the same way.

And the keys are on the floor. Not round his neck, not shut in his fist. Lying at the front of the picture where anybody in the room could reach down.

Here's the good news I promised you. Nobody has been keeping anything back from you. The answers were honest, most of them were free, and every single one was an answer to the question you handed over.

If you take one thing out of this letter, take this. Being told something is not the same as being answered.

### "Just tell me. Don't make me ask."

Some version of that reaches me most weeks. A reader I know had a client put it flat: you tell me, you're the psychic. Fair enough. I've just done it, with nothing from you at all.

And you can see where that runs out, because I've been straight with you about the cut. This card landed on everybody at once this morning. ### Back to the courtyard, and the good news I owe you

Nothing in that picture is an emergency.

Have another look. No gate hanging off, nobody packing, no storm over the town. Out of every hard card in this deck the Ten of Pentacles is the only one where nothing has actually gone wrong.

The old man was never walked out of that courtyard. He stopped being spoken to, in a place where the coins kept coming and the dogs kept getting fed.

Here is the good news, and it is the one I promised you. Nothing in your house has to fall over for anything in it to change. There is no crisis coming to give you permission and there was never going to be one.

If you take one thing out of this letter, take this. Nothing being wrong is not the same as something being right.

Nearly every woman I've read on this card was waiting for something bad enough to happen that she'd be allowed to call it. Nothing bad enough happens. The days stay civil and the years go.

### The question you came with

Most women write to me with some version of should I stay or should I go.

I'll give you a straight answer instead of a kind one. No cut I lay is going to make that decision, and anyone selling you that decision is selling you a coin toss with a candle next to it.

Here's the one I'd rather have. **What would I have to hand back?**

That question has an answer and the answer is yours. It will be small and specific and a bit embarrassing, and you will know you have found it because you won't want to write it down. Write it down anyway.

So [send me that question]((booking link)?c=2&s=the-marriage-ended-years-ago-and-im-still-in-it), in your own words. It doesn't have to be tidy and it doesn't have to be fair to him.

So, Sarah. What do your days actually look like?

[Tell me that, and I'll lay the other six]((booking link)?c=3&s=the-marriage-ended-years-ago-and-im-still-in-it).

I'm not going to tell you this ends with you leaving. Fourteen years of these and the women who stayed are not the ones who got it wrong.

Here's what does change. You stop running the same audit every few months and coming back with nothing to show anybody. You stop needing him to do something bad enough to count. And you get to say one true sentence in your own kitchen on an ordinary evening, and find out what the house does with it.

— Marcus, at the table before it's light
