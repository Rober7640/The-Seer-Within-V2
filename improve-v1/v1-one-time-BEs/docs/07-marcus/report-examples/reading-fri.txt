[answer 1 · I have run my own business for eleven years. It pays the mortgage and it has never once let me take a proper holiday. My husband stopped asking how it was going about four years ago. I'm 52 and a friend has just offered me a salaried job for eight thousand less. What has this business actually cost me, and what has it actually paid me — because I genuinely can't tell any more which column is bigger.]

Marcus here. Nine cards on the table this morning, Helen. I cut once before it was properly light and I have not moved them since.

Three of them you saw in the email. The other six went face down, and those six sit underneath everything you asked me.

Before any of it, the part I can't do. I can't see your husband and I can't see your friend. No card here tells me what either of them will decide.

The eleven years I can read. They are yours, they are lying in front of me, and nothing in these six calls you a fool for spending them.

One promise before I start. There is a card in the payment column I was glad to see land where it did, and I'll name it before the end.

[the answer]

The cost column is heavier. Not by money — the money side has been paid in full, and the mortgage is the proof of it. What tips it sits on the side of the ledger nobody ever sends you an invoice for.

[4 · The second cost · Ten of Wands]

A man bent nearly double under ten long staves, gathered in both arms against his chest and tall enough to cover his face. Ahead of him, down the road, a town with its lights on.

Look at his arms. Both are under that bundle, so there is nothing left over. He can't open a gate and he can't put a hand out.

Here is the cost you would never write on a list. You have carried it so long it reads as the weather.

Some women tell me it's faster to do a thing than explain it. Either way the arms stay full, and full arms are why the asking stops.

Why it stopped in your house I can't see, and I won't guess. For eleven years the answer has come from under a bundle.

[5 · The third cost · Three of Swords]

A red heart held up in a grey sky with three swords straight through it. Rain behind it in even lines, right down to the bottom edge. No person anywhere in the picture.

I sat with this one longer than I meant to.

Nobody is holding those swords. No hand, no arm, nobody off to the side having done it. That matters, because this is the cost that landed on somebody other than you.

Eleven years of Sunday evenings with the business in them. A holiday that stayed a sentence.

Somebody else paid part of that, Helen. What it cost him I don't know and this card won't tell me. There is no name on that heart, and nothing in the picture is shouting.

You are not the villain of your own reading. You were working, and there is still no hand on any of those swords.

[6 · The second payment · Eight of Pentacles]

A young man at a bench, mallet in one hand and chisel in the other. One coin flat in front of him, half cut. Six more finished and hung on the post beside him.

Count them with me. Six done and hung, one in his hands, one at his feet. Eight, and every one made the same way at that bench.

He has not looked up at the town behind him once.

Here is your second payment and there is no money in it. Eleven years bought you a skill. You could not do it at forty-one, and nobody put you on a course to learn it.

Nobody can take it back off you either. Not a bad year, not a buyer, not a friend with an offer.

The next payment is bigger, and it is the card I promised you.

[7 · The third payment · Queen of Wands]

A woman on a stone throne with lions carved into the back. A sunflower in one hand, a staff with green shoots in the other. Down by her foot, a black cat looking straight out of the card.

She is the one I was glad to see. There is a short list of cards I am pleased to find in a payment column and she is near the top.

What you would hand back the day you stop is the deciding. Eleven years of being the one they come to when it goes wrong on a Friday.

Look at the cat, because the cat is the other half of her. It is at her feet and it is watching you, not her.

Some women tell me the business is where they keep the part of themselves nobody at home has met.

Both columns are on the table now. The next card weighs them.

[8 · The balance — which column is heavier · Justice]

A figure seated between two grey pillars in a red robe, a purple cloth hung across behind. A sword held straight up in the right hand. Brass scales out to the side in the left.

You asked for straight, so here it is straight.

The cost column is heavier, Helen. The payments are real and I have just spent two cards on how real. They still weigh less than what has gone out.

The tipping did not happen this week, and nothing here says your friend's offer caused it. That sum finished adding up a while ago.

Look at the sword. Held up, not swung. Nothing in that figure has moved.

There is no telling-off in this card and I want that said plainly. A reckoning is arithmetic.

What the scales will not do is tell you what to do about it. Weighing is the whole job, which is why there is one more card.

[9 · What leaving would cost · Ten of Pentacles]

An old man at the left edge in a cloak covered in vines and grapes, two white dogs with their heads under his hand. Behind him an archway, a man and a woman standing in it, a child holding her skirt.

He is at the edge of it. Everybody else is inside the arch.

Here is the exit price, and nobody puts it in the sum. The day you stop, the week has a hole in it the shape of eleven years.

What you would hand over is the sentence you have had ready every time anyone asked what you do.

And the arch stays up. The house does not come down, it carries on with somebody else in the middle of it.

Put the exit price on the ledger with everything else, Helen. It does not change what the scales did.

Nine cards. The whole sum they make is one line long.

[keep]

The ledger will never add up in your head, and your head is the only place you have ever kept it.

[answer 2 · Should I take the salaried job my friend has offered me, for eight thousand less?]

Same table, Helen. I haven't re-cut and I am not going to. The nine are where they fell.

Three more cards came off that same cut this morning with no positions written on them. They have positions now, and your second question is what decided them.

[the answer]

Take it. The eight thousand is the smallest number in this question, and it is the only one you have been doing arithmetic on.

[back to the table · Justice]

Justice was weighing eleven years when I first turned it over. With your friend's offer in front of it, those scales hold one figure and nothing on the other side, which is not a weighing.

[2.1 · The thing as it is · Wheel of Fortune]

A great wheel in a yellow sky with letters cut into the rim. A blue sphinx on top with a sword across its knees. A snake down the left side, a jackal-headed figure rising up the right.

Nobody in that picture is pushing the wheel. The thing is drawn mid-turn, which is the only way it has ever been drawn.

So here is the plain state of it. Eleven years of the same week ended the morning your friend said a number out loud. Whatever you do next, the version where nothing has changed is gone already.

None of that is how it looks from inside the business. It is not the four-in-the-morning version either.

Some women come to me deciding whether to change something. The wheel turned once already without your hand on it, so what is in front of you is a direction.

[2.2 · What's holding it there · Seven of Swords (reversed)]

A man walking away from a camp of striped tents with five swords bundled in his arms. Two more still standing in the ground behind him. He is up on his toes, looking back over his shoulder.

The card came off the cut upside down, so all of that arrives turned over. The swords slide out of his arms and the ground is above his head.

What has held this still is that the sum has been private. Eleven years of numbers done in your own head, and a short version said out loud.

Nobody has been given a chance to weigh eight thousand against eleven years with you. Some women tell me they'd have to explain the whole eleven years first.

The two swords he left in the ground are the part he never carried off. Turned over, they are the first thing to fall.

One thing would have to give, and it is that. The arithmetic stops being yours alone.

[2.3 · What moves it · The Chariot]

A man standing in a stone chariot under a canopy of stars. Two sphinxes out in front, one black and one white, both lying down. Look at his hands: there are no reins in them.

No reins anywhere on the card, and the sphinxes are lying down. He steers by facing a direction, and the facing is the only part he does himself.

The move is one figure, said out loud, to a person who can hear it. Your figure. The salary you would say yes to on a Tuesday without checking anything first.

And here is what it costs, because it does cost. You have never once had to say a number and let somebody else weigh it. That is the part that goes.

What your friend does with your figure is not on this table. No card here shows it, and I would not read one that claimed to.

Twelve cards, and one small thing left.

[keep]

The wheel turned the morning your friend said the number, and the only part still yours is which way you are facing.

[close]

So, the small thing, and it is small on purpose. Tonight, on the back of anything, write one figure down. The salary you would take without going near the ledger first.

Don't send it anywhere and don't tell anyone yet. Just have it in your own handwriting before you speak to your friend again. Fourteen years of doing this, and the women who know their own number before they sit down are a different woman in the room.

And take the week off, Helen. Before the decision, not after it. One week is the only test anybody has ever run on a business that says it needs you every Monday.

Marcus.
