The date isn't in the card and I'd sooner say so. What I get is a house, somebody leaving it, and a child in the yard being lovely about it. Whose house, I can't see.

[4 · What has kept it alive · The Hierophant]

A man on a stone seat in a tall three-tiered crown, a grey pillar either side. One hand is up with two fingers raised, the other holds a staff with three crossbars. Two priests kneel in front of him, two crossed keys on the floor between them.

Ceremony has kept this alive. The card puts a rite where a Tuesday ought to be. Witnesses, a lease with both names, a public version of you filed somewhere.

[5 · Who it still serves · Four of Pentacles]

A man on a stone bench with a city behind him. A gold coin lies flat on his crown, one is hugged to his chest with both arms, one under each foot. He is touching every coin he owns and can't stand without losing three.

I sat with this one longer than the other four. The true reading sounds like a telling-off if I set it down badly.

The arrangement works. It has paid you every time, on the day. You have never been the one still in the flat while somebody else went quiet.

There's a second wage and you named it yourself. The last one is still good to think about because it stopped while it was good.

Nothing in that picture is greed. A man holds all four coins in all four limbs when nobody has shown him letting go gives anything back. You're not cold and you're not a coward. You are well defended, and the defence has been earning since the courtyard.

What it spares you is being watched up close and found ordinary.

[6 · How you'd know it when it surfaces · Knight of Swords]

A knight at full gallop on a white horse, sword straight up above his head. His cloak streams out flat behind him, the horse's mane blown back. In the distance the trees are bent one way and the clouds torn the same way.

Here's your tell, and the tell is speed.

The current shows up as certainty. Fully assembled, in a hurry, usually inside a fortnight of the first talk about a lease. Every item in the case is true, which is why it never feels like a pattern.

Look at the picture again. Nothing in it is riding into the wind.

Some women tell me they hear it in their own sentences first, short and quick and already decided. Some tell me they catch themselves relieved, and the relief gives it away.

So the thing to catch is the fast good feeling, not the June afterwards.

[7 · What breaks the current · The Star]

A woman kneeling at the edge of a pool, one knee on the ground and one bare foot on the water. She pours from two jugs, one back into the pool and one onto the grass, where it runs off in five small streams.

The Star, then, and it's the only one of the five that hands you a method.

She pours a small amount continuously and never sets either jug down. What breaks the current has that shape. Dull true things about how you live, said early, at month two, while nothing is riding on them.

The cliff you keep arriving at is built out of a gap. Three months of the tidy version, and then moving in hands over the rest of you in one weekend. Close the gap a jug at a time and no cliff gets built.

[close]

Five cards, and four of them are about distance. Only the woman at the pool has her hands busy.

Here's the smallest move I'd give you. This week, tell one person one dull true thing about how you live. Not a confession, and not to somebody with a stake in you.

You're practising the sentence, not the man. That month only has power while every true thing about you is stacked up behind it.

I can't tell you what the last one would say now. No card here has met him, and I don't guess at men. What the five do say is that the current doesn't need him in order to stop.
