[3 · Behind door one — what it costs · Four of Swords]

A stone knight lying flat on his own tomb, hands pressed together. Three swords hang on the wall above him. A fourth is cut into the side of the tomb underneath him. Up in the corner a coloured window, daylight coming through, his eyes shut.

Door one costs you the deciding. Not the love and not the good Sundays. Those stay put.

Look at where the swords are. The only one he could get a hand on is the one he's lying on.

The bill never gets presented, Sarah. Six years of waiting on somebody else's yes is six years of not making a call, and the knack goes soft. Door one does pay, though.

[4 · Behind door one — what it gives · Six of Cups]

Two children in a walled yard. The bigger one, hooded, holds out a cup with a white flower growing out of the top, and five more cups stand round them on the stone, all flowering. A man in uniform walks off up the path.

What staying gives you is being known. Six years of shorthand, and nobody asking you to start the story at the beginning.

Some women tell me they'd give up the wedding before they'd give up that.

Everything in that yard has already been picked. Door one pays you in the six years you have, and it pays in the past tense. The third card is the one I sat with.

[5 · Behind door one — who she becomes · Nine of Swords]

A woman sat up in bed with her face in both hands. Nine swords on the wall behind her, stacked flat one above the other, and the wall itself is black. The quilt over her legs is stitched with roses.

I made a second coffee before I wrote this one. I don't enjoy handing a woman this card in a position marked who you become.

Here's why I did anyway. Not one of those swords is in her. They're hung up behind her head where she can't see them.

Now look at that wall. Every other card here has light getting in. This one has nothing for it to come through.

Door one further along is that room. Awake before the alarm, the sum laid out again, fine by nine, roses still on the quilt. The life stays nice, and that's what makes this card hard rather than sad. Door two now, bill first.

[6 · Behind door two — what it costs · Five of Pentacles]

Snow coming down. Two people passing a wall: a barefoot woman with a shawl worn through, and a man on crutches with a bell round his neck. Above them a window of coloured glass, five gold coins in it, the light on inside. Neither looks up.

Here's what this card won't give me. It shows the cold and keeps the length of the winter to itself. I won't invent a number the picture is holding back.

What it does give me is the bell. He has to announce himself everywhere he goes.

That's the cost of door two. Not the money, Sarah. The saying of it out loud in a queue, months after you're done, to somebody who only meant to be kind.

[7 · Behind door two — what it gives · Ace of Wands]

A hand comes out of a cloud holding a length of rough wood with green leaves still growing off it. Eight have shaken loose and are falling. Far below, a river, some trees, a castle on a hill a long way off.

One live thing, held out, once, and nobody holding the other end.

What leaving hands you is wanting something on your own account again. One woman described hers as an afternoon she hadn't cleared with anybody. Another said it was a coat. Green, ridiculous, bought on a Wednesday.

One green stick, then. The last card is who's holding it a year on.

[8 · Behind door two — who she becomes · The Star]

A woman kneeling at the edge of a pool with nothing on. One knee on the ground, one foot resting on the water. She pours from two jugs, one back into the pool, one onto the grass. One big star above her, seven small.

There's the thing I asked you to watch for. The knight gets his light through glass with his eyes shut. The woman in the snow gets hers off a window she is walking past. This one is under the whole sky.

Both jugs are tipped and she's the one pouring. Behind door two you become the woman who moves first.

One foot rests on the water. She hasn't tested it and she isn't going through it, and fourteen years in I still find that the kindest strange thing in the deck.

Six years of energy has gone into holding still. Behind that door it goes into the jugs.
