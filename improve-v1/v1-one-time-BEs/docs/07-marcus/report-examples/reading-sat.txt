Marcus here. Five cards, cut before the light came. I haven't moved one since.

You told me you weren't asking me to read his mind. I'd have drawn that line myself. I have never met the man, he isn't at this table, and not one of these five has his name on it.

What I can read is the shape the thing has taken. Fourteen months leaves a shape, the way a chair keeps the print of whoever sits in it. So when a card says what he thinks he's doing, I'm telling you what is painted on it. The last card is where that gets hardest to hold.

You stopped asking. Nothing here says you were slow, or that you should have known by now. You were handed something warm and you did what warm people do with it.

[the answer]

He is keeping it unnamed, and these five say the keeping is the whole of what he's doing. Not one of them shows a man on his way to a decision. This is the arrangement, not the run-up to one.

[3 · What he thinks he's doing — his story about it · The Hermit (reversed)]

An old man in a grey hood on a ridge of snow, head down. A lantern in one hand, a six-pointed star burning in the glass. It came up reversed, so the lantern hangs below him and the light falls into the snow at his boots.

I turned this one twice to be sure which way up it had landed.

The account runs like this, Chloe. Taking his time. Being careful with something good. Nothing in the picture calls that a lie, and I won't call it one.

What the card does say is that the looking stopped. Upright, the lamp goes out ahead of him and he searches by it. Turned over, he's on the same hill and the light reaches no road. The word for it never changed.

[4 · What he's protecting · The Emperor]

A bearded man square on a grey stone throne, four rams' heads carved into it. Red robe, armour at the shins, a gold crown. An ankh sceptre in one hand, a gold orb in the other. Behind him, bare orange mountains and nothing green anywhere.

The throne is the heaviest object in the deck. Stone, carved, sat in. Whatever this seat protects gets protected by nobody standing up.

What the card guards is the right to say what a thing is called. One person does that now, in rooms full of people, and nobody objects. A name needs two to agree to it, and after that the terms belong to both. Look at those mountains again. Nothing grows on them, and the seat costs him nothing.

[5 · What he'd have to admit · Two of Swords]

A woman on a stone bench, a white cloth tied over her eyes. Two long swords crossed above her shoulders, one in each hand, arms folded across her chest. Behind her, still water and a thin crescent moon.

Both swords weigh the same, and she is holding them up herself. Try it with two brooms and see how long you last.

What goes unadmitted in this seat is the effort. Not a feeling. Holding two answers exactly level is daily work.

Two things are up there and both have names. The cloth keeps them the same weight, because the moment one is looked at properly it gets heavier.

Some women tell me they'd take the cold answer over one more warm morning of nothing. I won't guess which he'd take. A blindfold is a thing somebody ties on.

[6 · What he's waiting on from you · Eight of Cups]

A figure in a red cloak and red boots, seen from behind, walking away over rocky ground. Eight gold cups left standing on the flat behind him, five along the bottom and three on top, with a gap in the upper row.

Careful here, because this seat has your name in it and I'm still not reading him. What I can read is what the arrangement needs to keep running. It runs on nobody asking.

[7 · What he does if nothing changes · The Hanged Man]

A man hanging head-down by one ankle from a beam of living wood, new leaves sprouting off it. Hands behind his back, one leg crossed behind the other. A ring of yellow light round his head, and his face is calm.

I said the last card is where the line gets hardest. I can't tell you what he does. No card on this table forecasts a man. What this seat asks is what a year of the same looks like, and the deck answered that.

A year of it looks like this picture, held. Nothing in the frame moves.

The wood he's tied to is alive. The growing and the hanging share a painting and never touch.

Nothing on this table needs him to be unkind for it all to stay exactly where it is.

[close]

The smallest real move here is yours and it has nothing to do with him. Tonight, write one line: what you'd call him if a stranger asked you at a party. Not what you hope to call him by Christmas. What is true today.

You have one thing he hasn't touched in fourteen months, Chloe. You can say what this is without needing anybody's permission for it. The Emperor's throne is carved out of stone. Nobody ever said the chair opposite it was.
