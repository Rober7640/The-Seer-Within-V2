## The chain

**What we found.** Every daily declares `Shape | 02-E3 (v2)` in its front matter, and the claim is
almost entirely true. Beat for beat they run 02-E3's skeleton: 2, 3, 4, 5/6, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13,
15, 16, 17. Two beats are missing across all seven, and **both are the beats where 02 does its
selling**:

- ⛔ **Beat 14 — the authority flourish — is absent from all seven.** 02-E3's is *"Why I'm using the
  Major Arcana for yours"*: craft knowledge plus a scarcity of access. No daily has an equivalent.
- ⚠ **Beat 16 — the transformation promise — shrank from a paragraph to a clause**, and the clause
  is the same clause seven times. All seven read *"Do that and you stop [misreading the card]."*

Big idea and problem statement survived better than the concern implies: **six of seven carry a real
big idea**, five carry a problem statement in her own words. What did not survive is the promise, the
close, and any argument that runs *between* the letters.

**What it means.** The operator's instinct is right but the diagnosis is one layer down. These are
not atmospheric letters with nothing in them — each one has a thesis. What they lack is a **close**:
nothing in any of the seven says why her life is different afterwards, and nothing says why this
morning rather than tomorrow. Worse, every letter ends by advertising a free spread tomorrow, so the
last argument she reads is an argument for waiting. And the one device the spec designed to turn the
week into a single argument — *"Six days, six things I couldn't tell you"* — was specified and never
built.

**What we do.** Five fixes, ranked. The top three are edits to two beats and one line, applied
across all seven; none of them touches the free read, which is the best work in the set. Nothing
needs rewriting from scratch. Wednesday and Thursday need one beat restored, not a rewrite.

**Promise:** *"Do that and you will find out which armful you can set down without anything falling
over."* That is an output of the reading. It is not a change in her life.
**Today:** nothing.

### Tuesday — The Two Doors
**Big idea:** you already chose, in small amounts, and this is the first morning anything said so.
*"You have already chosen. Not this morning, and not on purpose. You've been choosing in very small

**Problem:** *"Most people write to me with some version of will he change."*

### Wednesday — The Small Instruction
**Big idea:** ✅ *"An offer you never turned your head to see doesn't get refused. It stops being

**Promise:** ⛔ *"Do that and you stop being the man under the tree."* That restates the card. It is

**Today:** the practice — *"Name the fourth cup… Say it to yourself in the car or over the sink"* —

⭐ Wednesday is the clearest instance of the operator's concern. It is beautifully written and it

### Thursday — The Undertow

### Saturday — The Other Chair

## 3 · Is there a theme, or seven unrelated letters?

**There is a spine. It was never built, and it was specified.**

Read the seven beat-9 big ideas in order and the same argument is underneath six of them:

| Day | The claim underneath |
|---|---|
| Mon | *"you have done it for long enough that it stopped feeling like a decision"* |
| Tue | *"You have already chosen. Not this morning, and not on purpose"* |
| Wed | *"Folded arms are a decision. Sitting there is something he's doing"* |
| Thu | *"He picked a direction to look in and he's held it long enough that the two upright cups have stopped counting as cups"* |
| Fri | *"Nobody stays in a losing arrangement out of stupidity"* |
| Sat | *"An offer is not a decision"* |

That is one thesis: **you are making a decision every morning that you have stopped counting as a
decision.** It is a good one, it is Marcus's, and it is nowhere stated. No letter points at another.
Sunday does not collect them.

⛔ **The device to collect them exists on paper.** The design spec, §2, *Sunday's closing device: the
week, collected*:

> *"Mon–Sat each end on something the card **could not** tell him from a mass email. Sunday collects
> all six and shows they have the same missing piece: 'Six days, six things I couldn't tell you.
> Every one of them was missing the same thing.'"* — and the spec's own verdict on it: **"No other
> offer in the deck can do this… it is the strongest reason 07 should exist as a daily rather than a
> weekly."**

Shipped Sunday contains none of it. The only cross-references in the whole week are two asides about
*his* cut — Thursday's *"twice this week, and I'll admit I noticed"* and Friday's *"the deepest the
deck has opened all week"*. Both are about Marcus's mornings. Neither is about her.

**Is a spine the right answer for a recurring programme?** Yes, and the arithmetic makes it
compulsory. At ~25% opens on 76,718, most of the list sees a fraction of the sends, so seven strong
standalone letters is the only safe *floor* — a spine that only pays off on Sunday would be invisible
to most readers. But a floor is what the seven currently are, and a floor alone means the list learns
nothing from reading two. **The version that works is both**: each letter self-contained, and each
one naming the same thesis in that day's card's own words, so a reader who opens three of them
recognises it the third time without ever having needed the first two.

## 5 · The fix, ranked

⛔ Every proposed line below has been checked against the hard rules: no price, no delivery promise,
Marcus reads the cards not the man, no *"dear"*, no balanced-clause pairs, one announced aphorism per
letter (already spent), 1–3 sentence paragraphs. `scripts/copy-check.cjs` currently passes clean on
all seven dailies — its five open findings are on booking-page files, not on these.

### Fix 1 — rewrite beat 16 in all seven. The promise is the whole gap.

**The fault, quantified.** Seven letters, one grammatical frame: `Do that and you stop [X]`. Every
one of them promises a better reading. None promises a different life. 02's beat 16 is four
sentences and a refusal.

Give Marcus one floor promise for the programme, said each day in that day's card's words. It should
be the spine from §3 stated as an outcome: **you stop living inside a decision you don't remember
making.**

> **Monday, now:**
> *"Do that and you will find out which armful you can set down without anything falling over."*
>
> **Monday, proposed:**
> *"Do that and you find out which armful you can set down without anything falling over.*
>
> *And then the thing I actually care about happens. You stop picking things up at six in the
> morning without noticing you've done it. Whatever you carry after that, you'll have agreed to
> it."*

> **Saturday, now:**
> *"Do that and you stop reading a held-out cup as a given one, which is the most expensive
> misreading in this whole deck."*
>
> **Saturday, proposed:**
> *"Do that and you stop reading a held-out cup as a given one.*
>
> *I'm not going to tell you the spread makes this easy. It doesn't, and you'd be right not to
> believe me. What it does is tell you what you're waiting for, and how long you've already agreed
> to wait. That's yours to do something with, and at the moment you haven't got it."*

⚠ The refusal in Saturday's version is 02's own move — *"I'm not going to promise you the twelve
will make your life easy"* — and it is what makes the promise land. Use it on two or three days a
week, not all seven.

⛔ **Both proposed closes were written against the rules, not just the ear.** No price, no delivery
promise, no outcome claim about her or anybody else, no time-bound prediction, and no second maxim —
each letter's one announced aphorism is already spent at beat 12, so neither close is allowed to read
as one. That constraint is why they end on her agency rather than on a result.

### Fix 2 — restore beat 14. It is the missing beat and the missing urgency in one move.

Beat 14's job per [`00e §1`](../00e-FRAMEWORK-BEs.md): *"Justify the price. One piece of genuine
craft knowledge + one scarcity of access."* 07 cannot name a price, so it does craft plus scarcity —
and the scarcity already exists in the architecture and is asserted on the booking page.

⛔ **The booking page currently claims the letter said something it never said.**
`07-C1-the-booking-page.md` statement 4 has her tick *"I understand this cut was made once, this
morning, and won't be made again for me"*, and the note under it reads *"One cut a day is how Marcus
works **and the letter already said so**."* It does not. Not one of the seven says the cut is not
repeated for her.

The system supports the claim. Spec §1a: *"The paid cards are drawn at the same time as the free
ones and stored with them. Not drawn at fulfilment. Marcus cut the deck once, before it was light —
if the other six are dealt two days later when she pays, that sentence was untrue."*

⚠ **Do not write it as an expiry.** The CTA carries `&d=`, so she can open Tuesday's email on Friday
and still buy Tuesday's spread. The honest scarcity is not that today's cut runs out — it is that
tomorrow's is not a second attempt at today's question.

> **Proposed, new section between beat 13 and beat 15. Monday's wording:**
>
> ### One cut a morning
>
> *I cut once, before it's light, and whatever is sitting on the cut is what I read. That's the
> whole of the method and it's why I can do this every morning and still mean it.*
>
> *It also means these six are these six. I laid them before I knew who was reading, I don't lay
> them again, and the five I haven't turned are already sitting there in an order I didn't choose.*
>
> *Tomorrow I cut again and it'll give me something else. It won't give me a better answer to what
> you're carrying — it'll give me a different question. This is the morning that one's on the
> table."*

That last paragraph is the anti-wait argument, it is true, and it costs nothing on the compliance
side. Write it fresh per day off that day's cards, per the spec's never-boilerplate rule.

### Fix 3 — change what the sign-off teaser does. Today it argues for waiting.

Every letter's second-to-last line advertises tomorrow's free spread, placed **after** the final CTA:

| Day | The line |
|---|---|
| Sat | *"Tomorrow, the Zodiac Spread, twelve houses, and **the longest one I lay**."* |
| Fri | *"Tomorrow, the Other Chair, and two cards about somebody who isn't you."* |
| Mon | *"Tomorrow, the Two Doors, if the cut gives me two."* |

Saturday's is the worst: the last argument before the P.S. is *tomorrow's is bigger*. On a programme
where the ask is $35 today, that is a free reason to close the email.

Keep the teaser — it is a real retention device and it is why she opens tomorrow. Change what it
says about *this* morning.

> **Saturday, now:**
> *"Tomorrow, the Zodiac Spread, twelve houses, and the longest one I lay."*
>
> **Saturday, proposed:**
> *"Tomorrow, the Zodiac Spread. Twelve houses, a different cut, and a different question — it
> won't tell you what the arm is doing. That's this morning's."*

### Fix 4 — restore beat 13 to Wednesday and Thursday. One beat, not a rewrite.

Both replaced *the question you came with* with *one thing to do today*. The practice is good and
should stay — it is the forwardable content and Wednesday is the day designed to carry it. But
dropping beat 13 is what took the problem statement out of both letters, and beat 13 is where 07's
strongest device lives ([`07-P2`](../../copy/07-marcus/07-P2-the-device-set.md) device 3, *the
question reframe*: *"the strongest device in the set, because it is what 07 sells"*).

Run both. The practice is beat 13a; the reframe is 13b and carries CTA 2.

> **Wednesday, proposed insert before the practice:**
>
> ### The question you came with
>
> *Most people write to me with some version of should I say yes. I'll give you a straight answer
> instead of a kind one. A spread can't tell you that, because it's a decision, and I'm not going to
> take one of those off you.*
>
> *Here's the one I'd rather have. **What did it cost me last time I took something like this?***
>
> *That one has an answer, and it's the reason your arms are folded. It's sitting in the five I
> haven't turned over."*

Thursday's equivalent is already half-written inside its own beat 10 — *"Some women tell me it's
grief. Some tell me it's just how they are now. Some tell me it's nothing, and then tell me about it
for twenty minutes"* — which is the mirror stack with no claim after it. The claim it wants is the
one the letter already proves: *what you've been calling it is the top card, and the top card isn't
what's moving it.*

### Fix 5 — build Sunday's collection device, and give Sunday back its peak.

The spec's *"Six days, six things I couldn't tell you"* is the strongest unused asset in 07, and
Sunday is the only place it can live. It also fixes Sunday's missing big idea by construction: the
collected argument *is* the idea.

> **Proposed, Sunday, sitting where beat 8's precedent would go:**
>
> ### Six mornings, and the same missing piece
>
> *Six letters this week and every one of them stopped in the same place.*
>
> *Monday I could see what was in your arms and not who handed you each piece. Tuesday I could see
> both doors and not what was behind either. Thursday I could see the current and not when it
> started. Every morning the card told me the shape of the thing and then went quiet at exactly the
> point your name would go.*
>
> *That isn't six coincidences. It's what a cut made for a whole list can do and where it stops.
> Twelve houses go further than any of them, and they still stop in the same place."*

✅ **The raw material already exists and needs no new writing.** All seven letters state their own
withhold explicitly — *"Which is the thing this card won't tell me"* (Mon) · *"Here's what the Devil
keeps back from me"* (Tue) · *"It won't tell me why your arms are folded"* (Wed) · *"They don't show
me when it started"* (Thu) · *"What this card won't tell me is which year the grip started"* (Fri) ·
*"whether the arm ever comes down"* (Sat). Sunday only has to quote them back.

⚠ **One dependency:** it only works if Sunday reads as the heaviest of the week, and right now
Tuesday is 175 words longer. That is a length rebalance, not new writing.

## What is NOT wrong, and should not be touched

Ranked by how much damage a well-meaning edit would do.

1. **The free read, beat 10.** Picture before meaning, every time, in all seven. *"A man bent nearly
   double. Ten long staves gathered up in both arms… Down the road behind him there is a small town,
   and the lights are on in it."* This is the best writing in the deck and it is the reason the
   letters pass the strip-the-CTA test.
2. **The withhold.** The limit of his knowledge is named before the claim, every card, every day.
   It is the pitch and the honesty in one sentence, which is why it does not read as a pitch.
3. **The re-read, beat 12.** A promise opened at beat 9 and closed by name at beat 12, 7/7.
4. **Shame removal.** *"Nothing in that card says you were foolish"* · *"You have not been stupid"*.
   ⭐ This is the conversion mechanism, not warmth — the funnel only earns when she types her
   question, and shame is what stops her typing.
5. **The guardrail on Saturday and Sunday.** *"I read the cards, not the man"*, fired three times on
   Saturday. Correct and load-bearing.
