I sat with a woman once — I'll keep her nameless — who could tell me the exact moment she recognized him. Not fell for him. Recognized him. Five minutes in, across a loud room, her whole body went quiet, the way it does when something in you says: oh. There you are.

She spent the next two years explaining it away. Too soon. Too strange. Too much (there's that word again). Here's what seventeen years of this work has taught me, Sarah: recognition lands in the body long before the mind agrees to it. It's rarely wrong about the fact of a connection — only about what to do with it.

If you've felt that quiet certainty about someone and then argued yourself out of it, that's worth feeling into. Not so I can tell you it's destiny — I won't — but so you stop calling real recognition "imagination."

 
 
 
If you recognized someone too fast and it scared you, bring it to me. I'll feel what the cord actually is — recognition, longing, or a lesson — and tell you straight.
