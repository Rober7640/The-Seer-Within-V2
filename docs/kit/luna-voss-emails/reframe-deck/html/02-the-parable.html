It shouldn't have worked. It did.

 

I read for two people once, a few months apart, who both mentioned the same person without knowing it.

Different signs. Different elements. The kind of pairing that gets a flat "not compatible" from every quiz on the internet — fire and earth, a mover and a planner, someone who leaves the group chat and someone who never has.

On paper, it shouldn't have lasted six months. It's going on four years.

Here's what the quiz missed: synastry isn't sign-matching. It's aspect-reading. Two charts can clash on the surface and still lock in hard where it counts — a Venus that trades with a Mars, a Moon that steadies a wound the other one's Saturn keeps circling.

You think incompatible signs mean an incompatible relationship. It's actually about which planets are talking to each other — and two "opposite" people can have a conversation running underneath that no quiz result ever sees.

That's why couples who look perfect on paper sometimes fizzle, and the ones nobody bet on sometimes don't. The chart isn't measuring vibes. It's measuring what actually happens when two specific charts sit in the same room.

Sign compatibility is real — it's just the cover of the book. The chapter that matters is the aspects between two actual charts.
