A woman wrote to me last week. Four words: "does he like me?"

I get some version of that question more than any other. And almost every time, it's not the question that's actually keeping someone up at night.

Here's the tell: she'd already told me, three sentences earlier, that he texts back within the hour, remembers what she said last month, and canceled plans with his friends to see her. She knew the answer. She was asking anyway.

That's not a compatibility question. That's a permission question.

> **↳ THE DECODE.** "Does he like me?" is rarely about him. It's usually: *am I allowed to want this without getting hurt again?*

There's a placement behind that hesitation — often a guarded Venus, or a 7th house that's been burned before and is doing its job a little too well. It doesn't ask "is this safe" out loud. It asks "does he like me?" instead, because that question has an answer you can go looking for. The real one doesn't.

So I didn't tell her whether he liked her. I told her what her hesitation was protecting.

Next time you catch yourself asking whether someone likes you, and some part of you already knows — sit with the real question for one minute before you go looking for more evidence.

You've got a version of this too, dear. I can't tell you whose chart is running it without seeing it.
