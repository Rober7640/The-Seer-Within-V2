Two different people told me the same sentence this month: "I'm just not a relationship person."

Both had been in long relationships before. Both wanted one now. Neither flinched saying it — it came out smooth, rehearsed, like a line said enough times it stopped feeling like a confession.

That's the tell. Not the sentence — the smoothness of it.

> **↳ THE DECODE.** A line you've said so many times it doesn't feel like admitting anything anymore isn't a fact about you. It's usually a placement that's been burned and decided the safest story is "I don't want this," instead of "I want this and I'm scared of it."

Often it's a Venus or a Mars that took a hit once and rewrote the narrative to make the guard feel like a preference. "I'm not a relationship person" is easier to say than "the last time I opened up, it went badly, and I haven't tested whether that was the person or the pattern."

Here's the practice: notice the line you say most easily about yourself, romantically. Not the one that costs you something to admit — the one that comes out smooth. That's usually the one worth questioning.

Tell me yours, dear. I'd rather hear the sentence you say on autopilot than the one you have to think about.
