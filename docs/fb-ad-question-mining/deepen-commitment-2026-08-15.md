## 2. From sub-bucket to question

> 🔧 **Corrected 2026-08-16.** This section was re-derived through Step 3b (derive the
> injuries) and both question gates. Three things changed and each one changes what gets
> built: ROOMMATES had **one** injury and actually has **five**; the shipped injury turned
> out to be the segment's *weakest*; and every question needed rewriting twice — once to
> stand alone in a feed, once to stop casting the reader as the villain.

### The transformation, in four moves

Her words are a **description** — flat, no actor, nothing at stake. An ad question is an
**accusation she already believes**. Four moves get you from one to the other:

| Move | What it does |
|---|---|
| 1. Find the injury | Not the situation. What the situation *did to her* — and why she can't name it |
| 2. Give it an actor | Make him the subject of the verb. "It went quiet" → "he stopped reaching" |
| 3. Presuppose it | She already knows it's true. Never ask *whether* — ask why, how long, or which |
| 4. Pick the attack | **WHY** explain it · **HOW-LONG** quantify it · **BINARY** force the status · **COST/RIVAL** name what it took from her |

⛔ **"Will he ever ___" is banned.** It fails move 3 — it hands her back the doubt she came
in with, instead of naming what she already knows.

### Then two gates every question must pass

🔴 **Gate 1 — self-contained.** Read it cold with the heading covered. A feed has no
heading. "Is that all there is left?" — left of *what*? The tell is a dangling `that`,
`this`, `those`, `she`, `us`. Fix by naming the situation inside the sentence: *"a sexless
marriage"*, *"his wife"*. It costs words and it is worth it.

🔴 **Gate 2 — she arrives innocent.** A click is semi-public. If the sentence casts her as
the cheater or the other woman, clicking is confessing, and she scrolls on. **Presuppose
HIS failure or the situation's, never hers.** The VOC demands this — unprompted, she
defends herself before anyone accuses her:

> *"He loves his wife, **I'm not a marriage breaker** but still feel drawn to him"*
> *"**I'm not a home wrecker.** As that has been done to me, so I know the consequences"*
> *"He has a girlfriend... **I wouldn't try to destroy their relationship**"*

The wound is not "I want him". It is **"I am a good person in an impossible situation, and
I need someone to tell me I am not bad."** This is a decency rule as much as a conversion
rule: do not sell to these women by confirming their worst opinion of themselves.

### ROOMMATES → five injuries, not one

**How the wounds actually split** — 123 concerns matching the verified ROOMMATES pattern
across `love` + `someone`. Share is the honest measure here; see the money warning below.

| Injury | Share | Build? |
|---|---|---|
| **The clock** — the years are gone and I am running out | **39%** | ✅ build first |
| **Health / caretaker** — I can't leave, he is *ill* | 15% | ⛔ never target |
| **Someone else is already there** | 11% | ✅ build |
| **He stopped reaching** — *the one the first pass shipped* | 10% | ✅ build, but demoted |
| **Stuck** — nothing is wrong and I can't move | 7% | ✅ build |
| Tolerated, not wanted | 6% | ~ thin, fold into "he stopped reaching" |
| *no wound matched* | 32% | — |

🔴 **The first pass shipped the segment's weakest wound.** "He stopped reaching" covers
**10%** of its own segment and returns **$7,833 per 1,000 — below the $10,470 pool base**.
The dominant wound, the clock at 39%, had **no question written for it at all.**

🔴 **Do not rank these on money.** The piles run 7–18 rows with 1–4 buyers. "Someone else"
and "Stuck" both post ~$26,000 per 1,000, both clear the bootstrap bar — and both lose a
third of their value when you drop a single buyer, which is the exact failure that killed
TWOBOATS. **Rank on share. Treat every dollar figure in this section as a hypothesis.**

#### Injury 1 · The clock — 39%

> *"10 years… not even hand holding"* · *"we have not had sex for 7 years"* · *"33 yrs of
> unloved marriage and separate bedrooms"* · *"I'm 69"* · *"Are my husband and I just
> roommates at this point. Is that all there is left?"*

**What it did to her:** the years are spent and unrecoverable, and she is running out of
time to have anything else. She is counting — that is the tell.

- [ ] **HOW-LONG** — "How many more years in a marriage with no intimacy?"
- [ ] **BINARY** — "Is a loveless marriage all there is left for me?" — *HIGH: near-verbatim*
- [ ] **WHY** — "Why does my husband think I'll keep waiting to be wanted?"
- [ ] **COST** — "What have the years in a sexless marriage cost me?"

⚠ **Age is inside this wound** — "too old", "my age", "I'm 69". The banned *form* is the
second-person age assertion ("Are you over 60 and…"). Keep it first-person; never assert
her age back at her.

#### Injury 2 · Someone else is already there — 11%

> *"I'm stuck in a loveless marriage but it feels like someone is out there waiting for
> me"* · *"Loveless marriage. Met someone and I'm head over heels but don't know if he's
> honest"* · *"I am in a loveless marriage and have been walking around as a zombie for
> many years. Then I meet a person who showed me I still had value."*

**What it did to her:** she was invisible at home for years, and then someone was kind to
her. She did not go looking. **Gate 2 governs this whole injury** — every early draft made
her the cheater, and she is not one.

- [ ] **BINARY** — "Is this a real connection, or just the first kindness I've had in years?" ← *strongest: it offers her the sceptical reading of her own feelings, which is what an innocent person asks*
- [ ] **WHY** — "Why did a connection I never went looking for hit this hard?"
- [ ] **HOW-LONG** — "How long was I invisible at home before someone else noticed me?"
- [ ] **MEANING** — "What is this connection trying to show me about my marriage?"

⚠ **Dedup:** this borders UNAVAILABLE (he is married) and the retired TWOBOATS (she must
choose). Keep it anchored to *her* empty marriage as the cause, or it becomes a different
segment's ad.

#### Injury 3 · He stopped reaching — 10%

> *"He shows absolutely no affection at all"* · *"She will allow me but she never
> initiates"* · *"We don't have intimacy. No kissing for long time frames."*

**What it did to her:** nobody left and nobody cheated, so there is no event — and without
an event she cannot justify the grief or expect anyone to treat it as a loss.

- [ ] **WHY** — "Why did my husband stop reaching for me?"
- [ ] **HOW-LONG** — "When did my husband and I become roommates?"
- [ ] **BINARY** — "Is he still in this marriage, or just still in the house?"
- [ ] **RIVAL** — "What gets my husband's attention now that I don't?"

*Two of the original four were rewritten under Gate 1: "What replaced me in his day?" and
"How long have we been roommates?" both needed the heading above them to make sense.*

*Fold in "tolerated, not wanted" (6%) here — same actor, sharper edge:*
- [ ] **BINARY** — "Does my husband still want me, or just let me?"

#### Injury 4 · Stuck — 7%

> *"Loveless marriage, more like a roommate but I don't dislike him I'm just lonely and
> feel stuck"* · *"In a loveless, non intimate marriage for 30+ yrs. I dont know what to
> do."*

**What it did to her:** he is not cruel, he is absent — so there is nothing to point at
that would justify leaving, to herself or to anyone else.

- [ ] **WHY** — "He's a good man. So why does staying feel like giving up on my life?"
- [ ] **HOW-LONG** — "How long do I stay in a marriage nobody did anything wrong in?"
- [ ] **BINARY** — "Is my marriage worth saving, or already finished?"

#### ⛔ Injury 5 · Health / caretaker — 15% — DO NOT TARGET

> *"I'm in a totally loveless marriage that is only existing for insurance for my husband
> and he is ill. I am his caretaker."* · *"I'm in a loveless marriage and I'm scared to
> leave because I have chronic health issues."* · *"married to a dismissive avoidant, who
> happens to have terminal cancer"*

**Three times bigger than the ~5% the first pass estimated, and it is the second-largest
wound in the segment.** Health is Meta personal-attributes territory, so it gets no
questions — but a ROOMMATES ad *will* pull this audience whatever the copy says, because
it shares the vocabulary exactly. **The reveal must never assume the silence is his
choice. For her, it isn't.** Write no headline against this wound; write every reveal so
it doesn't wound her.

**Cascade arithmetic:** 4 buildable injuries × ~4 questions = **15 questions × 3 tarot
formats = 45 cells**, from 15 registry entries. The first pass named 4.

### UNAVAILABLE → the injury the first pass missed

The three injuries stand (not chosen / sunk years / rival), but Gate 2 rewrote nearly
every question — each one had a dangling `she` *and* framed her as the other woman.

🔴 **A fourth injury surfaced while fixing them: he lied about being available.**

> *"I was involved with a married man who **told me he was separated** from his wife. We
> had a daughter together and he returned to his first wife."*

For a real slice of this segment the wound is not forbidden love — it is **deception**.
She is the party who was lied to, not the intruder. That is truer, cleaner, and far more
clickable than anything in the first pass.

- [ ] **HOW-LONG** — "He told me he was separated. How long has he been lying to me?" — *HIGH: near-verbatim*
- [ ] **WHY** — "Why does a man who says he loves me keep going home to his wife?"
- [ ] **BINARY** — "I'm not a marriage breaker. So why can't I let him go?" — *HIGH: her own words, handed back*
- [ ] **RIVAL** — "What does his wife have that he won't leave?"

**Retired under Gate 2, no fix worth keeping:** "Does his wife know she's already won?" and
"How long has his wife known about me?" — both make her a contestant in someone else's
marriage.

**Rewritten under Gate 1** (dangling `her`/`she`): "Why does he keep choosing her?" →
*"Why does he keep choosing his wife over me?"* · "Why won't he leave her?" → *"Why won't
he leave his wife?"* · "How many years does a promise get?" → *"How many years have I
waited for a married man to leave?"* · "Was it ever going somewhere, or was I always the
pause?" → *"Was I ever going to be more than the other woman?"*

🔴 **UNAVAILABLE was never re-derived through Step 3b.** Three injuries is what the first
pass happened to find, not what the segment holds. Treat it as a floor and re-run.

## Appendix A — reveal copy, for whoever builds these

One **deck** = 3 cards, shared by all hooks on that injury group. One **hook** = one ad
question. `fb-tarot-add-card` builds the deck from a card-strip image; the hook is wired
in `client/src/content/tarotReads.ts` + `server/routes.ts` + `server/lib/prompts.ts`.

⚠ **Card art is optional, not a blocker.** A new question reuses the default deck
(`return-mhf`) art, so ship on that and add bespoke art only after a winner shows. It still
needs its own 3 reveal reads written into whichever deck it uses.

🔴 **These two decks were written on 2026-08-15, when ROOMMATES had one injury.** Deck 2
covers "he stopped reaching" only — the 10% wound. **The clock (39%), someone else is
already there (11%) and stuck (7%) have no reveal copy yet.** Build their hooks on the
default deck and write their reads; do not stretch Deck 2's cards over wounds they were
not written for. The Four of Cups is about a man who stopped reaching; it says nothing
about years running out.

### Deck 1 — UNAVAILABLE (not chosen / sunk years / rival)

*Bridge: "Some love has a door standing in front of it. Pick the card that shows what's
really behind his."*

- **Two of Swords** — blindfolded, refusing to choose.
  1. A blindfolded figure holds two crossed swords over her heart. The card of a choice being actively not made.
  2. A man holding two lives in balance on purpose. The blindfold isn't confusion — it's how he avoids the moment of choosing.
  3. The stalemate costs him nothing. That is precisely why it has lasted this long.
  4. Those swords come down in this card. Who lowers them first is the whole reading.
- **Seven of Swords** — what gets carried in secret.
  1. A figure slips away from a camp with five swords, leaving two stuck in the ground. The card of the thing carried quietly.
  2. Points to a man running a second life in a sealed compartment — and you are the one kept inside it.
  3. Secrecy stopped being a phase and became the shape of the relationship.
  4. He left two swords behind. There is always a part he couldn't hide, and it's usually the part that tells you the truth.
- **The Lovers** — the choice card, misread as the romance card.
  1. Two figures beneath an angel. Everyone reads this as romance; it has always been the card of a choice with a cost.
  2. Shows a man standing at exactly this fork, treating "not yet" as though it were a third road.
  3. He needs both roads to stay open. They don't stay open.
  4. This card resolves — it is built to. The only question is which way, and when.

### Deck 2 — ROOMMATES (he stopped reaching)

*Bridge: "A marriage can go quiet long before anyone says so out loud. Pick the card that
matches the silence in yours."*

- **Four of Cups** — the offered cup nobody reaches for.
  1. A figure sits under a tree with folded arms while a cup is held out to him. Nothing is wrong. He simply isn't reaching.
  2. A man who stopped reaching years ago and never announced it. What's offered is still in front of him; he's stopped seeing it.
  3. Not a fight, not another woman. Familiarity that hardened into not-looking.
  4. The fourth cup is still being held out in this card. Whether it gets picked up is the entire question.
- **Four of Wands** — the home you built, standing empty.
  1. Two figures raise flowers under a garlanded canopy — the wedding, the house, the years, the thing you built together.
  2. Suggests a man loyal to the structure, who has mistaken keeping it standing for loving you.
  3. The building got finished and nobody moved back in.
  4. The canopy in this card is open on all four sides. It was never meant to be the whole marriage.
- **The Hermit** — two people sharing a roof, each living alone under it.
  1. A robed figure stands alone on a summit holding up a lamp. The card of solitude — whether or not it was chosen.
  2. Points to a man who has quietly moved into his own company and grown comfortable there, without noticing who got left outside it.
  3. Two people can share a bed and a mortgage and still each be living alone.
  4. The Hermit's lamp exists to be seen from a distance. Someone has to look up.
