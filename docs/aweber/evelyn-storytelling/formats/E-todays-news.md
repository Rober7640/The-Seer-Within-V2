# E · Today's News

**Status:** 🧪 TO STRESS-TEST
**In one line:** a real, recent news story about love → Evelyn's warm, slightly funny take → the one thing it shows → her version → the chat.
**Source:** Furey, *Tao of Email Copywriting*, PDF p.58 — "Russian Sumo Dope Smokers Nailed" (dissected in "Dissecting the Psychology of Furey Emails"). Real news → his opinion ("this Fure-cat tends to think one of two things…") → a joke → bridge to a principle → two products → link.

## Shape for Evelyn (≈350–500 words)
1. The headline, plainly, in line 1 — what happened (dated, sourced).
2. The detail that makes people smile or wince.
3. Evelyn's take — "I tend to think one of two things…" (opinion, clearly hers).
4. The bridge: what it says about love/waiting/second chances.
5. Pitch: her version → one to one → 3 ➤ → link → button → P.S.

## Rules
- Real, verifiable, recent (≤2 weeks), from a reputable outlet; link the source in `## Sources`.
- ⛔ No tragedies, deaths, abuse, private people's pain, politics. Light, human stories only (late-life weddings, reunions, odd love records).
- Don't mock real private people. Public figures: gentle.
- **Picture:** never the real people's faces/likeness (implied endorsement + copies copyrighted news photos). Draw the moment from behind, no faces — people beat objects (05 swapped a rose for the couple on the tube).
- Evergreen check: the email must still make sense if sent a week late — or schedule it fast.

## Stress-test questions
1. Can we find a suitable story every time we need one? (Sourcing cost per email.)
2. Does a news hook beat a story hook for opens on this list?
3. Risk: a story that turns sour after sending (a "couple of the year" splits).

**Risk notes**
- **Sour-after-send** (stress question 3): real here, because Ken is 89. Rule: check the news and the family's public posts the day before sending. If anything sad has happened, don't send.
- **Grief next door:** light love stories about older people often sit next to a death (widowhood, a grandchild). Keep death out of the email, never use a family's spiritual claims about their dead ("my little brother sent Ken to her"), and tell the chat (Reading Recap) that Evelyn has no news about the couple.
- **Shelf life is short:** ≤2 weeks from coverage (05: last sensible send Sun 18 Oct). The format needs a fast lane: find → draft → operator → send within ~10 days. Date the news by month ("In September"), never "last week", so a late send is stale, not false.
- **Subjects:** line 1 must be the news, so a pain-led subject can't be paid off in line 1. Put the line that pays the subject off in the first ~100 words (05: the granddaughter's phone line).
- **Paywalls:** WaPo, NYT and others block fetches. *People* reads in full through Yahoo syndication. Count a paywalled outlet as a second source only for what its headline, caption or snippet shows.

**Lessons for the next E email (method):**
- A news story alone reads thin ("needs a bit more substance"). Give every E email a substance device after the take. The proven one is **Ask yourself**: 3 yes/no questions about HER life, each tied to one sourced fact. Check each anchor against the source: the operator's own draft had two inferences and one mix-up (who said "let's go").
- Find with Google News RSS, not web search: `curl -A Mozilla "https://news.google.com/rss/search?q=<terms>+when:15d&hl=en-US&gl=US&ceid=US:en"`. It lists dated headlines from every outlet; ~25 queries took ~10 min. Good terms: "found love again", "reunited married decades", "high school sweethearts", "lost touch years later", "second chance at love". Filter out obituaries.
- Google News item links redirect through a signed `batchexecute` call (decoder in the session scratchpad, `news-scan/gndecode.py`). Or search the exact headline.
- Blocked from search: apnews.com, people.com, bbc.*, theguardian.com, timesofindia (curl works for TOI). CNN pages work with curl.
- Pick the story for its **one concrete love detail** (05: the Tuesday rose), not for its headline. That detail is what turns onto her question, and it gives the moral.
