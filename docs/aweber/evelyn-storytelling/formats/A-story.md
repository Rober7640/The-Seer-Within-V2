# A · Story

**Status:** ✅ PROVEN — gold [`../emails/01-grandma-moses.md`](../emails/01-grandma-moses.md) (locked 2026-10-07)
**In one line:** one true story, told in order, with a clear moral → "3 things she taught me" → bold pitch.
**Source:** our own (built over 6 operator rounds). Furey's version of the idea: *"figure out how to write incredible stories… Imbedded in these stories are instructions on how to live a better life — as well as how to order your product"* (Tao of Email Copywriting, PDF p.8, Mistake #5 "No story").
**Best for:** trust, warmth, being read. **Repeatability:** high — any true public life with a setback and a comeback.

## The shape (≈650 words, ~60% content / 40% pitch)

### Content
1. **Opening reflection (4–5 short lines).** Evelyn's own thought about a phrase or feeling the reader knows. List three versions — **love first**. End: *"Let me tell you about her."* A reflection is a thought ("I've been thinking about…"), never an invented event, client or memory.
2. **Name line** — *"Her name was ___. Also known as ___."* Then the small photo.
3. **The story, strictly in time order.** No flash-forwards, no teasers, no "I'll come back to that". One story — drop side anecdotes, however charming.
   - **Setup** — who she was, her ordinary life, what she loved
   - **Problem** — the thing she loved is taken away
   - **Turn** — someone/something points another way
   - **Nobody cares** — it goes badly or nowhere (one wry, true detail — *"I won a prize for my fruit and jam, but no pictures."*)
   - **Break** — a stranger / chance / decision
   - **Setback** — the doubters (the reader's own doubt, said out loud by someone in the story)
   - **Payoff** — short, specific, true
4. **2 connectors inside the story, max**, at the two biggest turns: *"Here's where things get interesting."* · *"Now, here's one thing I didn't know."* Vary over time.
5. **"Here are 3 things [she] taught me."** Three bold-lead lessons, 1–2 lines each. Lesson 2 = **the moral the plot proved**, in words a 12-year-old gets. Lesson 3 **hands off to the pitch**.

### Pitch
6. *"That third one is why I'm writing to you today."* → one line linking the story to her.
7. *"So here's my invitation."* → name her version (love first) → **come and talk to me. Just you and me, one to one.**
8. What to bring + lower the effort: *"Type it the way you'd say it to a friend."*
9. *"Then I'll tell you plainly what I see:"* + **3 ➤ outcomes** (bounded — help her see, never a promised result).
10. **Text link** (CTA 1, see CTA rules) → honest line → free minutes → reason not to wait (story callback) → **button** → *— Evelyn* → **P.S.** callback + link.

**Subject style:** curiosity + pain about the story's idea (01: *🤐 {name}, the 2 words that keep people stuck*). **Preheader:** *The true story of [name], and 3 things [she] taught me.*
