# G · The List

**Status:** 🧪 TO STRESS-TEST — a variant of B · Watermelon
**In one line:** "3 signs / 5 things" — the WHAT in a numbered list; which one is HERS (the HOW) is the chat.
**Source:** Furey, *Tao of Email Copywriting*, PDF p.18 — "Give 'em a List": *"You make a list alright… They will acknowledge you as the expert… Only thing is that the person must commit to doing something… the WHAT to do won't change a thing… this cannot be done without further study."* Also the teaser-list email "3 Health Beverages That Make You Gain Weight" (p.15) — the list is promised, the reveal is in the paid issue.

## Two versions
1. **Open list** — give all 3–5 items (the WHAT); the chat says which applies to her.
2. **Teaser list** (p.15) — name that there ARE 3 (e.g. "3 things he does when he's still thinking about you"), describe the category, reveal in the chat. ⚠ Only if the chat actually delivers them — chat-test.

## Shape (≈400–550 words)
Hook (her worry) → "There are 3…" → the list (bold leads, 1–2 lines each) → "Which one is yours? That's not something a list can tell you." → pitch.

## Rules
- Items must be true/defensible observations, not invented "signs" presented as fact.
- ⛔ Furey's Mistake #4 (p.7): a list that teaches too much satisfies her — keep items as WHAT, never HOW.
- Avoid listicle tone; keep Evelyn's voice.

## Stress-test questions
1. Does a list read as "content" (good) or "clickbait" (bad) for this list?
2. Teaser version: is withholding fair if the chat delivers it?

**Method notes for the next G email:**
- **Start from one public-domain folklore collection** (Bergen 1896; Hazlitt 1905; Kelley 1919) — grep the Gutenberg/archive.org text for the theme. Each entry is numbered and place-tagged, so every list item can carry a source tag in the line itself ("an old Boston saying"), which is what keeps a list of beliefs from reading as claims.
- **Check the neighbours of every entry you pick.** Folklore books list rival meanings side by side (no. 373 "your lover is thinking of you" sits next to no. 374 "you will be a widow"). If the rival is dark, drop the item; if it's harmless, say "one old version".
- **Leave out the folk "who" tricks.** Many old signs come with a DIY way to name the person. Including one gives away the HOW the chat sells.
- **The last item hands off to the turn** (07: the shoelace says "your true love" — "It doesn't say which man that is").
- **One laugh, from the source itself** (07: the nose's "out of town / in town" → "Well, that narrows it down"). The funniest entry in Bergen (no. 1433, three sneezes = "a sign of a cold") was left out because it's gendered for men; don't bend a quote to fit.
- **Subject:** lead with her question, never the list ("7 signs…" = clickbait + promise).
- Teaser version (stress question 2): not tested here. Only build one when the chat is proven to deliver the withheld items.
