**Stress-test questions, answered:**
1. *Does "I dare you" feel playful or pushy from Evelyn?* **Playful — if three things hold.** (a) The dare is small and doable: 50 words, not "leave him" or "buy now". (b) It's set against the *wrong* dares (the rules that tell her to stay quiet), so Evelyn's dare reads as permission, not pressure. (c) The reader says yes in her own voice ("Evelyn, I'll take your dare"). It would tip into pushy if the dare is about buying, or if "dare" repeats — so the body dares ONCE and the P.S. does not dare again (Furey's own P.S., "I dare you to try", was left out on purpose). Operator's read decides.
2. *Strong enough alone, or best as a P.S. device?* **Both, depending on the story.** With a true dare that has a payoff and a number (50 words → outsold the 236-word book), it carried a full email in ~280 words, with a real takeaway. Without one, it's thin: "I dare you to ask the question you've been not-asking" alone is a closer, not an email. So: full format only when the bank has a verified true dare; otherwise use "I dare you" as a one-line P.S. inside other formats — never in two emails in a row.

**Learned while building:**
- The Columbo close needs a P.S. with a **new** point, not a recap. Here: 50 words are hard to read on your own because you know the story you cut. Furey: "this letter needs to be hard-hitting. It cannot be weak or flimsy."
