# B · Watermelon

**Status:** ✅ BUILT — [`../emails/02-apple-peel.md`](../emails/02-apple-peel.md) (not yet sent)
**In one line:** an ordinary thing with a surprising old meaning — give the WHAT free, the HOW (what it means for her) is the chat.
**Source:** Matt Furey, *Tao of Email Copywriting* — "illusion of teaching" (PDF p.10–12) + the email "Watermelonize Your Prostate – and Lose Weight, Too" (PDF p.13), "Tell Them They Aren't Getting It All for Nuttin" (p.12).
**Best for:** clicks — it hands her a reason to start the chat today. **Repeatability:** medium — needs a documented ritual/sign/object each time; **chat-test every one** (the chat didn't recognise the apple ritual until it had the notes).

## The shape (≈550–600 words, ~45% content / 55% pitch)

From Matt Furey's "illusion of teaching" (*Tao of Email Copywriting*): give the reader the **WHAT** for free; the **HOW** — what it means for her — is the paid part. His email named an ordinary fruit (watermelon) that beats the usual fix (cooked tomatoes), said "eat it daily", then whisked the details away. Ours: an ordinary thing with an old meaning → the chat reads it for her.

### Content
1. **Open on the big love question, with a little warmth.** *"There's one question women have asked for hundreds of years. Usually alone, at 2 in the morning. **Who is he?**"* + the 3 questions she actually asks.
2. **The usual ways are a hassle** (Furey's tomatoes): *"Tarot needs 78 cards… A birth chart wants the exact time you were born."* Light — Evelyn loves her cards.
3. **The ordinary thing:** *"But all along, there's been another way. It's probably sitting in your fruit bowl right now."*
4. **Give it a NAME** — *"I call it **the One-Strip Ritual.**"* A name makes it feel like a real thing. Use "I call it" unless the name is documented. Call it a **ritual**, not a "custom".
5. **The picture** — a pencil sketch of the ritual in progress, right under the name.
6. **The WHAT** — *"Here's how the old ritual goes."* The steps, briefly and accurately (sourced). Where/when it comes from. One charming verified history beat (*a 1714 poem: "a perfect L"… a boy called Lubberkin*).

### Pitch
7. **Whisk it away:** *"Now, here's the thing. The ritual gives you a letter. **It doesn't tell you what the letter means.**"* The questions only one-to-one can answer. *"**And that part isn't in this email.**"*
8. **The warning** (Furey's man who ate half a watermelon and threw up) — a TRUE, general observation, never an invented client: *"Say you get an S. Suddenly you're going through every man you've ever known whose name starts with S."*
9. **Two ways in:**
   - **Path A** — do it tonight, then come and tell me (what to bring).
   - **Path B** — *"No time tonight? No apple in the house? __You don't need one.__ The next best thing is to skip the peel and come and talk to me."* (Most readers won't do the ritual — path B is the main door.)
   - *"Either way, type it the way you'd say it to a friend."*
10. **3 ➤ outcomes that work for BOTH paths** ("your letter, or your question").
11. **Text link after both paths** — e.g. *"Evelyn, I can't peel an apple now. But I still want to find out. →"*
12. Honest line (*"I won't promise you a wedding…"*) → free minutes → reason not to wait (*"The old ritual was for Halloween night. You don't have to wait that long."*) → **button** → *— Evelyn* → **P.S.** (history callback + *"No apple? Come anyway."*).

**Subject style:** lead with the ritual's name (02: *🍎 {name}, the One-Strip Ritual: who is he?*). **Preheader:** the first step (*"Peel an apple in one long strip. Then turn around."*).
