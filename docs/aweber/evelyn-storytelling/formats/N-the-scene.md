## The shape (≈550–650 words, ~60/40 content/pitch — 11 ran 67/33, see the stress test)

1. **The frame (Evelyn, one line).** It must say the woman is made up, and give her a first name: *"Let me tell you a story. Diane is made up, but you might know her Tuesday night. Here she is."* Italic when the build supports it (it doesn't yet — plain line).
2. **Her story — first person, past tense, spoken, short lines.** Line 1 is the whole story + the prop (*"I ran into my ex at Walmart. He was with her. She couldn't have been more than 35. And I was holding a frozen lasagna that said SERVES 1…"*). Then: the place and the line (bold, alone) → the dignity beat → the car + the wrong reasons (one per line) → the weeks after (funny-sad, specific) → a "helpful" person who makes it worse → "I was done."
3. **How she finds Evelyn — only what's true of the V2 chat.** She clicks an email / types it all out / the first 3 minutes are free (she's new). Evelyn reads first, doesn't flatter, doesn't promise he'll be sorry, then asks **ONE question** in our own words (not the ads').
4. **The turn, inside her story.** The question makes her remember ONE moment as a little girl: one room, who said it, the exact sentence, one prop. Make each one new — not the ads' rooms, not a previous email's. Best when the old sentence is the very thing the scene seemed to *prove* (11: "After that, they all go younger." → "She couldn't have been more than 35.").
5. **Stop on recognition.** In her own words: whose sentence it was, and that it was never hers. No outcome.
6. **Evelyn steps back in** — the Pitch starts here: "[Name]'s made up. That sentence isn't." + at most one line of Evelyn's view → the substance device (Ask yourself: 3 yes/no questions, each tied to a beat of the story) → the pitch shape.
7. **Pitch.** Rotate the shape (sends log). Text link (first person, no precondition — works with no ex) → true free-minutes line → reason not to wait = a callback to the prop → button → P.S. that calls back the line.

## Rules

- **Truth — three rules:**
  1. **The frame line.** Evelyn says the woman is made up, before the story (and again after). No unframed "I" — the email is signed by Evelyn, so an unframed "I" reads as her own memory. A made-up customer presented as real is a fake testimonial (FTC 2024 rule, 16 CFR 465).
  2. **Evelyn's part matches the real chat.** Only what the live prompt does (typed; new users' first 3 min free; GIVE THEN ASK — reads first, at most one question; no comforting yes; a card pull only if it fits, and it comes last). Cite the prompt lines in the email's Notes. Never: letters, pages by morning, candlelight, Ireland, "first name is all she needs", or Evelyn predicting something that came true.
  3. **No outcome.** The story stops on recognition. No new man, no ex coming back, no "six weeks later", no "I've never been happier", no "you'll be chosen".
- **No composite claims.** Never "women tell me this every week", never "she's based on real clients". A composite needs real data + operator OK (it opens I · Session).
- **Biography is fine inside the story** (it's hers, and specifics make it real), but keep the scene one many readers have lived or dread, and let the reader with no ex in — through the Ask-yourself questions, the if-lines and the Continue Seed.
- **Never imply Evelyn knows the reader.** She knows Diane only because Diane is made up.
- **One scene. One line. One question. One room.**
- **The body is only ever self-blame,** absolved, never changed. No looks-based payoff ("you look incredible").
- **Dignity, not revenge.** No gloating, no comparing women.
- **Love first.**
- **Our own words.** Scene types are free to use; the competitors' scripts, props, questions and childhood rooms aren't.
- **Sensitive:** no bereavement scene without a deliberate decision; nothing sexual. Church/Mass: the ads ban it; for email, operator to decide.
- **Reader-echo check:** words she'll type back ("broke") — same rule as every format.

## Subject / preheader

- Test **her voice, in quotes** (the story's line 1): *🛒 "I ran into my ex at Walmart. She couldn't have been more than 35."* — never without the quote marks (a bare "I" from Evelyn is Evelyn's memory).
- The **cutting line itself, in quotes**, is the other natural subject: *💬 "Oh. Just someone from before."* · *"Mom, this is my friend."* · *"Is she pregnant, or…?"*
- **Preheader labels it:** *"Diane's story. She's made up. Her Tuesday night might not be."* — so the label is visible before she opens.
- ⛔ Not the object ("the Walmart email"); ⛔ no bare "he's with someone new" (from a psychic, it reads as a reading of HER man).

## CTA

- Text link, first person, no precondition: *"Evelyn, I want to find out whose sentence I've been living by →"*
- Button (vary per email, tied to the scene): *"Tell Evelyn about my aisle"*
- P.S.: the line again + one sentence pointing at the older one (*"If words like that are still rattling around in your head, I doubt they're alone in there."*)

## Reading brief

- **Big Idea:** "[Name]'s story (a made-up woman): [the scene], and whose voice the old sentence really was".
- **Reading Recap:** must say the woman was **made up**, retell her story in a few lines, list what Evelyn did in it, and state that no outcome was promised.
- **Open Loop:** "a moment like hers — what happened and what they told themselves after — or, with no ex, the sentence they still hear".
- **Continue Seed** (works if she skimmed or has no ex): *"You came from my letter about Diane, the made-up woman in the Walmart aisle… Has anyone ever said something about you, out loud, that you still hear? Tell me what it was."*
- **Chat test:** "is Diane real?" (must say made up) · "what did you tell Diane?" (no invented transcript) · "that happened to me at Target" · "my mom always said men leave" · "will he regret it?" (no comforting yes) · "I don't have an ex".

## Stress-test questions (answer when we build it)

1. Does the frame line kill the story's pull ("she's made up — why should I care")? Or does "you might know her Tuesday night" carry it?
2. Can the turn land without the ads' proof (Evelyn "knowing" a hidden fact) — when Evelyn only asks one question and the woman remembers herself?
3. Does a story that stops on recognition, with no outcome, still satisfy?
4. Can we find ≥5 more scenes, each with a NEW childhood room?
5. Does the chat really do what the story shows (the one question, no flattery) — chat test.

**What the builds taught (apply to the next one):**
- **Line 1 is the whole story** — who, where, the sting, the prop. Don't open on the setting.
- **The prop is the motif.** The lasagna comes back in the car, in the weeks after ("It did, in fact, serve one."), in the take-away ("enjoy your lasagna") and in the reason not to wait ("A frozen lasagna keeps for months. A sentence like that can keep for fifty years.").
- **Tie Evelyn's question to her wrong reason.** Diane's third reason in the car was "my one good chance"; Evelyn asks "Whose voice is that?" about exactly that line. The question then leads straight to the room.
- **Pick the childhood sentence the scene seems to prove** ("they all go younger" ↔ "she couldn't have been more than 35"), so "It was agreeing with my mother" needs no explaining. Add one line that keeps it from blaming the parent ("Mom probably got it from hers.").
- **The split:** a full first-person story runs content-heavy. With Evelyn's bridge + the Ask-yourself questions inside the Content, v2 ran 85/15; starting the Pitch at "Diane's made up." brought it to 67/33. Getting to 60/40 costs one story beat (the sister or the weeks after) — the operator's call.
