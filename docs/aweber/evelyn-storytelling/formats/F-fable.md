# F · The Fable

**Status:** 🧪 TO STRESS-TEST — a variant of A · Story
**In one line:** an old fable (animals, kings, villagers) → its moral, plainly → onto love → the chat.
**Source:** Furey, *Tao of Email Copywriting*, PDF p.45 — "Why The Cat is the Tiger's Teacher" (a Chinese fable: the cat teaches the tiger everything except how to climb a tree). He tells it in ~200 words, then: *"Moral of the story: …"*

## How it differs from A · Story
- Not true — and doesn't pretend to be. "There's an old Chinese fable…" / "Aesop tells a story…"
- No research load beyond confirming the fable and its origin.
- Shorter (≈350–500 words). Same "3 things" option, or just one moral.

## Shape
1. Name the tradition, tell only the necessary action (setup → problem → turn → ending).
2. **"Moral of the story:"** — one line, plain.
3. Onto love (her version).
4. Pitch (as A).

## Rules
- Name the tradition honestly; don't invent a "Chinese/Irish fable" — if it's a modern parable, say so.
- One moral; don't stretch the metaphor through the pitch.
- Avoid fables the reframe deck already used (lighthouse, jar, etc. — see reframe STATE.md).

## Stress-test questions
1. Does a fable feel as "real" as a true story to this list, or weaker?
2. How many fables have a clean LOVE moral?
