There’s one question women have asked for hundreds of years. Usually alone, at 2 in the morning.

Who is he?

Will I ever meet him? Have I met him already? Is he someone I know, and I just haven’t looked properly?

When you want to know, you’re usually told to get a reading.

Tarot needs 78 cards and someone who knows them all. (I love my cards. But that’s a lot of cards.)

A birth chart wants the exact time you were born. Plenty of us don’t know it. Our mothers were a little busy at the time.

But all along, there’s been another way. It’s probably sitting in your fruit bowl right now. No cards. No birth time. All you need is a knife.

I call it the One-Strip Ritual.

Here’s how the old ritual goes.

Peel your apple in one long strip. Don’t let it break. Some say swing the peel round your head three times. Then throw it over your shoulder. Most of the old accounts say the left one.

Turn around and look at how it landed. The old ritual says that shape is the first letter of the name of the one you’ll marry.

In Ireland, it was a Halloween night ritual. It turns up in Irish folklore records from the 1930s, from Cavan to Wexford.

And it’s older than that. An English poet put it in a poem in 1714. His girl’s peel lands as “a perfect L.” She was pining for a boy called Lubberkin. (I promise I didn’t make that up.)

Now, here’s the thing.

The ritual gives you a letter. It doesn’t tell you what the letter means.

Is it someone you already know? Someone you haven’t met yet? What if your peel snapped halfway? What if it could be a C, or a U?

The old ritual stops there. And that part isn’t in this email. It can’t be. Your letter isn’t anyone else’s letter.

Here’s what happens if you stop at the letter. Say you get an S. Suddenly you’re going through every man you’ve ever known whose name starts with S. Steve from school. Sam from your old job. The man at the post office whose name you’re not even sure of.

That’s a lot of worry for one apple peel.

So here’s my invitation.

Peel one tonight. Then come and tell me the letter you see. Tell me whether the peel snapped, what the shape looked like, and who came to mind first.

No time tonight? No apple in the house? You don’t need one.

The next best thing is to skip the peel and come and talk to me. Just you and me, one to one. Tell me who’s on your mind, or the question you’d have asked the apple.

Either way, type it the way you’d say it to a friend. You don’t have to make sense of it first. That’s my part.

Then I’ll tell you plainly what I see:

➤  what your letter, or your question, is pointing at

➤  whether it points to someone already in your life

➤  what to do with it, and what not to

The old ritual was for Halloween night. You don’t have to wait that long.
