**Content:**

There's a quiet little sentence a lot of us carry around.

**"Nobody's going to find me here."**

Here, since he left. Here, at my age. Here, in a life that's got smaller, one room at a time.

I've been thinking about a woman who had every reason to believe it.

Let me tell you about her.

Her name was Elizabeth Barrett. Her family called her Ba.

[IMAGE] ../assets/the-first-letter-ebb.jpg | Elizabeth Barrett, a woman with long dark ringlets, looking back over her shoulder | Elizabeth, in later years. / Photo: Library of Congress, Bain Collection (public domain)

At 38, she was one of the best-known poets in England.

But for years she'd barely left one upstairs room in her father's house in London. She'd been unwell a long time.

And her father wouldn't hear of any of his children marrying.

Here's where things get interesting.

In January 1845, a letter came from a poet she'd never met. Robert Browning. He'd just read her poems.

It began: **"I love your verses with all my heart, dear Miss Barrett."**

A few lines down, he went further: "and I love you too."

She wrote back the very next day: "I thank you, dear Mr. Browning, from the bottom of my heart."

He wanted to visit. She held him off. "I shall be afraid of you at first," she wrote, "though I am not, in writing thus."

On 20 May 1845, four months after his first letter, she let him in.

Days later, he told her he loved her. **She said no.**

So he waited. When he raised it again, she laid out every reason against herself. "The common gifts of youth and cheerfulness," she told him, were behind her.

Now, here's the part that got me.

He didn't argue, or pay her a single compliment. He just said he loved her, and would "to his last hour." He'd wait "twenty years," if she liked.

He didn't need twenty years. **It took twenty months.**

On 12 September 1846, she slipped out of the house with her maid and married him in secret. She was 40.

A week later she left her father's house for good, and went with Robert to Italy. Her father never forgave her.

At 43, she had a son.

And all through those twenty months, she'd been writing him love poems in secret. Months after the wedding, she pushed them into his coat pocket and ran back to her room.

One of them begins: **"How do I love thee? Let me count the ways."**

Here's her love story, step by step.

**One room.**

→ Her poems, in print.

→ One letter from a stranger.

→ One answer, the next day.

→ 573 letters in 20 months.

→ A wedding at 40.

→ "How do I love thee?"

Look at the first four steps. She didn't leave that room for any of them.

**Her door was shut. Her words weren't.**

She didn't go out looking for love. She kept writing, from the one room she had. And when a letter came, __she answered it.__

**Pitch:**

That answer is the step I keep thinking about. It was small. It began with a thank-you. The visits, the wedding, Italy, the poems: all of it came after she wrote back.

This isn't for everyone.

If your days are full and your heart's at rest, you can stop here. I'm glad for you.

But if your world has got smaller than you'd like…

If you've caught yourself thinking, "Nobody's going to find me here"…

Or if someone has been knocking, and you're not sure about the door…

Then write back to me. Tell me what your "one room" looks like right now: who's in it, who isn't, and how long it's been that way.

[LINK] Evelyn, I'm ready to open my door a little

You don't need the right words. Remember, she was afraid to meet him, but not to write to him. Writing is the easy part.

When a life gets smaller, it hardly ever happens all at once.

A loss. A hard year. A few nights in that turn into a few years.

__Keeping a door shut isn't weak.__ Plenty of us do it to stay safe after we've been hurt.

It just gets lonely in there after a while.

First time here? **Your first 3 minutes are on the house.**

It took her four months to open her door. __Mine's already open.__

**→ Write back to Evelyn**

— Evelyn

**P.S.** Her first letter to him began, "I thank you, dear Mr. Browning, from the bottom of my heart." Yours can begin, "I don't know where to start." That's enough.
