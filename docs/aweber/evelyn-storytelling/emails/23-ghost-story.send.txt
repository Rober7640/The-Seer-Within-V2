If you know that feeling, here's a true story for Halloween week. The story behind a ghost story.

Summer, 1816. Switzerland. "A wet, ungenial summer," Mary wrote years later. The rain kept her and her friends indoors for days.

Mary was 18. Her friends were two famous poets, Lord Byron and Percy Shelley, and Byron's doctor, John Polidori.

They passed the time with a book of German ghost stories. In one, a man goes to hug his bride, and finds himself in the arms of the ghost of the woman he left.

(Serves him right.)

Then Byron said:

"We will each write a ghost story."

All four said yes. The two poets started, then soon gave up. They found plain storytelling dull, Mary said.

And Mary? She had nothing.

Every morning, someone asked her, "Have you thought of a story?" And every morning she had to give "a mortifying negative."

That's an old way of saying an embarrassing no.

Then one night, Byron and Shelley talked late about what makes things alive. Mary just listened. In bed, she couldn't sleep.

Pictures came on their own: "the pale student… kneeling beside the thing he had put together." Then the thing began to stir.

She opened her eyes in terror. And the idea came, "swift as light":

"I have found it! What terrified me will terrify others."

She started writing the next day. It came out in 1818. Its name? Frankenstein.

Line her summer up, and it looks like this.

Rain.

→ "We will each write a ghost story."

→ The poets give up.

→ Morning after morning: no.

→ One sleepless night.

→ 1818: Frankenstein.

Look at the middle. The poets had a start, and quit. Mary had nothing, and said so, morning after morning. Hers is the ghost story we still read.

I think her "no" was really a "not yet."

"Not yet" wasn't the end of her story. It was the beginning.

Love asks us that morning question all the time. "What do you want?" "Is he the one?" "Are you staying or going?"

No one in your life right now? You still get asked. "Do you even want someone?"

And inside every one of those questions hides a little rule. You should know by now. At our age. After all this time.

That rule is a dare, if you think about it. It dares you to grab any answer, just to make the asking stop.
