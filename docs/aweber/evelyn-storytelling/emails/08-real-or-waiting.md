**Content:**

**Is it real, or are you just waiting?**

Most love questions come down to that one. "Does he love me?" "Is he the one?" "Why won't he commit?" Underneath, it's the same question.

I can't answer it for you in a letter. But I can show you what I look for. These are yours to keep.

**Signs it's real:**

• He reaches for you. He doesn't just look.

• When he says "soon," it happens.

• Your evenings are still your own.

• You think of him, and your body goes calm.

**Signs you're waiting:**

• He looks. He doesn't reach.

• "Soon" has meant "soon" for a long time.

• Your evenings are built around your phone.

• You think of him, and your chest goes tight.

Got something on both lists? Most of us do.

__A list can't tell you which side wins.__ That part isn't in this letter.

**Pitch:**

[IMAGE] ../assets/real-or-waiting-sketch.jpg | A pencil sketch of an older woman's hands laying seven cards face down in a row on a wooden kitchen table, a mug of tea nearby |  | 300 | noborder

So what really happens when you bring me a love question? Here's what we look at.

**1. How you asked.** Not just what you asked. The words you choose, and the ones you leave out, tell me something too.

**2. His name.** Say it out loud. (Go on, nobody's listening.) Does it stop hard, or trail off soft? I read those two very differently, especially when it comes to endings.

**3. What he wants, and what's sitting on top of it.** Not whether he read your text. Not where he was on Saturday. I don't read phones. I read people.

**4. The block.** The one pattern you can't see, because you're standing right inside it. Fair warning: when I name it, it may sting a little.

**5. One card, if you want to go deeper.** At the end, I lay seven cards face down, and you choose one. Only one. There's a reason I won't let you pull a second.

There are two ways in.

**Door one: one question.** The one that keeps you up at night. A single line is plenty.

**Door two: the whole story.** His name, what happened, how long "soon" has been going on. Or that there's no one yet, and you're tired of waiting. We'll take our time.

Both doors open the same chat with me. The only difference is how much you bring.

[LINK] Evelyn, I want to know if it's real, or if I'm just waiting

New to me? **The first 3 minutes are my gift.**

One more thing about "soon." __It never gets shorter on its own.__

**→ Ask Evelyn: real, or waiting?**

— Evelyn

**P.S.** Read the second list again, slowly. If one line made you wince, that's the one to bring me.

## Notes

**Substance device:** Signs it's real / signs you're waiting — two short mirrored lists, 4 lines each, given early (~55 words in) as the free takeaway. Mirror pairs in the same order: reaches / looks · "soon" happens / "soon" stays "soon" · evenings your own / evenings built around your phone · body calm / chest tight. Each line is anchored to Evelyn's own documented view in the live prompt (see Sources), so what the email says is what the chat says. Kept clear of 05's "Ask yourself" questions (shows up without being asked · knows where she stands without rereading texts · something silly) — no line repeats them. The last beat ("A list can't tell you which side wins") hands off to the pitch.

**Pitch shape:** Two doors — **Door one: one question** (a single line is plenty) / **Door two: the whole story** (or "no one yet"). Furey's "Two ways to get the job done" (p.49) and "Give 'em Options" (p.50), made honest: the product has ONE way in (the chat), so the two doors are two ways to *use* it, and the email says so plainly ("Both doors open the same chat with me. The only difference is how much you bring."). The text link sits after both doors, so it works for either. Different from 07's Straight question. None of the retired lines ("So here's my invitation", "Just you and me, one to one", "Then I'll tell you plainly what I see", "I won't promise…", "Type it the way you'd say it to a friend", the old button).

**Furey's shape, kept and changed:** kept — the numbered "here's what's inside" bullets, each dangling a reveal without giving it (his "an insidious food… called a 'vegetable.'
