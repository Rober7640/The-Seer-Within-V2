**Content:**

**Who did you dream about last night?**

Some dreams don't leave when the alarm goes off. They hang around all morning, like the smell of toast.

Girls once tried to dream like that on purpose. Not just any dream. A dream of the man they'd marry.

They had bedtime tricks for it. One book of old sayings, printed in 1896, has close to twenty.

[IMAGE] ../assets/dream-of-him-sketch.jpg | A pencil sketch of a woman in a nightgown lying in bed at night, seen from behind, sliding a small round hand mirror under her pillow, with stars in the window behind her |  | 300 | noborder

Here are five of my favorites.

**1. The bedposts.** Name the four posts of your bed for four different men. "The one you dream about you will marry." It was common almost everywhere.

**2. The clothes.** In Maine, you scattered your clothes into the four corners of the room. "The man you are to marry will bring you your clothes in a dream." (A man who picks your clothes up off the floor? Now that's a dream.)

**3. The stockings.** An old rhyme from eastern Massachusetts: "Point your shoes towards the street, / Leave your garters on your feet, / Put your stockings on your head, / You'll dream of the man you are going to wed."

**4. The stars.** On Prince Edward Island, in Canada, you counted nine stars a night, nine nights in a row. "The person you dream of on the ninth night will be your future partner in life." One cloudy night, and you had to start all over.

**5. The looking-glass.** In Newfoundland: "Put a looking-glass under the pillow, and you will dream of your lover."

Every one of them promised the same thing. **A face.** His face.

Not one of them asked what the dream was trying to tell you.

And that last one makes me smile. Hold up a looking-glass. Whose face do you see?

__Yours.__

That's what I believe about dreams. **A dream about him is a message about you.**

Most of us don't need a trick to dream of a man. He turns up all on his own. The man you're with. The one you're not sure about. The one you thought you were over.

And if no man has turned up in your dreams for years? That says something too.

What's yours saying? No old book can tell you that.

**Pitch:**

Now, the honest part.

I can't tell you who you'll dream about tonight. Not with a bedpost. Not with nine stars. Nobody can.

But the dream you've already had? The one that stayed all morning, or the one that keeps coming back?

**That one I can look at with you.**

Tell me what happened in it, and how you felt when you woke up. We'll look at what it might be saying about you.

[LINK] Evelyn, I want to know what my dreams are saying about me

Can't remember a single dream? Tell me who's on your mind at bedtime.

Waking up from a dream about him can color a whole day.

If that's happened to you, it isn't silly. And it isn't your fault. Nobody picks who walks into her dreams.

__Your heart just keeps working after you turn out the light.__

If you've never chatted with me before, **the first 3 minutes cost you nothing.**

And dreams don't keep. Most of them slip away unless you write them down. __Typing it to me counts.__

**→ Ask Evelyn about my dreams**

— Evelyn

**P.S.** A Harvard dream researcher, Deirdre Barrett, told Scientific American that just reading about dreams can help you remember your own for a while. So tonight's might stay with you. If someone turns up in it, you know where to find me.
