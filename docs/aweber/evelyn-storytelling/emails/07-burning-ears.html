Is he thinking about me right now?

It’s one of the oldest questions in love. It turns up at the sink, at a red light, or just as you’re falling asleep.

And you can’t exactly ring him up and ask.

So since at least Roman times, people have watched for signs instead. Little ones. Mostly in their own bodies.

Here are seven of the old ones, and what people said they meant. Somebody wrote every one of them down.

1. Your ears burn. Someone’s talking about you. Nearly 2,000 years ago, a Roman writer called Pliny said everyone believed it. (For him, the ears rang.)

2. You sneeze. In China, about 1,800 years ago, a scholar wrote that when people sneezed, they’d say, “Someone is talking about me.”

3. You want to sneeze, but you can’t. An old Boston saying: someone loves you “and doesn’t dare to tell it.”

4. Your eye itches. Someone wants to see you, and can’t.

5. Your nose itches. One old version says the outside means someone out of town loves you. The inside means someone in town. (Well, that narrows it down.)

6. A hairpin slips out of your hair. Your beau is thinking of you. In the 1890s, that one was known all over America.

7. Your shoelace comes undone. An old New York rhyme: “At that very moment / Your true love thinks of you.”

Which one is yours? A list can’t tell you that.

Now read them again. Every one of them says someone. Not one of them says who.

Even the shoelace only says “your true love.” It doesn’t say which man that is.

An old Latin verse talks to its own ear about this: “Chattering ear, why do you ring at me all night? You say someone, I don’t know who, is thinking of me now.”

That’s the part you really want, isn’t it? Not “someone.” Him.

And that part isn’t in this email. It can’t be. Your sign isn’t anyone else’s sign.

Here’s what happens when a sign comes with no name. Your ear goes warm, and off you go. Is it him? Is it someone else? That’s a lot to hang on one warm ear.

Who do you hope it is?
