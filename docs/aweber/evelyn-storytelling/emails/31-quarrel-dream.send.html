What does it mean when you dream you’re fighting with him?

You wake up with your heart still racing. The fight wasn’t real. It still feels real.

A little book printed in Newcastle, England, in 1805 had an answer for that.

Today we’d ask our phones at 3 a.m. Back then, you bought a thin paper booklet.

It was called The Interpretation of Dreams and Moles.

The Interpretation of Dreams and Moles, Newcastle, 1805.
Scan: Wellcome Collection, via Project Gutenberg (public domain)

Its cover makes a big promise. Inside, it says, are “Exact Rules, whereby a Man or Woman may know if they shall obtain the one they love.”

No book has exact rules for love. But I can see why people wanted one.

Nobody signed it. A copy is kept in a library in London, and anyone can read it free online. That’s where I found it.

Here’s how it worked. You had a dream. You looked it up. There are about 70 dreams in there, one line each, and the line told you what yours meant.

Some of those lines are wonderful.

Dream your sweetheart is driving a pig? That means “he is of a headstrong disposition, and likely to prove a drunkard.”

That’s a lot to pin on one pig.

Then I found the dream so many of us wake up from.

“For lovers to dream they fall out and quarrel, signifies constancy and affection.”

Put simply: fight in the dream, and it means you’ll stay true and keep caring. The book reads a lot of dreams backwards like that.

I think the old writer was onto something. Nobody fights, even in her sleep, with someone she’s stopped caring about. A dream like that says your heart is still awake. It still wants to be heard.

Here’s where I land on that little book. I’m fond of it. It meant well.

But it gave everyone the same line. Every reader who dreamed of a fight got the same comfort. It didn’t matter who he was, or what the fight was about.

It never asked what he said in the dream. Or what you said back. Or what’s been happening while you’re awake.

One line, for every dreamer. That’s all a book has. I’d rather hear the whole thing from you.

So tell me the dream. Or skip the dream, and tell me about him. Or about the one from years ago who still shows up at night. Half-remembered is fine.
