There's one small word a lot of women are scared to say out loud.

"More."

More love than you've been getting. More money than you've been making. More life than the one you've settled for.

I've been thinking about a woman who stopped waiting for someone to say she could.

Let me tell you about her.

Her name was Sarah Breedlove. You might know her as Madam C.J. Walker.

She was born in Louisiana in 1867, to parents who had been enslaved. By 7, she was an orphan. At 20, she was a widow with a two-year-old girl, Lelia.

In St. Louis, she washed other people's clothes for about a dollar a day. And she still got Lelia through school.

Then her own hair began to fall out. So she mixed her own hair grower and sold it door to door.

She married again, to Charles J. Walker, and took a new name: Madam C.J. Walker.

Here's where things get interesting.

In 1912, she went to a big meeting of Black business owners in Chicago, run by Booker T. Washington.

She wanted to speak. For three days, he ignored her.

So she stood up anyway.

"Surely you are not going to shut the door in my face."

"I am a woman who came from the cotton fields of the South. I was promoted from there to the washtub. Then I was promoted to the cook kitchen. And from there I promoted myself into the business of manufacturing hair goods and preparations."

"I have built my own factory on my own ground."

Twice, somebody else moved her. The third time, she moved herself.

Now, here's the part I like best.

Five years later, she ran the biggest Black-owned company in America. Thousands of women sold for her, and earned their own money doing it.

The papers called her a millionaire. She wrote to her lawyer and said she wasn't.

(Honest to the last dollar.)

Nobody handed her the next step. She took it.

You don't have to decide anything today.

You don't have to know what "more" looks like yet, or how you'd get there.

And you don't need anyone's permission to want it. Not his. Not your family's. Not even mine.

I can't make anyone rich. I can't promise you'll get it. Nobody honest can.

What I can do is look at it with you: what you want, what's been in the way, and one step that's yours to take.

Some of it may take a long time. Some of it may change once you say it out loud. That's fine.

So just tell me what you want more of. More love. More money. More room to breathe. Say it the way it comes out, even if it sounds like too much.
