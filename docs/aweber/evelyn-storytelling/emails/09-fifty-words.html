The bet behind Green Eggs and Ham, and why the short question is the true one.
‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ 
 

Someone once bet Dr. Seuss $50 that he couldn’t do it.

Do what? Write a whole children’s book using only 50 different words.

The man making the bet was his own publisher, Bennett Cerf.

Seuss took it. He kept charts and checklists to track every word. When a verse didn’t work, he said, it was like unraveling a sock: “You take some of your best stuff and throw it away.”

The book was Green Eggs and Ham. It came out in 1960.

His earlier book, The Cat in the Hat, used 236 different words. By 2000, the 50-word book had outsold it in America. More than 8 million copies.

And the $50? Seuss said later that Cerf never paid up.

Here’s why I’m telling you.

When you can’t use many words, you have to use the true ones.

Love questions usually go the other way. They come out long. What he said. What he didn’t say. What your friend thinks. “I know I’m overthinking this, but…”

And the rules we hear in love? They’re dares too. The wrong kind. Play it cool. Don’t text first. Don’t ask, you’ll scare him off.

Every one of them dares you to stay quiet.

So I’m daring you to do the opposite.

Say your love question in 50 words or fewer.

Cut what happened. Cut who said what. Throw some of your best stuff away, like Seuss did. And if you get stuck, carry this one question around today:

What would I ask if I wasn’t scared of the answer?

That’s usually the real one.

That’s the whole dare. Bring me your 50 words, about him or about love in general.
