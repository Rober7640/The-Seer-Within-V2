**Big Idea:** the "your name" riddle — what belongs to you, but other people use it more than you do? — and how the way a man says your name shows how he holds you

**Reading Recap:** You opened with a riddle: what belongs to you, but other people use it more than you do? Their name. (You joked that it isn't the TV remote, though that's a good guess.) You said what you think: the way a man says your name tells you a lot about how he holds you. You asked: does he use it, or is it always "hey" or "babe" or no name at all (and said pet names can be lovely — you call people "love" all the time)? Does he only say it when he needs something — some men only shout it from the other room when they can't find their keys? Or does he say it like he's glad you walked in? For readers with no one: who does say it that way — a friend, a sister, a grandchild at the door — and how do they say it to themselves (most of us only use our own name when we've dropped something, and not kindly). You gave one honest reason it's easy to miss: we hear our name so often we stop listening to how it's said. You gave them one question to carry into the weekend: "When did someone last say your name like it was good news?" — not called up the stairs, not tacked onto a favor, said like the day just got better. You said what his way of saying it means is too personal for a letter: it's a talk, and it starts with their name. In the P.S. you turned the riddle around: his name belongs to him, but who says it more? If it's them — to a sister, to friends, to themselves at 2am — listen to how it comes out: soft, tired, careful; their voice often knows before they do. With no one right now: say your own name out loud once, kindly — it's harder than it sounds. You said minding something this small isn't silly: a name is small, being noticed isn't, and wanting it said with a little warmth isn't asking too much. You did not say what any one way of saying a name means, you did not say or hint that he doesn't love them, and you promised nothing.

**Open Loop:** You asked them to tell you their name, and how it's been said lately — by him, or by whoever says it most — and, if they like, how their own voice sounds when they say his. They don't need a "him", and they don't need to have solved the riddle.

**Continue Seed:** You came from my letter with the riddle: what belongs to you, but other people use it more than you do? Your name. So let's start there. What's yours? And who says it most these days, and how? No one right now? Then tell me how you say it to yourself.

**Content:**

**What belongs to you, but other people use it more than you do?**

Give it a moment. (It isn't the TV remote, though that's a good guess.)

**Your name.**

[IMAGE] ../assets/your-name-sketch.jpg | A pencil sketch of a woman on a quiet pavement with a shopping bag, turning back over her shoulder as if someone just called her name |  | 300 | noborder

Other people say it all day. Here's what I think about that.

**The way a man says your name tells you a lot about how he holds you.**

Does he use it? Or is it always "hey," or "babe," or no name at all? (Pet names can be lovely. I call people "love" all the time.)

Does he only say it when he needs something? Some men only shout it from the other room when they can't find their keys.

Or does he say it like he's glad you walked in?

No one saying it right now? Then think about who does say it that way. A friend. A sister. A grandchild at the door.

And how do you say it to yourself? Most of us only use our own name when we've dropped something. __And not kindly.__

Here's why it's so easy to miss. **You hear your name so often, you stop listening to how it's said.**

So take this one into your weekend:

**When did someone last say your name like it was good news?**

Not called up the stairs. Not tacked onto a favor. __Said like the day just got better.__

**Pitch:**

What his way of saying it means? That's too personal for a letter. It's a talk, and it starts with your name.

**→ Tell Evelyn your name**

— Evelyn

**P.S.** Turn the riddle around. His name belongs to him. But who says it more?

If it's you — to your sister, to your friends, to yourself at 2am — listen to how it comes out. Soft? Tired? Careful? Your voice often knows before you do.

No one right now? Say your own name out loud once, kindly. It's harder than it sounds.

If you've ever felt silly for minding something this small, please don't.

A name is a small thing. Being noticed isn't.

A lot of us go years without hearing ours said gently. We just get on with things.

__Wanting it said with a little warmth isn't asking too much.__

Never talked with me before? **The first 3 minutes won't cost you a thing.** Everyone else gets to use your name. Today, you use it.

## Notes

**Substance device: One question to carry** — "**When did someone last say your name like it was good news?**", bold, on its own line, after "So take this one into your weekend:" (true: sends Saturday), then one plain line of what "good news" sounds like ("Not called up the stairs. Works for everyone: a friend or grandchild can say it like good news. Fresh wording — not "Who dries yours?"-style, and not 13's follow-up lines.
