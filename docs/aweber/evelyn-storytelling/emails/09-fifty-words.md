**Content:**

**Someone once bet Dr. Seuss $50 that he couldn't do it.**

Do what? Write a whole children's book using only **50 different words.**

The man making the bet was his own publisher, Bennett Cerf.

Seuss took it. He kept charts and checklists to track every word. When a verse didn't work, he said, it was like unraveling a sock: "You take some of your best stuff and throw it away."

The book was Green Eggs and Ham. It came out in 1960.

His earlier book, The Cat in the Hat, used 236 different words. By 2000, the 50-word book had outsold it in America. More than 8 million copies.

And the $50? Seuss said later that Cerf never paid up.

Here's why I'm telling you.

**When you can't use many words, you have to use the true ones.**

Love questions usually go the other way. They come out long. What he said. What he didn't say. What your friend thinks. "I know I'm overthinking this, but…"

And the rules we hear in love? They're dares too. The wrong kind. Play it cool. Don't text first. Don't ask, you'll scare him off.

__Every one of them dares you to stay quiet.__

So I'm daring you to do the opposite.

**Say your love question in 50 words or fewer.**

Cut what happened. Cut who said what. Throw some of your best stuff away, like Seuss did. And if you get stuck, carry this one question around today:

**What would I ask if I wasn't scared of the answer?**

__That's usually the real one.__

New here? **The first 3 minutes are on me.** Seuss needed charts and checklists for his 50 words. You only need one question.

## Notes

**Substance device: One question to carry** — "**What would I ask if I wasn't scared of the answer?**", bold, on its own line, followed by one plain line ("That's usually the real one."). It's anchored to the story: Seuss threw away "some of your best stuff" to fit 50 words; the question is the tool for cutting her own story down to the true question. WHAT, not HOW: the email gives the dare and the question; reading her 50 words is the chat.

**Pitch shape: Columbo close** (Furey p.53 — "appear to be closing the email. But then, in the P.S. you introduce another letter. And this letter needs to be hard-hitting."). The body ends fast: one line ("That's the whole dare. Bring me your 50 words…") → text link → button → sign-off. The real pitch is the P.S., with a NEW point the body never made: 50 words are hard to read *on your own*, because you know the story you cut and start explaining again — Evelyn isn't inside it. The P.S. also carries the free-minutes line and the reason not to wait. None of the retired lines ("So here's my invitation", "Just you and me, one to one", "Then I'll tell you plainly what I see", "I won't promise…", "Type it the way you'd say it to a friend"); no ➤ list.

⚠ **Build limit:** `build-email.py` keeps only the FIRST paragraph of the P.S. (`split('\n\n')[0]`) and adds "Start here." after it. So a Columbo P.S. must be one paragraph — this one is (≈75 words). A longer, multi-paragraph Columbo P.S. would need a build change.

⚠ **Link + button back to back:** with the body ending this fast, the text link sits right above the button. That's the Columbo shape working as designed; if the operator finds it heavy, put "Fifty words won't take you long." between them.

**Format (L-dare.md):** a true moment where someone dared the wrong thing — Cerf bet on "can't" (Furey's bowling partner daring a gutter ball) → Seuss did it anyway, and the 50-word book outsold the 236-word one → the love turn: the rules we hear in love are dares too, the wrong kind ("Every one of them dares you to stay quiet") → "So I'm daring you to do the opposite." → the dare → the question to carry → pitch.

**CTA:** text link "Evelyn, I'll take your dare" — first person, **no precondition** (she takes the dare *in* the chat; nothing to do first, works for a reader who has no one: "about him or about love in general"). Button "Ask Evelyn in 50 words". Free minutes "New here? The first 3 minutes are on me." (in the P.S.). Reason not to wait = a true callback (Seuss needed charts and checklists; you need one question) — no deadline, no scarcity. The auto "Start here." after the P.S. is the P.S. link.
