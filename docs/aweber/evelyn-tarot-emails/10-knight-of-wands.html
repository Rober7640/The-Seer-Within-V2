There are two kinds of flame, and only one of them keeps you warm.
‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ 
 

I drew a card for you this morning, dear — the one that arrives like a struck match, all heat and hurry. Women wait their whole lives for this man. So let me show you the warning painted right into his fire.

A knight charges across the desert on a horse the color of flame, dear — and where the other knight of the cups came at a slow, level walk, this one is all rear and lunge, the horse up on its hind legs, mane flying, barely held. His armor is bright, a plume streams from his helmet, and salamanders — the little fire-lizards of the old stories — run across his yellow coat. In his hand he lifts a wooden staff, and look: it isn't dead wood. Green leaves are sprouting from it. Behind him, three pale pyramids and a sky like a furnace.

This is the Knight of Wands. The card of fire on horseback — passion, adventure, charisma, the man who sweeps into a room and tilts it. This is the one who makes your stomach drop, dear. The exciting one. And I want you to have his card in your hand before you meet him, because the very thing that thrills you about him is the thing you'll need to read most carefully.

Look first at the horse, dear. Every other rider in the deck sits a steady mount. This one rides a rear — the animal up on its back legs, forelegs pawing the air. It's thrilling to watch and it is barely under control. That's the Knight of Wands' gift and his flaw in a single image: enormous fire, not yet steadied by a hand. He can gallop toward you magnificently. He can also gallop off just as fast, the moment the next horizon catches his eye.

But look at his staff, dear, because the card is fair to him too. It's sprouting. Living wood, new leaves. His fire is creative — it makes things grow, it lights up everyone near it, it is genuinely wonderful to be warmed by. Passion is not the villain here. The card is not telling you to go find a cold, safe man. It's telling you the one true question to ask of any fire: is it a flare, or is it a hearth?

Because a flare, dear, is glorious — it lights the whole sky for one breathtaking evening — and by morning it is cold ash and you can't think why you're shivering. A hearth is far less dramatic. A hearth is fed. Daily, dully, deliberately, one ordinary log at a time. The mistake women make with the Knight of Wands is reading the size of the first blaze as proof of the staying of the heat. They are not the same thing, dear. They have almost nothing to do with each other.

And if there's a man setting you alight right now, dear, and you can't tell whether he's a flare or a hearth — tell me about him, and we'll read his fire together.

For the one waiting for him to come: here's the gift and the warning, dear. You may be holding out for exactly this knight — the sweep, the fireworks, the man who makes the floor drop. Wonderful. But ask yourself what you actually want to come home to on a wet Tuesday in year three. Fireworks are for the first night. Warmth is for the long life. Don't disqualify a good, steady flame for the crime of not exploding. And when the dazzling one does ride in, watch one thing above all: does his fire warm you, or does it only ever perform for a crowd?

For the one unsure of the man she has: ask where his fire is really aimed, dear — at you, or at the chase. The Knight of Wands can be dazzling all through the pursuit and go strangely cold the moment he's won you, because the fire was for the hunt, not for the woman. Watch what happens after he has you. A heat that cools the instant it's secure was a flare, dear, not a hearth. And no amount of remembering how bright it once burned will warm a room he's already galloped out of.

For the one afraid to want passion again: don't let one flare that scorched you teach you that all fire is dangerous, dear. It isn't. The lesson of this knight was never avoid the heat. It was tend it, and choose a man who can. You are allowed to want to feel alive again. You'll simply watch, this time, for the one whose flame is steady as well as bright — who can light up a party and bank the coals for an ordinary night at home.

So here's your small thing tonight, dear — the two-fire test, and you can run it on anyone. Bring to mind whatever is lighting you up right now: a man, a hope, even a new plan. Ask it two plain questions. One: does it still burn on a dull Tuesday, with nobody watching? Two: does it warm me, or does it only excite me? Flare, or hearth. Sit with the honest answers. They'll tell you exactly what you're holding — and whether to feed it or let it burn down safely.

Tomorrow I'll turn over the Seven of Cups, dear — seven shining cups floating in the clouds, each one promising something, and the strange, hard task of closing your hand on just one. We'll talk about fantasy and the real thing. But tonight, the Knight. Flare, or hearth?

And the man who's set you alight, dear — or the one you're still waiting to feel that first spark from — if you can't tell the flare from the hearth on your own, tell me what's on your mind, and we'll take it from there. We'll read his fire together — your free minutes are still waiting.

— Evelyn
