I drew a card for you this morning, dear — the warmest one in the whole deck, and one of the most quietly dangerous. It's the card of the past reaching back for you. And I want to show you both sides of it.

A warm, golden courtyard in an old village, dear. A bigger child leans down and hands a smaller one a cup brimming with white, five-petaled flowers, and more flower-filled cups stand about the yard. Everything is soft light and safety and long-ago; you can almost smell it — a childhood summer, the safest, sweetest place your memory keeps. And there, in the background, a single figure is walking away down the lane, out of the frame. Hold onto that figure. We'll come back to it.

This is the Six of Cups. The card of memory — of nostalgia, of childhood's sweetness, of old loves and old friends resurfacing, of a gift handed tenderly across the years. It is the warmest card in the deck, dear, and one of the most quietly dangerous, and tonight I want you to see both at once.

First, the gift is real, dear — let's not pretend otherwise. There's a genuine sweetness in memory: the tenderness of who once loved you, the innocence you've still got tucked away somewhere, the good that truly did happen and can never be taken back. Those white flowers, handed cup to cup across the years, are a real gift. Honoring your past is not a weakness. Some of the best of you was planted back there in that golden courtyard.

But now that figure walking out of the frame, dear. The card marks an exit on purpose. The past is a place to visit — not a place to move back into and live. You can stand in the courtyard and be warmed by it; you cannot take up residence there. And the whole art of this card is being able to feel the sweetness without mistaking it for an address.

Because here is the danger, dear, and it comes most often when an old love resurfaces — the ex who messages “I was just thinking of you,” the one who got away suddenly back at your door. In that moment the Six of Cups is the card to hold very carefully. Because you will not be remembering the real man. You'll be remembering the golden, five-petaled, soft-lit version of him — with every reason it ended quietly cropped out of the frame. Nostalgia is a liar that only ever tells the sweet lies. It keeps the flowers, dear, and it throws the thorns away where you can't see them.

And if someone from before has been on your mind lately, dear — a voice from the past reaching back, or a memory you can't quite put down — tell me about the one from before, and we'll look at the whole picture, thorns and all, together.

For the one an old love has resurfaced to: dear, if a voice from the past has reached back for you, the Six of Cups is asking you to be careful with the sweetness. You are not remembering him — you're remembering the golden version, the good afternoons with the bad years neatly cut away. So before you step back into that warm old courtyard, make yourself do the one thing nostalgia never will: remember, honestly, why you walked out of it in the first place. If those reasons have genuinely, truly changed — wonderful, and go gently. But if you're simply lonely and the past happens to be warm, dear, that isn't a reunion. That's an escape wearing a familiar face.

For the one waiting for love to come: the Six of Cups warns the waiting woman about living backward, dear. When the present feels thin, it's so tempting to move into the past — to keep an old relationship alive in your head, to measure every new man against a memory polished to a shine no living person could ever match. But the figure is walking out of the frame for a reason. You cannot meet anyone new while you're still living in an old courtyard. Visit the good memories; be warmed by them; then come back out into the present, dear, because the present is the only place the actual love is allowed to happen.

For the one grieving a loss: for you, dear, the Six of Cups is mostly gift, and I won't muddy it. When you've lost someone, the sweetness of remembering isn't a trap — it's a comfort, and a kind of love that outlasts the loss itself. So let yourself visit the golden courtyard of who they were and what you shared. Hand yourself the cup of white flowers. Sit in the warm light of them as long as you need. Only keep one small thing in view: the gate swings both ways, dear, and there is a whole present still out there, waiting patiently for you to walk back out to it.

So here's your small thing tonight, dear — visit, but don't move in. Bring to mind one sweet memory: a person, a time, a love from before. Let yourself truly go there for a few minutes — the light, the warmth, the good of it — and honor it, because it was real. But then, deliberately, do the honest thing nostalgia refuses to do: name one true reason it belongs to the past. Not to spoil the sweetness, dear. To keep it in its rightful place — a courtyard you can visit and be warmed by, and then walk back out of, into the life that's actually yours now.

Tomorrow I'll turn over Strength, dear — the card of the woman who tames a great lion not by force but with a bare, gentle hand, and what that teaches about the softest and strongest kind of power there is. But tonight, the Six. Visit the past. Don't move back in.

And the one from before who keeps surfacing, dear — if you can't tell whether it's a real second chance or just the past being warm on a lonely night — tell me what's on your mind, and we'll take it from there. It helps to see the thorns and the flowers both, with someone beside you — your free minutes are still waiting.
