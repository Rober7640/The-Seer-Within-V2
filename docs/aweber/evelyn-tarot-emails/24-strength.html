The most powerful woman in the deck tames a lion with her bare, gentle hands.

I drew a card for you this morning, dear — and it isn't a warrior or a fortress. It's a woman with flowers in her hair and a great lion gone calm under her bare hands. She knows a secret the world keeps getting wrong about strength.

A woman in a white gown stands calmly beside a great tawny lion, dear. A chain of flowers is looped at her waist, and above her head hovers a soft figure-eight of light, like a halo laid gently on its side. And with her bare hands — no weapon, no whip, no cage anywhere in sight — she is closing the lion's jaws; or perhaps soothing them; and the lion is letting her. It doesn't cower, and it doesn't rage. It is simply, unmistakably, at peace beneath her quiet hands. Behind the two of them stands a single mountain.

This is Strength. And notice, dear, that it isn't a knight, or a soldier, or a high stone wall. It's a woman with flowers in her hands and a lion gone quiet at her side. Because the deck knows a thing the whole world keeps getting backwards: the strongest force there has ever been was never force at all.

Look at her bare hands, dear. She masters this enormous animal with no violence whatsoever. That is the entire argument of the card: real strength is not hardness. Anyone can pick up a whip; anyone can go cold and clenched and call it being tough. It takes the rare, deep kind of strength to gentle a wild thing with calm alone. The woman who has learned to steady herself with a soft hand is far, far stronger than the one who has simply gone hard.

And that lion, dear — it isn't some enemy out there in the world. It's the wild thing in you. The 3 a.m. fear. The sudden flash of jealousy. The panic that claws up your throat. The desperate ache that would make you do almost anything not to be alone. And look what she does with it: she doesn't slay it, doesn't cage it, doesn't pretend it isn't there. She lays a gentle hand on it, and it settles. Because you cannot force yourself calm, dear. You cannot bully your own fear into silence — it only roars the louder. But you can gentle it, exactly the way she does: with patience, with a steady hand, with kindness toward the frightened animal inside you.

See the soft figure-eight of light above her, too. Her strength isn't a single heroic burst — it's a steady, renewable, patient thing that goes round and round and never runs dry. Not the strength of one grand effort, dear, but of the quiet daily gentling. And that is the only kind that actually lasts a lifetime.

And if there's a lion you've been trying to fight lately — a fear or an ache you keep wrestling and can't seem to beat down — tell me the lion you've been fighting, and we'll learn to gentle it instead.

For the one worn out from being “strong”: dear, you've been told your whole life that strong means hard — chin up, feel nothing, need no one, carry it all without a single sound. And it has exhausted you, because that was never strength; it's armor, and armor is heavy, and it rusts, and it keeps everyone out including the ones you love. Now look at the real thing: a woman with flowers in her hair and a soft hand on a lion. Her power is precisely that she doesn't have to go hard to stay steady. You are allowed to set the armor down, dear. The gentle open hand holds far more than the clenched fist ever could.

For the one waiting for him to come: here's what Strength hands the waiting woman, dear — power over the one thing you can actually control. You can't force him to arrive, or hurry the timing, or bend the outcome; that lion belongs to the wide world, and grabbing at it only gets you clawed. But the panic in your own chest — the fear that whispers it's too late, the ache that would have you settle for anything just to stop being alone — that lion is yours to gentle. Lay a calm hand on it. A woman who has tamed her own fear waits from strength, not from desperation. And a woman like that never once settles for a scrap out of panic.

For the one unsure of the man she has: Strength asks how you both handle the lion when it wakes between you, dear — the argument, the jealousy, the sudden flare of hurt. Do you reach for force — sharpen your words, slam the door, make him pay? Or can you meet it with the steady hand? And just as important: can he? Watch, in the next hard moment, whether the two of you can gentle the lion together, or whether one of you always grabs for the whip. A love where both people carry a little of this card can survive nearly anything. A love where the lions run loose cannot.

So here's your small thing tonight, dear — name your lion, then lay a hand on it. Name the wild thing that's been loudest in you lately: the fear, the anger, the grasping ache, the panic. Don't fight it, and don't pretend it away — both only make a lion roar. Instead, do the Strength thing: speak to it, gently, the way you'd calm a frightened animal — I know, I know. It's alright. I've got you. Say it to your own fear, out loud, with a hand laid flat on your own chest if you like. It sounds strange, dear. It works. It is the softest thing a woman can do, and the strongest — and it is the whole of this card.

Tomorrow I'll turn over the King of Cups, dear — the emotionally steady man, the one who sits calm on his throne in the middle of a rough sea, and what real maturity actually looks like in a partner. But tonight, Strength. Find your lion, and lay a gentle hand on it.

And if you've been holding it all together by force so long you've forgotten there's another way, dear — if the armor has simply gotten too heavy — tell me what's on your mind, and we'll take it from there. Even the strongest woman gets to set the lion down with someone for a while — your free minutes are still waiting.
