I drew a card for you this morning, dear — the great turning wheel. And I turned it up because I think you've begun to believe this long, waiting season is the permanent shape of your life. It isn't. The wheel is turning even now.

A great wheel hangs in the sky, ringed with old letters and symbols, turning slowly on its hub. A calm sphinx rides at the very top of it. On one side, a serpent slides downward; on the other, a jackal-headed figure rises up. And in the four corners of the card, four winged creatures sit quietly reading, entirely unbothered by all the spinning. The whole picture is motion caught inside a frame: something sinking on the one side, something climbing on the other, and the great wheel turning, turning, the way it always has and always will.

This is the Wheel of Fortune. The card of cycles and timing — of fate turning, of seasons changing, of the plain truth that nothing in a life stays fixed at the bottom, or the top, for very long. And I turned it up for you, dear, because I suspect you've been living as though this low, thin, waiting season were the permanent shape of your whole life. The Wheel has come to remind you that it turns. It has always turned. It is turning right now, under you, whether you can feel it yet or not.

Here is the one thing this card knows for certain: nothing holds still. The season you're in is a position on a wheel — not the shape of the ground beneath you forever. Women in a long wait slowly come to believe the drought is simply how things are now, and always will be. And the Wheel says: no. What goes down comes back up. That isn't a wish or a comfort, dear — it's the mechanism. You are not stuck. You are at a point on a wheel that is, this very moment, mid-turn.

See the rising figure and the falling one — one sinking, one climbing, both at the very same time. Endings and beginnings are the same single motion. The thing that left made the room for the thing that's rising. When you're on the down-slope you cannot yet see the up — but it is built into the very same wheel, turning toward you even as the other side turns away.

And those four calm creatures in the corners, quietly reading while everything spins? Notice what steadies a person while the wheel turns, dear. They're the still point in all that motion. And you need your own version of them — the steady practices, the constant self that holds firm while your fortunes rise and fall around it. You cannot stop the wheel. But you can be the calm reader in the corner while it turns, instead of the one clinging white-knuckled to the rim, flung up and down and sick with every spin.

And if there's a season you're quietly afraid will never turn, dear — a low stretch that's started to feel like the permanent weather of your life — tell me the season you're afraid won't turn, and I'll help you find where the wheel's already moving.

For the one waiting for him to come: the Wheel gives the waiting woman the one thing the waiting steals — perspective, dear. When you've waited a long while, this thin season starts to feel like the permanent climate of your entire life. It is not. It's a position on a wheel, and wheels turn; that is the only thing they know how to do. Your task in the low season isn't to seize the rim and force it round — you'll only wear yourself out and get flung about. It's to be the calm reader in the corner: keep living, keep your wish named, stay whole. The turn comes on its own schedule, dear, never on yours — and it comes most kindly for the woman who used the wait to become someone steady, instead of someone frantic.

For the one at a low ebb: if you're at the very bottom of the wheel right now, dear, hear the one mercy this card was built to give — the bottom is not a resting place. It's the point of the sharpest turn. No one stays at the bottom, because the very same motion that carried you down there is already, this very moment, beginning to carry you back up. You simply can't feel it yet, from where you're sitting. What is down comes up. That is not me softening things for you, dear. That is the plain mechanism of the whole card.

For the one unsure of the man she has: the Wheel asks you to watch the cycles between you, dear — because every couple has them, the closeness and the distance turning round and round. One cool season is not a verdict; even good loves dip and rise. But look at the pattern across many turns. Does the wheel keep bringing you back around to warmth, to repair, to the two of you finding each other again? Or does each turn leave you a little lower than the last — the good seasons shorter, the cold ones longer? One turn tells you almost nothing, dear. The shape of many turns tells you very nearly everything.

So here's your small thing tonight, dear — find the still point. Name honestly where you feel you are on the wheel just now: near the bottom, mid-turn, climbing, unsure. Just name the position, without pretending it's higher or lower than it truly is. Then name one thing that stays constant for you no matter which way the wheel turns — one steady practice, one anchor, one part of yourself the seasons simply don't touch. That, dear, is your calm reader in the corner. Stand on it. The wheel will turn whether you clutch the rim or not — so let go of the rim, and keep your feet on the still point.

Tomorrow I'll turn over the Hanged Man, dear — the strange, still card of the one who hangs upside down on purpose, and what he learned by choosing to stop struggling and simply see the whole thing differently. But tonight, the Wheel. It turns, dear. It's turning now.

And if this season has gone on so long you've stopped believing it can turn, dear — if the wheel feels stuck fast at the bottom — tell me what's on your mind, and we'll take it from there. Sometimes another pair of eyes can see the turn already beginning — your free minutes are still waiting.
