The card of the spilled wine keeps two secrets standing right behind you.
‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ 
 

I drew a card for you this morning, dear — and after yesterday's raised cups, this one shows some of them fallen. But look closely, because grief is hiding something from you in this picture.

A figure stands alone in a long black cloak, dear, head bowed, shoulders curled inward. At the feet, three cups have tipped over, and their wine runs out dark across the ground. The whole body is bent toward that spill — the cloak drawn close, the face turned down, unable to look away from what has been lost.

But look behind the figure, dear. Behind, still upright, still full, stand two more cups. And past them a river winds, and over the river there's a bridge, and across the bridge a house waits with its lights on. The way home is right there in the picture. The figure simply cannot see it — because grief will not let the head turn around.

This is the Five of Cups. The card of loss, of mourning, of the spilled thing you can't stop staring at. It is the most honest card in the deck about grief, dear — and also the most quietly hopeful, once you understand what it's actually drawn to show you.

Start with that black cloak. Grief wraps you like a cloak, dear — it narrows the whole world down to the small circle of ground at your feet. That's not a weakness in you; it's what loss does to a pair of eyes. When something spills that mattered, you cannot see anything else for a while. The card doesn't scold you for that. It painted the cloak with tenderness.

But now count the cups, dear — because the card chose this number on purpose. Not all five fell. Three spilled. Two are still standing, upright and full, right behind you. Grief is not a liar — the three really did tip over, and no one gets to rush you past that. But grief is terribly near-sighted. It can only see what's on the ground. It cannot, on its own, turn the head to count what's still standing. That turn is the one thing the card is asking of you. Not today, maybe. But it's asking.

And that bridge across the river, dear, with the house and its lit windows? The card promises there is a way back — an ordinary, walkable way, over a real bridge, to a life still warm and waiting. Not a leap. Not a cure. A bridge. You cross it one plank at a time, whenever you're ready, and the first plank is only this: turning your head.

And if you've been bowed over the same spill for a long time now, dear — if you can't yet make yourself look up at what's left — tell me what spilled, and I'll help you find the two cups still standing.

For the one grieving a loss: this card was drawn for you tonight, dear, and the first thing it wants to say is that your grief is not too much. Three cups really fell. Honor them. But when you're ready — not a moment before — hear the second thing: the healing was never in staring harder at the stain. It's in the small turn of the head. Behind you there are two cups still full — a person who still loves you, a door still open, one good thing the loss did not take. Name them. That naming is you beginning to turn.

For the one unsure of the love she's in: ask yourself an honest question, dear — are you grieving the man in front of you, or the man you hoped he'd become? Sometimes we stand so long mourning the three spilled cups of who he was supposed to be that we can't see the two real cups of who he actually is, still standing and still full. Some men are worth turning toward. Just be sure you're weeping for a real person and not a picture you painted.

For the one afraid she'll never love again: do not let the spill convince you the cups are all gone, dear. They aren't. Two are still standing behind you, and the deck is stubborn about this: love did not drain out of the world when it drained out of that one relationship. It's behind you, upright, waiting for the turn of your head. You are not empty-handed. You are just facing the wrong way.

So here's your small thing tonight, dear — the turn of the head, done gently. First, name one cup that spilled. Say it out loud, and don't dress it up: this is what I lost. Honor it for one full breath. Then deliberately turn — and name two things still standing in your life this very night. Two. A person, a comfort, a door, a small mercy. Say of them, plainly: and these are still full. You have not betrayed the three by counting the two. You have simply, at last, turned your head.

Tomorrow I'll turn over the Knight of Wands, dear — all fire and forward motion, the card of the man, and the moment, that arrives like a struck match. We'll talk about the passion that lasts and the kind that only flares. But tonight, the Five. Turn your head. Count the two.

And whatever spilled for you, dear — if you've been standing over it a long while with your back to everything else — tell me what's on your mind, and we'll take it from there. We'll turn your head together, and count what's still standing — your free minutes are still waiting.

— Evelyn
