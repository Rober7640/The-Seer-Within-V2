I drew a card for you this morning, dear — and after the dark and the deep water of the last few days, this one throws the clouds wide open. It's the plainest joy in the whole deck. And it comes as a promise.

A great golden sun fills the whole sky, dear, its face open and calm, its rays streaming straight down and warm. Beneath it, a small naked child rides a white horse through a garden — no saddle, no reins gripped in fear, arms flung wide open, a red banner streaming overhead and a wreath of flowers in the hair. Along the wall behind, tall sunflowers have every one of them turned their faces up to the light. The sky is a clear, cloudless blue. There is not a single shadow in the entire card. Nothing here is hidden. Everything is simply, finally, lit.

This is the Sun. The deck's most uncomplicated joy — the card where the clouds break, the fog burns clean off, and there is nothing left standing in shadow. And after all the dark and the rain of these last cards, dear, I turned it up for you as a plain promise: the confusion clears. The heaviness lifts. The light comes back. It always, always comes back.

This is the deck's deliberate answer to the Moon, dear — the card we drew only days ago, where everything was silver and uncertain and you couldn't trust a thing you saw. Here, it's full sun. Everything is exactly the color it truly is — nothing exaggerated, nothing looming, nothing hidden. The Sun is the card of finally seeing clearly. And you'll notice how different the very same things look now: the shapes that terrified you in the moonlight turn out, in daylight, to be so much smaller and so much kinder than the dark insisted.

See the child, dear — naked, unafraid, arms wide. The Sun returns you to a kind of innocence: not foolishness, but the clean, unguarded gladness you were sure heartbreak had taken from you for good. The child is bare and unashamed because in full sun there is nothing left to hide and nothing left to fear. And that is the exact promise this card makes to a grieving woman: you will feel light again. Not heavy. Not braced. Not endlessly managing yourself. Light — the way you were before. It comes back.

And the sunflowers along the wall, all turned to the light? Joy is partly a turning, dear. The sunflowers don't manufacture the sun; they simply turn their faces toward it when it comes. So on the days the light returns, your only task is to turn toward it — to let yourself feel the good thing, sit in the warm patch, say yes to the small happiness — instead of staying turned to the wall out of old, grief-worn habit. The sun will do its part. Yours is only the turning.

And if there's something you'd give anything to finally see clearly, dear — a fog you've been squinting through for far too long — tell me what you'd love to see clearly, and we'll walk it out into the daylight together.

For the one coming out of grief: this is your card tonight, dear, and it comes as a plain, unhedged promise — the heaviness lifts. Perhaps not all at once, but it lifts. There will be an ordinary morning, sooner than you dare believe, when the light comes through the window and for one moment you feel nothing bad at all. Just warm. Just here. Just alive. And when that moment comes, dear, do not distrust it, and do not feel one flicker of guilt for it. That is not you forgetting your grief. That is the Sun coming back over the wall, right on schedule, exactly the way it was always going to.

For the one waiting for him to come: the Sun asks you, plainly, to stop waiting in the shade, dear. So many women pause their whole lives in a grey half-light — saving the joy, the trips, the good dishes, the full aliveness for the day he arrives to switch the sun on for them. But the sun is already up, dear. It has been all along. Go and stand in it now. Turn your face to your own good life the way the sunflowers do. A woman already standing in the sun is so much easier for love to find than one waiting in the shadows for someone to come and light her.

For the one unsure of the man she has: let the Sun burn off the fog, dear. You've been squinting at him in the Moon's half-light a long while, and the Sun's whole gift is clarity — which is kind, even when it isn't comfortable. So ask the plain daylight question, with no drama attached: in the clear, warm light of an ordinary day, does being with him feel like this card — open, unguarded, nothing to hide, arms wide? Or do you feel yourself reaching for the shadows, carefully managing what you let him see? The Sun does not lie, dear. And whatever it shows you in that honest light, you can trust.

So here's your small thing tomorrow, dear — go and find the warm patch. Sometime in the daylight, step out and physically stand in the sun for two whole minutes — a doorway, a window, the yard, wherever the light falls. Turn your actual face up to it, the way a sunflower does, and feel the warmth land on your skin. And while you stand there, name one thing out loud that is genuinely good in your life right now. That's the entire practice, dear. You're teaching a heart that's been living in the dark how to turn back toward the light — and it learns fast, the moment you remind it the light is really there.

Tomorrow I'll turn over the Four of Cups, dear — the card of the woman so lost in her own discontent that she doesn't notice the cup being quietly offered right at her elbow. We'll talk about the gift you're about to miss. But tonight, the Sun. Go and turn your face to the light.

And if the light feels a long way off tonight, dear — if you can't quite believe the heaviness will ever lift — tell me what's on your mind, and we'll take it from there. Even the longest grey stretch breaks eventually — and it's easier to wait for the sun with someone — your free minutes are still waiting.

— Evelyn
