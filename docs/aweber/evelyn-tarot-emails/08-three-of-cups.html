I drew a card for you this morning, dear — and after all that careful watching of one man's hands yesterday, this one lifts your eyes off him entirely. Onto the women standing in a ring around you.

Three women stand together in a ring, dear, in an open field at the close of summer. Each has lifted a golden cup high, and at the center of the circle the three cups meet and touch. Their robes are the colors of the season — one white, one red, one the gold of ripe wheat. There are flowers wound through their hair. One has half-turned to another, caught mid-laugh. And at their feet the ground is heavy with the harvest: pumpkins and gourds swelling in the grass, a vine gone loose with fruit. They are not waiting for the celebration to begin. They are the celebration.

This is the Three of Cups. The card of joy shared — of the harvest of the heart, of gratitude poured out loud, of the women who hold you while you dance and hold you again when you can't. And I turned it up for you on purpose today, dear, because I think you may have been trying to carry a whole heart on the strength of one relationship alone. Or on the strength of one you're still waiting for. This card has come to remind you how many hands were always meant to hold it.

Start with the number, dear. One cup is a single feeling. Two cups — the card of a few days ago — is a bond struck between two people. But three is the moment the feeling overflows the couple and spills out into the wider world: into friendship, into a circle, into a whole life that doesn't hang on one man's yes or no. Love was never meant to be a sealed jar kept between two people. In this card it has burst its lid and become a party.

Then look down at their feet, dear, at all that fruit. The joy in this card is a harvest — and a harvest is never luck. It's what you reap in autumn from what you planted in spring. The women who ring you now are the crop of a hundred small kindnesses you've sown over the years: the calls you made, the soup you carried over, the times you showed up. Nobody is handed a circle. It's grown. Which is the good news, dear — because anything grown can be planted again, however thin the field looks this year.

And notice the shape they've made: a ring. A circle has no head of the table and no foot — no one above, no one left out at the end. You are held on every side at once. It's very hard to fall down inside a circle, dear. There is always a hand.

And if you can't right now name three women who'd close a ring around you, dear — or if the ring you once had has thinned out over a few hard years — tell me who's left, in whatever words come, and we'll start rebuilding it.

For the one unsure of the man she's with: watch what he does to your circle, dear. The right love widens it — he learns your friends' names, he sends you off to dinner with your sister and is glad you come home full. The wrong one, quietly, thins it: always a reason the evening was a bad idea, always a coolness afterward, until one day you look up and the ring is just him. A man who needs you to have no one but him has told you something, and it isn't love. Real love adds cups to the table. It never quietly takes them away.

For the one waiting for him to come: these three women did not postpone the dance, dear. They didn't stand at the edge of the field with folded arms, saving the good wine for when the men arrived. They raised the cups they had, with the people they had, in the season they were in — and that, not the waiting, is what makes a woman magnetic. Fill your own life so full that a good man feels lucky to be handed a place in the ring. The full life is the lure, dear. The saved-up, on-hold one never was.

For the one afraid she'll be alone: look again at the ring and count, dear. There is not a single man in this card. The love holding these three up is not romance at all — it's the fierce, ordinary devotion of women to one another, and it is real love, and it has carried more hearts through more long winters than any marriage ever has. You are not one man away from being loved. You are already inside a circle, if you'll let the women in it close the gap. So call one. Let her hold a cup for you tonight.

So here's your small thing tonight, dear — name your three. Sit for one quiet minute and bring to mind three women, living or gone, who have ever made you feel held: a friend, a sister, a mother, the neighbor who checked on you when no one asked her to. Say their three names out loud. Then take the nearest one still living, and send her one message tonight — not a catch-up, not a favor, just I was thinking of you, and I'm glad you're mine. That message is you raising a cup, dear. That is exactly how the ring gets kept.

Tomorrow I'll turn over the Five of Cups, dear — the card of the spilled wine, of grief standing with its back turned to everything it still has. We'll talk about the cups that fall over... and the two that are always, always still standing. But tonight, the Three. Name your women, and raise a cup.

And if your ring has gone quiet, dear — if the women who once held you have drifted, or the losses have left the circle smaller than it used to be — tell me what's on your mind, and we'll take it from there. Joy is a harvest, dear, and a harvest can always be replanted — your free minutes are still waiting.

— Evelyn
