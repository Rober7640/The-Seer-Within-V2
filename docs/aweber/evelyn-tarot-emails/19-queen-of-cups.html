I drew a card for you this morning, dear — the wisest feeler in the whole deck. She sits right at the water's edge, feeling everything there is to feel, and she never once goes under. That's the trick I want to teach you.

A queen sits on a great stone throne at the very edge of the sea, dear, right where the water meets the shore. In both hands she holds a golden cup — and hers is the only cup in the whole deck with a lid, its two handles shaped like little angels. She gazes down into it, absorbed, tender, lost in it almost. Her throne is carved with sea-children and shells, and the tide laps close. But look at her feet: they rest on the solid, pebbled shore. Not in the water. At its very edge — and on the rock.

This is the Queen of Cups. The great tender queen of the whole watery suit — the master of feeling, of compassion, of intuition; the woman who can feel absolutely everything there is to feel. And she's come tonight, dear, to teach the one lesson a deep-feeling woman most needs, and least wants to hear: how to love with your whole heart, and still not drown in it.

Look at her feet again, dear, because that is the entire card. She is the queen of the sea of feeling — and she does not live in the water. She sits at its edge, on solid rock, feet dry. She can reach into the deep whenever she pleases; she can also stand up and walk away from it whenever she needs to. That, right there, is the whole difference between feeling deeply and being swept away. You are allowed to feel the entire ocean, dear. You were never required to live underneath it.

Now the lid on her cup — the only lidded cup in the deck. She holds her feelings; she treasures them, gazes into them, knows them intimately. But she has a lid, and she chooses when to lift it, and for whom. That is not coldness, dear. It's sovereignty. The woman who pours her whole open cup out to every man by the third dinner isn't being more loving than the Queen — she's being unguarded, and those are very different things. A lid is not a wall. It's a hand on your own cup, deciding.

And see that she gazes into the cup, dear — not out at the horizon. She looks into her own feelings to understand them; she's fluent in her own heart. She isn't scanning the sea for a ship to come and rescue her. She reads her own depths first — and a woman who can read her own depths is almost impossible to fool about anyone else's.

And if you honestly can't tell right now whether what you feel for him is love or an undertow, dear — whether you're on the rock or in over your head — tell me how it actually feels, and we'll find your footing together.

For the one unsure of the man she has: here's why you can't tell if it's right, dear — you're in the water, over your head, and no one thinks clearly underwater. Everything down there is intensity: the highs feel like proof, the lows feel like dying, and you've lost your own footing completely. The Queen's cure is simple and hard both — climb back onto the rock. Love him from the shore a while: feet on solid ground, close enough to feel it all, far enough to still think. Only from there can you tell a deep, clear love from a desperate undertow. And if stepping onto the rock makes the whole thing feel like it might vanish, dear — that was never love. That was drowning.

For the one who loves too hard: you were praised your whole life for how much you feel, dear — for giving everything, holding nothing back, loving until there's no you left — and no one ever warned you that a woman with no lid and no shoreline eventually goes under, and takes her own name down with her. The Queen is not one drop less loving for her lid and her dry feet. She is more — because she has kept enough of herself intact to still be standing there, year after year, for the ones she loves. Learn her lid. Learn her rock. It's the most loving thing you will ever do, for them and for you.

For the one afraid to feel again: the Queen has a gentle word for you as well, dear. You're frightened that to love again is simply to drown again — so you've backed all the way up the beach, out of the water entirely, feeling nothing on purpose because nothing is safer. But she doesn't ask you back into the deep. She only asks you to sit at its edge. You can feel again without being pulled under, dear. The secret was never to flee the water. It was to keep your feet on the rock while you let it wash, warm and harmless, over them.

So here's your small thing tonight, dear — go and find your rock. Bring to mind the person or the situation you feel most powerfully about right now. Notice honestly: are you on the shore, or in the water? Can you still think clearly about it, or does it pull you under the instant you touch it? Then do one small thing to put your feet back on solid ground — name aloud one part of your life that is entirely your own and has nothing to do with him at all. Your work, a friend, a joy, a plan of your own. Stand on that for one full minute. That, dear, is your rock. From now on, love from there.

Tomorrow I'll turn over the Sun, dear — the deck's most uncomplicated joy, the card where the clouds finally break and there is nothing at all left hidden. We'll stand in the warm light a while. But tonight, the Queen. Feel it all — and keep your feet on the rock.

And if you've lost your footing in him, dear — if loving has started to feel like going under instead of coming home — tell me what's on your mind, and we'll take it from there. Sometimes it takes a second pair of eyes to find the rock again — your free minutes are still waiting.
