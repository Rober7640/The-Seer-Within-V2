I drew a card for you this morning, dear — and after the dark of yesterday, this one breaks into full daylight and throws a rainbow across the sky. It's the brightest promise in the deck. But it has a job for you.

A man and a woman stand together on green grass, dear, arms lifted to the sky, her hand resting easy at his waist. And above them the heavens have thrown a great rainbow made of ten golden cups, arching clean across the world from one edge to the other. Beside them, two small children dance with their hands joined, spinning for the sheer joy of it. On a low hill there's a house. A river runs by; there are green trees, and an open, cloudless day. Everything in the picture is settled, and warm, and whole.

This is the Ten of Cups. The brightest promise in the entire deck — the card of lasting love, of family, of the whole picture actually come true. Not the thrill of the beginning, dear, but the deep, settled happiness of a life two people built together and kept. And I turned it up for you tonight for one reason: so you can see, plainly and in daylight, what you're really waiting for. So you never again mistake a fragment of it for the whole.

Look first at that rainbow, dear. A rainbow only ever appears after the rain — and there's been rain enough in these cards: the spilled Five, the pierced hearts still to come. The Ten is what waits on the far side of the weather. But notice it hangs up in the sky — it's a vision, an aim, a target held above the ordinary ground. It isn't describing your particular Tuesday. It's showing you where to walk. And you cannot walk toward a target you've never once let yourself look at.

Then the count, dear. This whole suit began with the Ace — a single cup, overflowing, the first sip of feeling. Now here we are at ten, the full number, the feeling grown all the way up into a shared life: the partner, the children, the house on the hill, the rainbow overhead. This is what love is actually for. Not the spark on its own — we watched that flare and gutter with the fiery knight — but the spark grown into a hearth, and the hearth grown into a whole warm home.

And here's the detail most people miss, dear. The two of them aren't gazing into each other's eyes. They're standing side by side, shoulders nearly touching, looking the same direction — up, out, at something larger than the pair of them. That is the old, quiet secret of a love that lasts: it faces outward, together, at a shared life and a shared sky. A couple that only ever turns inward to stare at each other eventually runs clean out of things to see.

And if you've never quite let yourself picture your own ten cups, dear — the real ordinary shape of the lasting love you actually want — tell me what your ten cups look like, and we'll bring the picture into focus together.

For the one waiting for him to come: the Ten of Cups hands the waiting woman her single most important tool, dear — a clear picture of the target. Because if you don't know exactly what you're waiting for, you will accept a fragment of it and call the fragment enough. So sit with the rainbow tonight. What does your Ten truly look like — not the wedding day, but the ordinary Tuesday inside a love that lasts? Once you can see it clearly, you stop wasting whole years on men who were only ever going to hand you one cup, and never the entire sky.

For the one unsure of the man she has: hold him up against the rainbow, dear — gently, and honestly. Not “is he perfect,” but “can I picture us twenty years on, still standing side by side, still looking the same direction?” Can you see the house, the weathered-together peace, the ordinary contentment of it? If you genuinely can't picture the Ten with him no matter how kindly you squint, that is worth knowing. And if the picture comes to you easily and warmly — that is worth knowing too, and worth protecting.

For the one afraid it's too late: look again, dear, because the family under this rainbow is not only the one you were taught to want. Ten cups of real love can be filled a hundred different ways — a chosen family, a handful of deep friendships, grandchildren, a home full of the people you love in whatever shape they arrive. The rainbow is not reserved for the young, or the married, or the ones who got it right the first time. It arches over anyone who keeps a heart open enough to be filled. It has not passed you by. Rainbows come after rain, dear — and you, of all women, have had rain enough to have earned one.

So here's your small thing tonight, dear — paint your ten cups. Take a few quiet minutes and picture, in real and specific detail, one ordinary evening inside the lasting love you actually want. Not the wedding — the Tuesday. Who is in the room. What it smells like. What your hands are doing. How it sits in your chest. Make it so clear you could almost step into it. That picture is your compass, dear. A woman who has never let herself see the target has no way at all to tell whether she's walking toward it or away.

Tomorrow I'll turn over the Hermit, dear — the card of the woman who steps back from the whole bright crowd, lifts a single lamp, and learns the one thing you can only ever learn alone. We'll talk about solitude as preparation. But tonight, the Ten. Paint your rainbow, and aim straight at it.

And if the whole picture feels far off tonight, dear — or you're no longer sure what your own rainbow is even supposed to look like — tell me what's on your mind, and we'll take it from there. It's easier to walk toward a life once you've said it out loud to someone — your free minutes are still waiting.
