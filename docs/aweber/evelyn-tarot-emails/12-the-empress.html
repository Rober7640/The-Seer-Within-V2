I drew a card for you this morning, dear — the most abundant woman in the whole deck. And she is doing nothing at all. Just reclining in a field of gold. I want to show you why that’s the lesson.

A woman reclines on soft cushions in a field of ripe golden wheat, dear. Behind her, a dark forest and a stream falling over rocks; above her, a crown of twelve stars. Her gown is loose and patterned with pomegranates, and by her side rests a heart-shaped shield marked with the sign of Venus. She is not striving. She is not chasing. She is not at the window watching the road. She simply reclines, sovereign and at ease — and the whole field leans toward her, heavy and ripe, as if it grew that way just to reach her.

This is the Empress. The great mother of the deck — the card of abundance, of self-worth, of the body and the senses, of a woman so rooted in her own value that good things grow toward her the way a field turns to the sun. And I turned her up for you tonight, dear, because I suspect you've forgotten she's an option. You've been the one who pours. This card is the one who lets herself be filled.

Look first at how she sits, dear. She reclines. The most abundant figure in the entire deck is at rest. She does not run after abundance, wrestle it down, earn it with exhaustion. She receives it. And I know how foreign that is to you — you, who have made yourself the one who holds everyone, who carries the meals and the worries and the phone calls, who would sooner go without than ask. The Empress is not lazy, dear. She has simply learned the thing you never did: that a field which is only ever harvested, and never rested, goes barren.

Then the crown of twelve stars, and the shield of Venus at her side. Her worth is not up for debate, dear. It's crowned — settled, sovereign, hers before anyone arrives to confirm it. That's the source of her ease. She isn't auditioning. She isn't hoping to be chosen so she can finally feel like someone. She already knows she's someone, and so she loves from fullness instead of from hunger — and that, dear, is the difference between a woman who is chosen well and a woman who is merely settled for.

And all that wheat, those pomegranates, the falling stream: she is fertile ground. Everything grows in her — love, yes, but also the garden, the work, the ideas, the life. But hear the quiet warning inside the abundance, dear: ground stays fertile only if it's fed and rested in turn. You cannot keep pulling harvest from a field you never once let lie fallow. The most generous thing you can do for everyone who leans on you is to become the Empress for a while — to sit down in your own field and let yourself be poured into, so there's something there to give.

And if you can't remember the last time you let yourself receive instead of give, dear — the last time you were the one held — tell me what you’ve been carrying, and let me pour a little back.

For the one worn thin by giving: dear, you have made yourself the one everyone leans on, and it is beautiful, and it is also exactly why your own cup keeps running dry. The Empress does not love you one ounce less for resting. She teaches the hard truth that a woman who never receives has nothing left to give but scrapings — and scrapings, offered long enough, curdle into resentment. Sit down in your field today. Let someone else carry the water for once. That is not selfishness, dear. That is how the ground stays alive.

For the one unsure of the man she’s with: the Empress asks one searching question, dear — do you come to him full, or empty? When you love from need, from the fear that you're no one without him, you can't see him at all clearly; you're too busy auditioning for a place to belong. Find your worth crowned and settled first — the way hers is, before any man rides in — and then look at him again. A woman who already knows she's enough chooses so much more wisely than a woman just hoping, at last, to be chosen.

For the one waiting for him to come: become the field, dear — not the woman at the window. The Empress never goes searching. She becomes so lush, so rooted, so unmistakably alive that arriving in her life feels, to the right man, like walking out of winter into high summer. Tend your own ground: your work, your body, your friendships, your joy. Make your life so full and so warm that a good man would count himself lucky just to be let in through the gate.

So here's your small thing tonight, dear — the receiving practice. Just once today, let yourself receive one thing without deflecting it. A compliment — and instead of arguing it away, only say thank you and let it land. An offer of help — and instead of “oh, I'm fine,” say yes. A small pleasure — and take it without earning it first. Watch how fast the old reflex jumps up to refuse. Override it exactly once. That reflex, dear, is precisely what's been keeping your good field dry.

Tomorrow I'll turn over the Eight of Cups, dear — the lonely, brave card of the woman who finally sets down what's only ever half-full, and walks off toward the hills to go and find the rest. We'll talk about the courage it takes to leave. But tonight, the Empress. Sit down in your field. Let yourself be fed.

And if you've been pouring yourself out for everyone else so long you've gone dry, dear — if you can't recall the last time someone filled you — tell me what's on your mind, and we'll take it from there. Let yourself be the one received for a change — your free minutes are still waiting.
