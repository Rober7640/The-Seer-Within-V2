The card nobody wants to draw keeps a kindness folded inside it.

I drew a card for you this morning, dear — the one nobody ever hopes to see. A heart, pierced through. I promised the Hermit we'd speak honestly about heartbreak, so here it is. And there's a mercy in it.

A single red heart, full and round, hangs against a grey sky, dear — and three straight swords have been driven clean through it, crossing at the center. Behind, the clouds are heavy, and rain is falling in even grey lines across the whole card. No people. No story. No comfort dressed up around it. Just the heart, the three swords, and the rain. It is the plainest picture of pain in the entire deck, and the old painters did not once look away from it.

This is the Three of Swords. The card of heartbreak, drawn plainly — of grief, of the pierced heart, of the sorrow there is no way around but straight through. Nobody wants to turn this card up, dear. But I've drawn it for you tonight on purpose, because there's a mercy folded inside it that you can only ever find by looking at it directly.

Look first at the heart itself, dear. It's pierced — but it's still a heart. It hasn't shattered. It hasn't fallen to pieces on the ground. Three swords are run through it and it is somehow, stubbornly, whole. That's the first quiet reassurance of this card: pierced is not the same as destroyed. Your heart can be run through and go on being a heart. Wounded, yes. Bleeding, yes. But not gone. Never as gone as it feels.

Now the rain, dear — and here is the real mercy. Behind the heart, it rains. And rain is not the sky punishing you. Rain is what falls so that things can grow. A grief that pours is a grief that is moving — draining, clearing, watering the hard ground for whatever is meant to come next. The heartbreak that frightens me, dear, is never the one that weeps. It's the one that never rains — the woman who seals it all up bone-dry, calls it “being strong,” and wonders years later why nothing green will grow in her. Let it rain. The rain is the cure, not the wound.

And notice the swords are swords — the suit of the mind, of thoughts and words. Because some of your pain, dear, is the true wound: the real loss, and that deserves every tear you have. But some of it is the swords your own mind keeps driving back in — the replaying at 2 a.m., the “what did I do wrong,” the cruel old story that says you're the kind of woman who gets left. There's the heartbreak itself, and then there are the blades your thoughts keep stabbing into it. The first, you must feel all the way through. The second, in time, you're allowed to set down.

And if there's a sword in your heart tonight, dear — a fresh one, or an old one you've never quite pulled free — tell me what pierced you, and we'll ease it out gently, together.

For the one in fresh heartbreak: don't let anyone rush you past this card, dear — not even you. The heart is pierced, and pretending it isn't only drives the swords in deeper. So feel it. Let it rain: cry the actual tears, say the actual name, ache the actual ache. That is not weakness; it's the wound draining so that one day it can close. The women I worry for are the ones who never let it rain, who lock the grief up dry and call it coping. Your tears are not the problem, dear. They are the mercy of this card, quietly at work.

For the one unsure of the man she has: sometimes the Three of Swords shows a truth you've been avoiding, dear — something you already half-know and can't quite bear to look at straight. So the card asks, gently: is there a sword in your heart you keep pretending is a decoration? A painful honesty about him, or about the two of you, that you've been talking yourself out of seeing? It hurts to draw it out and look at it in the light. But a truth faced is a wound that can finally heal. A truth denied just stays lodged there, quietly poisoning everything around it.

For the one afraid to love again: before you decide love simply isn't worth the risk, dear, look one more time at the heart in this card — pierced, and still, unmistakably, a heart. It didn't turn to stone. It didn't lose the power to love. The old painters drew it whole on purpose. You were pierced, yes. You were not destroyed. And a heart that can still be broken, dear, is a heart that can still, absolutely, be filled — the stone ones can't feel a thing, and yours clearly still can.

So here's your small thing tonight, dear — let it rain, just once, on purpose. Give yourself ten honest minutes to feel the thing you've been holding off. Name what pierced you, plainly, out loud, with no brave dressing on it. Let the tears come if they come — don't manage them, don't perform strength. Then, when the ten minutes are done, do one small, kind, ordinary thing to close the evening: a warm drink, a soft blanket, your own hand laid flat on your own heart. That isn't wallowing, dear. That's letting the necessary rain fall, and then stepping gently back inside. It's exactly how a pierced heart drains, and begins to mend.

Tomorrow I'll turn over the Queen of Cups, dear — the great tender queen who sits at the water's edge and shows you how to feel absolutely everything, love with your whole depth, and still not drown. We'll talk about loving without losing yourself. But tonight, the Three. Let it rain.

And whatever pierced you, dear — the fresh grief, or the old sword you've carried so long you've stopped calling it pain — tell me what's on your mind, and we'll take it from there. No heart should have to drain in silence — your free minutes are still waiting.

— Evelyn
