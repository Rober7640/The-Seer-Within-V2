I drew a card for you this morning, dear — the one I promised after the Death card. The card of how a heart actually mends. Not all at once. Like this.

An angel stands at the edge of a stream, dear. One bare foot rests on the dry land; the other is set down into the moving water. In each hand there is a golden cup — and between them, a thin thread of water is passing from the higher cup to the lower one, slowly, steadily, not spilling a single drop.

Behind, a narrow path winds up toward a low line of hills, and just where the hills meet the sky there is a small crown of light. At the angel's feet, a few yellow irises have opened in the wet ground.

This is Temperance. The card of the slow cure. Of two things — the old water and the new — poured patiently from one cup to the other, over and over, until they become a third thing that is neither, and is whole.

Yesterday the Death card cleared your field, dear. Today, this. Because clearing is not the same as healing — it is only the room that healing needs. And what this angel knows, the thing the whole card was made to tell you, is that a heart does not mend by being emptied out and filled back up. It mends by mixing. Slowly. The grief and the hope held in the same two hands and passed between them, again and again, until one day the water that pours is calm.

Look where she's standing. One foot on the land, one foot in the stream — and that, dear, is the whole secret of the card. She does not climb out of the water to feel better. She does not let herself sink under it either. She stands with a foot in each. You are allowed to be grieving and to be going on at the same time. The healing was never waiting for you on the far bank. It is here, in the standing — foot in the water, and not running from it.

Now watch the water between the cups. It moves at an angle no water should move — uphill, almost — which was the old painters' way of saying: this is not nature taking its course, dear. This is something being tended. A heart left entirely alone doesn't mix; it separates, the way oil sits on water, the grief hardening into a layer at the bottom. The mixing takes a hand. Yours. A little, every day.

And the path behind her, climbing to that small crown of light? The light is real, dear — but notice the card draws no shortcut to it. Only the long, winding way. Temperance will not lie to you about the distance. It only promises the direction is right.

And if there's an old water in you that won't seem to mix, dear — a grief, or a love, that's been sitting separate and hard for far too long — tell me what it is, and we'll start pouring it slowly.

For the one still raw from a loss: you've been told time heals, dear, and you've found that a half-truth — because time alone mixes nothing; it only passes. What heals is the slow pour: letting yourself feel the old water and set a little of the new beside it, the same week, the same hour. A walk you almost enjoyed. A laugh that caught you off guard. Don't wait until the grief is finished to let those in. Pour them in now, alongside it. That is the cure, and there isn't another.

For the one unsure of a love gone quiet: Temperance is the card of the long marriage, dear — the long anything — the patience that blends two people into something neither could be alone. Not every cooling is an ending. Some are only the water gone still, waiting for a hand to stir it again. Before you decide it's over, ask honestly whether anyone has been pouring lately. Yourself included.

For the one waiting for him to arrive: the angel isn't at the door watching the road, dear. She's tending what's in her own two cups. The women who are ready when love comes are not the ones who waited hardest — they're the ones who used the waiting to become whole: foot in the water, mixing themselves patiently into someone at peace. Tend your cups. He arrives to a woman, not to a vigil.

For the one simply worn out by how long it's all taking: you are not behind, dear. You are mid-pour. The mix isn't ruined because it isn't finished. Stand where you are — a foot on the land you've already gained, a foot in the water you're still in — and pour one more small cup today. That isn't failing to arrive. That, quietly, is the arriving.

So here's your small thing tonight, dear — the two-cup pour. Name the old water: the one grief, the one fear, the one ache you keep trying to tip out all at once. Then name one new thing from this very day — small is fine; a kindness, a warm cup, a few good minutes. And instead of choosing between them, hold them both the way the angel does, and let them sit in the same two hands for the length of one slow breath. You are not betraying the grief by letting the good in beside it. You are mixing the cure.

Tomorrow I'll turn over the Star, dear — the card that comes after the long dark, when a woman finally lifts her eyes and finds the sky hasn't forgotten her. But tonight, Temperance. A foot on the land, a foot in the water. Pour gently.

— Evelyn
