I drew a card for you this morning, dear — the gentlest one in the whole suit. A young thing stands by the sea holding a cup, and out of it, of all the unlikely things, pops a little fish. It teaches how love actually announces itself.

A young figure stands on the shore in a blue coat patterned all over with flowers, dear, holding up a golden cup — and out of the cup, absurdly, wonderfully, a little fish has lifted its head to look back. The Page tilts toward it, half-smiling, not the least bit alarmed by the strangeness of it. Behind them the sea rolls in, slow and easy, one wave folding after another. It's a small picture. Nothing grand is happening. And that is the entire point.

This is the Page of Cups. The youngest and most tender card of the suit — the card of the small, unlikely message that arrives exactly when you've stopped expecting it, and asks only one thing of you: that you stay open enough to receive it, instead of laughing it off before it can speak.

Look at that fish, dear. A fish is the old sign of the deep feeling-world, the messages that rise up from somewhere below thinking. And notice how it comes: not in a thunderclap, not on a white horse, not with trumpets. It pops up in a teacup. That's how the real messages nearly always arrive — small, odd, easy to miss. A stray thought that won't leave. A dream. A coincidence you shrug at. A stranger's unexpected kindness. The nudge to say yes to the thing you'd normally decline.

And watch what the Page does with the strange little fish, because this is the whole lesson. They don't drop the cup in fright. They don't grab at it. They don't say “that's ridiculous, fish don't belong in cups” and pour it out. They simply look, and wonder, and let it be a delight. That is the Page's gift, dear — the beginner's heart, still willing to be surprised. And a heart that has decided nothing surprising will ever happen again has quietly closed the very lid the good news needs to come through.

See too how soft the whole card is, dear — the flowers on the coat, the calm sea, the child's ease. After all the fire and grief and hard departures of the last while, the Page is the deck saying: soften now. Unclench. The signs coming to you are small ones, and you'll walk straight past every one of them if you stay braced and armored, scanning the horizon for something big.

And if a small, hopeful thing has popped up in your life lately, dear, and you've been half-afraid to believe it means anything — tell me the small sign, and we'll look at it together, the way the Page looks at the fish.

For the one waiting for him to come: the Page shows you exactly how love tends to announce itself, dear — and it isn't the cavalry charge you've been taught to watch for. It's a fish in a cup. The kind man in the class you almost didn't notice. The number a friend keeps trying to press on you. The odd little pull you keep explaining away as nothing. Stay open to the unlikely. Love very rarely knocks on the front door, dear. Far more often it just quietly pops its head up in the corner of your ordinary day, and waits to see whether you'll turn and look.

For the one unsure of the man she has: the Page asks a quiet question, dear — does he still surprise you with small tendernesses, the little unbidden cup, the fish neither of you planned? And do you still offer him yours? A long love rarely dies from one great blow; far more often it thins out because both people quietly stopped making the small offerings. Be the Page this week: give one unexpected, unrequired little kindness — and watch closely whether he receives it like a gift, or waves it off like a bother.

For the one afraid to hope again: I know this card unsettles you a little, dear, because staying open is exactly what got you hurt before. But look closer — the Page isn't naïve. He's unarmored, which is a different thing entirely. He can be delighted by a fish in a cup precisely because he never sealed the cup shut with grief. You don't have to fling your heart wide to everyone. You only have to keep one small opening — just enough that when a good, gentle thing does rise up, you're still able to see it, and not mistake it for a threat.

So here's your small thing tonight, dear — go and catch one fish. For one single day, treat every small, pleasant, unlikely thing that happens as if it might be a message: the unexpected text, the kind word, the strange good feeling out of nowhere, the coincidence that makes you smile. Don't analyze it to death. Don't demand it prove itself. Just notice it, and let yourself say quietly inside — I'll take that. You're training the exact muscle that long waiting tends to waste away: the plain ability to still be delighted.

Tomorrow I'll turn over the Moon, dear — the deck's most beautiful and most deceiving card, the one that shows how much of a man you can end up reading in the dark. We'll talk about what's real and what's only moonlight. But tonight, the Page. Stay open. Catch one fish.

And the small sign you keep talking yourself out of, dear — the little fish in your own cup that you're half-afraid to believe in — tell me what's on your mind, and we'll take it from there. Some signs are worth a second look before you pour them out — your free minutes are still waiting.

— Evelyn
