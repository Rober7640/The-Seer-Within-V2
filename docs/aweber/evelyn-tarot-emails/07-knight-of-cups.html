I drew a card for you this morning, dear — the one women hope for: a man riding toward them with his heart held out. But it teaches something sharper than romance. How to tell the real offer from the performance.

A knight rides toward you on a white horse, dear — but slowly. Not the charge you'd expect. The horse moves at a walk, hooves measured, unhurried. In his raised hand he holds a single cup, level, offered out ahead of him. There are small wings at his helmet and at his heels. His coat is patterned with fish. And the country he crosses is real — a river winding through it, low hills, dry ground and green both. He is not performing the approach, dear. He is simply, steadily, coming.

This is the Knight of Cups. The messenger of the heart — the card of an offer arriving. Of the man, or the chance, or the open door, that actually rides toward you with the cup in hand. But the card is careful, dear, and so I want you to be: it teaches you not only that an offer comes, but how to tell a true one from all the charming noise that only looks like one.

Because you have met the other kind, haven't you, dear. The man with the beautiful words and the empty hands. The one who talks about the cup, describes it, makes you feel for one warm evening that surely the cup is coming — and never once holds it out. The Knight of Cups is the cure for him. Look at what this knight is actually doing. Not talking. Not promising. Offering. The cup is already out of his hands and into the air between you.

First, the pace, dear. Every other knight in the deck charges — sword up, dust flying. This one walks. The real offer is rarely the whirlwind. It comes steady, a little careful, almost calm. If a man's arrival feels like a stampede — all urgency and heat and now, now, now — that is usually not this card. The true Knight of Cups can afford to move slowly, because he isn't running from something or chasing a thrill. He's only bringing you the cup.

And then the whole test, dear: his hand is open, and the cup is out. Not held to his own chest. Not described from across the room. Extended. When you're trying to read a man, stop listening to what he says about his feelings and watch his hands. Does he actually offer — his time, his plans, his ordinary Saturday, a real place in his real life? Or does he only narrate a cup he never quite hands over? The mouth makes promises, dear. The hands make offers. Trust the hands.

Those little wings at his heels mark him a messenger — he carries something from higher than his own appetite. The fish on his coat are the old sign of the deep feeling-world, worn openly, not hidden: a real one doesn't conceal that he feels things, he wears it where you can see. And notice he crosses real ground, dear — river, dry earth, green earth both. He doesn't only appear on the easy, candlelit nights. The genuine offer keeps coming across the ordinary terrain, the dull Tuesdays, the unglamorous weeks. That is how you'll know it from the visit that only ever happens by candlelight.

And if a cup is being held out to you right now and you can't tell whether it's real, dear — or if you keep being handed lovely words and empty air — tell me about him, and we'll read his hands together.

For the one waiting for him to come: here is what to watch for, dear, so you don't miss him while you're braced for a storm. He may not arrive as the thunderclap you were taught to expect. The real one often comes at a walk — steady, a little shy, offering small true things before any grand ones. Don't disqualify the quiet, consistent man for failing to sweep you off your feet. Sometimes a cup held plainly out is worth ten dramatic ones snatched back.

For the one unsure of the man she has: run the hands test, dear. Set aside, for one honest week, everything he says he feels, and look only at what he actually offers — his time, his plans, a steady place for you in his life. If the words are a fountain and the offering is a trickle, you don't have a Knight of Cups. You have a knight of talk. And no amount of beautiful talk has ever once filled an empty cup.

For the one not sure another offer will ever come: it will, dear, and the card is gentle about how. Not a cavalry charging in to rescue you. One steady rider, one cup, one ordinary day. Your task isn't to chase him down. It's to stay open enough that when the quiet offer comes across your plain ground, you don't mistake its calm for absence. The real one rarely knocks the door down. He just keeps coming back.

So here's your small thing tonight, dear — the hands test, and you can start it now. Bring to mind whoever is on your heart. Make two short lists: what he says, and what he actually does — what he has truly offered, with his hands, into your real life. Hold the two lists side by side. The gap between them is your whole answer. A man is not how he makes you feel for one evening, dear. He is what he keeps holding out on the ordinary days. Watch the hands. The hands have never once lied.

Tomorrow I'll turn over the Three of Cups, dear — what love looks like once it spills past the two of you and out into joy, into the women who'll hold you while it does. But tonight, the Knight. Watch the hands.

And the cup being held out to you now, dear — or the one you're still waiting to see ride in — if you can't quite tell the real offer from the lovely talk, tell me what's on your mind, and we'll take it from there. We'll read his hands together — your free minutes are still waiting.

— Evelyn
