Stop asking if you love him. Ask a better question.
‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ 
 

I drew a card for you this morning, dear — the one that settles the question you keep circling at two in the morning. Is this real, or am I only wishing?

A man and a woman stand facing each other, dear, and each one holds a cup. They are leaning in — slowly, evenly — to exchange them. Her cup toward him. His toward her.

Above their heads floats a strange and beautiful thing: a winged lion's head, rising over a staff wound with two serpents. The old symbol of healing — of two separate things becoming one. Neither of them is reaching out to take, dear. Both are offering. And the cups are level. Even. The same height in each hand.

This is the Two of Cups. The card that knows the difference between being met and merely wanting to be.

I want you to stop, tonight, asking the question you've been circling, dear. Do I love him? Is he the one? Is this real? You've worn a groove in yourself with it, and it has never once given you an answer — because it's the wrong question. It asks about your feeling, and your feeling has never been the thing in doubt.

The Two of Cups asks a different question, and a kinder one: are both cups raised?

What makes it a Two and not a lonely One is not the size of anyone's love. It's the exchange. Two people, both offering, cups held level. A true thing has both cups in the air. A wish has one person holding both cups out — full, trembling, hopeful — while the other simply stands there, and somehow it never quite gets returned. You already know which one you're in, dear. You've known for a while. You've just been asking the question that let you avoid the answer.

That's the caduceus above them — the two serpents winding up the staff, meeting in the middle. Healing has always been two things coming level. Not one person pouring endlessly into another and calling the exhaustion love. Two cups. Raised. Even.

And if you already know whose cups you're holding, dear, and the knowing aches — tell me about him, and we'll weigh them together.

For the one unsure if he's the one: the card doesn't measure how much you feel, dear. It measures whether it's returned — whether, when you lean in with your cup, his comes to meet you, level and unforced. Don't count your love. Count the cups.

For the one giving far more than she gets: a love where you're holding both cups isn't a Two, dear. It's you, alone, hoping beautifully. And no amount of filling the second cup yourself will turn it into a meeting. One cup raised is not yet love — it's an offer still waiting to be answered.

For the one in something calm, who doubts it because it's calm: the cups in this card are level, dear. No spilling, no grabbing, no drama. You were taught somewhere that real love should feel like falling — but falling is just losing your balance. The Two is steady on purpose. Calm is not the absence of love. Sometimes it's the proof the cups are even.

For the one still waiting to meet him at all: this is what the meeting will feel like, dear — not a chase, but a cup raised to meet your cup. You'll know it not by the thunder but by the level: that for once, you're not the only one holding it up.

So here's your small thing this week, dear: the two-cup test. Picture the one on your mind. Don't narrate it, don't defend it — just see it, like the card. Is your cup raised toward him and his toward you, both in the air, both level? Or are you holding both cups out, full and aching, toward someone standing still? Let the picture answer. The body knows the level long before the mind will admit it.

Tomorrow I'll turn over the one card everyone flinches at when it lands — and I promise you, dear, it doesn't mean what you think. But tonight, the Two. Both cups, or one. You already know.

And the two cups in your hands right now — whose they are, and whether they're truly level — if you've been circling that and never landing, dear, tell me what's on your mind, and we'll take it from there. We'll lift the cups in your hand and look at them honestly, together — your free minutes are still waiting.

— Evelyn
