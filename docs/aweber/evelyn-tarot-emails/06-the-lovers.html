I drew a card for you this morning, dear — the one everyone wants, and almost everyone misreads. They think it's about him. Let me show you what it's really asking.

A man and a woman stand in a green garden, dear, the way the very first two are always painted — bare, unashamed, the morning new around them. Above them, a great angel spreads its red wings wide in blessing, and behind the angel the sun blazes so bright you cannot look straight at it.

Behind the woman grows a tree with a serpent curled in its branches. Behind the man, a tree alight with small flames. And see where their eyes go, dear — the woman looks up, at the angel. The man looks across, at the woman. Between them, far off, a single mountain rises.

This is the Lovers. The card every woman hopes to turn over — and the one most often read wrong. Because she sees two people and a blessing and thinks: him. He's the one. The card said so. But that has never been what this card is about, dear. The Lovers is not a card about finding the right person. It is a card about a choice.

Look again at their eyes, because the whole secret is there. The woman does not gaze adoringly at the man. She looks past him, upward, at the angel — at the higher thing, the blazing sun, call it her own deepest knowing. And the man looks at her. The order is the teaching, dear: she tends to what's above first, and he meets her there. A true union in this card is not two people staring into each other and forgetting the sky. It is two people who each keep one eye on the light, and choose each other in front of it.

Behind her, the tree with the serpent — the tree of knowing, of appetite, of the voice that whispers just reach out and take it, never mind the cost. Behind him, the tree of living flame. Every Lovers choice stands between those two trees, dear: the one that pulls you toward what you want this minute, and the one that burns toward what is actually, lastingly alive for you. The card is not asking which man. It is asking which tree you are choosing from.

That angel overhead is not marrying them, dear. It is reminding them to choose from above the appetite, not beneath it. The women who get the Lovers wrong spend years asking do I want him? The ones who get it right ask the harder, kinder question: does choosing him make me more myself, or less?

And that single mountain, set between them in the distance — the old painters put it there on purpose. A real union does not erase the climb that is yours alone. Two whole people, each with a mountain of their own, choosing to walk the same direction. Not one woman dissolving into a man and calling the disappearance love.

And if you're standing inside a choice right now, dear — circling a him, a yes, a no, and never quite landing — tell me where you're stuck, and we'll look at it from above the appetite, together.

For the one trying to decide if he's the one: stop asking whether you love him, dear — you've worn that question smooth and it has never once answered you. Ask the Lovers' real question instead: when I'm with him, do I become more myself, or less? The right person is not the one who makes your heart pound hardest. It's the one in whose company you grow larger — not a smaller, careful, managed version of you. Watch who you are in the room with him. That is the answer, and it's been there the whole time.

For the one afraid to choose again: the Lovers does not ask you to be certain, dear. Look at them — they aren't sure, they're choosing, bare and unguarded, under a sun too bright to fully read. There is no version of this card where you get to know first and risk after. You choose toward the light with the knowledge you have, and the rest is shown to you in the walking. Being frightened is not a no. It is simply what choosing feels like with your eyes open.

For the one still waiting to meet him: become a woman who already looks up, dear. This union is built between two people who each keep one eye on their own light. If you arrive at love still hoping a man will be your sun, you'll mistake the first warm thing that comes along for it. Tend your own knowing now — so that when he comes, you meet him as the woman in the card: chosen and whole, not rescued.

So here's your small thing tonight, dear — the in-the-room test. Bring to mind the person, or the choice, you keep circling. Don't ask whether you love it, or him. Ask only this: who am I when I'm in it — bigger or smaller? more myself, or more managed? Sit with the honest answer for the length of one slow breath. You are not choosing a person, dear. You are choosing who you get to be. The Lovers has always known that's the only choice there is.

Tomorrow I'll turn over the Knight of Cups, dear — the one who actually rides toward a woman with his cup held out, and how to tell him from all the others who only ever talk. But tonight, the Lovers. Choose from above the appetite.

And the choice you keep circling, dear — the one you can't seem to make on your own — if you've been turning it over and over with no one to think it through with, tell me what's on your mind, and we'll take it from there. We'll look at it from above the appetite, together — your free minutes are still waiting.
