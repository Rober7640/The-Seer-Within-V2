I drew a card for you this morning, dear — the one that explains, better than any other, why you keep going round and round on whether he's really the one. It isn't him you're stuck on. It's the cups in the clouds.

A figure stands in shadow, dear, seen only as a dark silhouette from behind — and floating in the air before it, resting on a bank of cloud, are seven golden cups. But not one of them holds simple wine. From the first rises a glowing figure draped in a veil. From another, a face. There's a coiled snake, a castle on a crag, a heap of jewels, a wreath of laurel with a shadow beneath it, and a dragon rearing up. The figure just stands there, arms half-lifted, dazzled and frozen, unable to reach for any single one.

This is the Seven of Cups. The card of the daydream — of too many shimmering possibilities, of a mind so crowded with maybes that it cannot close its hand on one real thing. And I turned it up for you, dear, because I think you've been standing exactly here: staring at a sky full of cups, and wondering why you can't decide.

Notice first where the cups are, dear. They float on cloud. Not one of them rests on the ground. That is the whole secret of the card: these are fantasies, every one — things imagined, not things held. The Seven of Cups isn't scolding you for dreaming; a woman should dream. It's warning you that a dream you never bring down to earth stays forever a cloud — beautiful, weightless, and impossible to actually drink from.

And look what the cups are holding, dear. The glowing veiled figure — that's the fantasy of the perfect partner, and notice he has no face, because you've been filling it in yourself. The castle is the fantasy life. The jewels are the thing you're sure would finally fix you. The snake and the dragon are the temptations that glitter like treasure and bite like loss. And the laurel wreath with the dark shape beneath it is the prize that quietly costs you yourself. Seven shining promises. The figure can't tell which one is real — because it's trying to look at all seven at once.

Here is why you can't decide about him, dear, plainly. You are not choosing between this man and no one. You are weighing one real, flawed, present man against a whole cloud of phantoms — the ex you remember only fondly now, the stranger who might be better, the imaginary man in your head who would never once let you down. And no living man can win that contest, dear. He's the only cup in the picture with an actual bottom. The others cost nothing and promise everything, precisely because they aren't real.

And if you've been circling one man for months, dear, unable to say yes or walk away — tell me which cup you keep staring at, and we'll sort the real one from the clouds together.

For the one unsure if he's the one: this is your card tonight, dear, so hear it kindly and clearly. You cannot decide because you keep measuring him against ghosts. Take him down out of the clouds. Set him on the ground, on his own, and ask a real question — not is he everything I could ever imagine, because no one is, but is what's actually in this cup good, and true, and enough to build on? The imaginary men will always sparkle brighter. They never have to do the dishes, or apologize, or be tired. Judge him against a real life, dear. Never against a daydream.

For the one waiting for him to come: be gentle here, dear, because the waiting can quietly become its own cup in the clouds. It's easy to fall in love with the shimmering idea of “when he arrives” and, without noticing, to prefer it to any real, imperfect man who actually might. A fantasy asks nothing of you. A real one asks you to open the door. Make sure, in all your waiting, that you still want a flesh-and-blood man — with his ordinary weight, and his ordinary faults — and not just the lovely picture of one.

For the one afraid to love again: sometimes the seven cups aren't hopes at all, dear — they're fears. Seven glowing ways it could go wrong, seven phantom heartbreaks playing on a loop, each one so vivid it feels already true. But they're clouds too. Not one of them has happened. Reach past the frightening seven and take hold of the one real cup: this day, this breath, the single small step actually in front of you. Fear will always paint you seven futures. You will only ever have to live one.

So here's your small thing tonight, dear — name the phantom. Take a scrap of paper and write down what you keep measuring your real life against: the perfect imaginary man, the flawless timing, the version of you who has it all figured out. Look at what you've written, and label it honestly with one word: cloud. Then, underneath, name one real cup already in your hands — a man who actually calls, a step you could actually take this week, a good that's genuinely on the ground. Rest your eyes on the real one for the rest of the night. That is how a woman finally chooses.

Tomorrow I'll turn over the Empress, dear — the great mother of the whole deck, the card of a woman so rooted in her own worth that love grows toward her the way a field turns to the sun. We'll talk about becoming fertile ground. But tonight, the Seven. Choose one real cup, and set it on the table.

And if you're worn out from circling the same choice, dear — unable to tell the real man from the shining maybes — tell me what's on your mind, and we'll take it from there. We'll bring it down out of the clouds together — your free minutes are still waiting.

— Evelyn
