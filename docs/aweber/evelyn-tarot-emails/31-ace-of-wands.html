I drew a card for you this morning, dear — a single green spark held out from the clouds. You've been watching the road for a man. This card is the thing that almost always arrives first — and it's the very thing that makes him findable.

A hand reaches out from a cloud, dear, and in it holds a single wooden wand — but the wand is alive: green leaves are budding and falling from it as though it simply cannot help but grow. Below stretches a whole waiting country: a winding river, green fields, a castle on a far hill, trees just coming into leaf. The hand doesn't demand anything. It simply holds the spark out, open, offered. Take it, the card says. Here is the first flame of something new.

This is the Ace of Wands. The card of the new spark — the very first flicker of desire, of aliveness, of wanting something again after a long cold stretch. Every suit's Ace is a gift held out from the clouds, dear, and this one is the gift of fire: raw energy, a fresh beginning, the pilot light of a whole life quietly relighting. And I turned it up for you tonight because I think a spark has been trying to catch in you again lately — and I want you to notice it, and cup your hands around it before the wind can take it.

Look how the wand is alive, dear — sprouting, leafing, green. This fire isn't the destructive kind; it's generative. The Ace of Wands is desire in its cleanest form: not only romantic wanting, but the whole return of appetite for living — curiosity, the itch to make or start something, the first small stir of “oh, I'd like that” after a long numb season. When aliveness comes back after grief or a long wait, dear, this is exactly how it begins: one small green shoot of wanting, pushing up where nothing had grown for a while.

And notice it's a hand offering the spark from the cloud — a gift, freely extended. But a hand can only offer, dear; it can't close your fingers around the flame for you. You have to reach up and take it. And here's the waiting woman's peculiar danger: sometimes she's so braced against another disappointment that when the new spark is finally held out, she keeps her arms folded — and the spark, un-taken, withdraws back into the cloud. The Ace asks only one thing of you: when you feel that flicker of wanting return, take it. Don't stand there and talk yourself out of it.

And see the whole green country waiting beneath the little flame, dear — rivers, fields, a castle on the hill: everything that one spark could grow into. The Ace is only ever the seed. But the seed holds an entire life folded up inside it. Great loves, great works, whole new chapters — every one of them began as a single unremarkable spark that somebody decided to shelter from the wind long enough to catch.

And if there's a new spark in you — a want, a curiosity, a flicker of aliveness — that you've been half-afraid to fan, dear, tell me the spark you've been afraid to fan, and we'll cup our hands around it together.

For the one waiting for him to come: dear, the Ace of Wands has a small correction for how you've been waiting. You've been watching the road for a man — but what usually arrives first, before any man, is a spark: a flicker of wanting, a fresh curiosity, an unexpected surge of aliveness after the long cold. And that spark is not a distraction from love — it's the very thing that lights you up enough to be found. So when you feel it — the pull to start something, go somewhere, become someone new — reach up and take it. Cup your hands around it. A woman with a fresh fire in her is rarely waiting for long, dear, because that fire is exactly what draws the good ones near.

For the one afraid to want again: I know the spark frightens you a little, dear, because wanting is what makes you vulnerable, and you've kept yourself so carefully numb because numb felt safer. But look at the hand in the card — it isn't forcing the flame on you; it's just holding it out, patiently, offered. You get to reach for it in your own time. And you needn't begin with a man, or anything that could break your heart. Begin with the smallest safe spark: a want you'd forgotten, a curiosity, a thing that used to light you up. Cup your hands around that one. Aliveness comes back one small green shoot at a time, dear — and it is always, always worth relighting.

For the one unsure of the man she has: the Ace asks one clarifying question, dear — does he spark you, or dim you? Not fireworks; I mean the quieter thing: are you more alive around him, more curious, more yourself — or do you feel your flame turned down low to keep the peace? The right love is an Ace of Wands you get to keep: it makes you want things, start things, put out new leaves. If you've been slowly going out around him, dear, that matters. A good man fans your fire. He never asks you to bank it down to nothing.

So here's your small thing tonight, dear — cup one spark. Notice one small flicker of wanting or aliveness that's stirred in you lately: a thing you'd like to try, learn, make, or go and see; a curiosity you keep brushing off as silly, or too late. Name it out loud. Then do one tiny thing this week to feed it — look it up, sign up, tell one person, take the first small step. You're not committing to a bonfire, dear. You're only cupping your hands around a new little flame so the wind doesn't take it. That sheltering of the spark is precisely how whole new lives begin.

Tomorrow I'll turn over Judgement, dear — the great card of rebirth and the second chance, of rising up when you're finally called, of a life you thought was over standing up brand new. It's how we'll close this whole journey together. But tonight, the Ace. Cup your hands around the spark.

And if a spark has been trying to catch in you, dear — and you've been too tired, or too afraid, to give it any air — tell me what's on your mind, and we'll take it from there. A small flame catches faster with someone cupping their hands beside yours — your free minutes are still waiting.
