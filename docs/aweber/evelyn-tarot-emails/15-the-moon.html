I drew a card for you this morning, dear — the most beautiful and most deceiving one in the whole deck. And it explains, better than anything, why the man you lie awake decoding looks so different by daylight.

A full moon hangs low in a night sky, dear, its face half-turned, shedding a pale light down like falling dew. Below it, a path winds up from a still pool, past two dark towers, and off into hills you cannot quite see. A dog and a wolf lift their heads and howl up at the light. And out of the pool, awkward and half-submerged, a crayfish is crawling onto the shore. Everything is silver and uncertain. Nothing in this whole picture is the color it would be by day.

This is the Moon. The deck's most beautiful and most deceiving card — the card of illusion, of the dark, of a mind that fills every silence with creatures of its own making. And I turned it up for you, dear, because I think you've been lying awake reading a man by moonlight. And moonlight, dear, is the one light in the world you must never trust.

Here is why, and it's the whole card. The moon has no light of its own — it only reflects. In moonlight, everything familiar turns strange and silver: a bush becomes a figure, a coiled rope becomes a snake, a short text becomes a verdict. Because when your eyes can't see clearly, your mind quietly supplies the rest. So when he goes quiet, dear, you don't actually experience his silence. You experience the story your fear paints onto the silence. And then — this is the cruel part — you react to the painting as though it were the man. But it was moonlight. It was never him.

See the dog and the wolf, both howling at the same moon. Those are the two ways we read the dark — the tame hope and the wild fear, and at three in the morning they're both baying at once, and neither of them actually knows a single thing. And the crayfish crawling up out of the pool? That's the oldest fear of all, dear, rising half-formed from the deep — the primitive dread of being left, of not being enough. Much of what you think you “sense” about him in the dark is really that creature, wearing his face.

But the Moon is not only lies, dear — and this is the part that takes wisdom. There's a real path in the picture, winding up between those towers into the hills. Some of what stirs in you at night is true intuition, worth heeding. The whole art is telling the genuine knowing from the projected fear — and here is the one rule that works: you cannot tell them apart in the dark. Not alone, not at 3 a.m., not with your heart pounding. You tell them apart in daylight, by checking the moonlit story against what you actually, factually know.

And if there's a story you keep telling yourself about his silence, dear, that you can't stop replaying in the dark — tell me the story you keep telling yourself, and we'll hold it up to the daylight together.

For the one unsure of him: this is your card tonight, dear, and there's real mercy in it. Most of what you “know” about how he feels, you do not actually know — you're reading it in the dark. His short reply, his quiet week, the odd look at dinner: your fear has painted a whole story across those shadows, and you've been living inside it as if it were fact. So here is the Moon's discipline. The next time you catch yourself certain about what his silence means, stop and ask one question — did he tell me this, or did I read it by moonlight? Then wait for day, and check. You will be astonished how many of the night's monsters turn out, by morning, to be driftwood.

For the one waiting for him to come: the Moon warns you of one particular illusion, dear — the man made entirely of moonlight. The one you've barely met, or only imagined, and have filled in so completely in your own mind that you're already grieving a relationship that never actually happened. Be careful. A man seen only by moonlight is mostly your own longing, dressed up in a face. Wait until you can meet the real one in plain daylight — ordinary, three-dimensional, flawed — before you hand over your heart.

For the one afraid to love again: the fear that crawls up out of the pool at night — that you're unlovable, that it's simply too late, that it could only ever end the same way — feels like prophecy, dear, but it's the crayfish. Old, primitive, and it rises in the dark precisely because the dark is where it can. It is not the truth about your future; it's the residue of your past, and it always looks its very biggest at 3 a.m. So sign no verdicts on your life by moonlight. Wait for the sun. Everything is a different size by day.

So here's your small thing tonight, dear — bring one thing into the daylight. Notice something you feel certain of about someone: what he really thinks, what her silence meant, how it's all going to go. Write that certain story down in a single line. Then, just beneath it, write one more line — only what you actually, factually know: what was truly said, what was truly done, and not one drop of interpretation. Now look at the gap between the two lines. That gap, dear, is the moonlight. And the whole freedom of this card is realizing you don't have to live there.

Tomorrow I'll turn over the Ten of Cups, dear — the deck's brightest promise, and it comes in full daylight: a whole family beneath a great rainbow of cups, the very picture of a love that lasts. We'll lift our eyes off the dark and aim straight at it. But tonight, the Moon. Don't trust what you read in the dark.

And the story about him that keeps you up, dear — the one that feels so certain at midnight and so shaky by morning — tell me what's on your mind, and we'll take it from there. Some things only untangle in the light, and with another pair of eyes — your free minutes are still waiting.
