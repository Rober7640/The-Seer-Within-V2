The last card of the journey says the thing you most need to hear.

I drew a card for you this morning, dear — the last one of our whole month's journey together. It's the card of rebirth, of the second chance, of rising up when you're called. And it says the one thing I most want you to carry into everything next.

High in the sky, a winged figure sounds a great horn, and a bright banner streams from it. And below, all across the land, people are rising — women, men, children — lifting up out of open places in the earth, their arms raised, their faces turned up toward the sound. They aren't afraid. They look, every one of them, as though they have been waiting a very long time to hear exactly this — the call that says: rise now. It's time. Come back to life. Behind them, far mountains and still, calm water.

This is Judgement. The card of rebirth — of awakening, of the second chance, of a whole life you thought was finished standing up, new. It is the card of being called back — called up out of whatever ground a long grief or a hard year has buried you in, and asked, gently but unmistakably, to live again. And I saved it for the very last card of our journey, dear, because there is no better note to leave you on than this one: you are not finished. You are being called.

Look at the figures rising up out of the ground, dear. They were, in a sense, buried — and now they rise. That is the whole promise of the card. Grief buries you; a hard year buries you; a loss can leave you feeling as good as gone. And Judgement is the moment you discover you were never actually finished — only underground for a season, waiting for the call to rise. And the call comes, dear. It always, in the end, comes.

And notice they don't rise by force — they rise because they answer. They hear the horn, and they choose to lift their arms and come up. The second chance is offered, dear, but it has to be answered. And somewhere, I think, something has been calling you back to life for a while now — a stirring, a spark, a small quiet “get up” you keep hearing and hushing. Judgement asks you to finally answer it. To lift your arms toward the sound.

Now, its old name is Judgement — but the truest reckoning in this card, dear, is the one you make with your own past. Because to rise up new, you have to set down the verdict you've been carrying against yourself: the I failed, the I'm too late, the I don't deserve another chance. That verdict is the one thing heavy enough to keep you in the ground. This card is not here to condemn you, dear. It's here to absolve you — to let you forgive the woman you used to be, for all of it, and rise as the woman you're still becoming. You cannot stand up new while you're still sentencing yourself for the old.

And see that the ones rising are every age — children and the grey-haired alike. Judgement makes no exception for how old you are, or how long or how deep you've been buried. The call to come back to life goes out to the woman of seventy exactly as surely as the girl of seventeen. It is never, ever too late to answer.

And if something has been quietly calling you back to life, dear, and you keep hushing it — tell me what's calling you back, and we'll help you answer it.

For the one who thought her life was over: dear, if you've been feeling that the best of your life is behind you — that after the loss, the years, the disappointments, there's simply nothing left to rise for — then Judgement is the card I most wanted you to end this journey holding. Because it says, plainly and without hedging: you are not finished. You were buried for a season, not forever, and something is calling you back up into the light. It does not matter how long you've been underground, dear. It does not matter your age, or how thoroughly you'd counted yourself out. The horn is sounding. All you have to do is lift your arms and answer.

For the one carrying a verdict against herself: before you can rise new, dear, you have to drop the sentence you keep reading over yourself — that it was your fault, that you're too much or too late, that you had your chance and wasted it. That verdict is the only thing heavy enough to hold you in the ground. Judgement isn't here to condemn you. It's here to forgive you — to let you finally forgive the woman you were, for all of it, and stand up unburdened as the woman you still get to become. Set the verdict down, dear. It was always far too heavy — and it was never even true.

For the one still waiting for love to come: Judgement reframes your whole wait one last time, dear. Stop waiting to be found, and start rising to be alive — because a woman who has answered the call, who has stood up out of her own grave and come fully back to life, is radiant in a way no amount of waiting ever made anyone. Love is far more likely to find a woman who is up, and living, and lit from within, than one lying quietly in the dark, hoping to be dug out. Rise first, dear. The rising is what makes you findable.

So here's your small thing tonight, dear — answer the call, in one small way. Name the thing that's been quietly calling you back to life: the stirring you keep hushing, the “get up” you keep putting off, the part of you that wants to live again. Say what it's calling you toward, out loud. Then do one small thing to answer it — the first, smallest possible act of rising: make the call, book the thing, open the door, say the yes. You don't have to leap up whole all at once. You only have to lift your arms toward the sound. The rising, once you begin it, has a way of carrying itself the rest of the way.

And so we come to the end of our journey through the cards — from that first cup overflowing, all the way here, to a woman rising up new. But a deck that ends in rising can only lead to one card next: the Fool — the wide-eyed, fearless beginning, stepping out into a brand-new adventure with the whole world ahead. That's where we'll pick up soon. But tonight, Judgement. Something is calling you back to life. Rise, dear. Answer it.

And if you've been feeling finished, dear — buried under the years, or the losses, or a verdict you never should have carried — tell me what's on your mind, and we'll take it from there. No one should have to answer the call to rise entirely alone — your free minutes are still waiting.

— Evelyn
