## Email 1 — Segment B (Waiting) · Observation · No-door

**Subject:** The thing you keep saving until he arrives

**Sketch subject:** a hand pressing a flower bulb into a small pot of soil on a windowsill
*(full §5 prompt with this subject dropped in)*

> I watched a woman in the garden shop this morning, dear, buying bulbs. Just for herself. She held them a moment like she wasn't quite sure she was allowed.
>
> I've thought about it all afternoon. How many things we keep in the box marked *later.* The good plates. The trip. The bulbs. *When there's someone to share it with,* we say — as if our own life is a rehearsal we're not meant to enjoy until the real audience arrives.
>
> Here's what I'd tell her, and you. The waiting isn't empty time to be got through, dear. It's the soil. The woman who plants while she waits — who does the one thing she's been saving — isn't settling for less. She's becoming someone a real love walks toward, instead of someone it has to come and rescue.
>
> So this week, the work: name one thing you've filed under *when he comes.* The dinner out. The good sheets. The plan you keep deferring. Then do it. Alone, and on purpose. Not to prove you don't need anyone — to prove your life is already happening.
>
> You don't plant bulbs because you're certain of spring. You plant them because that's how spring arrives.
> — Evelyn

## Email 2 — Variety valve (whole woman) · Pressed fragment · No-door

**Subject:** Alone is a fact, dear. Lonely is a story.

**Sketch subject:** a single small feather taped to the notebook page
*(full §5 prompt with this subject dropped in)*

> Not a drawing today, dear. I opened the notebook and this fell out — a feather I picked up on a walk last autumn and pressed flat between the pages. I'd forgotten it entirely.
>
> I kept it, I think, because of the bird it came from. Out there on its own on the fence, that one. Not lonely. Alone. There's a whole world between those two words, and I want to give it to you tonight.
>
> *Alone* is a fact. You are one person, in one room, and that's simply true. *Lonely* is a story you lay over the fact — that being one means being unchosen, unfinished, lacking. The fact you often can't change. The story you can.
>
> So here's the small work, and it costs nothing: tonight, find one true thing you like about your own company. Not a brave thing — a real one. How you take your tea. That you can change your mind with no one to answer to. The quiet, when it's yours.
>
> The feather didn't need a flock to be worth picking up, dear. Neither do you. Sit with that a while. The morning will keep.
> — Evelyn

## Email 3 — Segment C (Bereft) · Overheard exchange · Door day

**Subject:** When feeling it again feels like betrayal

**Sketch subject:** a wedding ring resting on a windowsill
*(full §5 prompt with this subject dropped in)*

> A woman said to me last week, dear, almost in a whisper — *"I felt something. For someone. And then I felt sick, like I'd done a terrible thing."*
>
> I asked her who she thought she'd betrayed. She couldn't quite say it. Only that the flicker came, and right behind it came the guilt, fast — like a hand snatched back from a hot stove.
>
> So I told her what I'll tell you, if you've ever felt that lurch. The guilt isn't a verdict, dear. It's grief, doing the old job it knows — keeping you still, keeping you safe, keeping faith with what you lost. It loves you. But it can't tell the difference between *danger* and *unfamiliar.* And the first warmth after a long cold always feels unfamiliar.
>
> Here's the work you can do tonight, on your own. When the flicker comes and the guilt comes chasing after it, don't obey either one yet. Just ask the quieter question: *is this actually dangerous — or is it only new?* Name which. Most of the time, dear, you'll find it's only new. And new is allowed.
>
> That alone will loosen something. And if you'd like to bring me the flicker you can't say out loud to anyone — the one you're half-ashamed of — we can look at it together. Whether it's grief still talking, or something quietly asking to be let in. You've still got free minutes with me. But ask yourself the quiet question first, either way.
> — Evelyn
