Behind whom, dear?
‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ 
 

A woman said five words to me over tea yesterday, dear, and I've been turning them over since. “I just feel so behind.”

So I asked her one word back. Behind whom?

She opened her mouth, and found she had no name to put there. Only a feeling of racing a clock that someone, somewhere, must have set. But when she looked for them, there was no one there.

Here's the quiet cruelty of “behind,” and it's worth seeing plainly. The word smuggles in a finish line, and a field of other runners, and a time you were “supposed” to arrive by — none of which exist. There is no master schedule for a life, pinned up in some office. There's only you, and a pace you were handed long ago by a mother, a sister, a girl you sat beside at school who married first. Most of them long out of the race now, dear, if they were ever in it.

You can't out-run a feeling like that. Run faster and “behind” just moves the line ahead of you. The only thing that works is to stop and make it name names.

So here's the work, and you can do it today for nothing. Next time that behind feeling rises in your chest, stop and ask it flat out: behind whom, exactly? Name the person. And if you genuinely can't — if there's no face there, only the old pressure — then hear this, dear: you are not behind. You're racing a ghost. And you're allowed to stop running for someone who was never at the finish line.

And if you'd like to do that out loud — to sit at that second cup and work out, between us, whose pace you've been keeping all these years — that's the kind of thing I'm best at.
