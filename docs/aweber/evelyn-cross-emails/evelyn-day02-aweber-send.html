I drew a tree this morning that I've watched fall a hundred times, dear. Never in a forest. In people.

An oak, and one thin reed at its foot. You'll know the bones of the old story.

The oak was proud of how it stood. Never bent, never bowed, straight through every fair-weather day, and it pitied the reed for bending at every passing breeze. Then the real storm came. The reed lay flat and let the wind pass clean over it. The oak set its shoulders and stood. And stood. Until it cracked, all at once, and went down whole.

Here's the part the story doesn't say out loud, and it's the part I want you to have.

The oak didn't fall because it was weak. It fell because it had never practised bending. All those easy years it called rigidity “strength,” and got praised for it, and so it never learned the one thing that saves you in a real wind: how to give a little and spring back. You were raised to be the oak, I think, dear. Told that coping meant never showing the strain, that bending was the start of breaking. So you learned to stand through everything, and the people around you call you strong, the way they called the oak strong, right up until the season that asks more than standing.

The cure isn't to wait for the storm and hope you bend in time. You can't learn it in the gale.

So this week, bend on purpose, once, where nothing depends on it. Say “I've changed my mind.” Say “I was wrong about that.” Say “I can't today,” with no apology trailing after. It will feel like weakness. It isn't, dear. It's you practising the give, so that when the wind that matters comes, your body already knows it can lean into it and live.

And if you've been the oak so long that bending feels like falling, dear, come and talk it through with me — your first few minutes are free, and there's nothing to set up. We'll find where you're meant to give, and where you're meant to hold.

— Evelyn
