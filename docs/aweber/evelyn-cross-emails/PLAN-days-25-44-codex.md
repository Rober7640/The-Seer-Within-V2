# Evelyn daily — editorial plan, days 25–44

Planning only. No email copy, HTML, assets, schedule, or state has been changed.

## Editorial direction

- Write all 20 finished emails into one review file: `REVIEW-days-25-44-codex.md`.
- Keep one voice owner for every draft and final Evelyn pass.
- Preserve value before invitation: every email must still be worth reading if its CTA is removed.
- Use eight door days across the 20-day run, with no more than three in any seven-day stretch.
- Use four card-led readings, spaced apart. Each uses one actual card and the reader's question supplies the missing context; no second-card tease.
- Keep the sequence balanced across money, love, grief, reinvention, connection, self-worth, anger, and uncertainty.
- Do not use invented Evelyn memories or objects. Confession and pressed-fragment formats stay out of this run unless the operator supplies genuine canon.
- Treat every composite as a composite. Treat situation-library explanations as possibilities to examine, never facts about the recipient.
- Research and cite every named person, quotation, fable, folk belief, and tarot meaning before drafting.

## Subject-line discipline

The twenty subjects must not sound as though they came from one formula.

- No more than five question subjects.
- Never place two question subjects together.
- Rotate concrete action, quoted reader language, named-person fact, story opening, folk-sign scene, direct problem, and one-word/title forms.
- Do not repeat the same opening construction within five days.
- Draft three genuinely different subject mechanisms only after each body is accurate.
- The subjects below are working directions, not untouchable copy. A subject may change if the finished body earns a stronger truthful one.

## The 20-day slate

### Day 25 — the price she lowered first

- **Format:** overheard exchange, explicitly composite
- **Problem family:** money / asking for fair value
- **Day type:** soft invitation
- **Working subject:** `She lowered the price before anyone objected`
- **Subject mode:** concrete action
- **Source:** a clearly labelled recurring/composite line from women who hesitate to state a price
- **Value:** distinguish a buyer rejecting a price from the seller rejecting it on the buyer's behalf
- **Personal variable:** whether her number is wrong, or whether fear is changing it before anyone responds
- **Invitation:** ask her to bring the amount and the moment she begins reducing it; Evelyn helps separate the practical pricing question from the fear of asking
- **Asset:** notebook sketch of a price crossed out before it is shown
