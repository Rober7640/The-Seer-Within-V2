A man holding a handbag.

I fell a little in love with a stranger this morning, dear. A man, outside the chemist's, holding a handbag.

His wife had ducked back in for something she'd forgotten. And he just stood there holding her bag. Not on his phone. Not impatient. Holding it the way you'd hold anything that belonged to someone you loved, without a thought, without noticing he was doing it at all. I watched him a moment and felt my throat go tight, and I want to tell you why, because it matters for you.

We are sold love as escalation, dear. The grand gesture, the sweep of it, the music swelling. We're taught from girlhood to recognise love by its intensity — and so we learn to miss love in its other, truer form, which is maintenance. The quiet, daily tending. The bag held. The side of the bed warmed before you climb in. The cup made exactly how you like it, set down without a word.

And here's the thing the films never tell you. The escalating kind is often the unreliable kind — heat that has to keep proving itself. The maintenance kind is the love that stays. The man holding the handbag will still be holding it in thirty years.

So this week, do one quiet sum. Count the maintenance. Not the roses — the small, unglamorous tending you've stopped seeing because nobody taught you it counted. The lift offered. The tea remembered. The bag held. And ask yourself, honestly, how much love you've been calling “not enough” simply because it came in the plain wrapper instead of the bright one.

Some of you are starved at a full table, dear. Look again at what's actually on it before you go hungry for the music.

And if you look hard and still can't find one quiet shape of it around you, dear, bring me what you do see and we'll count it together. Some of it hides in the very places you were taught not to look.

— Evelyn
