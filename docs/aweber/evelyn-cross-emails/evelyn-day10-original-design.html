Let me tell you what I learned the hard way.
‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ 
 

I'm going to admit something I don't usually say out loud, dear. For years I was very bad at the one thing everyone comes to me for.

I drew myself at a window, a long time ago, to tell you this properly.

There was a stretch of years when I gave every tired woman at my table the exact patience I could not find for myself. I told each of them to rest, to be gentle, to set the weight down. Then I went home and worked myself thin proving I didn't need to take my own advice. I dealt out a kindness I wouldn't keep so much as a teaspoon of for me.

And it took me a long, long time to see why. So let me hand you the bit it cost me years to learn. The woman everyone calls “the strong one” isn't strong, dear. She's unrelieved. She's never once let herself be carried — and not because no one offered. Because being carried, even for an afternoon, would show how heavy it's all been. And then the people who lean on her might see her differently. Might pity her. Might even lean somewhere else. So she keeps the weight. Better to ache than to be seen aching.

That's the trap, and you don't get out of it by being told you “deserve a rest.” You've told a hundred women that and watched it bounce off you. You get out by gathering one small piece of evidence that the roof holds when you set the weight down.

So this week, tell one person one true thing. Not the whole flood. Just: “I'm tired.” Full stop. No “but I'm fine” chasing after it to take it back. Say it, and then watch them. They won't collapse. They won't pity you into dust. They'll just hold it a moment, the way you've held a thousand things for them. And some old, locked thing in you will move a little, dear, because you'll have proof at last that being carried doesn't cost you their respect. It was only ever you who believed it would.

And if you've been the strong one so long you've half-forgotten there's a woman in there under the weight, dear, let someone hold the reading for you this once. I should like that very much.

— Evelyn
