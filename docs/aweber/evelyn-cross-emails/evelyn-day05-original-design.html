I'm going to ask you the hardest question I know, dear. It isn't the one you're braced for.

Not “who takes care of you.” This one: when did you last let them?

There's a difference, and it's the whole thing. I drew an open hand for it. Palm up, empty. Not giving, for once. Waiting to receive.

Most of the women who write to me are wonderful givers, and I've come to see that giving isn't only love in them. It's also safety. When you're the one who gives, you're needed, which feels like being safe. You're never in anyone's debt. You never have to sit there, exposed, while someone watches how much you actually wanted the help. Giving keeps you in charge of the door, dear. Receiving means handing someone else the key.

That's why “just let people help you” lands so flat. It isn't helplessness you're avoiding. It's the surrender. The not-being-in-charge of how much you're seen.

So I won't ask you to receive everything. Too much. Just one small thing, this week. When someone offers — the cup of tea, the lift, the hand with the bags — don't deflect it. Don't say “oh you shouldn't have” and reach to repay it before they've finished. Let it land. Say thank you. And then sit still in the strange, exposed feeling that follows.

That feeling, dear, is not weakness arriving. It's the muscle you were never allowed to use, finally being asked to.

And if you reached the end of that question and couldn't find a last time, dear — let this be the first. Come and let me listen to you for a while. No giving. Just receiving.

— Evelyn
