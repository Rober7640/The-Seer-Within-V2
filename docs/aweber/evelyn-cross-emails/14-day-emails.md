## Day 1 — Overheard letter · soft sell
**[SKETCH: two hands, one gently cupping the other]**
**Subject:** A letter I wrote this morning — I think you need it too
**Preheader:** It was meant for one woman. It might be meant for you.

Most mornings I write a letter in my notebook, dear — sometimes to a woman who's written to me, sometimes to no one at all. This morning's was to a woman who feels guilty every time she rests. Partway through, I thought of you, and decided to send it on.

Here's what it said:

*"You apologise for sitting down. You call a quiet hour lazy, as if you have to earn it first. But you've held so many people up, for so long, that you've forgotten you were ever allowed to be held yourself. Resting isn't stealing. The hour was always yours."*

I drew those two hands at the top while I wrote it — one holding the other. Because that's the whole of it, dear: rest isn't doing nothing. It's letting yourself be held for a moment, the way you've always held everyone else.

So here's the one thing I'll ask of you today: sit down. Let the tea go cold. The people you carry will keep for the length of one cup.

— Evelyn

## Day 2 — Fable · soft sell
**[SKETCH: an oak and a reed by water]**
**Subject:** The oak and the reed
**Preheader:** You were raised to be the oak. Look who's still standing.

I drew an oak and a reed this morning, dear — for an old story I wanted to tell you. You'll know the bones of it.

The great oak stood by the river and pitied the reed bending at its feet. "I never bend," it said. "I stand." And it did — proud and straight, through every fair day.

Then the real storm came. The reed lay flat and let the wind pass clean over it. The oak set its shoulders and stood, and stood — until it cracked, and went down all at once.

You were raised to be the oak, I think. Told bending was weakness, that strong meant unmoved. But look again at my little drawing, dear — it's the reed still standing in the morning. Bending isn't giving up. Sometimes it's the only strength that lives through the night.

— Evelyn

## Day 3 — Observation · soft sell
**[SKETCH: a small bunch of flowers set back on a market shelf]**
**Subject:** The flowers she put back
**Preheader:** I've thought about her all afternoon.

I drew a little bunch of flowers for you today, dear, set back on a shelf. Let me tell you why.

I watched a woman at the market this morning pick up the flowers she wanted — the good ones, the ones she'd reached for first — hold them a moment, and put them back. She bought the cheaper bunch instead. I've thought about her all afternoon.

How quick we are to set down the small thing that would have given us a little joy — as if joy must be earned first, with enough suffering, before we're allowed it.

You're not waiting on anyone's permission, dear. The wanting was permission enough. Buy the flowers this week — not for an occasion. Just because you reached for them once. That was the whole reason.

— Evelyn

## Day 4 — Single-word morning · soft sell
**[SKETCH: the single hand-printed word "ENOUGH", large and alone]**
**Subject:** One word this morning
**Preheader:** I woke with just this.

I didn't draw a picture today, dear. I wrote one word in the notebook, large, and sat with it. Just this — *enough.*

It lands two ways, doesn't it. *I've had enough* — of the weight, the waiting, the proving. And: *I am enough* — as I am tonight, before I fix one more thing about myself.

I think it came carrying both on purpose, dear. The thing you've had your fill of. And the truth you keep setting down — that you, exactly as you are right now, already are.

— Evelyn

## Day 5 — The question · soft sell
**[SKETCH: an open hand, palm up]**
**Subject:** The question I keep asking everyone
**Preheader:** I won't answer it for you. Carry it today.

I drew an open hand this morning, dear — palm up, empty. It goes with the one question I've been asking nearly everyone who writes to me.

When did you last let someone take care of *you*?

Not do something out of duty. Not need you, not lean on you. Take care of you — gently, expecting nothing back, while you simply let it happen. That's the hand I drew, dear: palm up. Not giving, for once. Receiving.

I won't answer it for you. Some questions do their work by being carried, not solved. Take this one through your day, and notice what it stirs. What it stirs is the answer.

— Evelyn

**[SKETCH: two teacups on a table]**
**Subject:** "I just want to stop feeling behind"
**Preheader:** I asked her — behind whom, dear?

I drew two teacups today, dear — mine, and one across the table — because of something a woman said over them yesterday: *"I just want to stop feeling like I'm behind."*

So I asked her — behind whom? She opened her mouth and found she had no name. Only a feeling. The sense of racing a clock someone, somewhere, must have set — though when she looked, no one was there.

That's the trick of it. We run ourselves ragged keeping pace in a race we were never entered in. There's no schedule for your life pinned up in an office somewhere. Only you, and a pace you were handed long ago, by people mostly gone now.

Here's the work, and you can do it today: next time that *behind* feeling rises, stop and ask it plainly — behind whom? Name them. And if you can't, dear — perhaps you're finally allowed to stop running.

And if you'd like to say it out loud — to sit at that second cup and work out whose pace you've been keeping — that's exactly the kind of thing I do best.

— Evelyn

## Day 7 — Omen / folk-sign · soft sell
**[SKETCH: a small bird in an open doorway]**
**Subject:** A bird in the doorway
**Preheader:** The old women in my family had a saying.

I drew a little bird in an open doorway this morning, dear — because one came into the house and out again, and the old women in my family always had a saying for it.

A bird that comes in and leaves again, they'd say, is news only passing through. Don't grip it too hard, either way — not the good, not the hard.

I've come to think they were right about more than birds. So much of what lands on us in a week — a mood, a worry, a sudden hope — is a bird in the doorway. Here a moment. Gone by evening. The room still yours when it leaves.

Whatever comes through your door this week, dear, let it be a visitor. Not a tenant.

— Evelyn

## Day 8 — Overheard letter · soft sell
**[SKETCH: two chairs turned slightly apart]**
**Subject:** A letter — to a woman sitting a little apart from someone she loves
**Preheader:** It isn't always a verdict. Sometimes it's a season.

Another letter from the notebook this morning, dear — this one to a woman who loves someone, and lately sits a little apart from him. I drew two chairs for it, turned just slightly away from each other. I'm sending it on in case you know that exact distance.

*"You've started counting the quiet. The talks that used to spill now end early. You lie awake taking the temperature of it, asking the dark whether this is the slow end of something — or only a cold stretch in the middle."*

Hear me gently, dear: distance isn't always a verdict. Sometimes two people who still love each other just drift to opposite sides of a hard season — both tired, both a little frightened, each waiting for the other to reach first.

I can't tell from here which yours is. But I can tell you the reaching nearly always has to be done *before* you're certain it's safe. That's the very thing that makes it reaching.

Whatever you decide, dear, decide it from the love — not the fear of being first to move.

— Evelyn

## Day 9 — Fable (Taoist) · **CLEAR CTA** (love)
**[SKETCH: an empty boat drifting on water]**
**Subject:** The empty boat
**Preheader:** An old story about the anger you're still carrying.

I drew an empty boat on the water this morning, dear, for a story I keep near.

A man is rowing across a foggy river when another boat strikes his, hard. He spins round, furious, ready to shout — and sees the other boat is empty. No one in it. Just a boat the current set loose. And the anger has nowhere to land. It simply goes out of him.

The same collision, the story says. The same jolt. But whether you burn over it all day depends on one thing — whether you believe someone *meant* it.

Most of the boats that struck us, dear, were emptier than we think. People rowing blind in their own fog, loosed by currents we never saw. It doesn't make the hit hurt less. It changes what you're made to carry afterward.

Here's the work for today: bring to mind one boat you're still angry at. Just one. And ask, honestly — was it ever as occupied as it felt? Sit with that ten minutes; some of the weight loosens from the asking alone.

And if it won't loosen — if the boat had a face, and a name, and you can't yet tell whether it was empty — come and we'll look at the water together.

— Evelyn

## Day 10 — Confession / memory · soft sell
**[SKETCH: a younger woman at a window, rough]**
**Subject:** I wasn't always good at taking my own advice
**Preheader:** Let me tell you something I learned the hard way.

I drew a younger woman at a window today, dear — me, a long time ago. Let me tell you something I don't say often.

I wasn't always good at taking my own advice. There was a year when I gave everyone who came to me the patience I couldn't find for myself. I told every tired woman at my table to rest, to be gentle with herself — then went home and worked myself thin proving I didn't need to.

It took me far too long to hear my own voice. To notice I was handing out a kindness I wouldn't keep even a little of for me.

So when I tell you to set it down, dear — to rest, to be soft with yourself — know it isn't a lesson from above. It's a thing I learned slowly, and the hard way, and wish I'd learned sooner. I'm only passing you the shortcut.

— Evelyn

**Subject:** The man holding the handbag
**Preheader:** The small, unglamorous shapes love actually takes.

I drew a man's hand holding a handbag this morning, dear. It'll make sense in a moment.

I saw him outside a shop, holding his wife's bag while she ducked back in for something she'd forgotten. Not on his phone. Not impatient. Just holding it, the way you'd hold anything belonging to someone you loved. I don't think he noticed he was doing it at all.

We're sold such a loud idea of love, dear — the grand gesture, the sweep of it. And the real thing turns out quiet as that. A man holding a handbag. Someone warming your side of the bed. The cup made the way you like it, unasked.

If you're waiting to feel loved in some cinema way, dear, you may be walking past the smaller, truer shapes of it already around you. Look again this week.

**[SKETCH: a road forking in two]**
**Subject:** When you can't decide, you've usually already decided
**Preheader:** You're not confused, dear. You're scared of being certain.

A letter today, dear — to a woman circling a decision she won't name, even to herself. I drew a road forking in two to go with it.

*"You've written it out a dozen ways — pros, cons, what he said, what she'd think. Pages about the situation, and not one honest line about what you actually want."*

I notice that, dear. When someone circles and circles and never lands, it's rarely that the answer's unclear. More often they already know it, and they're frightened of what knowing will ask of them. You're not confused. You're scared of being certain — and those are different things.

Here's the work, and you can do it today, alone: stop asking *what should I do.* Ask instead — *what am I pretending not to know?* Sit with that ten quiet minutes, no phone, no other voice. The answer that drops your stomach a little is almost always the true one. You've known it a while.

And if you'd like a second pair of eyes on your fork — the parts you can't say even to yourself — that's exactly what I'm here for.

— Evelyn

## Day 13 — Pressed fragment · soft sell

**Subject:** Not a drawing today
**Preheader:** A leaf I pressed a while back, turned to by accident.

Not really a drawing today, dear — I sketched the thing itself: a leaf I pressed in this notebook a good while ago, flattened gold between two pages. I turned to it by accident this morning.

I kept it, I remember now, because it had gone its full gold *just* before it would have fallen. Caught at the very moment everyone calls the ending.

And I sat there thinking — how many of us are in our gold, dear, and mistaking it for the fall. Certain the bright part's behind us. Sure the good years were the green ones. When the gold — the deep, knowing gold — is the part we're standing in right now, and calling too late.

Look again at where you think you are, dear. You may be at your most beautiful in the very season you've been grieving.

## Day 14 — The question · **CLEAR CTA** (purpose)
**[SKETCH: a key resting in a lock, the door still closed]**
**Subject:** The door you decided was locked
**Preheader:** When did you last actually try the handle?

I drew a key resting in a lock today, dear — a door still closed. A question to close our fortnight, with a small noticing alongside it.

There's a door in nearly every life we decided, long ago, was locked. A person we assumed had moved on. A kind of work we told ourselves we were too old, too late to begin. A reconciliation we were certain would be refused. We walked past it so often that *locked* stopped being a guess and quietly became a fact.

But here's what I keep noticing, dear. Most of those doors were never actually tried. We read the lock from across the room and turned away. The "no" we were so sure of was one we said on someone else's behalf — before they ever got the chance to.

So, gently: which door have you decided is locked, without once putting your hand to the handle? Name it today. Just name it. That's the whole of the work for now.

And if you'd like to try the handle together — to feel out whether it's truly shut, or only one you stopped trying — come sit with me, dear.

— Evelyn
