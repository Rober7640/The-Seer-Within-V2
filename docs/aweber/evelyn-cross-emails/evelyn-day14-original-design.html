There's a door you've never once tried, dear.
‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ 
 

There's a door in your life you've decided is locked, dear. And I'd put money on this: you've never once actually tried the handle.

I drew a key resting in a lock to close our fortnight. A door still shut. A question to carry, and a small noticing to go with it.

There's a door in nearly every life we decided, long ago, was closed to us. A person we assumed had moved on and wouldn't want to hear from us. A kind of work we told ourselves we were too old, too late, too far behind to begin. A mended thing — a sister, a friendship, a self — we were certain would be refused if we reached for it. And we walked past that door so many times that “locked” quietly stopped being a guess and hardened into a fact.

But here's what I've watched, dear, over a great many years and a great many lives. Most of those doors were never tried. We read the lock from clear across the room and turned away. And the “no” we were so sure of? Nine times in ten it was a no we said ourselves, on the other person's behalf, before they were ever given the chance to answer. We lock the door from our own side, and then grieve that it's locked.

I won't promise you it opens. Some doors are shut, and honestly so. But you've a right to know which ones, and you have never let yourself find out.

So, gently, here's the whole of the work for today. Name the door. Just name it — the person, the path, the chance you decided was closed without once putting a hand to it. Don't open it yet. Just stop pretending you ever checked.

And if you'd like to try the handle with someone beside you — to feel out whether it's truly bolted, or only a door you stopped pushing on years ago — come, and we'll put a hand to it together.
