I asked her — behind whom, dear?
‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ 
Evelyn Cross

I drew two teacups today, dear — mine, and one across the table — because of something a woman said over them yesterday: “I just want to stop feeling like I'm behind.”

So I asked her — behind whom? She opened her mouth and found she had no name. Only a feeling. The sense of racing a clock someone, somewhere, must have set — though when she looked, no one was there.

That's the trick of it. We run ourselves ragged keeping pace in a race we were never entered in. There's no schedule for your life pinned up in an office somewhere. Only you, and a pace you were handed long ago, by people mostly gone now.

Here's the work, and you can do it today: next time that behind feeling rises, stop and ask it plainly — behind whom? Name them. And if you can't, dear — perhaps you're finally allowed to stop running.

And if you'd like to say it out loud — to sit at that second cup and work out whose pace you've been keeping — that's exactly the kind of thing I do best.

— Evelyn
