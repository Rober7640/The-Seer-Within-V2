# Days 16–17 — Evening letter variants (evening-send test)

Two emails written **as evening letters** — an opening device that meets the reader in the evening wind-down (**the day's done, the house is settling, before bed**), timed to the ~8:30–9pm send. **Not "late night"** — she's on the sofa at 9pm, not awake at 3am, so the frame is *evening / end-of-day*, never small-hours/insomnia. Tests the evening-send hypothesis (more time + readiness to start a chat once the day's work is behind her).

- **Day 16** — standard (~370w) · **door** (chat CTA: "set it down before bed") · purpose · the night-mind, handed as a tool *in the evening, for the night ahead*.
- **Day 17** — breather (~230w) · soft · love · the loneliness that surfaces once the evening goes quiet.

## Day 16 — Evening letter · **CTA (door)** · purpose · `day16-nightmind`
**Subject:** I know what your mind will do the moment the light goes off
**Preheader:** Something small to take up to bed, dear.
**[SKETCH: a single lit window glowing across darkening rooftops in the evening]**

The evening's drawing in, dear, and one by one the windows are lighting up across the rooftops. The house is settling for the night. Before you go up, let me hand you something to take with you — because I think I know what your mind gets up to once the light goes off.

When you finally lie down tonight and the dark settles in, your thoughts are going to get louder. I want you to know, before they do, that they'll be lying to you. Not on purpose. But they will.

The mind in the small hours doesn't hand you new information, dear. It hands you the very same worries you carried at noon — only now with the brakes off. In daylight your sense of proportion is awake too; it stands beside each fear and says, *yes, but you can ring them in the morning,* *yes, but you've come through worse than this.* In the dark that steadying voice goes to sleep before you do. So the fears get the floor entirely to themselves, with no one left to answer them back. Nothing has actually grown, dear. Your defences against it have simply clocked off for the night.

That's why "just don't think about it and go to sleep" is such useless advice, and why you've never once managed it. You can't out-argue a mind that's sent its own good counsel to bed. You can only refuse to hold court until the counsel comes back in the morning.

So here's the thing to have ready for tonight. When it grips you, don't try to solve it — nothing solvable gets solved at that hour, and somewhere you know that. Instead, say to the thought, plainly: *"You may well be right. And I'll look at you properly in the morning, with the light on and the kettle going."* Then let it be. Half the time, dear, you'll reach the morning and find the monster was only a coat on the back of the door. The other half it's real — but a daylight-sized real, the kind you can actually take a step about.

And if the nights have been very long lately, dear — if you've been lying awake more than you've slept — you don't have to carry it up the stairs alone tonight.

— Evelyn

*CTA:* **Come and set it down before bed →**
*Friction:* Your first few minutes are free, dear, and there's nothing to set up. Better to hand it to me now, while the kettle's still warm, than carry it up to bed with you.

## Day 17 — Evening letter · soft · love · `day17-nightquiet`
**Subject:** The feeling that waits for the day to go quiet
**Preheader:** A short note before you turn in, dear.
**[SKETCH: an empty armchair by a window in the evening, the moon outside]**

There's a particular quiet that comes in the evening, dear, once the day has finally done with you and everything's settled. If you know it — and the feeling that tends to arrive with it — this short note is for you, to take up to bed.

It isn't that the feeling is bigger in the evening. It's that the evening is the first moment all day with nothing left to *do* about it. All day the doing covers it over — the errands, the calls, the keeping-on. When things finally go quiet, the cover lifts, and what was there all along is simply, at last, visible. The quiet doesn't make the loneliness, dear. It only stops letting you outrun it.

So when it finds you this evening, try not to take it as a thing gone wrong with you. It's only the day's noise, lifted. And here's the small mercy folded inside it: a feeling you can finally *see* is a feeling you can finally tend. You can't comfort what you're forever too busy to feel.

So feel it a moment, here in the quiet — not to sink into it, just to put a hand on its shoulder the way you would for anyone sitting alone. *There you are. I see you. It's only the hour.* Then carry that small kindness up to bed with you.

— Evelyn

*Close (soft):* And if the evenings have been heavy for a long while now, dear, you can always **come and keep me company a while**. Some evenings sit easier with the second chair filled.
