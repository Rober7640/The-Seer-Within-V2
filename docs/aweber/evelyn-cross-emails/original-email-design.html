,
 

A hand emerges from a small grey cloud, gripping a thick wooden staff. The staff is not polished. It is rough, alive — green leaves are sprouting from its sides, small shoots of new growth bursting through the bark. Below the hand, a green hill. A castle in the distance. A small river. The sky is yellow and pale.

 

The wand is, quite literally, growing.

 

That is the first thing I want you to see, dear. The wand is not a finished tool. It is not a smooth, polished, ceremonial object. It is a piece of the world that has been pulled from a tree, and the tree has not stopped giving it leaves. The hand holding it is holding something alive.

 

This is the Ace of Wands.

 

The card of the spark.

 

 

We have walked through the Heart, the Mind, and the Body. Tonight the suit changes for the last time. Tonight we come into fire.

The Ace of Cups offered receiving. The Ace of Swords offered clarity. The Ace of Pentacles offered the seed. The Ace of Wands offers something different from all three.

 

The Ace of Wands offers the spark you have been told to apologize for. 

 

Most of you, in this audience, were trained against your fire. Quietly, by mothers who had been trained against theirs. Bluntly, by partners who needed you to be smaller. Subtly, by professional cultures that rewarded the well-managed version of you over the alive version. The fire became a problem. The fire became, in some seasons, an embarrassment.

 

You learned, by your forties, to bank it.

 

The Ace of Wands says, gently and without apology, that the fire is back.

I know what you will tell me.

 

You will tell me that you do not have a great unused passion. You will tell me that you are not the kind of woman who has a creative urge stuck inside her. You will tell me that the women on the internet who are taking up pottery and writing memoirs in their fifties are a different kind of woman. You are practical. You are tired. You have responsibilities. The spark is for other people.

 

I want you to set that down for ten minutes, dear, and listen to me.

 

Every woman has a spark.

 

Not the same one. Not creative-class. Not Instagram-shaped. Not a TED talk.

 

The spark is the small, specific thing that you, alone, are particularly alive when you are doing.

 

For some of you it is a recipe. The actual making of it. The hands in the dough. The smell of the kitchen. The way time changes when you are doing it.

 

For some of you it is a garden. The transplanting. The way you know, by the leaf, what is wrong.

 

For some of you it is a conversation — a particular kind of conversation, the kind where you ask a question that changes someone’s afternoon, and you walk away buzzing for two days.

For some of you it is the body in motion. Not exercise. The specific movement that, when you do it, returns you to a self you forgot you had.

 

For some of you it is writing. Reading. Singing. Walking. Drawing. Building. Restoring. Teaching. Hosting.

 

The spark is not generic, dear. The spark is yours.

 

You know what it is. You have known what it is for a long time.

You have just been told, by quieter and quieter voices over the years, that it does not count.

 

Look at the leaves on the wand.

 

The wand is not pretty. The wand is functional. But it is sprouting. The hand holding it has not pruned the leaves. The card honors the leaves. The leaves are the proof that the wand is connected to something living.

 

Most of you, when you reach for your spark, are reaching for the polished version. The book that is well written. The recipe that comes out perfect. The class that makes you a master. The hobby that, eventually, becomes profitable.

 

The Ace of Wands does not ask for the polished version.

 

The Ace of Wands asks you to pick up the rough wand with the leaves still on it.

 

That is the difference between fire and performance.

 

Performance wants the finished product. Fire wants the green shoots coming through the bark.

The hand emerges from a cloud, by the way, exactly as it does in every other Ace.

 

I want you to notice this.

 

The Ace of Wands is being offered to you the same way the Ace of Cups was offered. By the same hand. From the same cloud. The fire is not, in this card, a different category from love or clarity or material grace. The fire is in the same family.

 

You were not built without it. You were built with it.

 

It is, in fact, the same source as the Ace of Cups offered the cup from.

 

The hand that pours water is the hand that holds the burning wand. They are the same hand. They have been holding both for you, all along.

For the one who has been single a long time and has stopped believing she has any fire in her:

You have it, dear.

 

You have been calling it tiredness. You have been calling it middle age. You have been calling it being settled. The fire is still there. Find the small thing that, when you do it, makes you lose track of an hour.

 

Do it once this week. The leaves will start to come back.

 

For the one in the long marriage where her own fire has, somewhere along the line, been folded into the household’s fire:

 

The household’s fire is a real fire. But yours has not gone out. It has been quiet.

 

The card asks: what did you used to do that lit you up, before everything became about everyone else?

 

Do that, just once. Do not announce it. Do not ask permission. The household will be fine. You will be more alive in it.

 

For the one in the after of an ending:

The Ace of Wands often arrives in the strange clear weeks after a leaving. Energy returns. Surprising energy. You forget, briefly, why you have been so tired.

 

The card is honoring that return. Take the spark seriously. Light something with it.

 

For the one whose person has died:

You may have been told, in the months of mourning, that to feel your fire would be a betrayal of them. It is not, dear. The dead do not require us to put out our own fires to honor them.

 

They would, almost always, want the leaves to come back.

Pick up the wand. They are not jealous of it.

This week, dear, do one Ace of Wands thing.

 

Find one hour this week and spend it doing the thing that, when you used to do it, made you lose track of an hour. Read. Cook. Walk. Write. Draw. Sing. Garden. Move. Talk. Build. Whatever it is — pick the small one — and do it without announcing it, without photographing it, without making it productive.

 

Just light it.

 

The hand holds the wand. The leaves are sprouting. You are not too old, dear. You are exactly old enough.

Tomorrow, dear, the Two of Wands will rise. We will look at the world held in the palm of the hand — at vision, at the small fact that what you can see from your tower is the beginning of what you can build.

 

But tonight — tonight is the Ace.

 

The wand is in your hand.

 

The leaves are coming.

 

Light it, dear.
