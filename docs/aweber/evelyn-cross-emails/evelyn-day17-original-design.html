A short note before you turn in, dear.
‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ 
 

There's a particular quiet that comes in the evening, dear, once the day has finally done with you and everything's settled. If you know it — and the feeling that tends to arrive with it — this short note is for you, to take up to bed.

It isn't that the feeling is bigger in the evening. It's that the evening is the first moment all day with nothing left to do about it. All day the doing covers it over — the errands, the calls, the keeping-on. When things finally go quiet, the cover lifts, and what was there all along is simply, at last, visible. The quiet doesn't make the loneliness, dear. It only stops letting you outrun it.

So when it finds you this evening, try not to take it as a thing gone wrong with you. It's only the day's noise, lifted. And here's the small mercy folded inside it: a feeling you can finally see is a feeling you can finally tend. You can't comfort what you're forever too busy to feel.

So feel it a moment, here in the quiet — not to sink into it, just to put a hand on its shoulder the way you would for anyone sitting alone. There you are. I see you. It's only the hour. Then carry that small kindness up to bed with you.

And if the evenings have been heavy for a long while now, dear, you can always come and keep me company a while. Some evenings sit easier with the second chair filled.

— Evelyn
