I watched a woman buy the wrong flowers this morning, dear. On purpose. I've not been able to put her down since.

She reached for the good bunch first. The ones she wanted. Held them a moment, set them back, picked up the cheaper ones, paid, and left. And I stood there understanding exactly what I'd seen, because I've done it myself a hundred times.

She wasn't being thrifty. Watch closely and you'll see it isn't about the money at all. It's a small private rule, learned so long ago it doesn't feel like a rule anymore: my wanting a thing is not, by itself, reason enough to have it. The want has to be justified first. “I'll get the good ones when there's company.” “I'll treat myself when the work's done.” The cheaper bunch is a tax she pays to be allowed any flowers at all.

And here's why “just treat yourself” never works on a woman like that. She hears it as one more thing to earn. Permission from outside slides straight off, because the rule isn't out there, dear. It's in her own hand, setting the good bunch back.

So I'll give you the small thing that does work. This week, buy the good ones. No occasion. No company coming. And when the guilt arrives — and it will, right there at the counter — don't argue with it. Just name it, quietly: “There's the tax. I'm not paying it today.” Then carry the flowers home anyway.

You're not buying flowers, dear. You're proving to yourself that wanting a thing was always permission enough. It was the only permission there ever was.

If you've been last on your own list so long you'd forgotten you were on it, dear, come and tell me whose rule you're still keeping. I've a feeling we'll find it was handed to you a long time ago.

— Evelyn
