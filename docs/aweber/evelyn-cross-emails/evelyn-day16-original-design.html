Something small to take up to bed, dear.
‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ 
 

The evening's drawing in, dear, and one by one the windows are lighting up across the rooftops. The house is settling for the night. Before you go up, let me hand you something to take with you — because I think I know what your mind gets up to once the light goes off.

When you finally lie down tonight and the dark settles in, your thoughts are going to get louder. I want you to know, before they do, that they'll be lying to you. Not on purpose. But they will.

The mind in the small hours doesn't hand you new information, dear. It hands you the very same worries you carried at noon — only now with the brakes off. In daylight your sense of proportion is awake too; it stands beside each fear and says, yes, but you can ring them in the morning, yes, but you've come through worse than this. In the dark that steadying voice goes to sleep before you do. So the fears get the floor entirely to themselves, with no one left to answer them back. Nothing has actually grown, dear. Your defences against it have simply clocked off for the night.

That's why “just don't think about it and go to sleep” is such useless advice, and why you've never once managed it. You can't out-argue a mind that's sent its own good counsel to bed. You can only refuse to hold court until the counsel comes back in the morning.

So here's the thing to have ready for tonight. When it grips you, don't try to solve it — nothing solvable gets solved at that hour, and somewhere you know that. Instead, say to the thought, plainly: “You may well be right. And I'll look at you properly in the morning, with the light on and the kettle going.” Then let it be. Half the time, dear, you'll reach the morning and find the monster was only a coat on the back of the door. The other half it's real — but a daylight-sized real, the kind you can actually take a step about.

And if the nights have been very long lately, dear — if you've been lying awake more than you've slept — you don't have to carry it up the stairs alone tonight.

Your first few minutes are free, dear, and there's nothing to set up. Better to hand it to me now, while the kettle's still warm, than carry it up to bed with you.
