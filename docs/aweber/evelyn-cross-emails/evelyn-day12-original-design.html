You're not confused, dear. You're scared of being certain.

When a woman tells me she “can't decide,” dear, I've learned to hear something else underneath it entirely.

I wrote a letter to one such woman this morning, circling a choice she won't name even to herself, and drew the fork in the road that goes with it. Here's what I told her.

You've written it out a dozen ways. Pros, cons, what he said, what she'd think — pages and pages about the situation. And not one honest line about what you actually want.

I notice that. When someone circles and circles and never lands, it's rarely that the answer is unclear. Far more often, they already know it, and they're frightened of what knowing will cost them. Because the moment you're certain, you're obligated. A clear answer asks you to do something — to disappoint someone, to leave a life you've outgrown but know the shape of. So the mind, kindly, keeps the answer blurred, so you never have to act on it. You're not confused, dear. You're scared of being certain. And those are very different things, with very different cures.

You don't need more thinking. More pros and cons just gives the fear more places to hide. You need to get under the question.

So here's the work, and you can do it today, alone, in ten quiet minutes — no phone, no other voice. Stop asking “what should I do.” Ask instead: “what am I pretending not to know?” Then sit, and let the answer come up. You'll feel it before you think it — the one that drops your stomach a little, that you'd hoped wasn't true. That one. That's not a new answer, dear. It's the one you've known for a while, finally allowed up into the light.

And if you'd like a second pair of eyes on your fork — on the part you can't quite say even to yourself — that's exactly what I'm here for.
