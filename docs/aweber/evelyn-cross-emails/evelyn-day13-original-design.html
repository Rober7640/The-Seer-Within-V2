A leaf fell out of my notebook this morning, dear. One I pressed years ago and forgot. And it told me I'd been grieving the wrong thing.

I didn't draw today. I sketched the thing itself — the leaf, flattened gold between two pages, the little date I'd written beside it.

I kept it, I remembered as I held it, for one reason. It had gone its full gold just before it would have fallen. Caught at the exact moment everyone calls the ending.

And I sat there with it and felt something turn over. Because how many of us are doing precisely that — standing in our own gold and mistaking it for the fall. Certain the bright part is behind us. Sure the good years were the green ones, the young ones, the busy ones. Already half in mourning for a life that is, in fact, still happening.

Here's the cruel little trick of it, dear, and it's worth knowing. We can only ever name our “best years” looking backwards. Which means we are always, always grieving a present we haven't yet realised was gold. The woman certain her finest season is behind her is very often standing in the deepest gold she'll ever have, calling it “too late,” missing it while she mourns it.

So I'll give you a small thing to break the spell. Name one thing about your life right now — today, this ordinary week — that you'd ache to have back in ten years. The particular weight of someone against you. A body that does this thing easily that it won't always. A person still here to ring up. Name it, look at it plainly, and let it be the proof: you are not after the gold, dear. You're standing in it.

Don't let the leaf go gold and fall before you've once looked up and seen the colour you're already in.

And if you've pressed some whole chapter flat and labelled it “over,” dear, let me read it once before you close the book. I've a feeling there's more gold left in it than you've let yourself see.

— Evelyn
