*"If they wanted to, they would."* You've heard it a hundred times, dear. Maybe you've said it. It's stitched onto mugs now.

And it's true. Let me say that plainly, because I'm not here to sell you false hope. If someone wants to call, they call. Wanting shows up as behaviour, not as the excuses you invent for them. That half of the saying is iron. If you've been translating silence into secret longing, let the iron do its work. Believe people when they show you they don't want to.

But here is the other half, the half the mug leaves off, and it matters more for you than anything about them.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** "If they wanted to, they would" puts the whole story on *their* wanting, and quietly lets you skip the only question you can do something about: why do you keep standing at the door of people who don't?

Because that's the pattern, isn't it, dear. It's rarely one person. It's a type. The unavailable one, the busy one, the one with a foot already out. The saying, satisfying as it is, lets you close the book on them without ever opening the book on you. It turns a mirror into a verdict.

The reframe I'd offer you is this. Stop asking whether they wanted to. You have your answer, and it's freeing. Start asking what it is in you that keeps choosing the person whose wanting you have to wonder about. **Someone who trusts their own worth doesn't audition for lukewarm.** They feel the lukewarm early, and they're gone. Not out of pride. Out of a self-respect they can feel in their chest.

So here is your question for today, dear. Not *"did they want me?"* That one's answered. The new one: *what would I have to believe about myself to stop finding these people interesting?* Sit with that. It's the door out.
