You keep seeing it, don't you, dear. 11:11 on the clock, every time you look. Or the same bird. Or their song, in three shops in one afternoon. And someone, somewhere, told you what it means: they're thinking of you. The universe says wait. A sign.

I'm going to tell you something gentler and truer, and I hope you'll trust me with it.

The sign is real. What it means is not what they told you.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** A thing you keep seeing is not a message about them. It's a message about where your attention lives.

Out of a thousand things your eye could land on today, it keeps landing there, because some part of you has left a question open, and it will keep flagging the world until you answer it. The sign isn't the universe pointing at them. It's your own heart pointing at the unfinished thing.

This is why it matters, dear. If you read 11:11 as *"they're coming, keep waiting,"* you hand your peace to a clock and you wait longer. But if you read it as *"there's something here I haven't let myself decide,"* it becomes useful. **The sign stops being a leash and becomes a lamp.**

So the next time it happens, don't ask what the sign is promising you. Ask what it's asking of you. What is the question your attention keeps circling, dressed up as a number or a bird or a song? Nine times in ten it isn't *"will they come back."* It's *"how much longer am I willing to hold my life still for a maybe?"*

That's the reading hidden inside your sign, dear. And it's a better one, because it's a question you actually get to answer.

Tell me your sign, the thing you keep seeing. I'll tell you what I think it's really pointing at. Not at them. At you, and at the choice you already half-know you need to make.
