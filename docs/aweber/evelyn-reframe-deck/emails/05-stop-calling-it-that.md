You call it *"giving them space,"* dear.

It sounds so generous. So evolved. You're not needy, you're not chasing, you're graciously stepping back to let them miss you. Everyone will nod when you say it.

But let's put the gentle word down for a second and look at the real thing underneath, because they're not always the same, and the difference is costing you.

Sometimes *"giving them space"* is exactly what it says. And sometimes it's a frightened person who has decided that if they go quiet first, it won't hurt as much when the other one does.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** You're not giving them room to miss you. You're withdrawing to a safe distance so you're never the one caught wanting more. You call it space. Your body calls it **bracing.**

Here is why we do it, dear, and it isn't weakness. We rename our fear as a virtue because the virtue is bearable and the fear is not. *"I'm giving them space"* lets you keep your dignity. *"I'm terrified I care more than they do, so I'm hiding it"* does not. So we reach for the kinder word, live inside it, and wonder why the distance we manufactured keeps turning real.

Because that's the cost. A person cannot close a gap you're secretly widening on purpose. Half the time they read your *"space"* as indifference and give it right back, and now two people who wanted each other are both performing not-caring across a silence neither of them chose.

So try this, just for a day. Every time you catch yourself calling it *"giving them space,"* tell yourself the truer sentence and see if it fits: *"I'm protecting myself from wanting them too openly."* If it doesn't fit, good, you really were being generous. If it lands with a thud in your chest, now you know what you're actually doing.

And if it landed, dear, don't sit in that alone. Come and talk to me. I'll help you see what you're bracing against, and whether the distance is protecting you or robbing you.
