# 04 · The tell

- **Format:** [The tell](../formats/04-the-tell.md)
- **Type:** interactive
- **Reframe:** A sentence said twice → the one we don't quite believe.
- **Cast:** neutral ("someone" / "they")
- **Subject:** `{{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}, they said "I'm fine on my own" twice. That's the tell.`
- **Preheader:** We repeat the sentence we don't quite believe.
- **CTA:** Tell me the sentence you keep repeating → `campaign=reframe-04-tell`

---

*"I'm fine on my own."* Someone said it to me twice in five minutes. And the second time is how I knew.

We don't repeat the things we're sure of. You have never once said *"I'm fine on my own"* about your ability to read, or to breathe, or to make a cup of tea. You know those things. We repeat the sentence we're trying to convince ourselves is true.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** The words we say twice are a map to the exact place we're bleeding.

So when someone tells me twice that they're fine alone, I don't hear independence. I hear a wall, built years ago, out of a hurt they never got an apology for. *"I'm fine on my own"* is rarely a fact about strength. It's a decision made in a bad moment, defended ever since, so they never have to feel that particular disappointment again.

And it works, dear. That's the trap. The wall keeps the disappointment out. It also keeps out the thing they say they want. **You cannot build a fortress against being let down and leave a door open for being loved. It's the same door.**

Here is what I'd have you notice, and it costs you nothing. For the next few days, catch the sentence you say twice. To a friend, to yourself, in the shower. *"I don't need anyone." "I'm too old for this." "I don't even want a relationship anymore."* The one you repeat is the one you're guarding. And the thing you guard that hard is almost always the thing you want most and trust least.

You don't have to tear the wall down, dear. You just have to catch the sentence, and be honest about what it's protecting.

And if you already know your sentence, the one you say twice, I'd like to hear it. Come tell me. I'll tell you what it's really keeping out.

**→ Tell me the sentence you keep repeating**

— Evelyn
