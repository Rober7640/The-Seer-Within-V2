There was a woman who watered a dead plant for a year.

A little fern, on the windowsill. A gift, from someone who left. It browned, it curled, it dropped every leaf. And still, every Sunday, she gave it water.

Her sister thought she'd lost her grip. *"It's dead,"* she'd say. *"You're pouring water into a pot of dirt."* And the woman would nod, because she knew. She wasn't confused. She knew the fern was gone.

Here is what her sister couldn't see.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** She wasn't trying to keep the plant alive. She was keeping alive the part of herself that still tended something — that still showed up on a Sunday, and hoped.

That is what we do with the dead things we won't let go of, dear. The texts we reread. The keepsake we won't box up. The anniversary we still mark alone. It looks like we can't accept the loss. Usually it's the opposite. We've accepted it completely, and we're terrified that if we stop the ritual, the part of us that can love goes into the ground with it.

**It doesn't.** The capacity to tend, to hope, to show up gently — that isn't buried with what you lost. It's yours. It was always yours. The fern was just where you kept it safe.

So here is what I told her, and what I'll tell you. Don't throw the pot out in a fit of moving-on. Plant something living in it. Same pot. Same windowsill. Same Sunday water. **Give the ritual a thing that can grow.**

If you know exactly which dead plant is yours, dear, come sit with me. I'll help you find what it's really been protecting.

**→ Come tell me what you're still watering**

— Evelyn
