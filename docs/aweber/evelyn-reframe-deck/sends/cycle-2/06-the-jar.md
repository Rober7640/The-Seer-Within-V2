She has been putting coins in the same jar for eleven years. She has never counted it.

It sits on top of her fridge. A big glass thing, coins and folded notes, heavier every year. It's for a trip. Somewhere with water, she says. She'll go when things settle.

Everybody who sees that jar approves of it. Thrifty. Patient. A sensible woman putting a little aside for something lovely.

Here is what none of them can see.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** The jar is not a plan. Count it and you get a number, and a number can be held against a price, and then *when things settle* has to become a date or be admitted as never. Uncounted, it stays a wish. A wish costs nothing and cannot fail. A decision can do both.

We all have a jar, dear. The account we don't open. The profile we made and never finished. The message we'll send that person when the time feels right. We keep adding to these. We never measure them.

There's a reason for that, and it isn't laziness. Anything unmeasured cannot be evaluated. It can't come up short, it can't disappoint you, and you can't be wrong about it. So the thing you want most gets kept in the one place where it stays perfect, which is also the one place where nothing can happen to it. **The safety is the trap.**

Which is why *be patient, your time will come* helps you so little. It sounds like faith and works like an anaesthetic. It blesses the not-counting. It never once asks the wish to name a figure or a Tuesday.

So count the jar today, dear. Whatever your jar is, put a real number or a real date on it before tonight. It doesn't have to be the right one. A wrong number is useful, and a frightening one is more useful still, because now there's something you can argue with, move toward, or set down on purpose. A wish can only be carried.

And if the number frightens you, dear, that fear is the most honest thing you'll meet all week. Bring it here and we'll read it together.

**→ Come and count it with me**

— Evelyn
