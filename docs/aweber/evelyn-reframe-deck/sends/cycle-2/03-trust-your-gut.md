*"Trust your gut."*

You've heard it a hundred times, dear. It might be the only advice everybody agrees on.

And it's true, so let me say that plainly before I complicate it. Your gut is fast. It reads a room, a pause, the half second before someone answers an easy question, all of it filed while your polite mind is still saying how nice. It catches real things days before you could build an argument for them. Don't throw that away.

But here is the half nobody puts on the mug.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** Your gut doesn't only read the person in front of you. It carries every person before them. Half the time the feeling in your stomach isn't reading this one, it's remembering the last one. And from the inside, a signal and a memory feel exactly the same.

That's why the saying leaves people stuck, dear. It sounds like wisdom and works like a gavel. You feel the dread, you call it knowing, and you either walk away from someone decent or spend months braced against someone who hasn't done anything.

So learn to tell them apart. Real signal is specific and calm. It points at something you actually saw. A story that changed. The way they talk about the last person who loved them. An old wound is general and urgent. It points at a category, *people like this*, *it always goes this way*, and it insists you act immediately. **Urgency is the tell.** What's true can wait an hour. What's frightened can't.

One thing I won't muddy: if a person frightens you, you don't owe anyone a test first. Fear of a person is its own instruction.

So today's question isn't *"is my gut right about them?"* It's *"is this a signal, or a memory?"*

Here's how you answer it, dear. Next time the feeling arrives, say out loud what you observed in the last ten minutes that produced it. Naming makes the feeling show its work. Name the thing and you can treat it as data. Name nothing and it's the old one talking, and that one deserves your compassion rather than your obedience.

If you can't tell which is which, bring it to me and we'll pull the thread together.
