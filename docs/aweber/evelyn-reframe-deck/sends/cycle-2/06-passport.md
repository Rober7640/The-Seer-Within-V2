There's a woman in my town who has renewed the same passport three times. She has never once left the country.

Every ten years it comes around. The photo, the fee, the forms, the wait for the stamp. Then the little book goes into the drawer of the hall table, under the spare keys, and stays there.

Her sister finds it sad, dear. A small pretence kept up at some expense. *"Who is it for?"* she says. *"You're never going anywhere."*

But I don't think that passport was ever about a trip.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** She isn't waiting to go somewhere. She's keeping her permission current. That book never said *I'm going.* It only ever said *I could.* So on the day something worth going for finally turns up, she won't lose a year getting ready.

That is what we keep current, dear. The good coat that still fits. The friend we message twice a year for no reason. The half of a language we won't let go of. Small renewals for a life that hasn't arrived yet.

Here is the part almost nobody sees. From the outside, waiting and readiness look the same. Someone alone, keeping something up, with nothing to show for it. Inside they are opposites. Waiting hands your calendar to somebody else's timing. It puts the good plates away, calls that patience, and closes you a little more each year. Readiness never asks when. It only refuses to let a door lock. It costs a fee and an afternoon, and keeps you someone who can say yes on short notice.

So when they tell you to be realistic, to stop holding out, to get on with your life, watch what that advice actually reaches. They are aiming at the waiting. From where they stand the two look identical, so they hit the readiness instead. **The readiness is the part worth keeping.**

Today, renew one thing that only says *I could.* The lapsed hobby. The friendship gone quiet. The skill you let rust. Pay the small fee, spend the one afternoon, and attach no plan to it. Nothing has to come of it. That is the point.

If you know which passport of yours sits in a drawer, come and tell me.

**→ Come tell me what you've kept current**

— Evelyn
