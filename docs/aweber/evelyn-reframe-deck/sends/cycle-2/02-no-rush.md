*"There's no rush."* He said it in March. He said it again in July, and the four months in between are the tell.

Same three words, dear. Nothing had moved. Nobody had pushed him. The second time it came unprompted, tacked onto a sentence that was going fine without it.

It sounds generous. That is what makes it so hard to argue with. He isn't demanding, he isn't hurrying you, he's giving you room. Who complains about room?

But a real preference gets said once and set down. *I don't like the aisle seat.* You don't circle back a season later to say it again and watch someone's face. When a sentence comes back around, it isn't information anymore. It's maintenance. Something is being held open on purpose.

Not the relationship. The question.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** *"There's no rush"* isn't patience. It's insurance against an answer. A question never asked can never come back *no*, and un-asked is un-refused.

Look at what that buys him. While nothing is decided, nothing can be declined. **The same quiet that spares him your no also spares him your yes**, and he'll take both losses to avoid the one. He keeps every good part of you and none of the exposure of having chosen. What he pays is that he lives in a maybe, and he pays it gladly, because a maybe has never once made anyone feel foolish.

Now the part I'd rather you sat with. You have probably said it back to him. *Oh, same, no hurry here.* Twice, maybe. Being easy. Not being needy. That is the same insurance, dear, written in your handwriting.

It made sense once. You asked someone for a straight answer and it cost you. So you keep your own question un-asked, and call it patience, and wait for a certainty that can only come from the question you are both refusing to ask.

So this week, count your own sentences. Not his. Find the one you say twice. The one you repeat is the one you are negotiating with.

You don't have to ask him tonight, dear. Find your sentence first.

Then come say it out loud to me.
