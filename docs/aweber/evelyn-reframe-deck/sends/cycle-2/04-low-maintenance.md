# 04 · Stop calling it that

- **Format:** Stop calling it that
- **Type:** conversion beat
- **Reframe:** "I'm low-maintenance" → pre-declining: making the ask small enough that it can't be turned down.
- **Cast:** neutral
- **State:** valve — the whole person
- **Subject:** `⚠️ {{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}, you call it "low-maintenance." I'd call it something else.`
- **Preheader:** The kind word we reach for when we'd rather not find out.
- **CTA:** Show me what I've stopped asking for → `campaign=reframe-c2-04-low-maintenance`

---

*"I'm low-maintenance."* You hand it over early, dear, like a credential.

It does land well. No drama, no demands, never a fuss about the late reply. People believe you when you say it. One or two of them look relieved.

Turn the phrase over, though. Something is printed on the back.

For some, *"low-maintenance"* is plain fact. They never needed much, and their lives are lighter for it.

For a lot of us it started elsewhere. Not with needing little, but with deciding early what was safe to want.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** You don't ask for little because you need little. You ask for little because a small enough request can't be refused. That isn't being easy to love. That's **pre-declining** yourself, so no one else has to.

We do it because a virtue can be carried in public and a fear cannot, dear. Underneath *"I'm low-maintenance"* sits a quieter sentence you'd never say at a dinner table: *I decided in advance not to find out whether anyone would meet me.* Silence, in exchange for never hearing no.

The trade has a price. People can only meet the needs you show them. Price yourself cheaply and you get treated exactly that cheaply, and then the cruel part: you read the cheap treatment back as evidence. *See. I wasn't worth more after all.* You built both ends of that loop.

Here is the test. For one day, every time that phrase comes up, swap in the honest one: *"I don't ask, because I don't want to know the answer."* Some of you will find it sits wrong. Wonderful. You were telling the truth the first time.

But if it fits, and something in you goes quiet, make one ask out loud today, and let it be slightly inconvenient. The ride to the station. The phone call instead of the text. Then notice what the asking itself cost you. That price is the one you've been paying all along, and it's almost never as high as you budgeted for.

If the words won't come at all, dear, that's worth looking at with someone. Come tell me what you've quietly gone without, and we'll find the day you decided you'd better not ask.

**→ Show me what I've stopped asking for**

— Evelyn
