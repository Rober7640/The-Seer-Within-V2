# 05 · The sign

- **Format:** The sign
- **Type:** interactive
- **Reframe:** "The photo resurfaced, so they must be thinking of me" → "The photo arriving was chosen for you. Going back to it wasn't, and that count is the message."
- **Cast:** neutral
- **State:** C — bereft
- **Subject:** `{{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}, that old photo came back. You opened it four times.`
- **Preheader:** A phone chose the day. Nothing chose the rest.
- **CTA:** Tell me why I kept going back → `campaign=reframe-c2-05-the-photo`

---

Your phone did that thing this week, dear. It gathered up a day from years ago and handed it back to you. The two of you in some kitchen, mid-laugh, a whole life you can still smell.

And it landed on the exact day you'd been thinking about them.

You didn't tell anyone. But you know what you'd have been told. *They reached for you. The timing was theirs. It was meant.*

Let me take that apart with you. Not to take it from you.

A phone chose the day. I'll be straight about that, because the true thing here is better than the story. Nothing chose what came after. You closed it. Then you went back. Maybe once more before bed, maybe five times. You know your own number.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** The photo arriving was not the sign. Going back to it was. The day was picked for you. The count was not, and the count is the message.

Nobody made you go back. Not the second time, and not the last one. That number is yours, dear, and a number like that only ever comes from one place. We keep returning to what we never finished, whether the person is a phone call away, or behind a door that shut without a conversation, or gone from the world entirely. It isn't what we loved most. It's what we left mid-sentence.

Now weigh the two readings. One says *they're reaching for me.* Then the next move belongs to them, and you spend the week watching a screen for permission to feel better. The other says *I left something unsaid in that kitchen.* That reading has today in it. **Same photo. One version holds you still. The other tells you exactly where you are.**

So do this before dark. Open it one more time, on purpose. Then put it into one sentence that starts with *I* and not with *they*. Then do the smallest honest version of it, even if the only person it involves is you.

And if the sentence won't come on its own, tell me the count and what was in the picture. Between us we can find it.

**→ Tell me why I kept going back**

— Evelyn
