Three swords straight through a red heart. Grey cloud behind it. Rain coming down over all of it. When this card lands on the table, dear, I watch people stop breathing.

They're reading the swords. **The card is about the heart.**

Look again. Three blades went all the way through, and the heart is still a heart. Not shattered. Not scarred over into something hard. Whole, and pierced, and holding its shape around the blades.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** The Three of Swords isn't a picture of a heart that broke. It's a picture of a heart that stayed soft enough to be pierced. That softness isn't the damage. It's the part you'll love from next.

A sword can only go through something soft. That is the whole cost of having loved someone.

For some of you, the person died. I won't soften that. For others, an ending. A door closed while you were still talking. For others the slow drift where nothing was said out loud and it ended anyway.

And here is what the card actually warns about. Not the grief. What people do next. The instinct after a piercing is to go hard, and cure yourself of the softness.

That is when the advice arrives. *Protect yourself. Grow a thicker skin. Don't be so open next time.* It sounds like care, and it costs more than anything else you'll be told this year. Because hardening works backwards as well as forwards, dear. To keep the guard up, you have to go back and re-tell the love you had as a mistake. You gave too much. You should have seen it coming. So the defence reaches back and spoils the very thing it was protecting. You lose it once when it ended, and again every time you agree it was foolish.

So today, find one place you've gone hard just to be safe. The reply you keep two words long. The hug you end first. The question you turn into a joke. Pick one, and let it be soft for one conversation. Not your whole heart, dear. One conversation.

And if you already know which three swords are yours, you don't have to sit under that rain by yourself.

**→ Show me what's still whole**

— Evelyn
