*"Everything happens for a reason."*

A woman told me her sister said it to her at the graveside, hand on her back, meaning nothing in the world by it but kindness. That was two years ago. She has been looking for the reason ever since.

Let me grant the true half first, because it's a large one and I won't sell you false hope by skipping it. People do come out of the worst year of their lives carrying something they would never have gone looking for. Gentler. Harder to impress with things that don't matter. Able to sit with someone in a hospital corridor and not fill the silence. I have watched it happen to hundreds of people and I would not take it from a single one of them.

But here is the half your sister could not have known to say.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** The reason is not sitting inside what happened, waiting for you to be wise enough to find it. Nothing arrives with one attached. The reason is made afterwards, by the person who walks out, and yours has your name on it or it has nobody's.

Told the other way, the sentence quietly hands you a job, dear. Search the wreck for the meaning that must be in there somewhere. It is a restful sort of job. It has no end, so it can hold a person for years, and while you are searching you are excused from everything else.

There's a cruelty in it too, that nobody intends. If a reason has to exist, it has to be big enough to be worth the price. So you go hunting for something that justifies the loss, and you will not find it, because most of what happens is not payment for anything.

So the question I'd give you isn't *why did this happen to me.* It's this one: **what do I want this to have made me?** Then answer it before tonight with one small thing. If it made you someone who says the true thing sooner, say one true thing today to a person who won't be expecting it.

And if you can't get near an answer yet, dear, that's not failure. Come and we'll look at it together.
