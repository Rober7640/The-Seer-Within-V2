He bought the ring four years before he met her.

Twenty-nine, on his own, nobody in sight. He walked past a window on a Tuesday, went in, and spent more than he could sensibly spend on a small gold thing for a woman who did not exist yet. Then he put it in a drawer and told two people.

Both of them laughed. Kindly, but they laughed. His brother said it was the saddest thing he'd ever heard, and for four years that ring sat under the socks being evidence of a man who wanted something too much.

What his brother was looking at, he couldn't read.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** He wasn't buying a ring for a stranger. He was deciding, in a quiet year when nothing at all was at stake, what he'd be saying yes to. So that when it came, he would not have to be brave and lucky in the same week.

Because that's the part nobody warns you about, dear. Almost everyone works out what they'll accept while it is being offered to them. Standing in a kitchen at midnight, four months in, with someone you like looking at you and an answer needed tonight. That is the worst hour of your life to be setting your own terms, and it's the hour most of us pick.

You are not a good judge of what you'll settle for while somebody is waiting on you. Nobody is. The wanting is loud, the loneliness is older than the person in front of you, and every reason to say yes is standing right there being warm and specific, while every reason not to is a thing you'd have to defend out loud.

So decide one today, while it costs you nothing. Not a list, dear. One. One thing you won't talk yourself out of next time, and one thing you've decided you're allowed to want without apologising for it. Write it somewhere you'll find it in a year.

He did meet her, by the way. The ring didn't make that happen and I won't pretend otherwise. It only meant that when she arrived, he wasn't deciding. He'd decided. All that was left was to recognise her.

And if nothing came to you just now when I asked for one thing, that empty drawer is worth an hour of anyone's week. Come and sit with me and we'll find what's meant to be in it.
