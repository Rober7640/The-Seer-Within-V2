Two dogs howling at the sky. A crayfish hauling itself out of a black pool. A road running between two towers, off towards something you cannot see from here.

When I turn this card over, the first thing people ask me is who's lying to them.

**They're asking the wrong question.**

Look again at what's actually in the picture, dear. There's no villain in it. There's no other person in it at all. It is a landscape at night, and the only trouble in the whole card is the light. The dogs, the pool, the road, all of it was there at noon and none of it has moved. You simply cannot see it properly. And what the eye can't finish, the mind finishes for you, out of whatever it has lying around.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** The Moon is not a card about someone deceiving you. It's the hour when everything you look at hands you back your own fear, dressed up as evidence.

You know this hour. At one in the morning, a short reply is proof. The two hours before he answers are proof. The way she said *fine* on Thursday is proof, and you can feel the whole case assembling itself. At nine the next morning the same facts are just a short reply, a busy afternoon, a tired woman on a Thursday. Nothing about the facts changed overnight. The light did.

And here is the mercy in this card that almost nobody takes. Most of the deck asks something of you. This one asks you to postpone. It is the only card in there that says you are allowed to not know yet, and that not knowing tonight costs you nothing.

So sit with this today. Name the thing you only ever know after dark, the one that arrives at midnight and has quietly gone by the time you've had your coffee. Then make yourself one small rule, dear: nothing decided, nothing sent, and nothing concluded between eleven and daylight. Whatever is true will still be true at nine, and it will be wearing far less.

If you already know which hour of the night this card is describing for you, come and say it out loud in daylight.

**→ Show me what I actually saw**

— Evelyn
