You call it *taking it slow*, dear.

It's the most reasonable sentence you own. Nobody argues with slow. Slow is careful, it's grown up, it's the thing half the people you know say they wish they'd done last time. Say it at a dinner table and watch everyone nod.

Let me put the kind word down for a moment and look underneath.

Sometimes *slow* is exactly what it says. After a death. After a divorce. When there are children who've already watched one person leave. When two people both know precisely where they're going and have agreed on the walking pace. That's real, and it's often the wisest thing in the room.

But slow is a speed. And a speed only means anything next to somewhere you're going.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** Ask what the destination is. If neither of you can name one out loud, you aren't going slowly. You're **parked**, with the engine running, calling it a journey.

We reach for the phrase because it does two jobs at once, dear, and neither of them is dishonest. It answers everyone who asks, and it gets you out of the one conversation that would settle things. Best of all, it's never a lie. Nothing *is* happening quickly. Every word of it is true, which is exactly why it's so hard to put down.

The bill comes in a currency you don't notice leaving, dear. Not the months, though people always count the months. It's that you slowly stop asking for things, because asking would break the pace you agreed to. A year in, you have trained yourself out of wanting what you came in wanting, and you'll be calling that maturity.

So here's the test, and give it a full minute. Picture this exact arrangement six months from today. Not better, not worse. Identical. If that picture sits fine because you can see what it's building toward, good, you're genuinely in something slow. If your stomach drops, then it was never the pace, and you already know which conversation is missing.

If it dropped, don't spend another six months being patient about it on your own. Come and tell me the thing you can't ask them.
