# 01 · The question behind the question

- **Format:** The question behind the question
- **Type:** conversion beat
- **Reframe:** "How do I show her I've changed?" → "Would I still want to be this person if she never came back?"
- **Cast:** male letter-writer (male POV)
- **State:** A — uncertain / auditioning
- **Subject:** `{{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}, he listed everything he'd fixed. He still asked the wrong question.`
- **Preheader:** The question under his question.
- **CTA:** Ask me your real question → `campaign=reframe-01-changed`

---

A man wrote to me last night. Four lines.

> *"I stopped drinking. I go to the gym now. I call my mother on Sundays. How do I get her to see I'm not that guy anymore?"*

I read it twice, dear, because everything in it is good, and the question at the end is still the wrong one.

He thinks he's asking *how do I prove it.* But look. Every fix on his list has her name written under it. They aren't a life he's building. They're a case he's presenting to a jury of one.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** He isn't asking how to show her he's changed. He's asking whether she'll take him back if he does. And change that's pointed at a person is just the old chasing in cleaner clothes.

That's the trap, dear. When you rebuild yourself *for* someone, they become the reason, and the day they're gone, so is the reason, and so is the new you. It looks like growth. Underneath, it's the same held breath, waiting for someone to clap.

So here is the one question that tells the truth: *would you keep this if they never saw it?* If she never comes back, do you still want to be the man who does these things? If yes, you've changed, and it's yours, and no one can take it. If no, you've just found a prettier way to wait by the phone.

You have a version of this too. A question you keep asking about them, with a truer one hiding right under it. Bring me the one you're really asking, and we'll find the question you actually get to answer.

**→ Ask me your real question**

— Evelyn
