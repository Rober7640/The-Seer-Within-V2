*"You'll find love when you stop looking."*

Someone says it to you every few months, dear, usually someone who found theirs years ago and forgot how. And there's a real thing inside it, which is why it stings and sticks.

Here's the true half: the frantic search wears you down. When every coffee is an audition and every date a job interview for the rest of your life, people feel it. The desperation leaks. You aren't present, you're scanning, and scanning isn't attractive, it's exhausting, to them and to you.

But watch the cruelty the saying smuggles in.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** "Stop looking" gets heard as *stop wanting, go numb, disappear, and love will sneak up on whoever cares least.* That isn't wisdom. It's a dressed-up instruction to abandon the very thing you want, and to feel serene about giving up.

There's a difference, dear, and it's the whole thing. Stop *auditioning* — yes. Stop performing, stop interviewing strangers for a lifelong role, stop reading every silence like a verdict. But don't stop *wanting.* Don't stop showing up in rooms, saying yes, letting yourself be seen.

Because "stop looking" was never really about looking. It's about where your eyes point. Fixed on the horizon, hunting an arrival, you miss your own life and everyone already in it. Turned back toward that life, building it fuller, you become someone a person is glad to find, not because you gave up, but because you finally faced the right direction.

So here's your question for today. Not *"why hasn't it happened yet?"* The truer one: *"where am I auditioning, when I could just be living?"*

If you can feel the difference but can't quite find it, come and tell me.
