There's a man on my street who repaints his fence every spring. The same color, always. A soft green nobody else on the block would choose.

His wife chose it, the last spring she was here. She's been gone four years now.

The neighbors think it's sad, dear. That he's stuck, that some part of him never took in that she isn't coming back to see it. *"He can't move on,"* they say, watching him kneel there with the brush.

But I watched him once, and he wasn't grieving. He was consulting.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** He isn't failing to let her go. He's keeping alive one decision the two of them made together, because that green fence is the last place his life is still a *we* and not an *I.*

That is what we do with the things we won't repaint, dear. The side of the bed we still don't sleep on. The setting we won't change. It looks like we can't accept they're gone. Usually it's the opposite. We know exactly. We're just not ready to become a person who decides everything alone.

And here is the thing that isn't buried with them: the part of you that builds a life *with* someone, that considers another person, that asks "what would you think?" That capacity is still yours. It didn't go into the ground. The fence is only where you kept it safe.

So don't strip the color off in some fit of moving on. Keep the ritual, and let it turn toward the living. This spring, choose the green with someone still here in mind, even a friend.

If you know which fence you keep painting, come and sit with me.
