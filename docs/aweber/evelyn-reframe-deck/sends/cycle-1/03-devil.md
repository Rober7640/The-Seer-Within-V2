When I lay down the Devil, dear, I watch the whole body go still across the table. Horns. Firelight. Two figures chained at the foot of it. Everyone reads doom.

They're looking at the wrong part of the card.

Look at the chains. Look at where they sit. They are loops, dropped loose over the heads of the two figures, wide enough to lift off with two hands and a decision. No lock. No key needed. The people in the card are not trapped. They are *staying.*

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** The Devil isn't a fate arriving. It's a mirror. It shows you something you've been carrying that only *looks* locked, and it asks the question you've been dodging: why haven't you taken it off?

Because that is the honest answer, dear, and it isn't weakness. The chain is familiar. The cage has your shape worn into it. Wanting someone who keeps you a little hungry, checking a phone that rarely lights up, staying near a warmth that only reaches you in flickers. These become homes. And a known cage feels safer than an open door you'd have to walk through alone.

The dread you feel when this card turns over is not the curse. It's recognition. Some part of you already knows which loop is around your neck. The flinch is you meeting it.

So here is the practice, and it's smaller than you fear. Don't tear anything down today. Just name the loop. Say the one true sentence out loud, *"I keep going back to the one who keeps me hungry,"* and notice it doesn't strike you dead. Naming the chain is the first hand on it. Lifting comes later.

If you already know which loop is yours, come sit with me, and we'll look at what it's really tied to.

**→ Show me what's really got me**

— Evelyn
