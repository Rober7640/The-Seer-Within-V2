# 05 · Stop calling it that

- **Format:** Stop calling it that
- **Type:** conversion beat
- **Reframe:** "Protecting my peace" → sometimes a real boundary, often a wall that keeps everyone out and calls the numbness calm.
- **Cast:** neutral
- **State:** A — uncertain
- **Subject:** `🚩 {{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}, "protecting my peace" is sometimes just a locked door.`
- **Preheader:** The gentle word we use to hide the frightened thing.
- **CTA:** Show me what I'm really guarding → `campaign=reframe-05-peace`

---

You call it *"protecting my peace,"* dear.

It sounds so healed. So evolved. You're not being cold, you're setting a boundary. You're not shutting people out, you're curating your energy. Everyone nods when you say it now. It's the phrase of the year.

Let's put the gentle words down for a second and look at the real thing underneath.

Sometimes *"protecting my peace"* is exactly right. You cut off someone who was truly hurting you, and the quiet afterward is real. That's a boundary, and good for you.

And sometimes it's a door you've locked on a room with the lights off, and called the dark calm.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** A boundary keeps one harmful person out. A wall keeps *everyone* out. You can tell them apart with a single question: is your peace aimed at the one who hurt you, or at anyone who might get close enough to?

Here's why we reach for the phrase, dear. *"I'm protecting my peace"* lets you keep your dignity. *"I'm too scared to let anyone near me again"* does not. So we take the kinder words, and we call the empty room serenity.

But that's the cost. Peace with no one in it isn't peace. It's an empty room you're guarding with your life, and after a while the quiet stops feeling safe and starts feeling like the very thing you were running from. And the ones who would have been gentle with you never get to prove it. They knock once, hear nothing, and decide you meant it.

So try the truer sentence, just for a day: *"I'm keeping everyone at a distance so nothing can reach me."* If it doesn't fit, good, you really were protecting something. If it lands with a thud, don't sit in that room alone.

**→ Show me what I'm really guarding**
