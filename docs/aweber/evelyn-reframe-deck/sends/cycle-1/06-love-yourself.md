*"You can't love anyone until you love yourself."*

You've heard it a hundred times, dear. It's printed on the mugs. It's the thing a well-meaning friend says while you're crying. And half of it is real, so the whole thing slips right past you.

Here's the true half, and keep it: if you're certain you're worthless, you'll take scraps and call them a feast. You'll audition for people who were never going to choose you, and read their crumbs as a language of love. Nobody can fill a cup you're sure has a hole in the bottom. That part is true.

But watch what the saying does with that truth.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** It turns self-worth into an *entrance fee.* Finish loving yourself, it says, and only then are you allowed to be loved. And that quietly disqualifies the people who are starving most: the ones still learning they're worth it get told the door stays locked until they're done.

Here is the part they leave off, dear. Self-love is not a finish line you cross before love is permitted. Far more often it is the thing that *teaches* you. Being chosen well, being stayed with, being seen on a bad day and not left. That is frequently where a person first learns the worth they could not find alone.

And notice what the "fix yourself first" story is really protecting. It is so much safer to *work on yourself* forever than to risk being loved right now. The project has no end date. The door has no witnesses. You can hide inside "once I'm better" for years and call it growth.

So here is your question for today. Not *"have I healed enough to deserve this yet?"* The truer one: *"where am I turning love away because I've decided I haven't earned it?"* That is the door.

If you can feel where you're standing outside it, come and tell me.
