Cycling and old films on one side. Gardening and live music on the other.

Imagine a woman comparing those lists after a date she enjoyed:

> *“He's into cycling and old films. I like gardening and live music. We've been out a few times and I really like talking to him. But when friends ask what we have in common, I can't think what to say. Is that a bad sign?”*

She can list their hobbies, dear. What she can't list yet is what their differences would feel like inside a relationship.

That deserves a closer look.

Shared interests are useful. They give you easy invitations and something to enjoy together. If sharing a particular activity matters deeply to you, you don't have to pretend otherwise.

But a matching list leaves several things unanswered.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** Under *“do we have enough in common?”* may be a more useful question: *“Could a life together have room for what matters to each of us?”*

Someone who never gardens might listen with interest when your tomatoes finally ripen. Someone who gardens every weekend might ridicule the way you do it. The hobby alone doesn't tell you which experience you'll have.

And *“opposites attract”* skips just as much as *“find someone like you.”* Neither tells you whether your ideas about commitment, kindness, or how to spend your time can fit together.

Try looking at one difference that's troubling you. Consider what it would change in an ordinary week. Would you miss having a companion for that activity? Could you enjoy it separately? Does it touch something you need a partner to share?

Then recall how they responded when the difference came up. Curiosity, room for separate plans, pressure to give it up: those are different experiences. If it hasn't come up, that's something you still need to learn. You needn't fill the gap with a prediction.

You aren't being difficult by caring about this, dear. You are getting more precise about the companionship you want.

Tell me which difference is bothering you when we chat. We can explore what it would mean in your daily life, so you can decide what deserves more conversation before getting closer.

**→ Understand which differences matter to me**

— Evelyn
