Picture a hand reaching for the kettle. The ring is gone, but a pale band remains. Suddenly making tea has become a question about the rest of a life.

If that image is familiar, dear, you may know how much can gather around one small patch of skin.

After a death or a separation, taking a ring off can mean many things. Keeping it on can, too. Neither needs an explanation for everyone who notices.

But perhaps you notice the mark and think, *It's still there. I must be meant to keep holding on.* Or you worry about the opposite: *When it disappears, will that mean I'm leaving them behind?*

There is tenderness in wanting a visible record of something that mattered. A trace you can touch may feel steadier than all the questions around it.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** The mark records that you wore a ring. It cannot decide what loyalty, love, or a next step should look like now.

The difficulty begins when a physical trace becomes a rule. You might put off a welcome invitation because the mark remains, or feel hurried toward change because you notice it less. Either way, something happening on your hand gets asked to settle something happening in your life.

Advice to *just take it off* misses that. So does advice to wear it until you feel completely different. Neither tells you what you actually want on an ordinary afternoon.

Try this when a reminder catches you, dear. First notice only what is there: a pale line, a familiar indentation, the place your thumb reaches for. Then notice the instruction that follows it: *wait*, perhaps, or *hurry*, or *prove you still care*.

Let a breath come between those two things. You can finish making your tea before deciding whether that instruction deserves a say.
