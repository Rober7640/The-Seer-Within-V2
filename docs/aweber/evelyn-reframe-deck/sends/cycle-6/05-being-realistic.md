*“I'm being realistic.”* Imagine saying it as you close a community garden's volunteer page, dear.

Their regular morning clashes with your work. Besides, everyone there probably knows each other already. You can picture yourself standing beside the tool shed while conversations carry on around you.

That would be uncomfortable. Perhaps something much like it happened at the last group you tried.

And *“being realistic”* sounds like learning from experience. You have responsibilities. You don't want someone waving them away with a cheerful *“just go for it.”*

I wouldn't wave them away either.

The work clash is real. The welcome you haven't received yet is still unknown. Put them in the same sentence, though, and they begin to sound equally settled.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** Sometimes *“being realistic”* lets a prediction borrow a fact's authority. “I can't attend that morning” quietly becomes “there's no place for me there.”

A previous disappointment gives you a scene you can picture. An unfamiliar possibility gives you very little. It's understandable that the scene with faces and a bad memory feels more convincing. But its detail comes from somewhere else.

The cost is small enough to miss: you stop gathering information. There might be a weekend session. There might not. Either way, you've made the decision without learning that part.

**Give the unknown one factual check.**

Choose a possibility you've recently dismissed. Find one question an actual person or published schedule can answer. For the garden, it could be: *“Do you have a weekend introduction for new volunteers?”* Ask the organiser, or check their current information. You don't have to volunteer by asking.

If the answer leaves the practical barrier in place, you can set the idea aside. If an option appears, you get to consider it. Neither answer requires pretending you'll be welcomed everywhere.

Keep your good sense, dear. Give it something current to work with.

Tell me about an idea you've ruled out when we chat. Together we can sort through the limits you know, the outcome you're expecting, and one detail that would be useful to check.
