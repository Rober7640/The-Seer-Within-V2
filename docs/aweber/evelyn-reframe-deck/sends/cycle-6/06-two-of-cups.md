Two gold cups, held between two people. A winged lion watches above them. Lower down, a hand reaches across the little space between their bodies.

The Two of Cups makes it tempting to ask, *Is this the person I've been waiting for?*

But linger over that reaching hand, dear. There is an exchange taking shape. Each person has brought something, and each has someone else's offering in front of them.

It's easy to look only at the cup you hold. Is it full enough? Have you been thoughtful enough? What else could you give?

**There is a receiving part, too.**

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** I read the Two of Cups as a meeting made possible by offering and receiving care. It gives you something to practise, rather than proof that a destined person has arrived.

Imagine someone you're getting to know offers to carry one of your shopping bags. The handle is digging into your fingers. Yet *“Oh, don't trouble yourself”* comes out before you've considered saying yes.

There may be good reasons to decline an offer. But if even welcome kindness gets turned away, the other person has fewer chances to learn what pleases you. You get plenty of practice being considerate, and very little experience discovering how it feels to be considered.

That's why advice to *show them you care* can miss something. More giving doesn't necessarily make receiving easier. Sometimes it keeps you comfortably in the role you already know.

At your next ordinary exchange, notice one small offer you would actually welcome, dear. Accept it with a simple thank you. Let them carry the bag without immediately listing favours you could return.

You still get to choose your boundaries. A kindness doesn't purchase another date, a confidence, or affection. Receiving it can be just that small.

If you would like to explore this with me, bring an offer you accepted or found yourself brushing aside. Our chat can help you consider what felt comfortable, what felt owed, and whether those were the same thing.

**→ Make room to receive care**

— Evelyn
