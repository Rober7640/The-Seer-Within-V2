In this little story, a man plays the beginning of a piano piece for twenty minutes. His neighbour never hears the end.

Four lovely bars. A wrong note. A sigh.

Then the same four lovely bars.

His brother, passing through the room, says, *“Keep at it. You'll get there.”* And he does keep at it, dear. Every evening, the same stool, the music propped open, another careful attempt from the top.

The beginning gets smoother. The fifth bar stays awkward.

One evening he leaves his hands where the mistake happened. He plays just the few notes around it, slowly enough to watch his thumb. There's a movement he keeps rushing. He tries it again without going back to the opening.

For once, the neighbour hears something different.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** Starting again after every mistake gave the part he already knew most of his practice. The passage that needed his attention received only a few hurried seconds.

The beginning offers a small comfort. You sound capable there. Returning to it feels orderly, as though a clean start will carry you safely past the troublesome bit this time.

But *“keep trying”* leaves an important detail out: what gets another try?

You may recognise this in a project you've restarted. The opening paragraph becomes beautifully polished. The difficult explanation further down remains a blank space. Another fresh document can leave you with exactly the same unfinished part.

Today, give that part five minutes of its own.

Go to the precise place where you usually stop. Make one slow attempt there. Notice what is missing: a fact you need, an instruction you don't understand, a movement that needs breaking down. Work on that small piece, then stop when your five minutes are up. You can decide how to connect it to the rest afterwards.

Your earlier work still counts, dear. You don't have to earn your way back to the hard part each time.

When we talk, describe the place you keep getting stuck. We can consider what that particular part needs and make the next attempt smaller.
