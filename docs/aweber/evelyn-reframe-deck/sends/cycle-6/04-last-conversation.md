*“I need one final conversation.”*

Imagine keeping Saturday empty again, dear, just in case this is the weekend they finally agree to talk.

You aren't asking for much. An honest account of what happened. The chance to finish a sentence that was interrupted. Perhaps an apology you shouldn't have to write for them in your head.

A conversation can help. It can correct a misunderstanding, settle an arrangement, or let two people acknowledge that something mattered. Wanting that is understandable.

The difficulty begins with the word *need*. Especially when it starts attaching itself to things the conversation was never meant to decide.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** If every present decision must wait for a final conversation, the person who won't arrange it gets to decide how long your everyday life stays on hold.

They may not intend that. They may have their own reasons for being unable or unwilling to talk. But the effect shows up in an empty Saturday, whichever reason is true.

This is why *“just move on”* can feel so unhelpful. It asks you to stop wanting an explanation. You may still want one next week. Your wish deserves more care than being ordered away.

**An unanswered question can remain unanswered while something ordinary gets done.**

Look at one arrangement you've postponed. Choose something small and reversible that belongs to you: make a plan for Saturday afternoon, or move the spare chair so your kitchen is easier to use. Leave shared responsibilities for the conversations they actually require.

Afterwards, you may still feel sad. The chair moving doesn't settle the relationship. It simply lets the kitchen work for the person using it today. That is enough work for one small decision to do.

Tell me in our chat, dear, what you're waiting to hear and what you've postponed meanwhile. We can separate the matters that need their participation from the ones you can gently attend to now.
