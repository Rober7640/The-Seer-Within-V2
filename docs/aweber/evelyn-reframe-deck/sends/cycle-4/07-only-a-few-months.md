*"It was only a few months."*

They got that in early, dear. Before I'd asked how long anything had lasted, and before I'd asked a single thing about it at all.

Then again ten minutes later, unprompted, the way a hand comes up before anyone has swung.

A thing you're settled about only needs saying once. The second time is never for the listener.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** *"Only a few months"* isn't a description of what happened. It's a case argued in advance, for a grief you've quietly decided you haven't earned the right to.

Watch how many of these there are once you know the shape. *"It wasn't even official." "We were only talking." "I knew him three weeks." "It was just a pet." "It was only a job."* Every one a small legal filing, submitted before the trial, in case someone thinks you're taking up more room than your receipt entitles you to.

But grief was never sized by the calendar. It's sized by how much future you'd built and then had to take down, and you can build twenty years of future in a fortnight. That's not foolishness, dear, that's what a heart in good working order does with a real possibility. So the loss is the right size. Only the permission is short.

Here's what the shrinking costs. A grief you've ruled inadmissible doesn't go anywhere. It sits there unmourned while the ache keeps arriving on schedule, and because you've already agreed the thing was too small to hurt, the only conclusion left is that there's something wrong with you for still hurting.

So try this for one week. When you tell someone about it, leave the number out entirely. Don't say how long. Just say what happened, and what you'd hoped for. Notice how much harder it is to say without the disclaimer, and notice who treats it as smaller once the number is gone. Usually nobody does. The one shrinking it was you.

Then tell me yours. Not the sentence you use to make it smaller. What you actually lost.
