# 03 · The sign

You'd decided, hadn't you.

Some ordinary Thursday. You were going to take the photograph down, or come off the apps, or finally stop keeping a space in your week for someone who hasn't asked for it. You'd got as far as having your hand on the thing.

And then it came. The robin on the rail. Their initials on a stranger's parcel. The one song, in the shop, at that exact hour.

It came, and it came on that evening of all evenings. I won't explain that away, dear, and I have never once thought these things arrive at random.

What I'd change is only where you're looking. Not at what turned up. At when.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** Nobody asks for a sign while they're genuinely unsure. You ask on the day you'd already decided, because a decision made alone, in a quiet flat, with nobody to hold the other end of it, feels like it needs a second signature.

That is the reading, and it's a better one. The sign is not the message. **The hour you went looking for it is the message.** It marks the exact evening you arrived at the end of your own patience, and that is worth more than any answer it seemed to give you, because it is dated and it is yours.

Now weigh the two ways of taking it. Read as *hold on a little longer* and it moves the deciding to some future week the sign never names, and you can run that loop for years. Read as *this is the evening I got to the end of waiting* and it hands you back something you can actually use, tonight, dear.

So the next time one arrives, don't ask what it's telling you. Write down what you were about to do in the ten minutes before it came. That sentence is the thing your own life has been trying to get past you, and the sign is what stopped it at the door.

Then tell me both. The sign, and what you were about to do when it turned up. I'll tell you which one I'd read.
