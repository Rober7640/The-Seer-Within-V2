# 02 · The question behind the question

- **Format:** The question behind the question
- **Type:** conversion beat
- **Reframe:** "Is that awful?" → "Do I have to be on my way out to be worth coming back to?"
- **Cast:** female letter-writer (the format's first woman after three men)
- **State:** A — uncertain
- **Subject:** `{{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}, she asked me the same question at both ends.`
- **Preheader:** She thought she was asking about a tactic.
- **CTA:** Ask me the question under your question → `campaign=reframe-c4-02-is-that-awful`

---

She asked the same question at the top of her message and again at the bottom. Nothing in between had changed the answer.

> *"He only comes back when I go quiet. If I answer him quickly he disappears for days. So now I leave my phone in the other room on purpose. Is that awful? It works, though. Is that awful?"*

**That is not a question about a phone.**

Look at how she's built it, dear. The same words at both ends, and *"it works"* laid down in the middle like a receipt. Nobody asks twice for a ruling they'd accept from a stranger. She isn't after a verdict on which room her phone sits in.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** The question under *"is that awful"* is almost never about the thing you did. It's *do I have to be on my way out before I'm worth coming back to?*

And here's the part that keeps a person there for years. The tactic works. That's the trouble with it. Every time the silence brings him back, she gets proof of the rule, so she runs it again, a little longer each time. What she can never find out, because the experiment forbids it, is what he does when she is simply there and reachable and glad to hear from him. She is getting better and better at absence, and further from the only answer she wants.

So I didn't tell her whether it was awful. I told her it was expensive. **Nothing that requires you to be leaving can tell you whether you'd be kept.**

Here is what I'd have you do with your own version. Don't judge the tactic. Price it. Count the hours this week you spent not answering someone you wanted to answer, sitting in a room away from your own phone. That number is the rent you're paying, and you should at least know the figure before you decide it's worth it.

You have a question you'd ask me twice, dear. Bring it, and I'll show you the one sitting underneath it.

**→ Ask me the question under your question**

— Evelyn
