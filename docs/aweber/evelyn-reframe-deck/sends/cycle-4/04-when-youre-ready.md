*"You'll meet someone when you're ready."*

Somebody says it to you every few months, dear, usually with real kindness behind it.

The true part of it is properly true, and I'd rather say it well than rush past it. You do carry your state into a room. Someone still bleeding tends to reach for whoever stops the bleeding and mistake the relief for love. Someone braced from the last one interviews the next one for crimes they haven't committed yet. That's real, and working on yourself is nobody's wasted time.

The other half almost never gets said out loud, and it's the half that's costing you.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** *"Ready"* was never defined. There's no exam, no examiner, and no pass mark, so the only proof of readiness on offer is the very thing you're waiting for. Which means until it arrives, you can always be told you weren't ready yet.

Sit with how that loop runs, dear. Still alone in March, so you weren't ready. Still alone in August, so there must be more work. It cannot be disproved, it never finishes, and worst of all it is comforting, which is why it lasts. It gives an unbearable, arbitrary wait a reason, and puts the reason inside you where you can do something about it.

Then it hands you a bill. It quietly turns your loneliness into a report card. **You were unlucky, and the saying talked you into reading it as unfinished.**

So take a different question into today. Not *"am I ready?"* The better one: **what would I do this month if I decided I already was?** Then do one of those things this week. Book the trip you were saving for the version of you who has someone. Say yes to the Saturday thing. Wear the good coat to nothing special. Not to attract anyone, dear. To stop running an exam you set yourself.

And if you can't name what you're still supposedly getting ready for, that's worth an hour with me. It's usually one old sentence, and it's usually not yours.

— Evelyn
