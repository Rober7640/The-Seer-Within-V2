# Format 04 — The tell

*Read [PLAYBOOK.md](../PLAYBOOK.md) and [hooks.md](../hooks.md) first. This spec only adds what's specific to this format.*

**Type:** interactive (drives replies) · **The turn:** a repeated, self-deceiving line → the thing it's really guarding

## 1. Purpose / when to use
Take a small sentence people say — and *repeat* — and reveal the repetition itself as the tell, then show what the line is guarding. It's an **interactive** day: it closes with "tell me your sentence," which pulls replies (the strongest deliverability signal we have). Use when you want engagement plus a mirror the reader can apply to themselves before they've finished reading.

## 2. The reframe mechanic
The words we repeat are the ones we *don't* believe — the ones we're trying to convince ourselves are true. So the reframe has two moves: (1) the repeated line is a **map to the wound**, and (2) the "strength" it claims (independence, not caring, being fine) is really a **wall** built from an old hurt. Rename it from virtue to protection — gently, because the wall made sense once.

## 3. The hook
**Pattern:** the line said twice ([hooks.md](../hooks.md) #4). Quote the small line; flag the repetition as the giveaway.

Hook bank:
- "*'I'm fine on my own.'* Someone said it to me twice in five minutes. And the second time is how I knew." — *gold; the count is the tell.*
- "*'I'm not even looking anymore.'* She told me that three times, dear. Three." — *repetition dramatized.*
- "He said *'I don't really do relationships'* before I'd asked a single thing about relationships." — *the unprompted defense as the tell.*
- "*'It's fine, I'm used to it.'* Watch for that one. *'Used to it'* is where a lot of quiet grief hides." — *names the phrase as a category.*
- "Someone told me *'I'm just picky'* twice yesterday. *'Picky'* is often *'scared,'* wearing a nicer coat." — *the rename previewed in the hook.*

## 4. The skeleton
1. **The line + the repetition** (hook) — quote it (italics or quote box); *"the second time is how I knew."*
2. **The principle** — we don't repeat what we're sure of; we repeat what we're trying to believe.
3. **The reframe** (pull-quote) — *the words we say twice are a map to the exact place we're bleeding.*
4. **What the line is really doing** — the wall, the old hurt, the defense; rename virtue → protection.
5. **The trap / mechanism** — the wall *works*, and that's the problem: it keeps out the thing they want too. (*"It's the same door."*)
6. **The practice** — catch the sentence *you* say twice this week; the one you guard is the one you want most.
7. **The reassurance** — you don't have to tear the wall down, just catch the sentence.
8. **Interactive ask** — tell me your sentence; I'll tell you what it's really keeping out.

## 5. Subject + preheader
Often **no emoji** — the quoted line *is* the hook. Formula: `{first}, they said "<line>" twice. That's the tell.`
- `{first}, they said "I'm fine on my own" twice. That's the tell.`
- `{first}, "I'm not looking anymore" — she said it three times.`
- `{first}, the sentence you say twice is the one you don't believe.`

Preheader: *"We repeat the sentence we don't quite believe."*

## 6. The ask / CTA
Interactive — a reply-style prompt. Benefit-worded: **"Tell me the sentence you keep repeating."** The click leads to the chat where they can actually say it. Single CTA. Tag `campaign=reframe-04-tell`.

## 7. Formatting notes
- The repeated line in *italics* every time it appears (or a quote box if you open on a fuller quote).
- The reframe **pull-quote** carries the "map to where we're bleeding" line.
- *Italics* on the little bank of example sentences the reader scans for their own.

## 8. Do / Don't
- **Do** pick a line that's genuinely common and self-deceiving — not a strawman no one says.
- **Do** stay gender-neutral (*someone / they*).
- **Don't** shame the reader for the wall. Evelyn is explicit that *"it isn't weakness"* — the wall made sense once.
- **Don't** play it as a gotcha. The tell is a gentle noticing, offered so they can use it, not a trap sprung on them.
