# Format 06 — The myth

*Read [PLAYBOOK.md](../PLAYBOOK.md) and [hooks.md](../hooks.md) first. This spec only adds what's specific to this format.*

**Type:** insight (contrarian) · **The turn:** grant a popular maxim's truth → reveal the more important half it hides

## 1. Purpose / when to use
Take a widely-repeated piece of love-wisdom — *"if they wanted to, they would," "you'll find them when you stop looking," "communication is everything," "time heals"* — and complicate it: grant what's true, then reveal the half it leaves off, usually turning the mirror back on the reader. It builds Evelyn as a **wise authority who doesn't traffic in platitudes**, which is rare and trust-building. Pure insight day. Use when the reader is leaning on a slogan that's keeping them stuck.

## 2. The reframe mechanic
The maxim is **half-true**. The reframe grants the true half first (this is what buys credibility and keeps it honest — no false hope), then reveals the more important **omitted half** — which almost always shifts the frame from *them → you*, from *verdict → agency*. The reader arrives thinking the slogan settled something; they leave with a better question they can actually act on.

## 3. The hook
**Pattern:** the half-truth ([hooks.md](../hooks.md) #6). Quote the maxim; call it half a truth (or half-wrong).

Hook bank:
- "*'If they wanted to, they would'* is half a truth. The other half matters more." — *gold; grants and withholds in one line.*
- "*'You'll find love when you stop looking.'* Half-wrong, dear, and the half that's wrong is cruel. Let me split it." — *flags the cruelty of the bad half.*
- "*'Communication is everything,'* they say. I've watched couples communicate themselves right out of love." — *a counter-example that stops the nod.*
- "*'Trust your gut.'* Sometimes your gut is wisdom. Sometimes it's an old wound with a loud voice. You have to know which." — *splits the maxim into two cases.*
- "*'Time heals.'* No. Time doesn't heal — something else does, and it's worth naming." — *flat contradiction + a promise to name the real thing.*

## 4. The skeleton
1. **The maxim** (hook) — quote it; *"you've heard it a hundred times."*
2. **Grant the true half** — plainly, no false hope. *"It's true. Let me say that clearly."* (This earns the right to complicate it.)
3. **The pivot** — *"But here is the other half the [mug/slogan] leaves off."*
4. **The reframe** (pull-quote) — the omitted half, usually pivoting the story to the reader.
5. **The pattern / mechanism** — why the half-truth is comforting *and* stuck-making (it lets you skip the question about you).
6. **The better question** — reframe from verdict → agency; give the new question to sit with.
7. **Ask** — if you want help seeing your pattern, come pull the thread with me.

## 5. Subject + preheader
Usually **no emoji** — the intelligent register earns the open. Formula: `{first}, "<maxim>" is half a truth. The other half matters more.`
- `{first}, "if they wanted to, they would" is half a truth. The other half matters more.`
- `{first}, "you'll find them when you stop looking" is half-wrong. The cruel half.`
- `{first}, "time heals" is a comforting lie. Here's what actually does.`

Preheader: *"The advice that sounds strong and leaves you stuck."*

## 6. The ask / CTA
Soft-to-medium (insight day that can convert). Benefit-worded: **"Help me see my pattern."** Single CTA. Tag `campaign=reframe-06-<maxim-slug>`.

## 7. Formatting notes
- The maxim in *italics* wherever it recurs.
- Reframe **pull-quote** carries the omitted half.
- **Bold** the new question or the pivot line (the "someone who trusts their worth…" beat).

## 8. Do / Don't
- **Do** genuinely grant the true half *first* — skip it and the whole thing reads as contrarian noise. The concession is what makes the turn land.
- **Do** turn the mirror to the reader's **agency**, not their blame.
- **Don't** be contrarian for its own sake. The reframe has to be *truer*, not just edgier.
- **Don't** leave them in a verdict ("he didn't want you"). End on a better *question* they get to answer.
