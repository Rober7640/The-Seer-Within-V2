# Format 02 — The parable

*Read [PLAYBOOK.md](../PLAYBOOK.md) and [hooks.md](../hooks.md) first. This spec only adds what's specific to this format.*

**Type:** insight (pure value) · **The turn:** a strange behavior/object → what it's *really* protecting

## 1. Purpose / when to use
A short story — someone's small, strange ritual, or a spare fable — that lands on a reframe. This is a **pure value day**, no sell pressure. It builds Evelyn as a storyteller, deepens trust, and lets the reader recognize themselves at a slant (safer than being told directly). Use it to give the deck breathing room between conversion beats, and when the theme is tender (grief, clinging, moving on).

## 2. The reframe mechanic
Take a behavior that *looks like* one thing — denial, weakness, being unable to let go — and reveal what it's **actually doing**: protecting a capacity, keeping hope safe, honoring something worth honoring. The story is the vehicle; the reframe is *"here's what the observer couldn't see."* The reader supplies the connection to their own life; you never have to force it.

## 3. The hook
**Pattern:** the strange act / image ([hooks.md](../hooks.md) #2) — the exemplar pattern of the whole deck. A concrete, faintly irrational behavior, stated flat, with the reason withheld.

Hook bank:
- "She watered a dead plant for a year. I finally saw why." — *the gold standard: strange act + withheld reason.*
- "He kept one shirt in the closet that wasn't his. For three years, dear." — *object + oddity + a time-span that implies devotion.*
- "Every morning she set two cups. She lived alone." — *ritual + the flat second sentence that turns it.*
- "There's a man in my town who still walks his dog's old route. The dog died last spring." — *tender, specific, a small ache.*
- "She learned his coffee order by heart the week *after* he left." — *the wrongness of the timing is the snag.*

## 4. The skeleton
1. **The strange act** (hook) — stated flat, one line.
2. **The scene** — a detail or two: the ritual, the object, and who's watching (a sister, a neighbor, the town).
3. **The observer's obvious read** — *"Everyone thought she'd lost her grip"* (the surface interpretation, dramatized).
4. **The turn line** — *"Here is what they couldn't see."*
5. **The reframe** (pull-quote) — what the act was really doing.
6. **Generalize to *we / you*** — mirror it to the reader's life: *"the texts we reread, the keepsake we won't box up."*
7. **The reassurance + mechanism** — name the thing that isn't actually lost; why it isn't.
8. **The practice** — transform the ritual, don't kill it (*"plant something living in the same pot"*).
9. **Soft ask** — if you know which dead plant is yours, come sit with me.

## 5. Subject + preheader
Usually **no emoji** (the quiet register earns attention by image, not noise). Formula: `{first}, <the strange act>. <withheld reason>.`
- `{first}, she watered a dead plant for a year. I finally saw why.`
- `{first}, he kept a shirt that wasn't his for three years.`
- `{first}, she set two cups every morning. She lived alone.`

Preheader — the quiet turn: *"It was never about the plant."*

## 6. The ask / CTA
Soft, value-day close. Benefit-worded and gentle: **"Come tell me what you're still watering."** Single CTA; it's an invitation, not a push. Tag `campaign=reframe-02-parable`.

## 7. Formatting notes
- The reframe **pull-quote** lands right after *"here's what they couldn't see."*
- *Italics* on any quoted dialogue (the sister's lines).
- No quote box needed unless there's a spoken line worth setting apart.

## 8. Do / Don't
- **Do** keep the central image concrete and a little strange — that oddity is the hook.
- **Do** vary the protagonist's gender across uses.
- **Don't** state the moral before the reframe; let the story earn it.
- **Don't** slide into greeting-card sentiment. The reframe must be a real mechanism ("we keep dead rituals to keep a capacity alive"), not a warm feeling restated.
- **Don't** over-"dear" it — the story carries the warmth; **2 is plenty here** (the gold parable uses 2).

## 10. Fill-in template
```
Subject: {{ subscriber.first_name | capitalize }}, <strange act, stated flat>. <withheld reason>.
Preheader: It was never about the <object>.

<Someone> <did the strange act> for <a telling length of time>.

<A line of scene: the object, the ritual, the detail.>

<The watcher> thought <they'd lost their grip>. *"<the obvious-read line>,"* <they>'d say. And <the person> would <nod>, because <they> knew. <They> <weren't confused>.

Here is what <the watcher> couldn't see.

> **↳ THE REFRAME.** <They> weren't <the surface act>. <They> were <what it was really doing — keeping a capacity/hope alive>.

That is what we do with <the dead things we won't let go of>, dear. <The reader's versions: the texts, the keepsake, the anniversary.> It looks like <the surface read>. Usually it's the opposite.

**<The reassurance — the thing that isn't actually buried.>** <Why it's theirs to keep.>

So here is what I told <them>, and what I'll tell you. <The practice: transform the ritual, don't destroy it.>

If you know exactly which <dead plant> is yours, dear, come sit with me. <soft invitation.>

**→ <benefit CTA>**

— Evelyn
```
