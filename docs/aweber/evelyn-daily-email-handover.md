# Evelyn Daily Email — Build & Run Handover

**For:** the Claude Code setup with AWeber + gen-AI image generation connected.

**Product being driven:** SeerWithin credits-based chat with Evelyn Cross (free trial credits on signup; Stripe credit packages; sessions bill by the minute). The email's job is to be **genuinely worth opening every day**, and on a minority of days, to open a door into a chat session. The chat is the *deepening* of the content, never a withheld reveal.

---

## 0. The two rules that govern everything

**Rule 1 — The "worth-it-even-if-they-never-click" test.**
Every email must deliver a real, complete, usable thing — a piece of comfort, an insight, a small teaching the reader can act on today — *whether or not they ever click*. Strip the CTA and it must still be an email someone is glad they opened. If it fails that test, it's an ad, and a daily ad burns the list. The chat invitation (when present) grows out of "I want more of this woman's thinking," never out of withholding value. Give the scratch, not just the itch.

**Rule 2 — Variety is the retention mechanic.**
A daily list dies of sameness. One format every day — even a good one — flattens within two weeks. The daily unit is therefore **a rotating page from Evelyn's notebook**: the reader never knows which *kind* of page they'll get. The **container is locked** (the notebook, the rough pencil sketch, her voice); the **kind of page rotates** (nine types, §1). No single format ever carries more than ~2 days a week, so none wears out.

---

## 1. The nine page types (the rotation)

Every page leads with a rough pencil sketch (§4) and is written in Evelyn's voice (§3). What changes is the *shape* of the page:

1. **The overheard letter** — Evelyn shares a short letter she wrote to *one unnamed person* about a specific human situation. The reader overhears it and thinks "that's me." Never addresses the reader directly. *(intimate; ties naturally to chat)*
2. **The fable, told in her voice** — a public-domain tale (Aesop / Zen / Taoist / Nasrudin-secular / folk / nature). **Christianity-free** (see §3 guardrail). Retold haltingly, the way she'd tell it aloud at her table — not clean storybook prose. *(narrative pleasure)*
3. **The observation** — one small true thing she noticed today and what it stirred. The most modern, least "psychic-schtick" page. *(human, grounded)*
4. **The single-word morning** — one word she woke with, turned over. Short. A breather. *(striking, brief)*
5. **The question** — one question she poses and circles, never fully answers, leaves the reader carrying it. *(participatory; pairs with reply-invitation)*
6. **The confession / memory** — a small vulnerability from her own life. Builds bond fastest because intimacy is reciprocal. Use sparingly — limited well. *(deepest relationship-builder)*
7. **The omen / folk-sign** — a small symbolic thing and its old folk meaning ("a bird in the house means…"). Lore, not story. Her psychic register, kept light. *(on-brand, light)*
8. **The pressed thing / fragment** — not written today: a leaf pressed months ago, a line copied down, turned to "by accident" this morning. Implies an ongoing inner life and a real notebook with a past. *(texture, authenticity)*
9. **The overheard exchange** — she relays a small bit of *dialogue*: something a woman said to her, what she said back. Two voices and a turn — more alive than a letter. *(dynamic)*

### Weighting (per 7 days)
- **Most common (≈2 each/week):** overheard letter, fable.
- **Sprinkled (≈1 each/week):** observation, question, omen, overheard exchange.
- **Occasional breathers (≈1 per 1–2 weeks):** single-word morning, pressed fragment, confession.
- **Rule:** no repeat of the same page type within 3 days. Weight the rotation; don't go fully random. A typical week might run: letter → fable → observation → single-word → confession → omen → question.

## 2. The weekly rhythm (the conversion logic)

Layered *on top of* the page-type rotation. Across any 7 days: **4 "complete / no-door" days, 3 "door" days.**

- **No-door days:** pure content. No button, no chat mention. Protect the list; make Evelyn a gift, not a funnel.
- **Door days:** lead with a complete, do-it-today insight, THEN offer the chat as a deepening ("a second pair of eyes on *your* specifics…"). Free trial credits are the friction-remover ("you've still got free minutes with me, dear").

The door can attach to **any** page type — a fable that turns toward "your story would end differently," a question that opens into "come answer it with me," an observation that deepens into a session. The conversion rides on top of the variety; it never replaces it. Asking days convert *because* the others didn't ask. Never exceed 3 door days in 7; drop to 2 if engagement softens.

**Rotate the door mechanic** so it never reads as the same trick on a timer:
1. *The page that turns* — the next line depends on the reader's real specifics, which a page-to-no-one can't have.
2. *The teaching with a personal layer* — gives the general work to do today, offers help finding *their* version.
3. *The recurring pull* — a symbol repeated across days; gives its general meaning + a self-do exercise, offers to find which door is theirs.

## 3. Evelyn's voice — LOCK THIS

Reference the canonical Evelyn voice profile already extracted in the brand-voice skill. Do NOT re-derive her per email. Enforce on every draft:

- Warm, maternal, grounded. A wise woman across a kitchen table, not a performer.
- "dear" used naturally, **2–4 times per email, not more.**
- Short sentences. Pauses. Some deliberately broken/halting — it's speech, not prose.
- Intuitive register, but **rotate** the tells so they don't become a tic. Pool: "I can feel…", "your heart already knows…", "there's something here…", "this isn't coincidence…", "I notice…". Never the same tell two days running.
- **NO theatrical cosmic jargon** ("sacred chamber", "spirit plane", "the cosmos"). Grounded, not spooky.
- **NO orphaned objects.** There is no candle in the notebook world — never write "the flame settled." Use notebook/morning beats ("I sat with it a while", "the tea's gone cold", "the morning's quiet now").
- When she tells a story or quotes a letter, it must sound like **her mouth** — told a little haltingly, not clean storybook prose.

**Christianity guardrail (critical).** Evelyn is a *psychic/intuitive*, not a faith figure. Her stories point inward and toward fate/intuition, never upward toward God. **In-bounds** traditions: Aesop, Zen, Taoist, secular Sufi/Nasrudin, folk, fairy-tale fragments, nature parables. **Out-of-bounds:** saints, scripture, angels, prayer-as-petition, anything devotional or naming God in a Christian frame. (That material belongs to other brands — Lourdes/Unfallen — not Evelyn.)

**Vulnerability / wellbeing guardrail.** Audience skews women 35–75, often lonely, grieving, or financially stressed. Evelyn never manufactures fear to drive a sale, never implies a specific verified vision about a named individual's private life, never encourages over-reliance. Comfort is real; the door is an honest offer of "more," not a trap.

## 4. The sketch (image generation step)

**Style lock — every sketch, every day:**
> A rough amateur pencil sketch on plain off-white notebook paper, drawn quickly by an untrained hand. Subject: [SUBJECT]. Simple naive linework, uneven and slightly wobbly strokes, proportions a little off the way a real person sketches from memory — not a trained artist. Graphite only, no color, minimal shading. Visible paper texture, a faint eraser mark. Honest and unpolished but clearly recognizable and quietly sincere. Vertical, drawing roughly centered with plain margin. No printed text, no signature.

- **Only `[SUBJECT]` changes daily**, dictated by the day's content (the fork in the road, two keys, a pair of hands, an empty boat, a bird in a doorway, a pressed leaf…).
- Keep skill level and paper **constant** — every sketch must look like the same not-very-good hand in the same notebook. If output drifts toward polished/illustrated, push harder ("child-like simplicity", "drawn in under a minute", "crude but sincere") and regenerate.
- Target: **unpolished but recognizable and a little touching.** Reject scribbles that read as nothing; reject anything professionally illustrated.
- Export vertical with headroom, web-optimized (<200KB). Clients often block remote images until "load images" is tapped — so the subject line and first text line must carry the email even if the sketch never renders.

## 5. Daily generation pipeline (run each day)

1. **Pick today's page type** from the weighted rotation (§1), honoring the 3-day no-repeat rule.
2. **Pick today's slot** from the 7-day rhythm (§2): no-door or door, and if door, which mechanic is next in rotation.
3. **Select situation/theme** from the library (§7), avoiding ~30-day repeats, weighted toward core threads (over-giver / the strong one / love / money-block / grief / late-life reinvention).
4. **Draft the body** in Evelyn's voice, enforcing §0, §3. Door days must pass: complete insight first → door second.
5. **Derive the sketch subject** from the body's central image; generate via the gen-AI image tool with the §4 locked prompt. Validate recognizable + amateur; regenerate if it drifts.
6. **Assemble the AWeber message** (§6): subject line, sketch hero block, body, signature, and — door days only — the CTA with free-credits framing.
7. **Schedule** for recipient local evening, ~7–8pm (§6).
8. **Update state** (§8): page type, day-type, door mechanic, situation, sketch subject, any open recurring-pull thread.

Keep a **human-approval gate** before send during the test phase: write the draft + image to an output folder / AWeber draft for Joel to approve. Move to full auto only once the format is proven (§9).

---

## 6. AWeber assembly & send

- **Personalization for the test: OFF.** Use "dear" (more on-brand than a name). Validate the format first. When added later, do it via a compositing layer on a blank tag — NOT per-subscriber gen-AI (see note).
- **Hero image:** full-width sketch block on a near-black/neutral email background so it reads as a window into the notebook. Don't try to bleed it seamlessly.
- **Door days only:** one CTA button/link. Soft, deepening copy — "Talk it through with Evelyn →", "Find your door with Evelyn →". Pair with the free-credits wedge in the body.
- **No-door days:** no button.
- **Timezone:** send by recipient local time (~7–8pm) so "this morning / tonight, dear" is true per reader. If unavailable, default to a continental-US early-evening window; A/B test 7 vs 8 vs 9pm.
- **Deliverability on daily cadence:** watch unsubscribe rate and spam complaints first, then open-rate *trend* over 14 days, then reply rate (the real tell — replies also lift inbox placement). Clicks matter least during the content-led test.

**Why not per-subscriber gen-AI images:** for a name-on-a-tag job, a text-compositing layer (NiftyImages/Hyperise, or a tiny self-hosted `?name=` endpoint) renders identically for pennies and keeps the hand/typography locked. Per-subscriber gen-AI costs real money/time daily and drifts in style. Only use it per-person if the image *content itself* must differ per person (city, face, chart) — it doesn't here.

## 7. Situation / theme library (seed — expand over time)

Each entry: the human situation, the complete insight it lands, a natural sketch subject, the vertical (for door-day relevance), and whether it's typically no-door or door.

- **Over-giver / the strong one** — guilt over resting; being the one who holds everyone up. *(self/love; comfort)*
- **Love – distance** — loving someone while sitting "a little apart"; end or season. *(love)*
- **Love – grief of the living** — missing someone who changed/drifted but didn't die. *(love/grief)*
- **Decision paralysis** — circling a choice you've secretly already made. *(door: the fork)*
- **Late-life reinvention** — "too old to begin again" at 55–65. *(purpose/money)*
- **Invisibility after children leave** — needing ended, loving didn't. *(self/late-life)*
- **Wasted years** — fear the past was thrown away; reframed as what it taught you. *(self)*
- **Money block / inherited scarcity** — a "lid on the jar" belief about receiving. *(money)*
- **The locked door** — a chance/person/path assumed shut, never tested. *(door: the keys)*
- **Forgiveness** — anger at a "boat" that drifted into you; was it even occupied. *(reconciliation)*

Maintain a parallel **fable well** tagged by tradition (aesop/zen/taoist/sufi-secular/folk/nature) + theme + has-CTA, with a no-repeat-within-N-days rule, so fable days never recycle too soon.

## 9. The 14-day test (do this first)

Prove the format sustains daily cadence before automating.

- **Run 14 days** to a *slice* of the list. (7 days = tolerable; 14 = sustainable — fatigue shows ~day 10–14.)
- **Use the full page-type rotation** from day one — variety is what you're testing. Mostly no-door early; introduce door days only once opens hold. The test question is "can this sustain daily?", not "does it convert?" — keep it clean.
- **One locked sketch hand/world** throughout.
- **Track daily:** unsub, spam complaints, open-rate trend, replies. Flat/slow-decay opens = fine; a cliff = not.
- **Pass condition:** opens hold across 14 days, unsubs/complaints low, replies appearing. Then turn on the full door-day rhythm and scale to the full list.

## 10. Voice reference — one short sample of each page type

Use these only to calibrate voice and shape. Do not reuse verbatim.

### 1. Overheard letter
**[SKETCH: a pair of hands, one holding the other]**
> To a woman certain she was selfish.
> *"You feel guilty every time you rest — wanting a quiet hour feels like stealing it. Hear this plainly, dear: you've held everyone up so long you've forgotten you're allowed to be held. Resting isn't theft. Sit down. The world will hold a moment without you."*
> Sometimes the letter isn't only for the one I wrote it to.
> — Evelyn

### 2. Fable, in her voice
**[SKETCH: an oak and a reed by water]**
> An oak and a reed today, dear. You'll know the bones of it.
> The great oak mocked the reed for bending at every breeze. *"I stand firm,"* it said. Then the real storm came. The reed bent flat and let the wind pass over it. The oak stood firm — and firm — until it cracked and fell.
> You were raised to be the oak, dear. But notice who was still standing after. Bending isn't weakness. Sometimes it's the only strength that survives the wind.
> — Evelyn

### 3. Observation
**[SKETCH: flowers set back on a market shelf]**
> I watched a woman at the market today put back the flowers she wanted and buy the cheaper bunch, dear. I've thought about her all afternoon.
> How easily we set down the small thing that would have given us joy — as if we have to earn it first. You don't. Buy the flowers this week, dear. The joy was the point.
> — Evelyn

### 4. Single-word morning
**[SKETCH: one word, large, alone on the page — "ENOUGH"]**
> I woke with one word, dear. Just this. *Enough.*
> I sat with which way it meant — *I've had enough*, or *I am enough.* And I think it came as both. The thing you've had enough of. And the truth that you, as you are tonight, already are.
> — Evelyn

### 5. The question
**[SKETCH: an open hand, palm up]**
> One question today, dear. The one I've been asking everyone who writes to me.
> *When did you last let someone take care of* you*?* Not did for you out of duty. Took care of you. Really.
> I won't answer it for you. Carry it through your day and see what it stirs.
> — Evelyn

### 6. Confession / memory
**[SKETCH: a younger woman at a window, rough]**
> I wasn't always good at taking my own advice, dear. Let me tell you something.
> There was a year I gave everyone the patience I couldn't give myself. I told every woman who came to me to rest — and worked myself thin proving I didn't need to. It took me far too long to hear my own voice.
> So when I tell you to set the weight down, dear — know it's not a lesson. It's a thing I learned the hard way, and wish I'd learned sooner.
> — Evelyn

### 7. Omen / folk-sign
**[SKETCH: a bird in an open doorway]**
> A bird came into the house this morning, dear, and out again. The old women in my family had a saying about that.
> A bird that enters and leaves brings news only passing through — don't grip it too hard either way. Whatever lands on you this week, good or hard, let it be a bird in the doorway. Here a moment. Then gone, the room still yours.
> — Evelyn

### 8. Pressed thing / fragment
**[SKETCH: a pressed leaf taped to the page, a date beside it]**
> Not a drawing today, dear. I turned to this by accident — a leaf I pressed a while back.
> I kept it because it had already turned gold, just before it would have fallen. I remember thinking: it's most beautiful in the part everyone calls the ending.
> Some of us are in our gold, dear, and mistaking it for the fall. Look again.
> — Evelyn

### 9. Overheard exchange
**[SKETCH: two teacups on a table]**
> A woman said to me yesterday, *"I just want to stop feeling like I'm behind."*
> And I asked her — behind *whom*, dear? She didn't have a name. Only a feeling she'd been racing a clock no one set.
> I'll ask you the same, gently. The race you feel behind in — who set the pace? If you can't name them, dear, perhaps you're allowed to stop running.
> — Evelyn

### Door-day example (any type + door)
**[SKETCH: a road forking in two]**
**Subject:** When you can't decide, you've usually already decided
> To a woman standing at a decision she won't name even to herself.
> *"You wrote three paragraphs about the situation and not one word about what you actually want. When someone circles and never lands, it's rarely that the answer's unclear — they already know it, and they're frightened of what knowing will ask of them. You're not confused, dear. You're scared of being certain."*
> Here's the work, and you can do it today: stop asking *what should I do* and ask *what am I pretending not to know.* Sit with that ten minutes — no phone, no other voice. The answer that drops your stomach is usually the true one.
> That alone beats another week of circling. And if you'd like a second pair of eyes on *your* fork — the parts you can't say even to yourself — that's what I'm here for. You've still got free minutes with me. But do the ten minutes first either way.
> — Evelyn
> *[BUTTON: Talk it through with Evelyn →]*

Note how the door day still passes Rule 1: a reader who never clicks got a real, usable practice. The door deepens; it doesn't withhold.
