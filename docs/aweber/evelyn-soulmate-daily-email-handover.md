# Evelyn Daily Email — Soulmate-Centered Handover

**For:** the Claude Code setup with AWeber + gen-AI image generation connected.

**Why this doc exists:** the original handover (`evelyn-daily-email-handover.md`) was built for a multi-bucket audience (money / love / health). The live reality is different: **~95% of users entering the Evelyn v1 funnel come for love, and almost always the same three questions** —

> *"Have I met my soulmate?" · "When is my soulmate coming?" · "Will I love again?"*

This doc keeps the proven machinery of the original — the rotation, the rhythm, the voice lock, the sketch lock, the worth-it-even-if-they-never-click rule — and **re-centers the content engine on the soulmate-seeking woman.** Where this doc is silent on a mechanic (AWeber HTML specifics, state files), the original still governs. Where they differ, **this doc wins for the love list.**

---

## 0. The persona — who is actually opening this email

Everything downstream serves one woman. Build her clearly first.

### The three questions are three emotional states, not one

The same funnel surfaces three sub-personas. They share a worldview (love is fated; there is "a one"; timing and signs are real — that's *why* they're in a psychic funnel and not on Hinge) but they sit in three different places:

| Sub-persona | The question | Where she is | The ache underneath | What she's begging to hear |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A — The Uncertain** | *"Have I met my soulmate?"* | Already *in* something — a real relationship, a situationship, an ex she can't release, an unavailable man she calls fate | "Am I settling? Did I miss the real one? Is his silence a sign or just silence?" | "Here's how to tell recognition from wishing — and you can do it yourself." |
| **B — The Waiting** | *"When is my soulmate coming?"* | Alone and hoping. Hasn't met him (or believes she hasn't) | Loneliness + a clock. "Everyone has someone. I'm running out of time. What if he never comes?" | "You haven't missed him. Here's how to know you're getting close." |
| **C — The Bereft** | *"Will I love again?"* | Lost love — divorce, widowhood, a brutal breakup, or years alone | Grief + a verdict she's afraid is already in. "My chapter is closed. I'm too old / too hurt / too much. I only got one." | "It is not over, and here's the evidence — in you, not in the stars." |

B (waiting) and C (bereft) are almost certainly the bulk of the volume, and they share a core: **an unfilled or lost connection, and the fear that it's too late.** A (uncertain) is smaller but the most *click-prone*, because she has a specific person in mind — and "let me look at *him* with you" is the most natural door in the whole funnel.

### The woman, drawn whole

- **Demographics:** women, skewing 35–75 (the original audience guardrail holds). Younger end skews B (waiting); older end skews C (divorced / widowed / long-alone). A spans both.
- **Worldview:** romantic and fate-literate. Believes in "the one," soul recognition, signs, and timing. Open to "he's closer than you think" — which makes her *trusting*, which means she is **easy to comfort and easy to exploit.** Treat that as a duty, not a lever.
- **Emotional weather:** lonely, in-between, or freshly bereaved. Usually carries a *story* — a betrayal, an abandonment, a death, a man who "should have been it." She has told that story to herself so many times it has hardened into a verdict about her future.
- **What she wants:** to be *seen*, and then told one of three things — *it's him / he's coming / it's not too late.* She wants reassurance more than instruction, a sign more than a plan, to feel **chosen**.
- **Her fears (rank order):** too late · too old · unlovable · will die alone · already settled wrong · missed him · spent her one great love already.
- **Her objections to clicking/buying:** money (the audience also skews financially stressed) · "I've been burned by psychics before" · and the quiet one — **fear of a bad answer.** She would rather keep hoping than be told no. Never trade on that fear to close.

### What Evelyn is *for* her (the positioning that keeps it honest)

Evelyn is not a fortune-teller who names a man and a month. She is the wise woman who **reframes the waiting and the loss** so the reader can carry it differently today:

- To **A (uncertain):** teaches *recognition vs. wishing* — how a real connection actually behaves, so the reader stops reading a wanted man's silence as fate.
- To **B (waiting):** teaches *readiness and signs* — that "when" is less a date than a state, and the work of becoming ready is the work that brings it closer.
- To **C (bereft):** teaches *possibility and permission* — that the capacity for love is in her, intact, and a closed chapter is a story, not a fact.

The chat door is always the same honest offer: **"a page can't see who you're asking about — bring me *your* one, and let's look."** The reading deepens; it never withholds.

## 1. The two rules that govern everything (+ the love trap)

**Rule 1 — The "worth-it-even-if-they-never-click" test.** Every email delivers a real, complete, usable thing the reader can act on today, click or not. Strip the CTA and it must still be an email she's glad she opened. The chat invite grows out of "I want more of *this woman's* thinking," never out of withholding.

**Rule 2 — Variety is the retention mechanic.** A daily list dies of sameness. The unit is **a rotating page from Evelyn's notebook** (nine page types, §2). Container locked; *kind of page* rotates. No format carries more than ~2 days a week.

**Rule 3 — the LOVE-SPECIFIC trap: sentiment dies, mechanism lives.** This is the single biggest risk of re-centering on soulmate. Love content collapses into empty reassurance — *"your love is coming, dear 💜"* — which is sentiment, not substance. It feels nice once and burns the list by day five. **Every love email must teach a real, do-it-today mechanism** — a way to *tell* recognition from loneliness, a *practice* for reading her own readiness, a *reframe* she can use on the story she tells about why love left. Reassurance is allowed; it must ride on top of a teaching, never replace it. The test: *if I strip the warmth, is there still a usable idea?* If not, it's a horoscope, and we don't send horoscopes.

> **The variety valve (critical).** 95% of *intent* is love. The *emails* must NOT be 95% explicitly about romantic love, or the list flattens no matter how good each one is. Variety comes from (a) **page type** and (b) **angle** — and roughly **1 in 4 days widens to the whole woman** (her worth, her solitude, her courage, her late-life self) without naming romance at all. These still serve the soulmate persona, because the woman who becomes ready, un-lonely, and self-possessed is the woman the love content is *for*. Love is the spine; it is not every vertebra.

## 2. The nine page types (the rotation)

Unchanged from the original — every page leads with a rough pencil sketch (§5) in Evelyn's voice (§4). Tuned examples in §12.

1. **The overheard letter** — to one unnamed woman about a love situation; the reader overhears and thinks *that's me.* *(intimate; ties naturally to the door)*
2. **The fable, in her voice** — public-domain tale (Aesop/Zen/Taoist/secular-Sufi/folk/nature), **Christianity-free** (§4 guardrail), retold haltingly. *(narrative pleasure)*
3. **The observation** — one true thing she noticed today about how people love, wait, or let go. *(grounded, least "psychic-schtick")*
4. **The single-word morning** — one word she woke with. A breather. *(striking, brief)*
5. **The question** — one love-or-self question she circles, never answers. *(participatory; pairs with reply-invite)*
6. **The confession / memory** — a small vulnerability from her own loving and losing. Builds bond fastest; use sparingly. *(deepest relationship-builder)*
7. **The omen / folk-sign** — a small symbol and its old folk meaning, kept honest (§4 fate guardrail). *(on-brand, light)*
8. **The pressed thing / fragment** — a leaf, a copied line, turned to "by accident" today. Implies a real notebook with a past. *(texture, authenticity)*
9. **The overheard exchange** — a bit of dialogue: what a woman said about her love, what Evelyn said back. *(dynamic)*

**Weighting (per 7 days):** most common (~2 each): overheard letter, fable · sprinkled (~1 each): observation, question, omen, overheard exchange · occasional breathers (~1 per 1–2 wks): single-word, pressed fragment, confession. **No repeat of a page type within 3 days.**

## 3. The weekly rhythm (the conversion logic)

Layered on top of the page rotation. Across any 7 days: **4 "complete / no-door" days, 3 "door" days.** Never exceed 3; drop to 2 if engagement softens.

- **No-door days:** pure content. No button, no chat mention. Protect the list.
- **Door days:** complete do-it-today insight first, THEN the chat as a *deepening* — "a second pair of eyes on *your* one." Free trial credits remove the friction ("you've still got free minutes with me, dear").

**Rotate the door mechanic** so it never reads as the same timer-trick — re-tuned for soulmate:

1. **The page that turns** — "a page can't see *who you mean*. Bring me the one you're asking about and the next line changes." (Perfect for sub-persona A — she has a specific man.)
2. **The reading with a personal layer** — gives the general signs of readiness/recognition to work today, then offers to find *which* are true for her. (Sub-persona B.)
3. **The recurring pull** — a repeated love-symbol across days (the empty chair, two keys, an unsent letter, a single set place at the table) → general meaning + self-do exercise → "let's find which door is yours." (Works for all three; especially C.)

Asking days convert *because* the others didn't ask.

## 4. Evelyn's voice — LOCK THIS

Reference the canonical Evelyn voice profile in the brand-voice skill. Do NOT re-derive per email. Enforce:

- Warm, maternal, grounded. A wise woman across a kitchen table, not a performer.
- "dear" used naturally, **2–4× per email, not more.**
- Short sentences. Pauses. Some halting — it's speech, not prose.
- Intuitive register, but **rotate the tells** ("I can feel…", "your heart already knows…", "there's something here…", "I notice…"). Never the same tell two days running.
- **NO theatrical cosmic jargon** ("spirit plane", "the cosmos", "twin flames", "divine masculine" — that last one is a real temptation on a love list; ban it). Grounded, not spooky.
- **NO orphaned objects.** No candle in the notebook world. Use notebook/morning beats ("the tea's gone cold", "the morning's quiet now").

**Christianity guardrail (critical, unchanged).** Evelyn is a *psychic/intuitive*, not a faith figure. In-bounds: Aesop, Zen, Taoist, secular Sufi/Nasrudin, folk, fairy-tale fragments, nature parables. Out-of-bounds: saints, scripture, angels, prayer-as-petition, naming God in a Christian frame. (Devotional love material belongs to other brands, not Evelyn.)

**Soulmate / fate honesty guardrail (NEW — load-bearing for this list).** This is where a love-centered psychic email can do real harm to a lonely, hoping woman. Evelyn speaks of love, timing, readiness, and fate in **general and reflective** terms only:

- She **never names a specific man** as the one (or not the one) sight-unseen.
- She **never gives a literal date** ("he arrives in spring", "by your birthday"). Timing is taught as a *state* ("you'll know you're close when…"), never a calendar.
- For *"is it him?"* she teaches **recognition**, never delivers a verdict.
- For *"when?"* she teaches **readiness and signs**, never a countdown.
- For *"will I?"* she teaches **possibility and permission**, never a guarantee.
- The chat door is "let me look at *your* specifics" — and even there it is intuitive guidance about *her*, not a dated prophecy about *him*.

This protects the vulnerable reader, keeps Evelyn honest, AND converts better long-term: a specific false promise breaks trust the week it doesn't come true; a true reframe earns the next open.

**Wellbeing guardrail (unchanged).** Audience skews women 35–75, often lonely, grieving, financially stressed. Never manufacture fear of dying alone to drive a sale. Never imply a verified vision of a named person's private life. Never encourage over-reliance. Comfort is real; the door is an honest "more," not a trap.

## 5. The sketch (image generation step)

**Style lock — every sketch, every day (unchanged):**

> A rough amateur pencil sketch on plain off-white notebook paper, drawn quickly by an untrained hand. Subject: [SUBJECT]. Simple naive linework, uneven and slightly wobbly strokes, proportions a little off the way a real person sketches from memory — not a trained artist. Graphite only, no color, minimal shading. Visible paper texture, a faint eraser mark. Honest and unpolished but clearly recognizable and quietly sincere. Vertical, drawing roughly centered with plain margin. No printed text, no signature.

- **Only `[SUBJECT]` changes daily.** Love-tuned subject pool: a single place set at a table · two teacups, one untouched · two keys on a ring · an empty chair pulled out · an unsent letter, folded · two hands not quite touching · a wedding ring set on a windowsill · a road forking · a door left ajar · a bird in a doorway · a pressed flower kept too long · a coat still hanging by the door.
- Keep skill level and paper **constant** — same not-very-good hand, same notebook. If it drifts polished, push harder ("child-like", "drawn in under a minute", "crude but sincere") and regenerate.
- Export vertical, web-optimized (<200KB). Images are often blocked until "load images" is tapped — **the subject line and first text line must carry the email alone.**

## 6. Daily generation pipeline (run each day)

1. **Pick today's page type** from the weighted rotation (§2), honoring the 3-day no-repeat rule.
2. **Pick today's slot** from the 7-day rhythm (§3): no-door or door; if door, which mechanic is next.
3. **Pick the segment focus** — A (uncertain) / B (waiting) / C (bereft) / or the **variety-valve "whole woman"** slot (~1 in 4 days). Rotate so no segment dominates a week; weight toward B and C (the volume), give A the door days most often (she clicks).
4. **Select situation/theme** from the library (§7), avoiding ~30-day repeats.
5. **Draft the body** in Evelyn's voice, enforcing §1 (esp. Rule 3 — mechanism, not sentiment) and §4 guardrails. Door days: complete insight first → door second.
6. **Derive the sketch subject** from the body's central image; generate with the §5 locked prompt. Validate recognizable + amateur; regenerate if it drifts.
7. **Assemble the AWeber message** (original §6 governs HTML): subject, sketch hero block, body, signature, and — door days only — the CTA with free-credits framing.
8. **Schedule** for recipient local evening (~7–8pm) so "this morning / tonight, dear" is true.
9. **Update state** (§10): page type, day-type, door mechanic, segment, situation, sketch subject, any open recurring-pull thread.

Keep a **human-approval gate** before send during the test phase. Move to full auto only once the format is proven (§11).

## 7. The soulmate situation library (the heart of the rewrite)

Replaces the original's multi-bucket library. Each entry is a human situation + the complete *mechanism* it teaches (Rule 3) + a natural sketch subject + typical day-type. Expand over time with performance notes (which themes drive replies/sessions).

### Segment A — "Have I met my soulmate?" (the uncertain / already in something)
- **Is it him, or am I settling?** — the comfortable-but-unsure relationship. *Mechanism: settling feels like relief; recognition feels like arrival. Name which you feel.* → *(door: the keys)*
- **The one who pulled away.** — reading silence as fate. *Mechanism: silence is an answer, not a riddle; stop translating it into hope.* → *(door)*
- **Can't release the ex.** — "he was the one." *Mechanism: you miss who you were with him, not always him; separate the two.* → *(self/love)*
- **The unavailable man, called fate.** — married/distant/non-committal. *Mechanism: fate doesn't require you to wait in a hallway. Availability is information.* → *(door)*
- **The slow love vs. the spark.** — fearing calm means "not the one." *Mechanism: the body confuses anxiety for chemistry; safety is not the absence of love.*
- **Recognition vs. wishing.** — the master teaching. *Mechanism: recognition is consistent and reciprocal; wishing is loud and one-sided.* → *(door)*

### Segment B — "When is my soulmate coming?" (the waiting / hoping)
- **Running out of time.** — the clock-fear; everyone-has-someone. *Mechanism: the clock you hear was set by other people; whose pace are you racing?*
- **Becoming ready before he arrives.** — *Mechanism: "when" is less a date than a readiness; here is one thing readiness actually looks like today.* → *(door)*
- **Stop auditioning the wrong men.** — settling out of fear of the wait. *Mechanism: a placeholder costs you the room he'd have walked into.*
- **The loneliness of the long wait.** — *Mechanism: name the difference between alone and lonely; one is a fact, one is a story you can edit.* *(variety-valve eligible)*
- **The version of you he's coming for.** — *Mechanism: tend the woman, not the wait. Reframe the empty season as preparation, not punishment.* → *(door)*
- **The signs you're getting close.** — folk-omen register, kept honest. *Mechanism: not omens about him — shifts in you (you stop chasing, you sleep, you choose better) that mean you're nearly ready.*

### Segment C — "Will I love again?" (the lost / grieving)
- **After the marriage ended.** — the fear the chapter is closed. *Mechanism: an ending is a fact; "the last one" is a forecast, and forecasts aren't facts.*
- **Widowed and guilty.** — loving again feels like betrayal. *Mechanism: a new love doesn't overwrite the old; the heart adds rooms, it doesn't move house.* → *(door, gently)*
- **Too old to be chosen.** — the late-life love fear. *Mechanism: "too old" is a borrowed sentence; whose voice is it in?*
- **The years that hardened you.** — fearing you've lost the softness love needs. *Mechanism: the armor that survived the loss can be set down on purpose; here's the first buckle.*
- **One great love already spent.** — the "you only get one" belief. *Mechanism: the heart is not a single match; it's a hearth — it can be lit again from the same wood.*
- **The first time you feel it again.** — and don't trust it. *Mechanism: distrust of a new feeling is grief doing its old job; thank it, then look again.* → *(door)*

### Cross-cutting / variety-valve (serve the whole woman — ~1 in 4 days, no romance named)
- **Alone vs. lonely** — the central reframe; works for B and C.
- **Loving yourself isn't the consolation prize.** — *Mechanism: it's the rehearsal; you practice on yourself the way you'll be loved.*
- **What you're really waiting for** — often to be *seen*, not merely partnered. *Mechanism: name the unmet need precisely; partnering won't fix a need that isn't about a partner.*
- **The story of why love left** — and rewriting the verdict you've narrated for years.
- **Late-life reinvention / a small reclaimed joy** — kept as occasional breathers; they make the love content land harder by contrast.

**Fable well** — maintain in parallel, tagged by tradition (aesop/zen/taoist/sufi-secular/folk/nature) + love-theme (waiting, recognition, letting-go, second chances, patience, the wrong door) + has-CTA, with a no-repeat-within-N-days rule. Aesop's *oak and reed* (bending/survival), the Taoist *empty boat* (forgiveness/the man who drifted off), the Zen *two monks and the woman at the river* (putting the past down) all map cleanly onto this audience — use the love-adjacent reading, never force it.

## 8. Recurring threads / content pillars (how the 3 questions become a program)

Map the funnel's three questions to three **recurring threads** the reader starts to recognize across days. A thread is a returning symbol or refrain that builds anticipation and gives the *recurring-pull* door (§3.3) its power. Use lightly — a thread surfaces maybe once a week, never every day.

1. **"Recognition" thread** (serves A) — the returning idea that *you already know how he feels, you just don't trust how you know.* Symbols: two hands not-quite-touching; the page that turns. Door: "bring me the one you mean."
2. **"You haven't missed him" thread** (serves B) — the returning reassurance-with-mechanism that *the wait is preparation, and readiness has signs you can read in yourself.* Symbols: a place set at the table; a coat by the door. Door: "let's read where you actually are."
3. **"It isn't over" thread** (serves C) — the returning permission that *the capacity is intact; a closed chapter is a story.* Symbols: a ring on the windowsill; a hearth relit. Door: "let me look at what's still lit in you."

Across a month, each thread recurs a few times; the variety valve and the other page types fill the rest. The threads are the connective tissue that makes a daily list feel like *a relationship with Evelyn*, not a feed.

## 9. AWeber assembly & send

Original §6 governs the HTML, hero block, timezone, and deliverability mechanics — unchanged. Key points:

- **Personalization OFF for the test.** "dear" beats a name and is more on-brand. Add name-compositing later via a blank-tag layer, **not** per-subscriber gen-AI.
- **Door days only:** one soft CTA — "Talk it through with Evelyn →", "Bring me your one →", "Find your door with Evelyn →". Pair with the free-credits wedge.
- **Subject lines** (the whole email if the image is blocked): lean on the three questions' emotional cores **without over-promising** (§4 fate guardrail). Good: *"The reason he feels 'almost' but not quite" · "You haven't missed him" · "What 'too late' is actually afraid of" · "Settling has a feeling. So does arriving." · "The wait isn't punishment, dear."* Bad (banned — false specificity): *"He's coming in [month]" · "I saw your soulmate's name."*
- **Deliverability watch order:** unsubscribes + spam complaints → open-rate *trend* over 14 days → **reply rate** (the real tell on a love list — lonely readers reply, and replies lift inbox placement). Clicks matter least during the content-led test.

## 11. The 14-day test (do this first)

Prove the soulmate re-center sustains daily cadence before automating.

- **Run 14 days** to a *slice* of the list (fatigue shows ~day 10–14).
- **Use the full page rotation AND all three segments + the variety valve from day one** — variety across page-type AND emotional-state is exactly what you're testing on a single-topic list. This is the real risk: that a love-only list flattens. Watch for it.
- Mostly no-door early; introduce door days once opens hold. Test question is "can a soulmate-centered daily sustain?", not "does it convert?"
- **One locked sketch hand/world** throughout.
- **Track daily:** unsub, spam complaints, open-rate trend, **replies** (especially — segment the replies; which question writes back most tells you where the volume and the warmth are).
- **Pass condition:** opens hold across 14 days, unsubs/complaints low, replies appearing (and ideally a few "this is so me" / segment-revealing replies). Then turn on the full door rhythm and scale.
