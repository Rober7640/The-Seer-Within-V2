# V1 money bucket — what people actually ask, in their own words

**What this is.** Every conversation in the V1 funnel where the person chose "Money & Abundance", read and sorted into themes. For each theme: what it is, who the person is, and their own words.

**Data.** 12,640 money conversations, 2,449 of them buyers. 10,514 of those wrote something real when asked what was weighing on them; those are the ones sorted here, and they contain 2,373 of the buyers. Every quote below is verbatim, spelling and all. Date range 31 Jan – 12 Aug 2026.

**How to read the buy-rate.** 22.6% of these people bought. A theme's **index** is its buy-rate against that. 1.30 means people who raise that theme buy 30% more often than average. Themes are multi-label — one concern can carry up to three — so the shares add to more than 100%.

**Read the front end and the back end separately.** Main/buyer is flat at $35–38 everywhere — everyone pays the same price. So the entire spread in `$ per 1,000` is conversion × back end. Retirement earns double job-hunting per thousand chats because it converts half again as well *and* its buyers are worth $17 of back end against $5.

**"Reached U1" is the column that explains most of it.** `upsell_offered` only flips when the buyer's browser actually lands on `/welcome1`, so it measures *reach*, not willingness. Only 35% of money buyers ever get there. That splits the back-end problem in two:

- **A reach problem** — the buyer never sees the offer. Retirement 56.1% vs debt 29.9% vs job hunting 20.9%.
- **An accept problem** — they see it and say no. Career 53.1% take it, the partner theme 26.5%, job hunting 25.8%.

**Debt is a reach problem, not an accept problem.** Debt buyers who reach the upsell take it at **42.5%** — better than the bucket's 40.4% and better than retirement's 39.1%. Their weak back end is almost entirely that only 3 in 10 arrive at the page. Fix the path and debt's economics move without changing a single ad.

**Only two themes have a genuine offer-match problem.** The partner theme (26.5%) and job hunting (25.8%) are the only ones well below the 40.4% average on the offered denominator. Job hunting is the one theme that is bad at both — fewest arrive, fewest of those say yes — which is why it is the floor of the whole bucket at $7,573.

**Order bump: not measurable here, don't read anything into it.** The bump launched at the end of July 2026; this window is 31 Jan – 12 Aug. Just **5 of 2,373 money buyers** ever saw one (4 of them in August, off 42 buyers). There is no per-theme bump uptake to report yet — re-run this table after ~30 days of bump-era money traffic.

That is not a reason to stop advertising to debt — it is a third of the bucket and produces the most buyers in raw numbers. It is a reason to stop assuming the loudest pain is the most valuable one. What to do about debt specifically is below.

## 1. Debt and bills — 3,189 concerns (30.3%), index 0.93, $9,613 per 1k

**What it is.** Present-tense survival. Not a goal, a countdown: the gas bill, the rent, the card company calling. Most of these are short — this is the theme people type in ten words because they are typing it between other worries.

**The persona — "Sixteen days from payday."** Working or recently stopped working. Income exists but does not reach the end of the month. They are not asking for wealth; they are asking for the bleeding to stop. They have usually been in this exact state for years, not months — when the chat asks how long, the answer is "a long time" or "about 40 years." They discount their own situation as they describe it ("I'm honest man", "I know things are changing, but…"). Several tell the reader up front they have no money to spend, then buy anyway.

**Their words**

> "16 days from getting paid and I have 6 cents to my name. My grandson needs a place to live and I need to help him plus I can't paid all my bills" *(buyer)*

> "I'm struggling with money, we don't have enough to pay bills, even to eat sometimes, and I don't have the energy to work, I'm really struggling" *(buyer)*

> "I'm struggling financially and got myself behind and I'm really struggling, I have credit cards calling me, my gas bill is up to $500 and that's just a couple things. I'm super stressed" *(buyer)*

> "If I need money to pay for is reading my bank account is empty just wanted you to know this before I we get started ok" *(buyer)*

> "I need room to breath financially. I'm tired of living paycheck to paycheck. Every time I seem to get ahead, something happens to sabotage my plan." *(buyer)*

> "So many bills no money an no food" *(non-buyer)*

> "I have $42.00 until my SS funds on may 12. I can barely make ends meet before I receive my funds." *(non-buyer)*

**For copy.** Their word is *breathe*, not *abundance*. "Room to breathe" and "get ahead" are theirs; "manifest" and "prosperity" are ours.

## 2. "When does it change?" — 1,377 concerns (13.1%), index 0.84, $8,600 per 1k

**What it is.** Not a description of a problem — a demand for a date. They have accepted the situation and want to know when the sentence ends. The weakest-converting theme in the bucket.

**The persona — "The one who has been waiting."** Has already been told, by somebody, that money is coming: another psychic, a dream, a prophecy, a feeling. They are not looking for a diagnosis, they are looking for a timestamp. This is why they convert badly — a reading that reveals a hidden block answers a question they did not ask. They wanted a calendar.

**Their words**

> "I need to know when my serious money problems will end." *(buyer)*

> "I've been waiting for the breakthrough..where things can finally come together" *(buyer)*

> "Living check to check, is it always gonna be like this?" *(buyer)*

> "Well they keep saying that a large amount of money is coming but nothing is there when is this going to happen" *(buyer)*

> "Will I ever be okay again financially?" *(non-buyer)*

> "I having a little hardship when can I see more money coming in my life" *(non-buyer)*

**For copy.** They ask "when". Everything about the offer answers "why". That gap is the conversion problem — either give them a timing beat early, or stop paying to acquire this question.

## 3. The invisible block — 1,208 concerns (11.5%), index 1.32, $14,083 per 1k

**What it is.** Something is in the way. Sometimes named (a curse, a neighbour, black magic, a generational pattern), often not — just a force that removes money at the last moment. **This is the theme the offer was built for, and it converts accordingly.**

**The persona — "It's not me, something is doing this."** Has evidence: near-misses. A deal that fell through at the last step, a blessing that vanished in days, a business that was almost sold for years. They have often already paid someone to remove it — one buyer had spent close to $11,000 on rituals over three years. They arrive already believing in the mechanism; they are shopping for the practitioner.

**Their words**

> "I keep trying to get a head in life and no matter what I do nothing seems to work out it's like something is holding me back" *(buyer)*

> "I sometimes feel I,am close to love , happiness money only to have it vanish ." *(buyer)*

> "I keep getting messages from different fortune tellers that I am going to come into some money, however, there is always a block, then it tells me about Karmic debt." *(buyer)*

> "Sometimes I see light and when I am suppose to get a breakthrough then all of sudden its gone what makes to be like that" *(buyer)*

> "I met Indian neighbor and he look at me and said. There are people blocking my luck and my future." *(buyer)*

> "i m being attack on every single levels so i cannot access to wealth and joy" *(buyer)*

> "Everything appears to be reaching me but doesn't. What's my future like?" *(non-buyer)*

> "It seems I have something blocking me from achieving abundance" *(non-buyer)*

**For copy.** The near-miss is the hook, not the poverty. "Every time you get close, it disappears" is their sentence, said back to them.

## 4. Housing — 1,125 concerns (10.7%), index 1.07, $11,938 per 1k

**What it is.** The house is the money worry, in three forms: cannot get one, cannot keep one, cannot afford to fix one. Frequently paired with debt (358 concerns carry both).

**The persona — "The roof is the problem."** Either the repair they cannot fund, the mortgage they are two months behind on, or the ownership they never reached. Older ones are stuck: cannot afford to stay, cannot afford to move. Younger ones want the door key as proof they have arrived.

**Their words**

> "I don't want to move but I can't afford to stay with all the repairs my house needs. I don't know what to do." *(buyer)*

> "My house is in preforclosure. My roof is Leaking." *(buyer)*

> "I am almost 2 months behind on my mortgage. I am not dating I am very lonely." *(buyer)*

> "I am being assessed a special assessment for new roof I live in a condo my share is 6,500.00 and I don't have it I don't know what to do!" *(buyer)*

> "My parents and my mom kid took my home I paid for away and im homeless with nothing and no one" *(buyer)*

> "I'm struggling to keep the house going by myself and only just making enough money with not much spare for repairs" *(non-buyer)*

**For copy.** Housing is concrete and dated — a foreclosure has a clock on it. Good for urgency without inventing any.

## 5. Providing for family — 979 concerns (9.3%), index 1.10, $12,927 per 1k

**What it is.** The money is not for them. It is for a daughter, a grandson, a sibling with a disability, a parent in care. Many are the sole support for several households at once.

**The persona — "It lands on me."** Middle-aged to older, the family's designated capable one. Their fear is not poverty, it is failing the people who count on them, or leaving debt behind when they die. They will spend on themselves reluctantly and on their family instantly — the offer converts when it is framed as protecting the people behind them.

**Their words**

> "I have over 150 people including family relying on me. A lot of who I thought was friends have burnt me and trying to destroy it." *(buyer)*

> "I have spent my money on hospital bills for family and have fallen behind in my bills. I'm 85 years old" *(buyer)*

> "I am in the process of retireing. My sons dont have proer jobs and rely on my wife and I financially." *(buyer)*

> "Pay off bills so it doesn't fall on my family" *(buyer)*

> "I need a generational wealth" *(buyer)*

> "I am struggling to help my daughter. I help her as uch as I can financialy because she lost her husband so young." *(buyer)*

> "Members of my family are really struggling, I'd like to be able to help them more" *(buyer)*

**For copy.** "So it doesn't fall on my family" is the strongest line in this theme. The motive is protection, not gain.

## 6. Worked hard, no reward — 972 concerns (9.2%), index 1.06, $11,372 per 1k

**What it is.** The fairness wound. Effort has been supplied and the return has not arrived. Distinct from the invisible block: no supernatural claim, just an accounting that does not balance.

**The persona — "I did everything right."** Long career, real qualifications, sometimes real past success. They compare — to peers, to family, to their own earlier self. What they want from the reading is an explanation, because the absence of one is what hurts. Just over a quarter of these (27%) also carry the block theme; that is the moment the fairness wound converts into a spiritual one.

**Their words**

> "I haved worked like a dog and am very capable yet finish up with little money." *(buyer)*

> "Why can't I get out of the rut. Every time I get close it's ripped out from under me" *(buyer)*

> "Pattern is work really hard make a lot of money, then bust! Lose it all." *(buyer)*

> "I feel not getting what I deserve rightly." *(buyer)*

> "Whatever I try there are no results that show me progress." *(buyer)*

> "its just not staying everytime i earned it" *(buyer)*

> "I'm trying to find My Inner peace & understand why I have to work so hard whilst others don't have to?" *(non-buyer)*

**For copy.** "Why do I have to work so hard when others don't?" is the theme in one line, written by a customer.

## 7. Money tangled with a partner — 834 concerns (7.9%), index 1.14, $11,003 per 1k

**What it is.** The money problem has a person in it: a divorce, a partner who took it, an ex who stopped paying, a romance that turned out to be an extraction. Also the reverse — needing money before they feel able to be loved.

**The persona — "It went out with them."** Recently or messily separated, or still inside it and calculating whether they can afford to leave. Financial abuse appears often and by name. A distinct sub-group is being romance-scammed in real time and is asking the reader to confirm the fantasy.

**Their words**

> "Money is extremely tight after my husband left me for his daughter-in-law. He isnt paying alimony" *(buyer)*

> "I've been thinking about parting ways with my boyfriend of 25 years and wondering how I'm going to pay bills and move either out his help" *(buyer)*

> "I have been a victim of fraud for $150,000. I am so far in debt that I really don't know where to turn. I have never before even been late on a payment and now my credit is ruined." *(buyer)*

> "I want to start working again. I got involved with a man who asked me to give up my high paying job saying he would take care of all my expenses and needs." *(buyer)*

> "I have been sending money to a soldier in Iraq to allow him to come back to the United States… We have fallen in love with each other and look forward to our first meeting at the airport" *(buyer)*

> "I'm bing told by friends and family that I'm being financially abused by my partner" *(non-buyer)*

**Handle with care.** The romance-scam group is being actively defrauded. Confirming their story in a reading is a real harm and a real refund/chargeback risk. Worth a policy decision, not a copy decision.

## 8. Job loss and job hunting — 783 concerns (7.4%), index 0.84, $7,573 per 1k

**What it is.** No job, or a job about to end. Concrete, urgent — and, with "when does it change", the weakest-converting theme in the bucket.

**The persona — "Applying and hearing nothing."** Often long-term: two years, seven years, "8years now being jobless". Older ones suspect their age is the real reason. Identity is bound to the lost role — one describes their job as "my identity and my pride". They want a job, and the offer does not sell one, which is exactly why they browse and leave.

**Their words**

> "I've had at least six jobs in the last 10 months and 90 interviews in the last year and none of them have been permanent jobs" *(buyer)*

> "I lost my job after 14 years and don't know where it what to do" *(buyer)*

> "It's been 8years now being jobless, everytime I get money it get finished before I can even çount it" *(buyer)*

> "worry about losing my job, my finances and my identity as they are all intertwined" *(buyer)*

> "I lost my job of 20 yrs , feel really lost , don't know what's in store for me , my job has been my identity and my pride , I feel abandoned" *(non-buyer)*

**For copy.** They want a job, not a clearing. If you target this group, sell timing and direction ("what's coming, and where to look"), not blockage removal.

## 9. Money owed or awaited — 706 concerns (6.7%), index 1.19, $12,497 per 1k

**What it is.** Money that exists somewhere else and has not arrived: a lottery win they expect, an inheritance, a settlement, back pay, a debtor who stopped answering. Just over a quarter of it (27%) is explicit lottery or gambling hope, converting at 29%.

**The persona — "It's coming, I just need to know when."** They are not asking whether — they have decided. They want confirmation and a date. Overlaps heavily with the waiting group, but converts far better, because there is a specific object to read on. Some are inside a scam and asking whether it is real.

**Their words**

> "Well they keep saying that a large amount of money is coming but nothing is there when is this going to happen" *(buyer)*

> "I'm just wondering if my luck will ever change for me to win a large sum of money to live comfortably for the rest of my life ??" *(buyer)*

> "The same as what I have told you before.I am scared,the people who owed me seems does not want to pay me anymore.They promise to pay me on May 30,but did not" *(buyer)*

> "My grandads will. I was meant to get a lot of money and the will got changed." *(buyer)*

> "I believe I am involved in a money scam regarding receiving a large sum of money plus a car which keeps asking me for more money ." *(buyer)*

> "When is my inheritance(wealth)arriving" *(non-buyer)*

**Compliance note.** Lottery and windfall promises are the sharpest legal edge in this bucket. Read the pattern, never the outcome.

## 10. Money tangled with illness — 680 concerns (6.5%), index 1.05, $12,010 per 1k

**What it is.** Illness or disability is the cause of the money problem: cannot work, medical bills, care costs, a disability claim being denied. The longest concerns in the bucket after grief — these people explain themselves at length.

**The persona — "My body took the income."** Frequently over 60. Either their own diagnosis or a spouse's, and often both plus caring duties. Fixed disability income against rising costs. They are matter-of-fact about the medical facts and emotional about the dependency.

**Their words**

> "I'm 69 and at 47 became disabled. Therefore I don't get much money." *(buyer)*

> "Pain financially physical my body just hurt I have lupus arthritis @ serve thyroid cancer too bone on bone hip spin problems I need something I just don't know what" *(buyer)*

> "I've been on a year long journey with Brest cancer, I've lost my job and home due to this." *(buyer)*

> "Just had hip replacement, so pain. At 78 I thought I would be set financially. But truth is , I am alone and see very few clients." *(buyer)*

> "My biggest issue is lack of money I am on disability and I only get 1000 a month and I have my granddaughter in my care and I feel useless and like a loser because of this lack" *(buyer)*

> "financial debts and the Veterans Administration continue to denied my disability claims" *(buyer)*

**Do not build ads on this.** Health and disability are restricted personal attributes on Meta. This is a real and sizeable group and a hard no for targeting — serve them once they are in, do not acquire on it.

## 11. Business and investment — 626 concerns (6.0%), index 1.24, $14,586 per 1k

**What it is.** A venture that has not paid off yet: a startup, a farm, a book, a crypto position, a property deal. The only theme where people write from a position of momentum rather than damage.

**The persona — "It's so close."** Has already staked something real — savings, years, a house's equity. The question is timing and whether to continue. Buys well because they are used to paying for information, and because there is a specific decision the reading can attach to.

**Their words**

> "Every business I begin, fails. It is urgent for me to begin a business that changes my financial status completely. This is because it is tied to my purpose" *(buyer)*

> "Stuck in Business since January 2024. Created 4 assets to generate revenue. Looking for the breakthrough to begin money flow to my bank account." *(non-buyer)*

> "I have been working night and day to complete a large fuel contract without success or any financial gain. Every time we are close to closing something interferes." *(buyer)*

> "I trusted an investor with my house a year ago trying to utilize my equity. Now the house is in foreclosure and I have never received my equity" *(buyer)*

> "My business is finally growing and after a very turbulent year 2026 is doing well. How do I keep the momentum going?" *(buyer)*

> "when will my business scale, when will new opportunities knok the door" *(non-buyer)*

**For copy.** "Every time we are close to closing, something interferes" — the block theme, in a businessperson's mouth. That crossover is the highest-value combination in the bucket.

## 12. Career decision — 608 concerns (5.8%), index 0.98, $11,291 per 1k

**What it is.** A live decision at work: quit or stay, a promotion that keeps not arriving, a manager cutting their hours, a pivot they cannot commit to. Underpayment is the recurring grievance.

**The persona — "Standing at the door."** Employed, competent, and stuck between a wage they need and a role they resent. They want permission and a direction. Converts at exactly average — the decision is real but not desperate.

**Their words**

## 13. Retirement and old age — 554 concerns (5.3%), index 1.24, $15,146 per 1k

**What it is.** The money has to last now, and the earning years are closing. This is by far the most age-identified theme in the bucket — people state their age unprompted ("I'm 61", "at 77", "I'm 85 years old") far more often here than anywhere else.

**The persona — "I thought I'd be set by now."** Sixties to eighties. Retired, retiring, or unable to. Fixed income against rising prices. Many are still working at 70+ or supporting adult children out of a pension. The emotion is not panic, it is a quiet arithmetic dread: *will it outlast me*. They buy well — time is short and they know it.

**Their words**

> "I retired at age 72 and have been having physical problems which are getting worse. Pain is wearing me out but I'm worried that I'll outlive my few savings." *(buyer)*

> "generating income at 77 yrs old" *(buyer)*

> "Prices are going up but my pension is not. I need to find a way to increase my income." *(buyer)*

> "I want to retire, but to afraid I won't be able to survive." *(buyer)*

> "I'm 61 and have been single for many many years… I'm worried that I'll have to work till I'm very old because I have no savings or superannuation." *(buyer)*

> "I retired last January and am robbing Peter to pay Paul every month." *(buyer)*

> "Never have enough with Social Security the only income." *(non-buyer)*

**For copy.** "I thought I would be set financially by now" is the emotional centre. Age is stated by them, so it can be reflected — but keep it their words, not a targeting claim.

## 14. Money and life purpose — 518 concerns (4.9%), index 1.19, $12,805 per 1k

**What it is.** Money framed as the thing standing between them and a mission — a book, a charity, a calling, a destiny. The problem is not survival, it is unrealised significance.

**The persona — "I'm meant for more than this."** Spiritually fluent — talks about destiny, missions, thresholds, chosen paths. Uses the vocabulary the funnel already speaks, which is why they convert well and are cheap to persuade. Their frustration is being ordinary while feeling assigned.

**Their words**

> "I keep hearing I'm here for bigger things but can only do that if money prosperity is there in the picture" *(buyer)*

> "I feel stuck but I know I'm destined for greatness! What do I need to do to get where I need to be in life!!!?" *(non-buyer)*

> "Moving forward in my purpose… I've been in the testing grounds for a while" *(buyer)*

> "I'm so close to doing my life purpose for work. But for several years, my income has been throttled. Why is that? What is blocking my massive generous abundance?" *(non-buyer)*

> "I know something good is waiting for me and i need to know what to do so i dont miss it" *(buyer)*

> "I want to be debt free, so I can pursue my dreams" *(buyer)*

**For copy.** This group answers to "what you're here to do", not "what you're owed". Highest-fit language of any theme with the existing Evelyn voice.

## 15. Shame and deserving — 399 concerns (3.8%), index 1.19, $12,927 per 1k

**What it is.** The problem located inside themselves. Not "money is blocked" but "I am the block": undeserving, a failure, a burden, letting people down.

**The persona — "I'm the reason."** Says the harsh thing about themselves before anyone else can — *loser*, *failure*, *useless*, *just existing*. Often generous to others and unable to receive. This is the most fragile group in the bucket and the one where a reading lands hardest, because being told the fault is not theirs is the thing they came for.

**Their words**

> "Obviously I'm just a loser" *(buyer)*

> "I feel like I'm letting everyone down every single day" *(buyer)*

> "I feel like a failure I keep messing things in my life I let opportunities pass me like I'm not even here" *(buyer)*

> "I appear to feel that I don't deserve things or relationships" *(buyer)*

> "I grew up with a lack mindset and my subconscious is ingrained with lack even though I try to tell myself I will receive." *(buyer)*

> "I feel useless and like a loser because of this lack" *(buyer)*

> "I have always felt that I am in the wrong place and time, that something makes me different from others… I feel like I have failed at life." *(non-buyer)*

**Handle with care.** Some of these edge into genuine distress ("I just have nothing left in my life to even think of living"). Worth a safety check on how the funnel responds to that phrasing — it should not be sold to.

## 16. Death, funerals, estates — 364 concerns (3.5%), index 1.30, $14,556 per 1k

**What it is.** A death caused the money problem. Three shapes: the income that died with the spouse, the funeral or care costs that emptied the account, and the inheritance being fought over or withheld. **The best-converting theme in the bucket** and the longest — average 211 characters, nearly double the bucket norm.

**The persona — "It changed the day they died."** Widowed, often recently, usually older. They can name the date. Frequently they spent their savings on the care and then lost the second income too. The estate sub-group is angry rather than sad — a changed will, a sibling who took it, a lawyer going nowhere for years.

**Their words**

> "I lost my husband suddenly 18 months ago, and we had just moved into this home, and without his income, I am barely hanging on" *(buyer)*

> "My husband died from complications of Multiple Sclerosis… I had to quit my job at 62 years old to care for him 24x7. I spent all of our savings to hire help in the last 9 months of his life." *(buyer)*

> "I need money for my Dad's funeral. It's in a month from now & there are so many things expected of me as the oldest child. I need money & urgently too." *(buyer)*

> "My grandads will. I was meant to get a lot of money and the will got changed." *(buyer)*

> "My friend recently died and promised me $10,000 because he knew I was struggling His girlfriend Kathy took the entire $30,000 and won't give me the $10,000 he promised me. He died without a will!." *(buyer)*

> "I'm afraid I won't have enough to retire comfortably… I lost a good part of the inheritance my Mother left me." *(buyer)*

> "I'm running out of money chasing a dream that psychics have told me is coming my way. I am $15,000 in debt because of this. I start doing this a year ago when my partner died unexpectedly." *(buyer)*

**Read that last quote twice.** Someone $15,000 into psychic spending since their partner died, saying so in the intake box, and buying again. Bereavement is the best-converting theme in this bucket precisely because grief plus money is the most exploitable state a person can be in. Whatever you do here, do it deliberately.

## Cross-cutting findings

**The longer they write, the more they buy.** Under 50 characters: 16% buy. Over 400: 38%. The intake box is the single best predictor in the dataset — more than any theme.

| Concern length | Conversations | Buy-rate |
|---|---:|---:|
| under 50 chars | 4,172 | 16.1% |
| 50–99 | 3,076 | 23.5% |
| 100–199 | 2,120 | 27.5% |
| 200–299 | 649 | 31.1% |
| 300+ | 571 | 36.2% |

Anything that makes people write more at that step is worth more than any theme-targeting change.

**They want relief, not riches.** When asked what life would look like with the money solved, the answer is almost never wealth. It is peace, sleep, breathing, and the absence of dread: *"It would feel like I'm free from a cage, because that's what I'm feeling now — being in a cage."* Wealth imagery (mansions, jets) appears but is a small minority.

**The money bucket is a love bucket too.** 11.7% of these concerns mention a partner, soulmate, or marriage unprompted. People pick one door and bring both problems through it.

**Specific belief signals over-convert.** Explicit block/curse language: 33% buy-rate. Prayer, God or the universe named: 33%. Lottery hope: 35%. "Someone told me money is coming": 39%. These are small pools but they identify people who already believe in the mechanism.

**Geography.** Money-bucket traffic is heavily non-US: Johannesburg, Lagos and Cape Town are three of the six largest cities. But Australian cities convert best (Sydney 30%, Melbourne 26%, Brisbane 22%) against 13–19% for most US cities. Auckland sits mid-pack at 19%.

**One in sixteen is in acute crisis** — homeless, no food, nothing in the account. They convert at the bucket average, which means the funnel is selling to them at the normal rate.

## Is the debt theme just a broke audience?

Reasonable worry, wrong variable. Broke people buy. What loses money is *bare* debt — debt stated with no story around it.

Revenue below is front-end plus order bump plus both upsells, divided by conversations, so it is what a thousand chats of that kind are actually worth.

| Segment | Conversations | Buy-rate | Upsell 1 take | Revenue per 1,000 chats |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| All money bucket | 10,514 | 22.6% | 14.1% | $10,714 |
| Debt and bills (all) | 3,189 | 21.0% | 12.7% | $9,621 |
| …in acute crisis (no food, empty account) | 467 | **23.8%** | 6.3% | $9,512 |
| …debt **and** an invisible block | 132 | 29.5% | 12.8% | **$13,485** |
| …debt **and** providing for family | 303 | 24.1% | **20.5%** | $12,597 |
| …debt **and** retirement | 160 | 25.6% | 9.8% | $10,825 |
| …debt **and nothing else said** | 1,089 | **17.3%** | 10.6% | **$7,544** |

**People with no money still buy.** The acutely broke — the ones who write "so many bills no money an no food" — convert at 23.8%, *above* the bucket average. They pay the $35 and stop; their upsell take is 6.3% against 14.1% overall. So they are not a bad audience, they are a one-purchase audience.

**The loss is in bare debt.** A third of the debt theme (1,089 chats) says only that money is short and nothing else. That segment buys at 17.3% and returns $7,544 per thousand — the worst measured segment in the bucket, 30% below average. It is not that they are poor. It is that "I have no money" gives the reading nothing to work with, so no block gets revealed and no story gets told.

**Add one frame and it flips.** The same debt, said by someone who also believes something is blocking them, is worth $13,485 per thousand — better than every theme except retirement and business. Debt plus family produces the highest upsell take of any segment measured (20.5%).

**Retirement makes the point.** The best-earning theme in the bucket ($15,146 per thousand) is people on fixed pensions who cannot cover rising costs. By income they are the poorest group here. They buy and they upsell — because they arrive with a story, a deadline, and someone to protect.

**So the rule is not "avoid broke people".** It is: acquire on the story, not the shortage. Every debt ad should carry a second element — the block, the family, the years of effort, the age. Ads that say only "struggling with money?" buy the worst third of this bucket.

The concern-length numbers say the same thing from another angle: 200+ characters returns $16,619 per thousand, under 50 characters returns $7,412. People who explain themselves are worth 2.2× the ones who don't, whatever their balance.

## Method and caveats

**How this was built.** All 12,640 money-bucket conversations were pulled from production. 10,514 had a stated concern of more than 8 characters. Themes were derived by reading ~250 concerns by hand (buyers and non-buyers separately) — not by word-frequency mining. Every one of the 10,514 was then classified against that theme list by Claude Opus 5, multi-label, up to three themes each. Concerns first marked unclassifiable were re-run with a stricter prompt, which recovered about a quarter of them.

**What to trust and what not to.**

- Theme sizes and buy-rates are computed on all rows, not a sample. They are solid to about a percentage point.
- Classification is a model's judgment, not a human's. I spot-checked roughly 50 rows by hand; the errors I found were concentrated in the vague bucket, which is why it was re-run. Small themes carry more relative error than large ones.
- 13.9% remains genuinely unclassifiable — greetings, names, "I need more money", replies to the reader. That is a fact about the intake box, not a gap in the analysis.
- Buy-rate is correlation. A theme that buys well may reflect who the ads brought in rather than what the theme does.
- The system's own `sub_bucket` labels (CAREER, FINANCIAL_STRESS, OPPORTUNITY, BLOCKED_ABUNDANCE) are keyword guesses written before anyone saw this data. They put 39% of the bucket in BLOCKED_ABUNDANCE; reading the actual words, the invisible block is 11.5%. Do not use those labels for anything that matters.

**Compliance flags for anything built from this.** Health and disability (6.5% of the bucket) are restricted personal attributes — do not target on them. Financial hardship claims need care under Meta's financial-status rules. Lottery and windfall themes must never be given an outcome, only a pattern. And the bereavement theme, the best converter here, is the one that most deserves a deliberate decision rather than a default yes.
