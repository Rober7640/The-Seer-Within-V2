## 1. What and why

The V1 close is 8 hardcoded messages, 132 words, in `handlePermission` (`useConversation.ts`).
Measured on Evelyn's own words, a session is **80% reading : 20% offer**, and the
commercial turn lands 63% of the way through.

**47–52% of non-buyers stop at PITCH** — the single biggest in-funnel leak, worth
~$8,117/30d at 25% recovery.

Against a standard close checklist, five of seven slots are empty: no mechanism, no price
justification, one line of proof, no objection pre-handling. Arm B fills **all five**.

**Honest limit:** buyers and non-buyers have the *same* 80:20 ratio today, so the data does
not show a thicker close converts better. It shows the offer is thin and the biggest leak
sits where a thin offer would hurt. This is a hypothesis, not a proven fix.

**The cost, in seconds.** Typing theater is `chars × 60ms`, capped at 5s, +0.4s between
bubbles (`lib/typing.ts`), so arm B is not just longer to read — it is longer to *wait
through*:

| | Bubbles | Before the buy button appears |
|---|---|---|
| Arm A | 8 | ~51s |
| Arm B | 26 | **~137s** |

Excludes the two AI segments, which run in both arms. 21 of arm B's 26 bubbles are long
enough to hit the 5-second cap. That is the "a longer close may tire her" risk with a
number on it. If it needs hedging before the start, the levers are a lower `baseSpeed` for
the deep arm or cutting the objections block (5 bubbles, ~27s, weakest evidence) — and
either must happen **before** the first exposure, never during.

## 3. Code changes — AS BUILT

The plan said four edit points. It took seven, because the arm needs a resolver of its
own (the framework has no generic "assign at lead capture" call — every V1 test has a
named resolver) and because the admin API refuses `v1_main_funnel` for an unlisted key.

| # | File | What |
|---|------|------|
| 1 | `client/src/types/chat.ts` | `closeDepth?: 'deep'` on `UserData` — the field the pitch reads |
| 2 | `shared/types.ts` | the same field on the server-side `UserData`, because the client posts the whole object to `/api/chat`. No prompt builder reads it |
| 3 | `server/lib/experiments.ts` | `V1_CLOSE_DEPTH_EXPERIMENT_KEY` + `resolveV1CloseDepth()`, shaped like `resolveV1Bump` |
| 4 | `server/lib/priceVariant.ts` | resolve + log the exposure inside `assignVariantIfMissing`, **above** the price idempotency guard, and return `closeDepth` on both paths |
| 5 | `server/routes.ts` | emit `closeDepth` on the `/api/lead` response, only when `deep` |
| 6 | `server/routes/admin/experiments.ts` | add the key to `V1_MAIN_FUNNEL_KEYS`, or create/start rejects the conversion type |
| 7 | `client/src/hooks/useConversation.ts` | capture it from the lead response · the `?close_depth=deep` preview · the pitch branch |

Plus `server/lib/closeDepth.test.ts` (9 tests) and the creation SQL.

### 3.1 Why the subject is `hashEmail(email)`, not the raw address

Every other email-keyed V1 test passes the raw address to `assign()` and hashes only at
`logExposure`. This one must not, because it is the first email-keyed test to set
`scope.freezeAssignment` — and the freeze looks the subject up in
`experiment_exposures.subject_id`, which stores the **hash**. Passing the raw address
would miss every time, so the freeze would silently degrade to plain re-bucketing and a
weight edit could move a woman who had already read the other close. Locked by a test.

### 3.2 Why it is resolved above the price idempotency guard

Same reason as the commitment gate and the order bump: below the guard, an arm can only
ever be drawn for an email with no stored price, so every returning visitor would land in
control. Safe above it because this arm is the purest UI-only one of the three — it
changes which chat bubbles are sent and touches no price, line item or CTA.

### 3.3 The pitch branch

`handlePermission`, `useConversation.ts`. `deep` is `chat.userData.closeDepth === 'deep'`.
**Arm A is byte-identical** — verified by extracting all 20 message literals from the
control path at `HEAD` and confirming each survives verbatim.

`deep` composes with the existing `woven` arm rather than overriding it (woven keeps its
naming line, deep swaps the ritual body). They cannot co-occur in live traffic — woven is
fb-palm-scoped, deep is fb-tarot — but both preview overrides can be set at once.

The price is **interpolated everywhere** (`$${pitchPrice}`), never spelled out. The first
draft wrote "Thirty-five dollars", which needed a `pitchPrice === 35` guard to stop a
`?close=55` preview or a funnel-wide rollout putting two different prices in one close.
Numerals removed the guard, match the "$35" in the bubble directly below, and are what a
woman skimming on a phone actually catches. Nothing in arm B is price-specific now.

### 3.4 The clearing gets its own materials — NOT the upsell's

"$35 covers what the night costs me" was the vague thing the price block existed to fix.

The first attempt itemised it with `UPSELL_RITUAL`'s kit (`client/src/lib/upsellMessages.ts`)
— altar, white candle, blessed water, sage, the 2–4am window — on a "one practice, not
two" argument. **That was wrong, on operator review, for two reasons:**

1. **It spends U1's reveal.** That kit is an 8-message curtain-lift she reaches on
   `/welcome1` about three minutes later, and part of why it lands is that it is the first
   time she sees behind the curtain.
2. **The clock invents a contradiction that does not exist today.** The close says the
   clearing takes 2–3 hours, tonight. U1 says its ritual runs "between 2 and 4am" and
   takes "about two hours". With no hour on the close, nobody can do that arithmetic;
   adding 2–4am hands it to them. The consistency argument had it backwards — the close
   described no materials and no clock, so there was nothing to contradict, only a gap.

What it does instead: the clearing's own verbs made touchable. It already says "sever that
**thread**" and "**seal** where it entered", so arm B gives it a length of black cord and
wax, in the **ritual** block where the step label already asks for sensory specificity.
Cord cutting is a practice this audience has a name for, so it reads as specific rather
than invented, and it costs U1 nothing. Two different workings should look different —
which also gives U1 a cleaner pitch: that was a cutting, this is a shield you can hold.

The price block then **points back** at the cord and the wax she has already watched being
used. Show it, then charge for it; introducing props at the moment of the ask is weaker.

🔴 **Things and hours, never a dollar breakdown.** "$9 of it is the candle" is a factual
claim about the business, and at this price an itemised total also makes the clearing feel
like the sum of its wax. What justifies $35 is that the materials are spent once and the
hours cannot be resold.

**Cut on operator review:** *"Because the women who need this most are usually the ones
who've already paid a great deal to be told nothing."* It builds solidarity, but it plants
"I paid a reader and got nothing" seconds before the buy button, and it delayed the block's
answer to its own "where it goes" promise.

## 4. Arm B copy — 26 messages, 448 words (measured, as built)

Every message stays inside Evelyn's 25-word limit — checked mechanically over the whole
pitch region; the only line above it is a pre-existing message on the retired
sliding-close arm. `${firstName}` interpolates as today.

const deep = chat.userData.closeDepth === 'deep'

// ── NEW BLOCK: mechanism. Why the ritual works — Hopkins' reason-why.

  "That's the whole of it. Nobody has ever said this thing out loud to you, so it kept its grip.",

## 5. The price block — INCLUDED, since this now runs first

Three messages, placed **before all three existing price messages** (not between them):

> §4 said "preceded by" and this section originally said "immediately before the
> guarantee line" — two different placements. Built as the first: it builds plain →
> sacred, where the other restarts a block that has already named the price.
> "The sacred offering is $35 — a declaration…" then closes it.

```ts
if (deep) await sendBotMessages([
  `Now the offering, ${firstName}. It's $${pitchPrice}. Let me tell you plainly where it goes.`,
  "The cord, the wax, and two to three hours of a night I can't give to anyone else. Spent once, for you.",
  `$${pitchPrice} covers the whole of it and keeps this honest. It's a sign you're ready. Not a wall.`,
])
```

It was cut in the first draft because `hours-55-35_tarot` owned that beat. Close depth now
runs first, so it owns the beat until it resolves and arm B is the full five-block
treatment — **~365 words, 26 messages**.

🔴 **If the order is ever reversed again, cut this block before starting, not during.**
Changing arm B's content mid-flight invalidates everything collected before the change.

**Objection wording must never name the downsell.** Pre-announcing that $25 exists has been
tested and lost — the sliding-scale arm was retired 2026-07-22 for exactly this. The block
argues *worth*, never discount. The $25 downsell stays reactive.

## 6. Evidence behind each block

| Block       | Basis                                                                                                                                                              |
|-------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Mechanism   | Hopkins' reason-why. Absent today — the ritual is asserted, never explained                                                                                         |
| Deliverable | Ogilvy's specificity. "5-7 pages" is a size, not a promise                                                                                                          |
| Proof       | Believable beats impressive. Reframes the result as small and concrete                                                                                              |
| Objections  | **Mined, not invented.** 517 non-buying sessions: price 23–32% in every hook family, "let me think" 3–8%. *"What if I feel nothing?"* was said **zero times** — an earlier draft answered it |

🔴 **Weakest-evidenced block: objections.** Only ~30% of non-buyers say anything at all at
the offer; the other 70% read it and leave. Pre-handling reaches the ones who would have
spoken *unless* the silent majority hold the same objection — which is the standard premise
of pre-handling, and **a premise, not a finding**. If arm B has to shrink, cut this first.
