# France: rules that change copy

Not legal advice. v2 after Codex's review (2026-11-18), which corrected v1 (abus de faiblesse,
L121-8, covers soliciting at home and similar cases; it is not a general ban on selling to
vulnerable people). The article numbers below are Codex's citations and have not been checked by
a lawyer. The operator chose no native or legal review of the language; these points are about
the offer itself, so they go to the operator as decisions.

## For the translator (applies to every segment)

- **Translate faithfully, add nothing.** No stronger fear words, no extra urgency, no new
  deadline, no new claim about Evelyn, her work or her clients.
- **No promised outcome** (misleading practices, L121-2). Evelyn senses and suggests; she never
  guarantees a return, a result or a date.
- **No health claims.** Nothing heals, cures or treats, under any verb.
- **Tag the risk, don't fix it.** When a line carries one of the risks below, translate it as
  it is and set `risk` so the review page groups it for the operator:
  - `urgency`: a limit on time, places or availability ("tonight only", "limited spots").
  - `danger`: what happens to her, her family or her life if she doesn't buy ("the shadow
    will grow").
  - `claim`: a fact that needs proof (customer counts, testimonials, Evelyn's biography, her
    doing the ritual by hand).
  - `ai-identity`: Evelyn presenting herself as a human.
  - `consent`: e-mail, text or tracking opt-ins, and withdrawal / refund wording.

## For the operator: decisions before any French traffic

1. **AI disclosure.** Under the EU AI Act (Art. 50(1), applicable since 2 Aug 2026), a person
   chatting with an AI system must be told so, unless it is obvious. In the French chat Evelyn
   is an AI persona. Decide how and where French buyers are told.
2. **False scarcity.** Saying something is available only for a very short time, or in limited
   numbers, when that isn't true is banned outright (L121-4 7°). The French copy must use only
   limits that are real.
3. **Danger if she doesn't buy.** Inaccurate claims about a risk to her or her family if she
   doesn't buy are banned outright (L121-4 12°). Exploiting a serious misfortune such as
   bereavement or distress can make a sale an aggressive practice (L121-6 / L121-7). A
   faithful translation doesn't make these lines lawful. They need rewriting in French, not
   only a label.
4. **Claims that need proof.** Customer counts ("2,400+"), testimonials (fake reviews are
   banned, L121-4 28°), Evelyn's lineage, and her performing each ritual personally.
5. **Checkout and withdrawal.**
   - Prices are TTC, in euros.
   - The pay button states the obligation to pay (L221-14).
   - The order bump starts unticked.
   - Classify each offer as a service (reading, ritual) or digital content. A service loses
     the 14-day withdrawal right only once fully performed, after an express request and an
     acknowledgement (L221-25, L221-28 1°). Digital content needs prior consent,
     acknowledgement and a confirmation (L221-28 13°, L221-13).
   - Physical items (stone, bracelet) keep 14 days from receipt.
   - From 19 June 2026, online contracts need a free online withdrawal function.
   - Before payment, recap the deliverable, total price, timing and delivery. For the chat,
     that means the price per minute and how credit is used.
   - The 30-day refund promise must be described separately from statutory rights.
6. **E-mail and tracking consent (CNIL).**
   - Marketing e-mails to a prospect need prior consent, never pre-ticked, with an easy way
     to unsubscribe.
   - The Facebook pixel needs cookie consent, and refusing must be as easy as accepting.
